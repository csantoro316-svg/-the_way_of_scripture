## Hebrews 3:1–6

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). John 5:45–47 expressly connects Moses' testimony with Christ; Ephesians 2:19–22 places the household within God's forming a people with Christ as cornerstone.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baseline's statement that holy brothers/heavenly calling necessarily includes every ethnic Jew in the same sense is an interpretive assertion, not a lexical proof the author addresses only unbelievers. Text's Christian confession/calling retained. No denial that mixed audiences may exist; neither their exact membership nor every individual's faith is known. Moses is honored as faithful. No universal hierarchy that Jews always rate Moses over Abraham, derogatory religious caricature, or assumption every reader was personally offering temple sacrifices is treated as history.

Builder image genuinely differs among checked commentaries: Calvin/Wesley identify Christ as builder, USCCB stresses God founder and Christ Son over house. Not presented as a difference over Christ's deity. No owner equation of Moses' house with only ethnic Israel and Son's with an entirely unrelated house. Holding fast remains an explicit condition; no conclusion about the owner's perseverance doctrine originated. BSB verse-6 footnote wording not silently moved to main text.

## Hebrews 3:7–11

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Hebrews 4:6–11 itself develops the still-offered rest beyond Joshua's arrival. Luke 9:30–31 brings Moses/Elijah alongside Christ's work at Jerusalem. Connection is explicit NT use; not a detailed hidden code for every wilderness event.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Psalm 95's Massah/Meribah testing must not be flattened into only one incident in Numbers 13–14. Numbers 14's rejection of the land is also relevant, but not the entire background of the psalm. No claim the psalm itself lists ten distinct tests. Its Hebrew word for wandering does not automatically mean direct demonic seduction; no unchecked word-study chain from astray to Satan's speech inserted.

God's anger is explicit; a universal rule that God never expresses a particular negative term toward His children was not independently verified. No certainty every wilderness adult was eternally lost; Numbers identifies exceptions and Hebrews later praises Moses. No modern political or cultural label substituted for a textual explanation of unbelief.

## Hebrews 3:12–15

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Hebrews 10:19–25 directly joins Christ-given access, holding hope, assembly and encouragement; Ephesians 4:11–16 shows the body's mutual work toward mature faith.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baseline insists warning can only address never-believing attendees and turns its call into a special assignment to expose posers. Today expresses real urgency, not a numerically known deadline or permission to declare every hesitant seeker beyond grace. Encouragement can include gospel teaching without being reduced to one mandatory sermon or altar-call format. No rule forbidding practical care, justice, healing prayer or community life is derived from the teacher's list of messages he rejects. Such care is not by itself the whole gospel, but Hebrews does not issue that blanket prohibition here.

## Hebrews 3:16–19

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). 1 Corinthians 10:1–13 explicitly uses the same wilderness generation as warning to Christians while affirming God's faithfulness and help in temptation.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Main BSB verse 16 is a rhetorical question about the generation; Calvin's older version some/not all is not substituted. Numbers 14's Joshua/Caleb/next-generation exceptions are real. The warning concerns the rebellious generation corporately; not every individual's eternal fate can be inferred from death outside Canaan. Fresh Hebrews 11:24–29 explicitly praises Moses and the Red Sea crossing by faith.

Baseline claims unbelievers cannot remain comfortable in church indefinitely without collective divine judgment, and that every church must force an immediate visible response; these institutional instructions exceed this group and are not made site policy. Hearing, belonging to the traveling group and receiving earlier deliverance do not make the call to continued trust unnecessary; that is the stated warning. Neither a new owner salvation test nor a decree on the eternal state of every Israelite is originated.
