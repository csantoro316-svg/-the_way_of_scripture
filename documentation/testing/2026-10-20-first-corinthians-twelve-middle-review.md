## 1 Corinthians 12:8–11

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Acts 4:29–31 explicitly places signs and healing alongside bold witness through Jesus’ name, illuminating gifts within the mission rather than personal status.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s exact wisdom/knowledge definitions are not adopted as lexical certainties. Neither the passage nor a plural noun proves every healer is restricted to particular diseases. Its insistence that every genuine healing must be instantaneous is not treated as consensus; Wesley explicitly allows healing alongside natural remedies. No promised cure or reason to avoid treatment is supplied. The baseline’s four-category prophecy scheme, inference that Hebrews 1/Revelation 22 independently date the canon’s closure, exact final-apostle chronology and universal end of three prophecy types are not established here and are omitted. The general question of continuation is fairly sourced, not settled as a site position. Tongues are not lexically restricted to a single disputed interpretation without directly reading its relevant entry and chapter 14. The baseline’s claim that every list includes tongues is not made a universal rule; Romans 12 is an actual counterexample. Gift lists are not an exhaustive skills test or a guarantee every claimed ability is a spiritual gift. Ephesians’ gifts/roles distinction is not used to deny the chapter’s explicit gift language. No exact dating of three letters as different decades is introduced. Claims about particular modern religious founders and automatic identification of every claimed insight as demonic are not adopted. No doctrine that discerners are invariably first to detect fraud is inferred.

## 1 Corinthians 12:12–13

Plan section: included, because (a): Ephesians 2:11–22 explicitly identifies Christ’s blood, one Spirit, common access to the Father and one household across the Jewish/Gentile division.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Catholic sacramental incorporation, Calvin’s Spirit-effective baptism, Wesley’s baptism connection and evangelical spiritual incorporation are distinguished accurately. The Assemblies of God explicitly treats 12:13 as conversion/incorporation; its further empowerment teaching is not misrepresented as excluding less-experienced believers from Christ. The baseline’s passive-verb argument is not made to settle all questions of human response or the order of conversion. No interpretation of every baptism passage is imported wholesale. The owner’s Baptism study supplies relevant further reading but not a direct settled interpretation of 12:13. Jews/Greeks/slave/free are the categories Paul actually gives; modern organizational membership or a specific gift is not added as a condition of belonging.

## 1 Corinthians 12:14–20

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): 1 Peter 4:8–11 explicitly connects varied grace and each person’s gift with service and God’s glory through Jesus.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The body comparison does not prove one fixed gift assigned at conversion can never develop or change. The baseline says gifts cannot be sought, but Paul later explicitly tells believers to desire gifts; no blanket prohibition on asking is imported. No guarantee every local congregation always has every needed skill internally is made. A physical-organ-to-church-office chart is not established as Paul’s intended scheme. Wesley’s suggested identifications are labeled possibilities in his own note, not adopted as required meanings. Spiritual value is not calculated by attention, results or comparison with a famous member. No unverified ancient body-rhetoric history or modern medical anatomy claim is used.

## 1 Corinthians 12:21–26

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Philippians 2:1–11 directly grounds regard for others in Christ’s humility, service and exaltation, adding a Christ-centered setting to mutual care.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Indispensable is not inflated into a new ranking that the weak are always the most important and everyone else has no worth except as a helper. The baseline’s restriction of weakness to needing someone’s particular gift is not imposed; Paul’s principle is broader than that classification. Need is not someone’s permanent identity or a reason to treat people as instruments for another’s reward. No claim losing a task or ability means a person ceases to have purpose or belonging. Suffering together does not require every member to have identical emotions or a medically identical experience. The shared life is not asserted to be the exclusive purpose for which believers remain on earth.
