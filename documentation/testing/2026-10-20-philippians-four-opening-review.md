## Philippians 4:1–3

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). John 17:20–26 connects the unity of future believers with witness to the Father’s sending of the Son and being with Him. Revelation 21:22–27 supplies the wider final setting of the Lamb’s Book of Life and life in God’s presence, beyond the immediate disagreement.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Neither the subject of the women’s disagreement nor the identity or office of the helper is specified. The baseline’s suggestion of a pastor or elder and Wesley’s suggestion of Silas are not made public facts. The BSB footnote allows Syzygus as a proper name; USCCB also recognizes the uncertainty. Clement is not confidently identified with the later bishop of Rome. The women’s actual gospel partnership is preserved without inferring a complete account of church office from these verses.

The baseline presents a fixed taxonomy of five crowns and takes the Book of Life here as proof of an entire once-saved-always-saved account. Those are broader interpretive systems, not established by the wording of this paragraph alone. Wesley’s warning about possible erasure shows that the baseline is not consensus. This short reconciliation page does not settle that separate dispute or assign the owner a position on it. Paul’s positive description of these coworkers is kept. No invented biography, exact imprisonment venue, ten/eleven-year interval, trial schedule or legal status is needed.

## Philippians 4:4–7

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). John 14:25–31 explicitly connects the Spirit’s coming, Jesus’ departure and His gift of peace amid coming trouble. Philippians 3:20–21 supplies the distinct future horizon of bodily transformation by the same Savior. This supports the relationship between present peace and future rescue without imposing a rapture timetable.
Position source: What Does Prayer Look Like?, section 6, lines 128–132.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The Lord’s nearness is not treated as lexical proof of a pretribulation sequence. The checked USCCB note recognizes both an approaching-return reading and an emphasis on present nearness. Modern punctuation in NASB versus ESV cannot establish the original sentence structure by itself; no argument rests on a semicolon. No psychological diagnosis or judgment about another person’s faith is inferred from distress. The invitation to pray and the promised peace are preserved without a blanket demand for instant emotional calm.

Plan section: included, because (c). Romans 12:1–3 joins renewed thinking with the whole person offered to God in response to mercy; Romans 8:29–30 gives the larger purpose of likeness to the Son and coming glory. This adds the direction of God’s renewing work beyond a list of moral qualities.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline discusses the meanings of every listed virtue, but no unverified Greek claim or grammatical inference from the plural whatever is reproduced. The shared vocabulary with ancient moral teaching noted by USCCB is not necessary to explain the text and is not expanded into an unresearched historical account. These verses are not presented as a technique for obtaining any imagined result or as a denial of the real difficulties Paul names elsewhere in the letter.
