# Genesis 4–5: Cain to Noah editorial evidence

Date: 2026-10-18. Standard: [Passage Editorial Standard](../architecture/PASSAGE_EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md). Six groups; complete drafted sections are in their source JSON records. No site position is invented.

## Baseline and research

Read Genesis baseline lessons 4A–4C (combined PDF pages 91–113), lesson 5 (114–122), and the Genesis 5:28–32 portion of lesson 6A (123–124). PDF: `content/assets/reference/stephen_armstrong/old-testament/01-genesis.pdf`, checksum retained in the collection catalog. Paid particular attention to faith/offering, Cain versus Christ and brother-love, the family line, image/likeness, long lives/death, Enoch/transformation, and Noah/comfort connections. The public prose is original and does not follow the PDF's lecture outline or copy its translation.

## Genesis 4:1–16

- Plan section: included, because (a). Hebrews 12:24 explicitly compares Abel's and Jesus' blood; 1 John 3:11–18 explicitly contrasts Cain's taking life with Jesus' giving His life and applies it to the family of believers. This adds beyond the common-reading section's explanation of Abel's faith.
- Position source: none. No developed site study addressing the offering/murder/sign was found in the source search. Exact approved no-position wording; existing empty deeper-study list retained.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's assertion that Eve announced she had birthed God is not established by BSB Genesis 4:1 or the checked acquisition entry; excluded. Her private intentions and later naming Abel to admit a mistake are not stated; excluded. The assertion that Abel also brought grain is not proved by also or plural gifts in Hebrews 11; no unrecorded ritual is reconstructed. Grain automatically meaning tithe and animal automatically meaning a pre-Mosaic commanded atonement ceremony are not established here; excluded. The unusual translation of verse 7 as a waiting sacrificial animal and a command to desire Christ as Master is not supported by the checked BDB entry/context and is not printed as consensus. The dictionary's sin-offering sense is acknowledged without importing that rendering. Cain's mark/sign appearance, a population of 20,000–30,000, a first burial inferred from blood entering the ground, and a final sentence fixing Cain beyond possible forgiveness are not established by the checked text; excluded. The statement that all early surviving Hebrew witnesses include the field invitation was not directly manuscript-verified; not asserted. All historical/physiological details would need independent sources before use.

## Genesis 4:17–24

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). The seven/seventy-seven vengeance and forgiveness reversal is labeled a comparison, not an explicit Matthew identification; Romans 12 supplies the Bible-wide refusal of personal revenge. Textual alternative in Matthew's number acknowledged.
- Position source: none. Exact approved wording. No developed site study settles Lamech's boast or the family record.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's blanket rule that east always means unrighteousness and west righteousness is not established by Scripture's diverse directional uses and is excluded. Cain's wife's identity and original genetic safety are not supplied by this passage; no speculative family member or medical explanation is asserted. Motives for naming Enoch, meanings assigned to every name/wife, fixed roles of the two wives, and seven as proof of completed moral corruption are not established by checked lexicons/texts and are excluded. The lecture briefly says two victims, then one parallel-described victim; the public text does not invent a second incident. No claim that music/metalworking are intrinsically unbelieving or that every member of a genealogy has the same spiritual state. Earliest-poem dating and specific timeline claims lack independent historical verification and are not used.

## Genesis 4:25–26

- Plan section: included, because (a). Luke 3 explicitly includes Seth and Enosh in Jesus' ancestry; the family continues after Abel's murder, without automatic inherited faith.
- Position source: none. Exact wording; no developed site position on the beginning-of-calling statement.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: exact delay, Eve's age-dependent thoughts, and God withholding all other children until a replacement are not stated; Genesis 5:4 records other sons/daughters. A precise worship institution/first service location/leader is not given; Wesley's public-worship reading is attributed, not turned into an established historical reconstruction. Baseline word-count numerology as proof of authorship is not independently established by the checked sources and is omitted. No conclusion that all Seth's descendants are regenerate.

## Genesis 5:1–17

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Luke 3 directly traces Jesus through this line; Genesis 9:6 and James 3:9–10 preserve the larger human-dignity theme rather than repeating only the list's death notices.
- Position source: none. Source studies were searched for the relevant chapters/people; no developed treatment of these particular records. Exact wording and existing relationships retained.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's claim that Genesis 1 describes likeness but not image is contradicted by Genesis 1:26–27 and BDB 6754; excluded. Its rigid pattern/nature versus bodily-appearance lexical split is not established by BDB 1823/6754. The stronger claim that all relation to God's image/likeness is absent after sin conflicts with Genesis 9:6 and James 3:9; not adopted. Genetic deterioration as the scientific explanation of long lives is not verified by independent scientific sources and is excluded. Speculative lifespans, current averages, Adam's hundreds of children, exact judgment psychology, calendar calculations, and transmission of a particular physical sin substance are not established by this passage and are not asserted. Long ages are reported as the text gives them, without manufacturing a tradition-wide scientific consensus.

## Genesis 5:18–24

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(b). Hebrews 11:5 explicitly interprets being taken without death; 11:39–40 places early faith within God's fuller purpose. 1 Corinthians 15's transformation is a labeled parallel, not a schedule deduced from Enoch.
- Position source: none. Exact wording; no owner-developed Enoch/end-times position is supplied.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Enoch's age as a coded complete number of church believers, a necessary pre-tribulation timetable from being taken before the flood, and contemporary-life overlap as an established comparison with Lamech are not established by the checked NT texts. Not asserted. The exact conversion date, mechanics/location of his transfer, and predictions read into all names are not supplied in Genesis. The baseline's reduction of the whole phrase about God taking him to just two Hebrew words is not needed or asserted. The walk/faith connection is grounded in Genesis and Hebrews rather than a fanciful dictionary gloss.

## Genesis 5:25–32

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Hebrews 11:7 directly explains Noah's faithful response and God's preservation; the ground/comfort hope continues into Genesis 9 and final curse removal in Revelation 22.
- Position source: none. Baptism's source discusses the later ark/baptism connection, not a conclusion on these genealogical verses or Noah's naming. No broader position is borrowed from it. Exact no-position wording.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Methuselah's name definitely meaning when he dies it will be sent is not established by BDB 4968; the entry instead offers conjectural alternatives. Excluded, including a definite prophecy/date from that name. Arithmetic claims about his precise death in relation to the flood are not used to establish how he died. Lamech mistakenly thinking Noah was the Messiah and receiving an unrecorded divine revelation are not stated; excluded. Noah as rest from earning salvation is a suggested analogy in the PDF, not Genesis's stated explanation of toil from cursed ground; the public plan follows the verified preservation/restoration theme rather than equating farming with an attempt to earn salvation. No interpretation of birth order depends on three simultaneous births, and Wesley's erroneous age wording in his verse 32 comment is not copied.

## Editorial and publication checks

Manual clarity review: the prose explains the family movement, practical lessons, and plan connections in ordinary language. Hebrew words are included where they clarify a real interpretive question, with script/transliteration/pronunciation/plain meaning and actual lexicon links. Common readings stay brief where no useful primary-documented tradition disagreement is necessary. All six positions remain open, and no baseline teacher/ministry identity appears publicly.

The three Genesis 5 quotation blocks were normalized directly from BibleHub BSB with their paragraph starts retained. Verification then exposed a checker bug: it removed every numeral, including ages such as 130 and 800, while attempting to remove printed verse labels. The checker now compares the complete quotation with either the exact numbered or unnumbered BibleHub text, preserving all numeric content. A regression test rejects missing/changed ages and incorrect verse labels. All 14 reviewed Genesis 1–5 groups pass this stricter check. No claim about a pre-existing Genesis 5 wording error is based on the old numeric-stripping comparison. Generated/source checks, browser review, preservation, and release results are recorded in the change record.
