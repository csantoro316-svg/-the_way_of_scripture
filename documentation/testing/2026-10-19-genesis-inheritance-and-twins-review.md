## Genesis 25:1–6

Plan section: included, because (c). Galatians 3:26–29 develops Abraham's heirs through Christ across peoples; Ephesians 1:13–14 and Hebrews 9:15 develop the inheritance still awaited and secured through Christ. These add the Bible-wide reach rather than repeating only the distribution of property.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: PDF identifies all concubines and their children as slaves with a universal fixed legal status; no independent history confirming that rule was obtained, omitted. Exact marriage/birth chronology beyond the passage's placement, unknown daughters, Keturah's former household job, and exact settlement motives are not stated in Genesis and not independently confirmed. Universal claims that inheritance can never be transferred during life are not established here; Genesis explicitly distinguishes gifts before death and Isaac's inheritance without a complete legal code. Hebrews 9:15–17 explains Christ/new-covenant inheritance and a will analogy; it does not explicitly call Abraham's covenant Jesus' personal last will, nor establish every theological inference in the PDF. The public page retains the actual Christ/inheritance connection without that expanded claim. No fixed identification of every Keturah descendant with modern Arab nations, total later assimilation, or claim all God's kindness to other sons ends after the first generation. Abraham/Isaac as Father/Son is not a reason to say Abraham literally has only one son here. Contemporary lottery anecdotes and reward formulas were not needed and were not independently verified or reproduced.

## Genesis 25:7–11

Plan section: included, because (c). Hebrews 11:13–16 supplies the faithful waiting through death theme; Jesus' resurrection teaching names Abraham in Matthew 22:31–32. Jesus is correctly described as discussing God's words to Moses, not explicitly explaining this burial.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF's physiological explanation of patriarchal longevity is not independently proved, omitted. Abraham's contentment does not reveal every inward thought or prove wealth played no part in his feeling; no exhaustive psychological account. Isaac and Ishmael bury him together, but neither Genesis nor Wesley's conjecture proves a complete reconciliation or its timing. Gathered to his people is not made proof of a precise intermediate-state location, chronology, or one tradition's complete resurrection scheme. Isaac's blessing continues after death, but that narrative sequence alone is not proof of universal inheritance law. No conclusion that God only begins blessing Isaac at that point, since chapter 24 already concerns His care and promise.

## Genesis 25:12–18

Plan section: omitted, because none apply. The relevant earlier promise/fulfillment is explained in context and common reading; adding the same material again would not add a distinct Bible-wide connection.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF treats east as invariably evil, all descendants as enemies rejected from grace, and all pre-Christ Gentiles as outside any divine grace. Genesis 17 expressly promises Ishmael blessing; direction/ancestry do not prove those claims, omitted. No mapping of each name onto modern national populations or permanent moral description of every descendant. The BSB wording hostility is retained without pretending every tradition/translation renders the underlying expression identically. Wesley's different rendering in his final note is not silently substituted for the verified BSB. His mention of Midian among Ishmael's descendants is not repeated: Genesis 25:2 instead lists Midian under Keturah. No assertion of an exact modern map beyond Genesis' stated landmarks.

## Genesis 25:19–28

Plan section: included, because (a). Romans 9:6–13 explicitly uses the twins when explaining why God's word has not failed; 9:24–33 carries the explanation into people called from Jews and Gentiles and righteousness by faith. The common section fairly distinguishes documented readings, while the plan follows Paul's stated movement rather than treating one system as the site's conclusion.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Twenty years between marriage/birth follows stated ages 40/60, but PDF's claim Isaac first decided to pray only after twenty years is not given, omitted. Wesley instead suggests prolonged prayer; neither unreported chronology is stated as fact. No claim every infertility or hardship is God withholding a gift until the sufferer asks, or every faithful prayer guarantees conception. Rebekah's exact method of inquiring, any visit to Melchizedek/Urim/teraphim, and God's exhaustive hidden motives are not specified. Number two is not an invariable prophecy code. No claim Esau's name literally means red; the text links Edom with red stew, while the lexical/name derivation is a separate question. BDB tam has contextual and moral senses; using the word to certify every action of Jacob as upright exceeds its evidence. Noah/Abraham's descriptions use related forms and are not a license to flatten their contexts. Jacob is not declared weak, physically unmanly, or a thief at birth; neither his occupation nor the heel wordplay establishes those stereotypes. Hunting is not intrinsically evil because Nimrod also hunts. No proof Esau already owned a fixed farm/territory, or that ordinary property ownership forfeits eternal inheritance. The text says God spoke to Rebekah, but not that Isaac never heard or she withheld it. Behold is not proof of Isaac's private surprise. Her sole motive for favoring Jacob is not exhaustively known. The promised roles of the twins/nations are not silently equated with a site-approved declaration of both individuals' eternal destinies. Catholic grace/free-response principles are stated only as broader teaching, not a fabricated verse-specific council ruling. No distinct Orthodox/Lutheran exegesis verified that adds a necessary separate reading here; none invented.

## Genesis 25:29–34

Plan section: included, because (a). Hebrews 12:14–17 explicitly names Esau's sale in its warning about grace/holiness; 12:22–24 gives the readers' heavenly-city/firstborn/new-covenant setting, adding the larger purpose beyond one hungry man's decision.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No independent source proves the PDF's universal death penalty for breaking such an oath, a single witness always sufficient, or every patriarch automatically legally bound by a sale. The narrative itself clearly states the oath/sale; extra legal reconstruction excluded. Esau's exact physical danger, sarcastic tone, every alternate meal in the camp, and Jacob's bowl gestures/inner calculations are not reported. Neither the bargain nor the tam adjective proves complete approval of Jacob's ethics; Wesley's own criticism is identified fairly. The PDF's claim Isaac's inheritance had little worth or no land cannot be inferred from Esau being a hunter and conflicts with the story's emphasis on substantial wealth; omitted. No exact total contents of the birthright or mechanically transferable control over God's choices are invented. Hebrews' warning is preserved without originating a position on whether it describes loss of salvation, loss of rewards only, or every later repentance question. The words support a serious warning and enduring consequences without requiring those additional systems.
