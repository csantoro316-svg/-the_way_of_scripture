FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s three-stage process of elimination is not treated as a lexical proof that only one tradition’s full account of salvation can be in view. English work out versus work for is not a self-sufficient Greek argument. The passage’s actual pairing of God’s work and the readers’ response is explained, with the doctrinal differences sourced directly.

The lesson’s passing Psalm 110:10 citation is a typo for the quoted Psalm 111:10; no mistaken reference is copied. Its claim that every weak response must trigger a specific corrective circumstance is not established here. Nor is a pastor or teacher said to contribute nothing simply because God supplies the power: the same chapter commends real human service. The teacher’s personal definition of prayer is not imposed on the owner’s prayer studies.

No guaranteed favorable reaction, defeat of every false allegation or claim that all suffering is caused by the sufferer’s failure to submit is made. The Bible’s light image is retained without saying that every grieved or frightened person is deliberately hiding from truth. Claims that the pillar of fire’s particular purpose was to repel wild animals were not independently verified and are omitted. An exact reward schedule or rapture chronology is not supplied.

The fresh Exodus passage establishes drink offerings accompanying regular offerings. It does not independently establish that every priest concluded every sacrifice by pouring wine at the same physical place. No universal ritual sequence is asserted. Paul’s possible pouring out is connected with costly service without inventing a certain immediate execution date.
