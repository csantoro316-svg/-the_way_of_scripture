## 1 Corinthians 15:1–4

Plan section: included, because (a): Luke 24:44–47 explicitly connects Scripture’s fulfillment in Christ’s suffering/resurrection with forgiveness proclaimed to all nations.
Position source: The Gospel According to Jesus — content/studies/the_gospel_according_to_jesus.md lines 33–49; core content only.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s claim first importance cannot mean highest importance was not independently established and is not imposed. Its precise account of Chloe’s report and alternate messages is not recorded here and is omitted. Vain is not lexically defined as never having believed at all; the warning and documented readings are distinguished. The source study’s central gospel conclusion is used without originating an owner position on final perseverance. No exact early date, formal-credal composition history or claim about where Paul personally received every phrase is asserted without verification. No generic assertion every religious conversation except one fixed presentation is useless is imported. The baseline’s historical swoon-theory narrative, exactly seventy-two hours in the tomb and claim that no other person ever returned from death are unverified or overbroad and omitted. No disparaging names for other religious adherents are reproduced.

## 1 Corinthians 15:5–11

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Galatians 1:13–24 explicitly places Paul’s gracious calling in the purpose of proclaiming the Son among the Gentiles and the churches glorifying God.
Position source: Who Shepherds the Church? — content/studies/who_shepherds_the_church.md lines 64–76, limited to James in verse 7.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The list’s final appearance to Paul is not expanded into proof Jesus never subsequently appeared in any vision. No account of the five hundred’s location, exact date or availability for modern interview is invented. The baseline identifies the Twelve appearance with the first evening and includes Matthias; John 20 explicitly lacks Thomas that evening, and Matthias is selected later in Acts 1. The Twelve is treated as the recognized group name without an invented headcount or exact meeting identification. No claim every New Testament writer was an apostle who personally saw Jesus, or that Paul wrote a numerical majority of the New Testament’s words, is adopted. No inference the lack of records about an apostle proves laziness or disobedience. Untimely birth is verified, but no exact medical or sexual metaphor is forced beyond Paul’s stated unworthiness/persecution. Grace and work are both retained without importing Calvin’s combative polemic or deciding every question of free will. The James position retains the owner’s likely qualification and does not make a ruling on the rest of the list.

## 1 Corinthians 15:12–19

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Jesus explicitly uses God’s relation to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob to affirm resurrection in Luke 20:37–38, connecting life beyond death with God’s faithful purpose.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Paul states some deny resurrection; their exact philosophical school, former occupation and every motive are not recorded. USCCB’s Greek/gnostic background suggestions and Wesley’s philosopher suggestion are not treated as established history. The hypothetical consequences are distinguished from Paul’s own affirmation in the next section. The actual 622 entry does not make perish an unambiguous lexical proof of nonexistence, so no new annihilation/intermediate-state doctrine is originated. The baseline’s C. S. Lewis formulation was not checked against its primary publication and is not quoted. The plan’s patriarch connection does not establish a complete future land/kingdom schedule as an owner position. Future hope is not reduced to a precise allocation of earthly rewards or used to deny every present benefit of Christian life.
