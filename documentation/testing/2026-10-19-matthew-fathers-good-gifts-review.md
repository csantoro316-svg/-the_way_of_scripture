## Per-group decisions

### Matthew 7:7–11 — Ask the Father for What Is Good

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Luke 11:9–13 is a closely related parallel naming the Spirit; Ephesians 1:13–14 places the promised Spirit within gospel faith, inheritance, and final redemption. The pledge explanation adds future assurance beyond the immediate parent-child illustration. No claim Luke is formally quoting or correcting Matthew.
- Position source: Prayer and Faith / What Does Prayer Look Like?.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's assertion this cannot concern prayer at all is not the common reading of the checked primary documents or Luke's prayer setting; not presented as consensus. Its claim every normal prayer interpretation must guarantee every requested item is a false restriction; the cited primary documents explicitly qualify prayer by God's will. No demeaning diagnostic analogy about Jesus returning to a subject, universal judgment of all unbelievers, or survey/political aside. Present imperatives do not by themselves prove an unending action or automatic outcome. No promise every parent always grants every good request or that every harmful experience was requested in error. No inference every failure against sin proves one never asked or preferred it. The Spirit's help is explained without inventing an instant cure or guaranteed private instructions. The physical gift comparison is retained; the site's spiritual focus is identified as its actual sourced position.

### Matthew 7:11 — The Father's Good Gifts

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Luke 11:13's Spirit promise, Romans 8:14–17's family relationship/cry to Father, and Ephesians 1:13–14's inheritance pledge link the gift with redemption, present belonging, and future hope. The texts support the wider connection without forcing a particular order of spiritual experiences.
- Position source: Prayer and Faith / What Does Prayer Look Like?.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: no universal claim all parents behave lovingly or that suffering proves Fatherly rejection. The comparison is from imperfect human giving to the Father's greater goodness. No replacement of Matthew's good things by Luke's Holy Spirit in the BSB quotation, and no inaccurate attribution of domata agatha to the Father's clause. No new site doctrine about when or how every Christian receives the Spirit, a second baptism, or a particular miraculous manifestation. The study's Spirit-centered reading is retained without originating an exclusivist lexical proof that the word good can refer only to spiritual gifts. Modern contract mechanics and precise future allotments are not inferred from Ephesians' pledge metaphor.
