## Philemon 1:8–22

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Shared identity across slave/free in Galatians 3/Colossians 3; limited reconciliation/cost parallel with 2 Corinthians 5; Joseph providence parallel from Genesis 50. Paul's maybe is distinguished from Joseph's stated outcome. No claim the NT explicitly names Onesimus as a type of Christ, that Paul's repayment atones for sin, or that this establishes an owner theory of imputation.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Verse 18's if does not establish a theft report, exact amount, motive or route. The text does not state the prison city, first-meeting scene, date or location of Philemon's conversion, nor that Paul discovered the ownership only after conversion. Baseline reconstructions were checked against the actual letter and remain unconfirmed; excluded. No independently checked Roman statute establishes a categorical legal duty to return this person, an automatic death sentence, or the claim that any Christian must obey every civil order regardless of its content. No current legal or political prescription inserted.

The baseline's descriptions of rising blood pressure, neighborhood pressure, Paul internally wanting wrongdoing, mandatory emotional enthusiasm as the meaning of more, and what most commentators supposedly think are not explicit text or independently supported consensus. Excluded. Paul expresses confidence; we do not override it by asserting we know he secretly expected rejection. Asking rather than commanding does not mean Paul threatened to ask God to coerce Philemon. Universal claims that doing right always feels wrong, that ordinary productive work is spiritually useless, or that every harm conceals a better near-term outcome exceed this text.

Paul writes the repayment undertaking himself; that does not establish that he physically wrote every word of this entire letter, never wrote elsewhere, had no money, or executed a particular enforceable Roman contract. USCCB confirms accounting terminology, not every proposed legal consequence. Personal anecdotes, consumer references and the teacher's private experiences are not historical evidence about Paul. The baseline later acknowledges uncertainty over the bishop Onesimus identification; that uncertainty is preserved, rather than turned into the letter's documented happy ending.

The baseline first says Paul never requests physical relief, then later recognizes verse 22's release hope. Actual Scripture controls. Calvin's reading expressly leaves release subject to God's will. No new owner prayer doctrine originated. The Lord's Prayer tangent is not transferred wholesale: Mark 5 does not state the woman's exact doubts, and James's rejection of God's tempting someone to evil does not cancel Genesis 22's explicit testing of Abraham. Neither is needed for this passage's exposition. The pattern-of-prayer versus fixed-prayer debate would require its own primary-tradition review, not a blanket dismissal here.
