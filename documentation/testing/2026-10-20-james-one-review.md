## James 1:1–4

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). 1 Peter 1:3–9 puts tested faith within resurrection hope and an unfading inheritance, while explicitly allowing grief.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's exact early date, earliest NT letter claim, Jesus' brothers' private motives and precise conversion event are not established by the letter or primary introduction; omitted. Biological half-brother identification is not represented as agreement across traditions. Claims that Jesus did not share Adam's human flesh conflict with the shared humanity emphasized in Hebrews 2:14; omitted. Stone-carpentry and exact public-ministry chronology are unverified and unnecessary. Claims that the letter has nothing to do with salvation because it addresses believers are interpretive assumptions, not a rule for reading it. Baseline Luther removed James from the canon is not verified against Luther's actual preface; omitted. The lexical word for trial can mean experiment in some settings but does not establish a God who learns unknown information. Emotional grief does not prove failure of a divine grading test, and repeated hardship is not diagnosed as immaturity. The text does not establish every illness, grief or addiction as a separately assigned test with an eternal score.

## James 1:5–8

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Matthew 7:7–11 connects asking with the Father's generosity and His children's life of dependence.
Position source: Should We Seek Signs From God? — seek_signs.md132–146.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baseline denial that the Greek can mean doubt fails the actual Thayer entry, which explicitly cites James 1:6; BSB retained. Divided loyalty and struggling confidence must not become a diagnosis of every anxious question or mental illness. The baseline's fictional father/judge story cannot verify that God privately instructed a specific decision, or that an uncertain outcome proves failure to listen. Therapy, sound advice and family counsel are not categorically forbidden by this passage; the owner study explicitly affirms believers' counsel. No guaranteed audible answer, private feeling or predetermined personal sign is promised here. The owner position is limited to wisdom and guidance, not a new verdict on every claimed revelation.

## James 1:9–11

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Revelation 2:8–11 gives a labeled parallel between outward poverty and riches in Christ's assessment.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baseline classification of every precise financial position as an assigned spiritual examination is not established by these verses. No prosperity formula, shame for unmet material needs or assumption that poverty reflects superior behavior is introduced. Calvin reads rich believers; Wesley notes brother is attached only to the lowly. Their shared practical point does not establish that every rich addressee has the same spiritual status. Exact desert weather/geography claims and quotation attribution anecdotes are unnecessary and not used. The only thing that survives into eternity being degree of spiritual maturity is not supported by the wider passages checked; omitted.

## James 1:12–15

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Romans 6:20–23 distinguishes sin's death from God's gift of life; Revelation 2:10–11 connects the crown with the risen Jesus, suffering and hope beyond physical death.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline treats crown exclusively as an extra reward and death exclusively as premature physical death/lost reward. Actual Calvin, Wesley and USCCB do not establish that as consensus; Calvin explicitly speaks of eternal death. The Greek for crown alone cannot prove a five-crown system or degrees of governing authority. No owner position chooses that system. Hebrews 10 and 1 Corinthians 5 do not settle James' meaning simply by citation; their doctrinal differences were already documented in their own reviews. The baseline's God has no choice but bench a believer is an illustrative claim, not a scriptural limitation on God. Temptation is distinguished from consent without claiming evil desires themselves are universally considered innocent: direct Catholic/Westminster differences are represented fairly. Thayer 551 does not mean God is ignorant that evil exists. Lord's Prayer as necessarily a particular figure of speech is not independently verified and is not used. The alleged universal rule that one must personally have committed evil to lead another into it is not established; omitted.

## James 1:16–18

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). 1 Peter 1:22–25 connects new birth with the enduring word and love; Romans 8:18–25 supports the wider creation-renewal connection, labeled as a theme rather than James' detailed timetable.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Firstfruits is not a guarantee of a particular resurrection schedule or universal salvation. Calvin's general consecrated people emphasis, Wesley's excellence emphasis and the editor's first Jewish converts proposal do not establish only one possible referent. The claim that an alleged absence of sanctification from Romans 8:30 proves maturity wholly optional and all other experiences independent of response is not established by that list. Father of lights' exact Dead Sea Scrolls verbal parallel was not independently checked; omitted. The claim that truly good gifts cannot occur in earthly life overstates the passage's source-versus-location distinction. God as source of good does not require denying that created gifts are good.
