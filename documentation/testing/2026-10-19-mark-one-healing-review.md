## Mark 1:21–28

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Luke11 explicitly connects Jesus' exorcisms with God's kingdom; Mark3 explicitly interprets the victory with the bound strong man; 1John3 explicitly describes the Son destroying the devil's works alongside righteousness and love.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No independent period source checked for all scribes' schools, degrees, two authoritative classes or the precise halakha-hadasha label, so no blanket first-century credential narrative is published. Mark's own contrast of authority suffices. No claim everyone in synagogue was ignorant of Jesus' identity or a diagnosis of losing mental composure from ekplesso. No claim Genesis6 happened after the flood (it precedes it), no treating disputed spirit/women reading as established, no speculative possession mechanics from comparing indwelling Spirit with demons, and no unworked absolute Christian-possession/Spirit-permanence doctrine. No geography/timing of all demons' final destruction supplied. No universal claim symptoms of mental/physical illness imply demons. No asserted spoken Hebrew idiom etymology without primary checking. Mark reports convulsions and departure; Luke's no-harm detail would require separate attribution and is not silently inserted. Exousia is not made a rigid right-versus-power contrast that actual Thayer does not sustain. No present-day deliverance technique prescribed from one account.

## Mark 1:29–34

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Matthew8 explicitly applies Isaiah53:4 to this same healing sequence. Mark10:42–45 explicitly joins Christ's service and His saving death with service among His followers; the woman's response is presented as an early picture, not a new formal office or extra quotation.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Fever alone establishes neither prolonged disease, exact diagnosis nor the reason she lived there. No mind-reading that Peter invited Jesus solely for a meal or that healing was not in mind. No universal Sabbath-healing ban or unverified reason every person waited; the public text retains actual evening/after-sunset narrative. No all-rabbis practice stereotype, literal modern census of the town or statistical claim every person in Galilee was healed. Simon's mother-in-law is real family context, not a polemic falsely saying all traditions deny Peter's marriage; 1Cor9:5 directly names Cephas' wife. No automatic illness-guilt equation is adopted from Calvin's historical application. No theory Wrede's messianic-secret work was universally disproved/rejected, no universal scholarly consensus claim, no all-Jewish two-Messiah belief or private premature-attack timetable. No assumption telling people Jesus' identity necessarily creates false conversion: Mark records His preaching and later direct teachings as well as actions. Silencing suggestions are attributed to Calvin/Wesley, not made complete mind-reading of Jesus' purposes. No promise every faithful sick person must recover now or incur blame if illness continues. Ordinary service word does not make the woman an ordained deacon.
