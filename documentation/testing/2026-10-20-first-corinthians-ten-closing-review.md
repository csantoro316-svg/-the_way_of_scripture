## 1 Corinthians 10:14–22

Plan section: included, because (a): Luke 22 expressly connects the bread and cup with Christ’s death and new covenant; Paul explicitly connects the shared loaf with one body.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s merely symbolic account is one interpretation, not Christian consensus. Westminster and the 1689 Baptist confession teach real spiritual reception through faith; Methodist Article XVIII likewise goes beyond only remembering, while Catholic and Lutheran documents affirm actual body and blood in distinct ways. Their broader explanations are not collapsed into one view. No Orthodox-specific difference is introduced without a checked primary source. Sacrificial meals are not reduced to public messaging alone: Paul explicitly describes participation with demons. Conversely, this is not proof every piece of formerly sacrificed meat carries an unavoidable spiritual contamination. No exact Passover-only identification of verse 18 is imposed. Universal claims about top-quality temple meat and the private motives of every Corinthian are unverified and omitted. Modern yoga, medicine and cultural practices are not declared demonic from this passage; the baseline’s broad applications have not received independent historical or medical verification. No combative confessional wording is reproduced.

## 1 Corinthians 10:23–30

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): the quoted Psalm places the food question within God’s ownership of creation; 1 Timothy 4 expressly connects good creation, thanksgiving and rejection of false religious food restrictions.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline identifies Paul’s quotation as Deuteronomy 10:14; the actual BSB note and Psalm 24:1 establish the direct quotation. No imagined shopkeeper conversation, fixed identity of the informer, or psychological reaction is treated as recorded history. Interpretive possibilities are not presented as text. Not asking about a meal’s history is not a general rule to avoid inconvenient knowledge. The previous prohibition of idolatry remains explicit, so the freedom slogan cannot authorize every action. No unsupported assurance that pleasing everyone is possible or that private gratitude cancels public harm is added. A modern alcohol policy is not originated from the baseline’s illustration.

## 1 Corinthians 10:31–33

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): John 17:15–23 expressly describes people sent into the world, protected from evil, sanctified in truth and united so the world may believe.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Giving no offense is explained in the passage’s concern for others’ salvation, not as permission to compromise truth or a promise nobody will object. The gospel is not reduced to conduct without proclamation. The baseline’s exclusive explanation for why believers remain on earth, and a detailed reward calculation, go beyond what these verses establish and are omitted. No claim Paul can guarantee another person’s salvation is made.
