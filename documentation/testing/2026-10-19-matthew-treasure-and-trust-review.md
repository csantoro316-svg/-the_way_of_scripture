## Per-group decisions

### Matthew 6:19–24 — Treasure and an Undivided Heart

- Plan section: included, because (c). Hebrews 11:24–26 gives Moses' costly choice and hope of reward in relation to Christ; Colossians 3:1–4 grounds a changed focus in risen life with Christ; 1 Timothy 6:17–19 connects generosity with true future life. These add redemption and future hope beyond repeating the warning against greed. No precise end-times allocation or hidden identity for each image.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: unverified superlative that accumulating treasure is the New Testament's most frequent topic; precise eternal houses, salaries, ranks, and government offices; all Pharisees' identical motives; current preachers' exact clothing/property/aircraft prices; and exact ancient security practices are omitted. Thayer's competing mammon derivations do not establish an unquestionable root meaning. No independently verified good-eye/evil-eye generosity idiom here; the public reading uses the actual picture, surrounding money context, and named primary interpreters. No claim owning any property is itself serving money; actual 1 Timothy 6 gives the rich directions for generosity. No new site judgment-seat, salvation, or investment doctrine.

### Matthew 6:25–34 — Trusting the Father's Care

- Plan section: included, because (c). Romans 8:31–39 ties God's care to His gift of the Son, resurrection, and intercession, while including famine and hardship; 2 Corinthians 11:27 confirms that faithful service can include hunger; 2 Corinthians 9:6–15 shows God's provision through a generous people. These add Christ's saving work and community care beyond the immediate creation illustrations.
- Position source: Prayer and Faith / What Does Prayer Look Like?.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: no inference that every believer suffering hunger, poverty, or distress must lack faith. No guarantee of an effortless life, particular lifestyle, wealth, or healing. No medical diagnosis or financial instructions drawn from the PDF's suggestions about jobs, homes, savings, and debt. Its exact reward timetable and claims that all worry comes directly from a particular demonic tactic are not independently established. Wesley's white royal robes, flower biology, and still rather than oven are not verified history or replacements for BSB. The study's language about not asking is represented alongside its companion's explicit distinction that asking is not itself sinful; no new blanket ban originated. No false Greek claim copied.

### Matthew 6:31–33 — A Different First Priority

- Plan section: included, because (c). Colossians 1:12–14 connects the Father's care with rescue into the Son's kingdom and forgiveness; Ephesians 1:7–10 describes that purpose reaching toward all things together in Christ. This adds the larger saving purpose to the immediate needs and priorities.
- Position source: Prayer and Faith / What Does Prayer Look Like?.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: verse 32 uses epizēteō and verse 33 zēteō, not literally the identical verb as the study says. The related meanings support a comparison of priorities without the erroneous claim. No stereotype that every individual Gentile throughout history lacks all knowledge of God. The promise concerns the needs named, not every desired possession. No implication that all economic hardship proves failed kingdom-seeking, or that asking itself is sin. The study's bread-as-Christ discussion remains in the developed study and the reviewed bread pages; its unverified claims about Origen/Jerome and word history are not repeated here. No new site doctrine beyond the existing studies.

### Matthew 6:33 — Seek His Kingdom First

- Plan section: included, because (c). Romans 14:17–19 applies God's kingdom priorities to righteousness, peace, joy in the Spirit, and building others up; Luke 12:29–34 links the Father's gift of the kingdom with trust and generosity. The Romans food-dispute context is identified, not treated as a direct quotation of Matthew or the same immediate dispute.
- Position source: Prayer and Faith / What Does Prayer Look Like?.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: no lexicon proves God's kingdom means a guaranteed future government post or righteousness a measurable rank. No transactional promise of material abundance or every requested result. No original site doctrine of the prayer's bread petition or a universal prohibition of asking imported into this verse. No private-voice claim, clinical assessment, or forecast for an individual's finances. Existing studies' trust and surrender reading is named and preserved narrowly.
