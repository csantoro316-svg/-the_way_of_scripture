## Acts 11:1–18
Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): the narrative explicitly recalls Acts 1:5, and Acts 15:7–11 directly explains this Gentile reception.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Luke identifies circumcised critics, not all apostles as critics. The baseline's anti-papal conclusions are not reproduced: Catholic infallibility is not equivalent to a leader never being asked a question, and this passage does not itself provide a comprehensive treatment of church government. No unverified motive of fearing additional persecution is imposed on the critics. Hebrews 12 and 2 Corinthians 7 are research leads rather than proof of every detail in the baseline's account of God granting repentance; no new owner position on a universal sequence is created. No invented claim that supernatural experiences were restricted to a fixed list of categories or that every believer must have an identical sign.

## Acts 11:19–26
Plan section: included, because (b)/(c): Galatians 3:26–29 supplies a supported connection between shared identity in Christ and the promise to Abraham; sustained teaching develops the community's life.
Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Luke resumes the scattering narrative; no rigid chronology requiring every Antioch conversion to occur only after Peter's report is established. The claim that Gentile conversion was impossible until Peter unlocked a barrier is not used. No population figures, city rankings, distances, exact nine-year interval, compulsory emperor worship or historical sewage metaphor is included without independent checking. Barnabas's private reason for choosing Saul and Jerusalem's precise skeptical motive are not supplied. The term Christian is not said to be formally coined by a named person, certainly an insult, or evidence of replacing every previous identity. Greeks/Hellenists textual alternatives are acknowledged internally rather than silently changing BSB.

## Acts 11:26 legacy
Plan section: included, because (b)/(c): the same supported Galatians connection and broader setting of sustained teaching.
Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The limits for 19–26 apply. Only verse 26 is quoted; broader context explains Barnabas and Saul's meeting with the community. No claim that this alone determines all later institutional or architectural practices.

## Acts 11:27–30
Plan section: included, because (b)/(c): Romans 15 describes a later collection with the explicit principle of material help and shared spiritual blessings; it is labeled a different event.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No claim that there were no welfare systems anywhere in antiquity, every Judean believer was disowned by family, or all distress had one cause. Josephus supports Judean famine, not a precise worldwide synchronized event or Luke's every chronological detail. No new ruling that all prophecy stopped when Revelation was written; the baseline's canon-based explanation is not established by this passage. The text gives the contribution to elders through Barnabas and Saul but does not explicitly narrate transfer from elders to deacons. Relief is described as mutual care rather than imposing a new ethnic hierarchy or a political rule on modern readers.
