## Genesis 22:1–10

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b). Hebrews 11:17–19 directly explains resurrection confidence; the wood/cross comparison is supported by John 19:17 and explicitly found in Wesley XXII.6, labeled a comparison rather than John's explicit explanation of Isaac.

Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It, Genesis 22:5 — worship expressed in humble obedience/trust.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Exact age of Isaac (PDF says 20–30), full knowledge/voluntary inner consent, exact location identical with Golgotha, literal use of Abba in Genesis, and a required uncut-stone altar are not established by Genesis 22 or the checked lexicons/commentaries; omitted. Three journey days are stated, but a precise three-day period of Isaac being mentally dead is a teacher's scheme, not Hebrews' statement. The public page reads the next verses' prohibition of harm with Deuteronomy 12:31. Existing Worship study calls Genesis 22:5 the first use of shachah; BDB explicitly cites Genesis 18:2/19:1 earlier. That erroneous claim and the study's nonmatching BSB quotation were not repeated in the passage position. Study correction remains a separate recorded follow-up, rather than silently rewriting owner-authored prose during passage work.

## Genesis 22:1–18

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b). Hebrews 11 and James 2 directly treat this event; Hebrews 6 cites the oath. Romans 8:32 supports the father/Son parallel, also explicitly drawn by CCC 2572. Isaac's rescue and Christ's actual death/resurrection are distinguished.

Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It, Genesis 22:5; Who is The Angel of the Lord?, Genesis 22:11–18.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Same age/consent/site/altar/three-day claims as above excluded. God's Father/Son love is not described as divine fear, doubt, or discovery of previously unknown personal feelings. The account does not provide a universal parenting formula requiring a child's perfect compliance. No claim Genesis 22:8 grammatically states God will sacrifice Himself: BDB supports provide/furnish, not that proposed literal translation. James' explicit faith/action use is acknowledged without inventing a site conclusion resolving the whole justification debate. Existing Angel study's imperfect BSB quotations are not reused; position is drawn from its developed interpretation, and public full quotation is independently verified.

## Genesis 22:11–19

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b). Hebrews 6:13–20 expressly uses the oath for assurance and leads to Jesus' priesthood. The ram dying instead of Isaac is compared, with stated limits, to the Lamb of God in John 1:29.

Position source: Who is The Angel of the Lord?, Genesis 22:11–18.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No proof obtained for a self-death penalty in this oath; Hebrews 6 explains God's truthfulness, not a requirement that He kill Himself. No identification of every feature of thicket/horns with the crown of thorns. No unsupported assertion that every Christian agrees on the angel's identity; Wesley/Augustine checked directly. BDB records see/provide and alternate discussion of the place-name; public explanation does not make one English name a hidden grammatical proof of incarnation. The PDF's wider Hebrews 10 judgment/rewards system is outside the verified conclusion and not assigned as a site position.

## Genesis 22:20–24

Plan section: omitted, because none apply. Preparation for Rebekah's arrival is already explained in context and does not require a second Bible-wide claim.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF says Haran died without daughters; earlier Genesis explicitly names Milcah/Iscah as Haran's daughters, so that claim is excluded. No exact years since Abraham last heard family news, definite Ur residence, or motives for every relative inferred. Family names are not converted into a prophetic number scheme.

## Genesis 23:1–20

Plan section: included, because (c). Hebrews 11:13–16 supports the Bible-wide theme of faithful strangers dying while awaiting fulfillment and a better country. It is not falsely said to quote this burial transaction directly.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF's precise Hittite land-tax system, seller's hidden motives, universal feigned-gift negotiation practice, acreage/value, claim price was ten times fair value, and modern currency conversion were not independently confirmed; omitted. Wesley's different old currency estimate is not independent confirmation. The narrative itself supplies field/cave/trees, public witnesses, silver payment, and transfer, which are sufficient without invented legal history. No claim the whole land promise was fulfilled by this burial purchase or that hope eliminated Abraham's grief.

## Genesis 24:1–14

Plan section: included, because (c). Continuity of the promised family and asking for wisdom/help connect with James 1:5, without making James teach sign-setting.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Servant's identity as definitely Eliezer is not established; chapter leaves him unnamed. Destination is Aram-naharaim/Nahor's town, not definitely Ur. Oath gesture's exact sexual/descendant-revenge symbolism, fixed journey mileage, camel-water calculations, ten as an invariable prophetic code, and accusations of servant laziness were not independently proved; omitted. Prayer is specific in this narrative, but neither it nor James establishes a universal foolproof spouse-sign method. Existing Signs study's general counsel is not promoted to an owner-approved exegesis of this passage. Independent historical sources were not secured for the PDF's detailed customs, so no such cultural reconstruction appears publicly.

## Genesis 24:15–61

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Ordinary generosity and faithful service carry the promised family story onward. Rebekah leaving the familiar for an unseen future is labeled a parallel to Abraham's journey (Hebrews 11:8–10), rather than a claim that Hebrews explicitly interprets her departure. Genesis 24:60's blessing visibly echoes 22:17.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No primary Scripture establishes the full equation Abraham=Father, Isaac=Son, servant=Spirit, Rebekah=Gentile Church, jewelry=Spirit's gifts/pledge, or travel=entire future church timetable. No claim every Spirit figure is unnamed or that Father/Son cease to participate in salvation. The father's alleged absence of authority, Laban's exclusively greedy motives, Rebekah legally already married before being consulted, and inability ever to return to family are not established by the narrative or verified independent culture sources. Rebekah's own agreement is preserved without importing a complete ancient consent law. Wesley's family-religion conjecture is not adopted as historical fact. Teacher's detailed Hurrian-only consent contrast and precise customary length of marriage celebrations not independently confirmed, omitted.

## Genesis 24:62–67

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Ephesians 5:25–32 supports the Bible-wide loving bridegroom/church theme; 1 Peter 1:8–9 supports love for the unseen Christ. Both comparisons are explicitly labeled, neither is called an NT exposition of Rebekah. The detailed actor/gift timetable is not asserted.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: PDF states Isaac rode out on a camel; BSB says he was walking in the field, so no Isaac-camel claim. No proof that Beer-lahai-roi ownership arose from Abimelech's well treaty, or that Isaac built a particular bridal home; not stated in this passage. Precise universal veil-removal rights, full wedding ritual, inability to recognize Jesus at His return, and a definite end-times timetable are not established here. The public page records veiling without unsupported custom claims. Isaac's love/comfort is not made a universal promise that marriage erases grief. BSB meditate is retained without a new claim settling the difficult Hebrew verb.
