## Per-group decisions

### Matthew 8:1–4 — Jesus Is Willing to Make Him Clean

- Plan section: included, because (c). Matthew 11:2–6 explicitly lists cleansed lepers among Jesus' answering works; Hebrews 9:11–14 supplies the larger cleansing theme and Christ's own sacrifice. The physical/spiritual comparison is labeled, not imposed as the man's secret diagnosis or a direct exposition of Matthew by Hebrews.
- Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It — Matthew 8:2, The Leper's Approach; limited to his reverent personal plea and recognition of divine authority.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: modern Hansen's disease, immunity percentages, disease course, transmission, armadillos, antibiotic treatment, and irreversible damage were not independently checked and are omitted. The passage does not identify a modern diagnosis. Neither a universal ban on every human contact nor a known lifetime without touch is documented. Leviticus supplies actual camp/examination/cleansing provisions, not proof of identical enforcement in every first-century town. The baseline's Hebrew “mark/blemish” gloss is not supported by the BDB entry read and is omitted. Miriam did not live before the Mosaic-law narrative: Numbers 12 was checked; the contrary chronology is excluded. Luke 4:27 addresses Elisha's time and cannot prove no Jew was ever cleansed after the Law; no temple records for this individual or universal nonuse of Leviticus 14 were found. No independent ancient primary document was checked for the proposed three “Messianic miracles” categories or mandatory investigation scheme, so neither is published. Matthew 11 provides the actual identity connection instead. No three-day journey, exact priestly motives, literal transfer of the man's disease to Jesus, or equation of illness with personal guilt is supplied by the passage. Mark 1:45 attributes Jesus' inability to enter towns openly to the publicity/crowds, not fear of Pharisees. Silence as exclusively a priest-first instruction is Wesley's proposed reading, not an established exhaustive explanation. The ten-miracle/ten-plague map remains a teacher's suggestion with no demonstrated authorial intent and is omitted.

### Matthew 8:2 — A Humble Request for Cleansing

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Hebrews 4:14–16 gives the supported parallel of approaching the compassionate high priest for mercy, adds His larger priestly welcome, and does not guarantee immediate healing of every illness.
- Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It — Matthew 8:2, The Leper's Approach; same narrow authorized position as above.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the old passage's unverified “Restored reading” quotation is removed; actual verified BSB replaces it. The study's own inexact BSB quotation is not copied. Bowing alone does not prove every element of a conscious deity/Messiah confession. Thayer expressly includes homage to people of rank, so a blanket claim that all kneeling is exclusive worship of God is excluded. Unverified ancient coin/icon claims and universal Pharisaic responses are unnecessary and omitted. The healing/diagnosis and history flags for 8:1–4 apply to this preserved overlap too. No new position on every detail of the Law is implied by the study's posture discussion.

### Matthew 8:5–13 — Trusting the Authority of Jesus’ Word

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Galatians 3:6–14 explains Abraham's blessing reaching Gentiles through Christ's redemption and the gift of the Spirit. Isaiah 25:6–9 provides the supported banquet/all-peoples/death-defeated parallel. These add the saving purpose beyond simply repeating the centurion's faith.
- Position source: none. Exact required notice retained.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Matthew describes a centurion, but does not identify a particular legion, ethnicity, service history, number of men physically present, or distance from the house; none is invented. The baseline's blanket Jewish/Roman hostility is not supported by Luke's account of this man's relationship with Jewish elders. Claims all Roman servants were disposable, that no officer would care for one, mandatory pagan participation in this unit, career persecution, private synagogue study, inner conscience, and temple behavior are unverified and omitted. Luke reports a highly valued servant and intercessors; it does not record the proposed biography. Matthew describes paralysis and agony, not a fever or demonic possession, so those inferred diagnoses are excluded. No claim this was the first Gentile faith in Matthew; chapter 2 has already described the magi. The word translated servant is not introduced with an unverified “young slave” gloss. The public page avoids an unnecessary Greek claim; Luke supplies further household context. Matthew presents a direct exchange and Luke messengers: both are explained without inventing a final face-to-face meeting or declaring Wesley's reconstruction fact. No unrecorded Genesis lesson, complete doctrinal understanding, or specific thoughts of Jewish elders are asserted. No theory of Jesus' surprise proves a new site Christology. The command's success is narrated; it is not a formula making every believer's desired outcome inevitable. The Spirit-as-engine/fuel picture, dismissal of every nonbiblical counselor's usefulness, and speculative mechanics of sanctification are not established by this healing and omitted. No precise thousand-year banquet schedule, fulfillment of Tabernacles, table layout, all Pharisees' self-title, or exact Lake-of-Fire identification is established by these verses. The definite article is not used to manufacture a lexical meaning of intense anger for gnashing. The warning remains serious without deriving a complete judgment doctrine or dismissing all Jewish people; the named patriarchs themselves are among the welcomed.
