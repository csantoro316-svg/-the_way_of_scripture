## 2 Timothy 1:1–5

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Acts 16:1–3 gives Timothy's actual entrance into Paul's company; 2 Timothy 3:14–15 connects childhood Scripture knowledge with salvation through faith in Christ. Family and teaching serve that wider purpose.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline supplies an exact AD 67 date, earlier trial/acquittal in AD 62, a precise renewed-imprisonment chronology and empire-wide effects of Nero's persecution. These details were not independently verified and are not public background. The text itself supplies chains and Rome later in the chapter, and anticipation of death in chapter 4. No assertion that Paul had no natural children, no exact occasion for Timothy's tears or population-wide parenting statistic is invented. His clear conscience is not paraphrased as a lifetime entirely without regrets or compromised conduct. Wesley and Calvin differ over the preconversion scope; that actual difference is named without creating a new owner ruling.

## 2 Timothy 1:6–7

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Acts 4:23–31 supplies a related example of Spirit-enabled bold witness under threats; 1 Corinthians 12:4–7 locates gifts within the common good. The purpose is service and the church's strengthening.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's two-part living-creature/fire etymology is not independently supported by the checked Thayer entry, which explains rekindling through embers/fire language. The image does not quantify Timothy's decline or prove he already abandoned preaching. No psychiatric diagnosis, nervous stomach explanation or biography of refusing duties is public fact. Its picture of an entirely new human spirit versus the Holy Spirit is not imposed as consensus; Calvin explicitly emphasizes God's empowering Spirit. Thayer supports moderation/self-control, not a guarantee of never feeling fear. No new doctrinal conclusion about gifts, demons, ordination transmission or mental health is assigned to the owner.

The actual Catholic and Baptist documents describe ordination differently and are represented fairly. No claim that Catholic clergy had no gifts before ordination or that Baptists reject the Spirit's work at commissioning is invented. The precise gift is not more specifically identified than the text warrants. The baseline's detailed Amos illustration was not independently researched for this batch and is omitted; an independently checked Acts parallel is used instead.

## 2 Timothy 1:8–12

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). The passage itself expressly connects salvation, God's purpose before time, Christ's appearing, death's defeat and gospel proclamation. Ephesians 1:3–10 and 1 Corinthians 15:20–26, 50–57 support the wider purpose and resurrection hope.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's interpretation of death here as only the second death, excluding bodily death, is not established from the word or this passage. The Corinthians resurrection connection preserves the final bodily defeat of death while allowing present suffering. No promise that faithful people avoid dying or that every unbeliever's dying psychology is known is included. No automatic inference that Timothy had already repudiated Paul, exact courier report, reconstructed motive or proof of a message solely from a martyr's sincerity is presented as history.

Westminster's unconditional election and Wesley's own foreknown-believers reading are directly checked, without turning either into a new site position or saying either teaches salvation earned by works. The baseline's exclusive eternal-reward definition of Paul's deposit in verse 12 is not consensus. Wesley says soul, Calvin eternal life; the BSB footnote and USCCB note also recognize a deposit entrusted to Paul. These are accurately named. No permanent reward schedule, exact loss calculation or automatic equating every experience with falsehood is added. Faithful trust in Christ and His ability to keep remain central.

## 2 Timothy 1:15–18

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Matthew 25:34–40 supplies the care-for-Christ's-brothers/prison parallel; Hebrews 13:1–3 directly joins brotherly love and solidarity with prisoners. This is a parallel, not identification with an unnamed person in Jesus' scene.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's suggestion that Onesiphorus himself later deserted Paul is unsupported and conflicts with the paragraph's actual positive testimony. It is left off the page. A prayer for mercy does not by itself prove a recent betrayal. His death is likewise not expressly reported; USCCB's suggestion is labeled an inference, and Wesley's including him in the household prayer is fairly reported. The exact visit's imprisonment, Onesiphorus's location when Paul wrote and his household's composition are not reconstructed as fact.

Catechism 958 explicitly allows prayer for the dead; Westminster 21.4 explicitly excludes it. These actual positions are distinguished without saying either proves Onesiphorus dead or alive. No general claim that every Asian believer or every local leader without exception abandoned the faith is inferred from Paul's description of desertion. The province is not the entire modern continent, per the checked BSB footnote. No hostile biography of the two named deserters or precise motives beyond the text are invented. Mercy on that day is not reduced to forgiveness for the baseline's speculative later betrayal or to an owner ruling about purgatory.
