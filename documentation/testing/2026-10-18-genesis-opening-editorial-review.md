## Genesis 1:1-31

Draft: `content/passages/genesis-1-1-31.json` (Context, Common Interpretations, plan, position, and existing deeper-study field).

- Plan section: included, because (a). John 1:1-3, 14 explicitly relates creation to the incarnate Word; 2 Corinthians 4:6 invokes the creation of light in speaking of illumination in Christ. Ephesians 1:9-10 supplies the larger purpose in Scripture's own terms.
- Position source: none. Existing site studies were searched for Genesis 1, creation, and related references; no developed treatment establishing a position on this passage or the length of its days was found. The exact owner-approved no-position wording is used. Existing deeper-study links are empty and remain so.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's exact chronology (including a creation date around 4000 BC and precise Mosaic authorship dates) was not established by the Scripture/primary-document/lexicon checks and is excluded. Its interpretation of the Spirit's hovering as the introduction of physical energy, and of the deep as formless matter rather than water, is not established by the checked lexical entries and is excluded. Its numerical claims about how often day means a normal day versus an age, its assertion that evening/morning invariably denote normal twelve-hour periods, and its explanation of why others accept day-age readings are not established by the checked lexicon or by the other readings' own primary documents; they are excluded. The claim that no animal death of any kind preceded human sin is not established merely by the cited discussion of human death in Romans 5; no site position is inferred. These are unresolved/excluded baseline claims, not claims left in public copy.

## Genesis 2:1-3

Draft: `content/passages/genesis-2-1-3.json` (same required section order).

- Plan section: included, because (a). Hebrews 4:3-11 explicitly cites God's seventh-day rest and develops the continuing promise of entering His rest. The section does not use this connection to decide the calendar dispute.
- Position source: none. No existing site study or owner-developed conclusion on the passage's Sabbath application was found. The exact approved no-position wording is used. Existing deeper-study links are empty and remain so.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's conclusion that the Sabbath's ultimate purpose excludes physical rest is not adopted. Exodus 20 describes real rest for the household and animals; Hebrews 4's further connection does not by itself establish that physical rest is irrelevant. The further claim that Genesis 2:1-3 directly pictures Christ sitting down after a finished sacrifice is not presented as this passage's stated meaning: Hebrews 4 is the explicit NT citation, while Hebrews 10:11-14 supplies a different finished-work comparison. The owner can develop a position or parallel later. The baseline's dismissal of another account reading as ill-informed is excluded as an editorial judgment rather than checkable contextual information.
