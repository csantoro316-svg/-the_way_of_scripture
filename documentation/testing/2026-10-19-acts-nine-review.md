## Acts 9:1–9
Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): 1 Timothy 1:12–16 explicitly explains the mercy shown to the former persecutor; 1 Corinthians 12 provides a supported connection to Christ's body.
Position source: Returning to the Word.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No horse is narrated. Saul's height cannot be inferred from being identified with Hermes; his number of languages, childhood timetable, citizenship registration certificate, medical schools at Tarsus and precise family migration history were not independently established. No imperial extradition treaty or involvement of Roman police is asserted from letters to synagogues alone. The name's spelling is not used alone to establish the language spoken; Acts 26:14 supplies Hebrew explicitly. Accounts 9, 22 and 26 are compared using their actual BSB wording rather than unverified grammatical harmonization. A dramatic interruption does not authorize a new owner ruling on every person's response or all traditions' teaching on grace.

## Acts 9:2 legacy
Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): the same wider encounter, mercy and Christ's identification with the disciples supply context.
Position source: Returning to the Word.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The limits for 1–9 apply. Only verse 2 is quoted in this preserved page. Its position develops the name of the community, not all subsequent theological questions. The study's statement that they were not called Christians is not expanded into a claim that the later Christian name was improper.

## Acts 9:10–19
Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Galatians 1:15–16 explicitly connects this call to the Gentile mission; the narrative also records care through another disciple.
Position source: Baptism.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Luke says Saul was praying but does not give a full prayer transcript or specify every request. The physical cause of blindness and material identity of the scale-like objects are not given. Straight Street's naming history is not independently established. No new doctrine that every need was directly caused by God is asserted. The claim that conversion had no miracles ignores the restored sight; absence of named tongues is not evidence that no supernatural event occurred. No absolute claim about the exact instant of salvation, all conversions being identical, Ananias's private fear level, or every delay in baptism is originated. The existing study's lexical distinction between washing and forgiveness is not independently established here and is not reused as a new word claim.

## Acts 9:20–31
Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Galatians 1:23–24 explicitly records believers glorifying God for the change; persecution begins the suffering named in 9:16.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Galatians does not say Paul met no disciples in Jerusalem; it limits the other apostles he saw and separately describes the churches of Judea. Acts explicitly says he stayed with the disciples. The baseline's claim that Peter personally watched him over Stephen's body is not in the account. Fifteen days to persuade Peter, a precisely ten-year Tarsus stay, two decades already elapsed, and a complete chronology of Arabia are not established here. Paul's third-heaven experience is not identified with the Arabia visit in the actual texts; that baseline connection is omitted. Aretas is named from Paul's account without inventing a Roman appointment, exact legal powers or the method by which the authorities became involved. Peace is not attributed to Saul's conversion as the sole historically proven cause.

## Acts 9:32–35
Plan section: included, because (b)/(c): the pattern of Acts 3 directs witnesses from the apostle's personal power to the risen Jesus.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The proposed lexical restriction of all residents to only Jewish residents is not verified and is not used. Luke's general description is retained without assigning exact population percentages or declaring every inhabitant individually converted. Lydda is not described as a seaport. Peter's unrecorded preliminary prayer and an assumption about Aeneas's prior faith are not made facts.

## Acts 9:36–43
Plan section: included, because (b)/(c): Mark 5 and 1 Kings 17 supply clearly labeled parallels of life restored and grieving people receiving someone alive.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The widows' display is not presented as a calculated attempt to earn a resurrection; Luke gives grief and the clothing, not that private motive. Sending for Peter does not establish that apostles already had a specific public reputation for raising the dead or that no other believer could ever have a gift. Tabitha's thoughts about returning to life are unknown. Peter's staying with a tanner is narrated without an independently unverified claim that all tanning legally made him unclean or that this proves deliberate abandonment of Jewish observance. The gazelle meaning comes from the actual lexicon, not its generated topical essay.
