## Matthew 26:1–5

Plan section: included, because (a). 1 Corinthians 5:6–8 explicitly calls Christ the Passover sacrifice. Exodus 12 supplies the earlier rescue; Acts 2:22–24 joins divine purpose, human wrongdoing, and resurrection. Retains the PDF's helpful big-picture connection without excusing the conspiracy.

Position source: none. Exact open-position wording.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's Thursday-crucifixion calculation assumes the precise days assigned to several earlier events; Walvoord openly acknowledges other chronologies and assumes Friday. This short group does not decide the week-day dispute. No claim that all Gospel writers explicitly date the Olivet discourse Tuesday, that sundown is universally 6pm, or that Genesis establishes fixed twelve-hour light/dark periods. Avoid disputed calendrical proofs as settled context. No unsupported million-pilgrim census, private rumors, precise location in wealthy upper Jerusalem, or motives beyond the recorded plot/fear. Annas' removal and Caiaphas' appointment details require separate historical checking and are omitted here. The same Greek verb across betrayal and God's saving purpose does not erase moral responsibility.

## Matthew 26:6–13

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). Jesus explicitly relates her deed to burial and worldwide gospel proclamation. Romans 12:1–2 supplies a labeled parallel of costly response to mercy; Philippians 2:5–11 grounds humility in Christ's obedience. This retains the PDF's service/sacrifice connection without claiming a detail-for-detail prediction.

Position source: none. Exact open-position wording.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Matthew does not name the woman or describe her private understanding. John identifies Mary in his Bethany account; the connection is explained with source distinctions. Wesley denies a conscious burial intention, while Walvoord suggests partial understanding: both are represented fairly. Her perfume as lifelong wedding savings, a private conversation with the Spirit, her imagined reluctance, precise modern dollar value, universal ban on women letting down their hair, and a unique burial honor surpassing every death in Jerusalem are not established by the checked passages or independent history. No blanket claim that all alabaster jars can only be opened once or all nonbelievers' charity is hypocritical. Deuteronomy 15:7–11 and Mark 14:7 directly prevent using Jesus' statement to dismiss the poor. No need to conflate this Bethany scene with Luke 7's earlier anointing.

## Matthew 26:17–19

Plan section: included, because (a). Luke 22:14–16 explicitly links Jesus' Passover before suffering with fulfillment in the kingdom. 1 Corinthians 5:6–8 adds the connection to Christ and the community's changed life, going beyond meal preparations.

Position source: none. Exact open-position wording.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF invents an unnamed man's second table, wife, surprising inner prompting, and complete uncertainty about the guests. None appears in Matthew, Mark, or Luke. Mark/Luke state that the room is furnished and preparations are to be made; they do not say the entire meal was already laid out by an uninformed host. The public page keeps the useful lesson of following Jesus' directions without supplying that biography or a requirement to obey all private impressions unquestioningly. The water-carrier as a universally impossible male domestic task is not independently established. Concealment from Judas may explain the directions, but is not the narrator's stated reason and is omitted here. Mishnah Pesachim 5 does not establish the baseline's thirteenth-day/9am national lamb scheme; no exact sacrifice clock is taught. No equation of a room with a modern claimed traditional site.
