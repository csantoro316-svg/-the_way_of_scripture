## Genesis 26:1–5

Plan section: included, because (c). Galatians 3:8–9 explains the nations-blessing promise given first to Abraham; Hebrews 11:9–10 names Isaac among waiting heirs. These develop the larger reach of the renewed promise without falsely claiming an NT quotation of this exact famine scene.

Position source: Prayer and Faith — Genesis 26:1–2, Isaac's Contrasting Response. Original deeper-study link retained.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: PDF says English obey is wrong because shama only means hear. Actual BDB includes obey/listen obediently, and the context lists keeping commands; public teaching does not erase obedience or replace it with mere hearing. No claim Abraham was sinless, and no new site ruling on faith/works beyond the applicable study. The source's precise geography, required pastoral route, exact promised boundaries phrased as Mediterranean-to-Euphrates, and first-ever private vision assertion are not independently established here, omitted. Isaac's earlier knowledge cannot be exhaustively reconstructed. The passage gives an instruction to Isaac, not a universal rule that every famine requires staying put or that every migration is unbelief. The original study's quotation is not reused as a substitute for direct BSB checking.

## Genesis 26:6–11

Plan section: included, because (c). Protection alongside exposure/rebuke develops the faithful-care theme; 1 John 2:3–6 adds the Bible-wide call for conduct to match professed trust, not a claim that John explicitly comments on Gerar.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No independent source confirms this Abimelech's precise identity/kinship with the earlier king, exact date, universal royal-title rule, or the PDF's detailed account of Philistine origins/brutality. Cretin is not a demonstrated derivation from Cretan, and no ethnic slur is repeated. The local men's only possible motive is not asserted; Isaac's fear is stated as his fear. Presence/absence of the twins is not enough to date the scene definitively before their birth. Exact medical extent of blindness is irrelevant here. BDB confirms marital caresses but not an unreported explicit sexual act. No universal ethical ruling about every possible deception, no claim God approves this one, and no exhaustive account of why the king looked through the window.

## Genesis 26:12–25

Plan section: included, because (c). Hebrews 11's waiting/tents theme includes Isaac; Romans 12 and 1 Peter 2 support restraint/entrusting judgment to God, with Christ as the stated NT example. No well-by-well prediction is claimed.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Genesis credits God for the harvest but does not describe the mechanism, exact ongoing drought conditions in every field, every neighbor's yield, or a special command to plant. No universal faithful-believer wealth guarantee. The PDF's assertions all wells could be dug only by the rich, Abraham's treaty automatically expired at death, the king personally commanded every blockage, and exact travel distances/legal rights were not independently confirmed. Verse 24 promises care, not a guarantee of immunity from every injury. Sitnah's relationship to hostility does not prove Satan personally engineered each quarrel. Wilderness/temptation comparison was investigated, but no clear Scripture-established correspondence for the successive wells, and Jesus being without water forty days is not stated in the cited Gospel pattern; omitted in favor of the well-supported restraint theme. The PDF says Isaac left Beersheba at the famine's start, whereas preceding narrative locates him near Beer-lahai-roi; that invented starting itinerary is not repeated. Rehoboth's immediate explanation follows the text without an unverified modern site identification or requirement that land ownership forfeits eternal hope.

## Genesis 27:1–29

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Hebrews 11:20 directly names Isaac's future-oriented blessing of both sons; Acts 2:23 supplies the wider coexistence of God's purpose and human responsibility. No identification of Jacob's disguise as Christ's sacrifice.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: PDF changes from saying Isaac did not know Rebekah's revelation to asserting he certainly knew it and was knowingly conspiring against it. Neither private conversation is reported; public page gives the oracle and actions without claiming exhaustive knowledge of motives. Exact ages 137/77, unreported deathbed legal formula, universal requirement for all-family witnesses, and automatic patriarchal enforcement of the prior sale were not independently established, omitted. Birthright and spoken blessing are related but not flattened into a complete legal code transferring every possession. Suspicion from voice/touch does not prove each detail of a guilty conscience. No claim Rebekah had no alternative because admitting overhearing would legally silence her. Jacob's concern about deception is not made proof he had always hated lying, and his false attribution to God is not called partly true in a way excusing it. Isaac's meal/smell is not made proof all sensory knowledge or hunting is inherently sinful. A word's sense field/territory does not turn the smell of clothing into a hidden prophecy of modern national geography.

The PDF's two goats/Day of Atonement parallel was checked against Leviticus 16:7–10, 15–22. There one goat is slain and another remains alive to be sent away; Genesis takes two goats for food and skins, without assigning one as each role or describing expiation. The NT does not identify this disguise as that ritual. No adequate independent support obtained for an established correspondence, so omitted and flagged rather than teaching it as God's stated purpose. Rebekah cannot be declared able to remove Jacob's moral responsibility merely by saying a curse should fall on her. God's successful purpose is not approval of her method or proof all four actors committed the exact sins assigned by the teacher.

## Genesis 27:30–46

Plan section: included, because (a). Hebrews 12:16–17 explicitly recalls Esau's sale and later tears; Hebrews 11:20 expressly treats the blessings concerning the future. Warning is preserved without a new site ruling on salvation/rewards.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Isaac's trembling is reported, but a Hebrew term for trembling does not by itself prove all his thoughts, repentance, or why God frightened him. Esau being physically firstborn remains true, despite selling the birthright, so his self-identification is not called another literal lie. No conclusion he received no property whatsoever: spoken blessing does not list a full distribution of assets, and later Genesis depicts Esau with possessions. Hebrews 12 is not used to prove a complete history of his inward repentance or that he only ever wanted money. BSB follows the away-from wording in 27:39; Wesley's commentary differs, and his paraphrase is not substituted for BSB. Edom's later history and the younger/older theme are acknowledged without an unverified century-by-century account. The PDF dates Jerusalem's destruction AD66 (the commonly documented destruction is AD70), claims all Edomites were wiped out then, and enforces perpetual hostility as a uniform national/eternal destiny; none is adopted. No direct identity of every descendant with the serpent's seed or prediction establishing a particular end-times system. Later reconciliation in Genesis also cautions against claiming the brothers can never have any common ground.

Rebekah's warning is relayed, but the passage does not identify the informant or make this another divine revelation. Murder threat is not a guarantee Isaac would execute Esau under a verified contemporary legal code, nor proof Esau would be immune once patriarch. Genesis records her fear of losing both. Exact travel mileage/months, death before Jacob's return, and complete explanation of her inward motives are not stated here. Her expected short absence can be contrasted with the later long stay without assigning an unreported date of death.

Actual BDB 5551 gives ladder; bishops' note prefers stairway. Neither establishes the teacher's literal-stairwell-only claim, so public page retains BSB and focuses on the dream's movement/promise. God's expressed promise of care is retained without guaranteeing all faithful people avoid suffering or asserting the teacher's complete causation system. No declaration Jacob wholly misses God's point or believes God is physically trapped at Luz; Wesley explicitly reads his awe differently, and Genesis 35 recalls presence wherever he went.
