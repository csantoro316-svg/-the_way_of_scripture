## 1 Peter 2:18–20

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). The immediate next paragraph explicitly relates endurance to Christ's innocent suffering; Romans12:17–21 links nonvengeance/doing good with trust in God's judgment.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baseline assertion most servants voluntarily entered slavery, or Greeks were mostly modern-style indentured workers, has not been independently verified; actual lexicon and beating context do not support reducing the passage to ordinary employment. No universal statistics used. Modern employment analogy must not become prohibition of seeking safety, reporting wrongdoing or taking available freedom. 1Cor7:21–23 explicitly allows freedom; actual Calvin also limits obedience by God. Cinderella anecdote not needed. The text calls treatment unjust, not morally permitted because a master acts. Heavenly credit-account mechanism is teacher's model, not an independently established quantitative rewards system. Nonvengeance does not confer saving power on the abused person's suffering. No owner position derived merely from discipleship links.

## 1 Peter 2:21–25

Sheep metaphor is not a judgment every nonbeliever lacks intelligence.
