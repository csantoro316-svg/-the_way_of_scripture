## 1 Corinthians 15:20–23

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Romans 8 explicitly connects Jesus’ resurrection and the indwelling Spirit with bodily redemption, adoption, glory and creation’s release.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Christ’s firstfruits are not reduced to an isolated return to ordinary mortal life. Exact claims about Enoch’s or Elijah’s present bodily condition are not independently established here and are omitted. The baseline’s universal claim resurrection was impossible in every sense before Christ is not imposed over the Bible’s accounts of people restored to life. Adam/Christ is Paul’s own comparison; no biological rule that sin passes solely through male reproduction, or that a virgin birth mechanically eliminates genetic sin, is inferred. Christ’s human life is not described as the divine Son being created. The own-coming statement is not made a lexical proof that coming never means His return to reign, nor is a separate pretribulation event made the owner’s conclusion. Those who belong to Him remain Paul’s stated group; neither automatic universal salvation nor a complete theory of all groups’ final outcomes is deduced from all in isolation. No exact Levitical calendar or modern harvest-history claim is asserted beyond the actual text checked.

## 1 Corinthians 15:24–28

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Ephesians 1:9–10 explicitly states the uniting purpose in Christ; Revelation 21:1–4 explicitly describes God dwelling with His people and the final absence of death and pain.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s full set of resurrection stages, a required distinction between church and Old Testament saints, and every detail of a thousand-year chronology are not present in these verses and are not stated as consensus. WCF/Wesley and DTS readings are attributed fairly from actual primary documents. No exact future state of unbelievers’ physical constitution is established here. The Father is not placed among the enemies subjected to the Son. The baseline’s proposed later single expression of the Godhead, disappearance of the Son’s human form and return from three distinct expressions to one is not established by this text or an independently checked source and is omitted. It must not become a new site position. The Son’s submission is described without asserting that He ceases to be divine or that Father/Son cease to exist distinctly. God being all in all is not made to prove every being is finally saved or absorbed into God. The public plan describes the stated biblical goal without imposing one disputed account of every stage leading to it.
