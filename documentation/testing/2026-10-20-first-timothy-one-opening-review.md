## 1 Timothy 1:1–2

Plan section: included, because (c). Acts 9:10–16 places Paul's commission within the mission to Gentiles, kings and Israel; Titus 2:11–14 links saving grace, Christ as hope and His purpose of a people for Himself. This adds the mission and future behind the greeting.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's reconstruction that Timothy had written a request to leave Ephesus, was unhappy as a pastor or preferred traveling is not stated in the letter and is not independently established. No such correspondence or motive is invented. True child in the faith is kept as a close gospel relationship without asserting an unrecorded exact conversion event. The claim about a traditional extra Jewish mercy greeting is unnecessary and unverified, so omitted. No ruling about the cessation of every use of apostle is originated from the greeting alone.

## 1 Timothy 1:3–7

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Ephesians 3:8–12 connects God's wisdom made known through the church with His eternal purpose in Christ; Ephesians 4:11–16 directly parallels teaching that forms a mature, truthful, loving body. The goal reaches beyond winning a dispute to a people formed under Christ.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The exact myths, genealogical system and office of the teachers are not identified. Wesley proposes Jewish pedigrees, USCCB proposes speculation about Old Testament figures, and the baseline offers a wider reconstruction. These are not turned into a certain named movement or invented curriculum. The text's stated effects and positive goal are enough for the public explanation.

The baseline's turn-aside phrase is not given an unverified out-of-joint etymology or treated as a lexical proof of eternal security. The next group's law discussion is kept in context. Paul states the goal as love flowing from a pure heart, conscience and faith; these are not reduced solely to teacher credentials. No blanket rule that disagreement itself proves false teaching or that all careful comparison of interpretations is forbidden is introduced. The owner's explicit fair-primary-source editorial standard remains the working standard.

## 1 Timothy 1:8–11

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Romans 3:19–26 explicitly moves from knowledge of sin through the law to righteousness through faith in Christ; Galatians 3:23–29 places the law in relation to Christ, sonship and shared inheritance. These support a wider purpose without assigning a complete covenantal system to the owner.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's broad statement that requiring believers to observe the law is always unlawful is not consensus on moral instruction. Actual Wesley 8–11 and Westminster 19.5–7 affirm moral guidance alongside the exposure of sin and distinguish this from earning justification. The public account does not impose every Mosaic civil or ceremonial provision on the church or erase all moral instruction.

The BSB list is quoted as verified, including its main slave-traders rendering and its kidnappers footnote. No unresearched legal history or claim that every earlier instruction directly names each present offender is invented. The main explanation concerns the role and proper use of the law, not a new owner position on every contemporary ethical dispute touched by a listed term. No lexical or historical claim is supplied for another Greek term without checking it.

## 1 Timothy 1:12–17

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Acts 9:10–16 explicitly makes the feared persecutor a chosen messenger; Titus 2:11–14 joins saving grace with a people redeemed and formed for good works while awaiting Christ. Paul's stated example to future believers displays that purpose in one life.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's exclusive prominent-not-worst explanation of protos is not established by Thayer and does not replace the BSB. No claim that taking sin seriously is itself a boast is adopted. Paul's account is kept without comparisons to named modern atrocities or a guessed count of victims. Acts distinguishes consent and persecution from a complete record of Paul personally committing every execution; no invented killings are added.

The role of ignorance and unbelief is stated as Paul states it, without turning ignorance into innocence or a universal guarantee of salvation. The baseline's distinction between qualification for office and forgiveness is not made the only possible meaning. A whole disputed sequence of election, faith and regeneration is not settled by a physical or grammatical analogy. The shared point of mercy, abundant grace and encouragement to later believers is retained; no party's broader system is silently assigned to the owner.
