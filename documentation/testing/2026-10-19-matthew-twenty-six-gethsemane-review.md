## Matthew 26:36–46

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). Psalm 75 supplies a labeled judgment-cup connection; Isaiah 53 and 1 Peter 2 explicitly connect Christ's suffering with bearing sins and entrusting Himself to God. Hebrews 5 joins prayer, suffering, obedience, and salvation; Hebrews 4 adds the sympathetic sinless high priest and present help. This retains the baseline's rich salvation/prayer/comfort connections while checking each passage directly.

Position source: What Does Prayer Look Like? / Prayer and Faith. Honest grief and repeated prayer under the Father's will, limited to what these studies develop.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's unique My Father prayer claim is not reliable: Matthew 11:25–27 also contains Father-addressed prayer and My Father in Jesus' speech, while Matthew 26 repeats My Father in 39 and 42. No uniqueness claim is used. No unsupported diagnosis of Luke's sweat comparison as a named medical condition, inference that omniscience makes Jesus physically feel future pain in advance, or ranking of His anticipation as exactly as painful as every later event. No minute-by-minute midnight timeline, private Roman warrant trip, or unseen thoughts attributed to Judas. The text's one-hour rebuke is not replaced by a supposed one-hour countdown before arrival. It does not say Jesus only managed one sentence before returning, nor that we lack more prayers solely because eyewitnesses slept. The third prayer occurs after His leaving the sleeping disciples in verse 44; the order is preserved. Temptation is not reduced to sleep alone. No equivalence of Jesus' sinless humanity with sinful flesh, claim His divine nature left Him, or attribution of rival divine and human persons. Psalm 75 is a supported wider connection, not proof that the Greek noun always means wrath. No claim all private prayer automatically supplies a known outcome, or that fear is a failure of faith. The actual position does not promise removal of the trial.
