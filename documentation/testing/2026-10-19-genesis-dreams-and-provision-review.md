## Per-group decisions

### Genesis 40:1–23 — The Cupbearer and the Baker

Context: royal officials' unspecified offenses, Joseph's attention to distress, God's interpretation, different outcomes, request for release, fulfillment and forgetting. The text permits seeking release while faithfully serving; it does not require passive approval of injustice.

- Plan section: included, because (c). Psalm 105:17–22 explicitly situates Joseph's prison/testing/release/appointment sequence within advance provision. Second Corinthians 1:3–7 supplies the wider comfort-and-care-in-suffering theme, identified as a comparison between different settings.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's precise poisoning plot, cupbearer's complete food-chain duties, baker's subordinate status, pharaoh's Hyksos ethnicity, eunuch diagnoses for both officials, and the best-prison reconstruction were not independently established; the text leaves their offense unspecified. Wesley also records conflicting offense conjectures rather than confirming one. The claim that only Joseph and Daniel in all Scripture interpret dreams is overbroad and unnecessary. No exact extra-prison years, Noah's construction duration, or curriculum of Egyptian legal studies is inferred. The suggested baker/cupbearer = unbelieving/believing criminals at the cross is not established by the men's responses or a direct NT explanation; not published as prophecy or as proof of either man's eternal destiny. Egypt-wide protected-bird behavior and precise afterlife consequences of the execution were not independently verified; omitted. The actual BSB outcomes suffice.

### Genesis 41:1–13 — The Dreams of Pharaoh

Context: two full years, cows and grain dreams, royal advisers unable to explain, cupbearer's tested recollection. His exact motivation for the earlier silence is not invented.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Acts 7:9–14 joins Joseph's eventual rescue/appointment with famine and the family's move. Psalm 105:19–22 supports waiting before release as part of the longer provision story.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: recurring pairs as a certain code for Christ's two comings is not established by the repeated dreams; Genesis itself will explain this repetition in 41:32. Cow = a particular goddess, grain = a fixed national symbol, and every dream's lasting psychological effect were not independently verified; not necessary to the narrative, so omitted. The PDF's exact mechanics of Satan's future knowledge, all diviners' powers, and all black arts exceed the passage; omitted. Cupbearer's exclusive self-protection motive and a claim that this was his first possible opportunity are not supplied by the text. No universal promise that every sufferer will receive Joseph's earthly promotion is drawn.

### Genesis 41:14–36 — Joseph Interprets Pharaoh's Dreams

Context: shaving/change of clothing as narrated, credit to God, common interpretation of both dreams, seven-plus-seven years, stated reason for repetition, a fifth collected, reserves. Interpretation and Joseph's proposed response are distinguished.

- Plan section: included, because (c). Psalm 105:16–22 places wisdom and authority within preservation during famine. First Corinthians 12:4–7, 11 gives the wider theme of God-given ability serving the common good; Paul's church context is expressly distinguished from Joseph's Egyptian setting.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's appeal to Herodotus is not sufficient to prove Joseph shaved every part of his body or underwent a painful process; only the narrated shave/change is used, without adding a costume/hygiene reconstruction. The story does not establish the timing of all later gifts or decide contemporary disputes about gifts. A distinct future stage of Christ's ministry is not inferred from a rigid division between Joseph's two abilities. Exact political/ethnic motives for royal trust are not independently verified. No current financial rule or guaranteed reserve outcome is prescribed to readers from the particular one-fifth plan.

### Genesis 41:37–45 — Joseph Given Charge of Egypt

Context: royal approval, God-given wisdom, delegated authority, ring/linen/chain/chariot/public obeisance, new name and marriage, observable clothing reversal.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(b). Acts 7:9–10 explicitly identifies God-given favor/wisdom and Pharaoh's appointment. Wesley XLI:44 gives a long-established Joseph/Christ authority parallel; Philippians 2:8–11 supplies Christ's suffering, exaltation, and universal homage. Egypt's delegated office is distinguished from Christ's greater lordship.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: precise divine reason for the age of thirty and a priesthood/Jesus age code is not given. Pharaoh's ethnicity as the explanation of trust, Egyptian literacy curriculum, naturalized citizenship rules, universal introduction of chariots by the Hyksos, and exact official title have not been independently verified; not stated. The proposed definite translation of Zaphenath-paneah is not needed, and the PDF and Wesley offer differing renderings; no uncertain etymology is taught. A conclusion about Pharaoh's conversion is not supplied. Asenath as a mandatory exclusively Gentile church-bride prediction is not established by the narrative/NT; omitted. Mixed ethnicity is not itself treated as moral guilt or as a genetic qualification for salvation. No claim that Joseph's limited power exhausts or exactly reproduces the Father/Son relationship is made.

### Genesis 41:46–52 — The Seven Years of Plenty

Context: thirty-year-old Joseph, collection/storage, abundant grain, two sons and Genesis' explanations of their names, gratitude amid continuing memory. Hebrew meanings checked against the actual BDB entries, not only modern topical commentary.

- Plan section: included, because (c). Psalm 105:16–22 gives advance provision; Genesis 48:5 gives the sons' later place in the promise family; Second Corinthians 1:3–7 supplies the wider theme of comfort within suffering.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: specific armies of builders/guards and undocumented administrative machinery are not narrated. The inference that the name Manasseh proves Joseph has erased all family memory, already fully forgiven each offense, or intentionally refused all contact is not established; Wesley XLI:50 likewise questions literal abandonment of the father's household. Forgiveness is not defined as inability to remember trauma. God's remembrance/forgiveness is not described as cognitive amnesia. The PDF's assertion that God personally inflicted every victim's injury through an offender and will send more injuries to anyone struggling to forgive is not established by these naming notices; not published. Ephraim's significance is fruitfulness as Genesis explains, without requiring the more specific double-fruit numerical scheme. No exact restored-wealth reward formula or assurance that pain ends with promotion is imposed.

### Genesis 41:53–57 — The Famine Begins

Context: famine as announced, preserved food, direction to Joseph, grain sold, surrounding peoples arriving, preparation for the brothers' visit. No unsourced worldwide geological extent or casualty count is inferred.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(b). Acts 7:11–14 directly connects famine, food journeys, and reunion. The provider-of-food/giver-of-life parallel is supported by Wesley XLI:44 and John 6:27–35; Joseph's grain sustaining bodily life is clearly distinguished from Christ's gift of eternal life. Psalm 105:16–22 supports the longer provision story.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's generalized claim that every famine/earthquake/war can be diagnosed as a specific means of bringing an individual to faith is not established by this narrated famine; omitted. Exact historical Nile regularity or chronology, extent of deaths, and a complete end-times Israel scheme are not independently verified; not published. God's saving use of this hardship is not a moral license for human-caused starvation. Joseph's food distribution is not described as identical to Christ's saving work or claimed to be a direct prediction named by John 6.
