## 2 Timothy 4:1–5

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). The charge explicitly invokes Christ's appearing, kingdom and judgment; Acts 10:39–43 joins testimony to Jesus' resurrection, judgment and forgiveness. Romans 10:13–17 gives the mission purpose of proclamation for hearing and faith.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's equation living=saved and dead=unsaved, with a necessary separate rapture/reward versus postmillennial judgment timetable, is not established by this verse. The public text retains Christ's judgment and appearing without imposing that scheme. No modern pulpit format, exclusive topical-versus-expository rule, claim that all teaching is private and all preaching must occur outside the church, or specific invitation technique is inferred from the checked proclaim verb.

No unverified cultural decline chronology since the eighteenth century, percentage of unfaithful churches or hidden motives of Timothy's congregation is public history. The tickling/itching-ears image is not given an invented child-tickling biological mechanism. No Laodicea name etymology or universal church-age chronology is imported. The baseline's precise emotional-reproof/intellectual-rebuke distinction is not independently verified as lexical fact. The passage's patient instruction, correction, encouragement and persistence are explained plainly. Calvin and Wesley supply actual interpretive agreement; no new owner policy is created.

## 2 Timothy 4:6–8

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Numbers 28:1–8 gives the drink-offering setting, Philippians 2:17–18 repeats Paul's own service/death image, and 1 Corinthians 15:50–58 places steadfast labor within resurrection and death's final defeat.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The biblical drink offering is checked; the baseline's asserted exact last ritual act and pouring on the ground are not independently established by Numbers 28 and are not added. Paul's life-service comparison is not another atoning sacrifice. No certainty that he had been told the exact day or mechanism of execution is supplied. No unsupported prisoner biography, execution procedure or specific imperial hearing is inserted.

Calvin and Wesley both connect future reward with God's grace; Calvin's hostile account of Catholics is not made their official teaching. No owner's conclusion on merit or universal kingdom land allotments, crown-to-badge conversion, twenty-four elders' precise identity or reward distribution timetable is originated. The future crown and shared hope are retained, without making Paul's expectation a claim that every believer can duplicate every aspect of his apostolic assignment or that feeling afraid always proves no faith.

## 2 Timothy 4:9–15

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Acts 13:13 and 15:36–40 supplies Mark's earlier departure and the dispute over taking him; Colossians 4:7–11 confirms later coworker relationships. Present usefulness shows earlier failure need not have the last word in gospel service.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline wrongly assigns Demas's desertion motive to Crescens and Titus; the text simply gives their destinations. Calvin and Wesley explicitly distinguish those departures. They are not all called apostates, nor is Titus identified as a different person from the letter's Titus without evidence. Demas's final salvation and later life are not reported; Calvin's limited interpretation and USCCB's broader possibilities are named fairly.

No claim that Luke wrote both books solely from Paul, that Paul had all Old Testament texts memorized, that the requested scrolls' contents are known, or that every journey was flight is invented. Tychicus is sent to Ephesus in the actual text, rather than certainly traveling to escort Timothy. Carpus's exact hosting role, cloak type and origin of each writing are unknown. Alexander's identity with 1 Timothy 1:20 is possible, not certain, and not made a public certainty. God's repayment is not a mandate for personal retaliation. Mark's earlier Acts history is checked and does not require judging Paul or Barnabas motives beyond the account.

## 2 Timothy 4:16–18

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). Verse 17 itself states the Gentile proclamation purpose. Daniel 6:19–27 is a supported, explicitly labeled rescue-and-witness parallel; Acts 23:11 directly describes the Lord standing near Paul and assuring witness in Rome.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The first defense's exact date, forum and place within a proposed two-imprisonment timeline are not reconstructed as fact. No assertion that every Roman citizen always received one execution procedure, that Paul was literally placed among lions or that lion certainly means Nero is made. Calvin explicitly prefers danger generally over one ruler. Daniel is labeled a parallel, not a claimed explicit quotation or proof Paul was in the same kind of den.

The baseline's exclusive second-death definition of every rescue is not simply imposed. Calvin emphasizes preservation from turning into evil; Wesley recognizes rescue beyond death. The text's actual heavenly-kingdom hope is retained alongside approaching physical death. No promise that a faithful person can never be injured or killed is created, and Paul's mercy request is not made a guarantee that every deserter was unconverted or that every wrong must be treated identically. No new owner perseverance ruling is originated.

No new owner office, healing, apostolic succession or Holy Spirit identity doctrine is inferred from the closing blessing. The BSB singular spirit and plural you all are retained; a footnote Amen is not promoted to main text.
