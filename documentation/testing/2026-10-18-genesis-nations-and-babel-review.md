## Genesis 10:1–32

Context follows the chapter's family/language/territory summaries and the actual names that prepare for chapters 11–12. The chapter-11 account explains the scattering already summarized here. Modern nations are not equated with ancient names.

Plan section: included, because (c). Shared origin and God's concern for the nations in Acts 17:24–27; blessing all families in Genesis 12:1–3 and Galatians 3:8–9; the nations worshiping in Revelation 7:9–10. Acts 17 is explicitly not presented as naming Genesis 10.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline assigns ancient names to modern Russia, India, Italy, and other populations and builds an Ezekiel 38 war identification on that map. Genesis 10 and Wesley's caution about mixed/lost ancestry do not establish those identifications; no independent specialist corroboration obtained, so omitted. The claim that one-third of living people descend from each son does not follow from three sons; no demographic evidence supplied, omitted. Nimrod's name as certainly meaning rebel, a claim that “before the LORD” must mean opposition, and a detailed Antichrist identification are not established by the passage; the verified Babel entries do not prove these separate claims. No such lexical/prophetic conclusion included. Peleg's division is not assigned a precise date or made a claim about continental drift; chapter 10 gives no such date or mechanism. A fixed symbolic rule for seventy and a supposed seventy translators are not necessary to the chapter and were not independently established; omitted. The source's broad east=evil/west=God rule and Eden's exact placement are not proved by this passage, omitted.

## Genesis 11:1–9

Context explains the stated motives, reversal, and BDB-verified wordplay. God's direction to fill the earth is in Genesis 9:1, 7; the builders' wish to avoid scattering is explicit in 11:4. The tower inspection is explained as a literary contrast, without inventing a limit on God's knowledge.

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Babel's self-made name contrasts with God's promised name/blessing in Genesis 12. Pentecost is labeled a parallel, not an explicit quotation/fulfillment of Babel. Acts 2 retains multiple languages; Revelation 7 gives the further picture of shared worship. Wesley himself compares Babel with Pentecost in his verse-7 notes, but the public teaching is grounded in the checked Scriptures.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Chapter 11 does not name Nimrod as the tower's builder, identify the original language as Hebrew, state that Eden was here, or say every later religion began in this tower. Read chapter 10 alongside 11 and checked BDB's Babel entry; those checks do not establish those additions. Omitted. The source's equation of modern English/internet communication with an end-times undoing of this judgment has no explicit support in the cited Scriptures, omitted. USCCB identifies a possible Babylonian tower, but no independent archaeological source was checked for this batch; no precise tower dimensions, construction date, or archaeological identification asserted. No reconstruction of the workers' conversations beyond what Genesis itself gives.

## Genesis 11:10–26

Context follows Shem's branch through Peleg rather than Joktan, notes the general—not strictly uniform—decline of lifespans, and uses Joshua 24 to avoid making genealogy itself proof of personal faith. Names and ages checked against BSB.

Plan section: included, because (a). Luke 3:34–36 expressly places these ancestors in Jesus' genealogy. The additional Cainan in Luke is acknowledged with the bishops' note identifying the Greek Genesis reading; no claim that both displayed lists contain exactly the same names.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's fixed rule that ten always means testimony, and that God deliberately delayed events in order to produce ten names, is not stated in Genesis or Luke; omitted after reading both. Exact overlapping-lifetime claims depend on chronological/textual decisions; BSB and USCCB's note about differing Hebrew/Greek/Samaritan ages do not establish the proposed historical chain of oral transmission. No asserted Adam-to-Jacob four-man chain or proof of how Moses obtained the account. Joktan's descendants are not called unbelievers collectively, nor Peleg's all believers: Genesis does not disclose that distinction. A special origin for the word Hebrew was not needed and was not independently researched beyond the commentators' proposals; omitted.

## Genesis 11:27–32

Context follows the stated relationships, death of Haran, childlessness, and departure with settlement in Haran. Acts 7 supplies the initial call before living in Haran. No undocumented reason assigned to the stop.

Plan section: included, because (a). Acts 7:2–8 explicitly connects the journey to God's promise before Abraham has a child; Hebrews 11:8–12 recalls both the journey and Sarah's later conception. The childlessness is the narrative obstacle, rather than a claim that infertility is proof of wrongdoing.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF's precise city population, acreage, wall thickness, invasion chronology, and claim Abram grew up under a specific occupying force were not independently confirmed. Read Genesis 11, Acts 7, and the bishops' Ur note; none confirms that reconstruction, omitted. No firm choice of a modern Ur site or miles traveled needed. The text does not state that Terah was born in Haran, named a son after that hometown, or stopped because he was sick; omitted. Wesley's age-130 reconstruction and the bishops' different chronological/textual explanation show that first-listed Abram cannot simply be assumed born at Terah's age seventy; public prose does not force a birth order or chronological solution. No conclusion about whether Terah ultimately believed, beyond Joshua's stated household idolatry. Do not turn the baseline's general faithfulness lesson into a new site position.
