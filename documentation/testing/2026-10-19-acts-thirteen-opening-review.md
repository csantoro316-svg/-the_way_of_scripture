## Acts 13:1–3
Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Acts 9:15–16 and Galatians 1:15–16 identify Saul's calling toward the Gentile witness; the community sends and supports him.
Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's assignment of the first three as prophets and final two as teachers, Saul as least and Barnabas as highest, is not established here. Lucius is not simply identified as Luke; Simeon's nickname does not establish a full biography or precise birthplace. No rank is inferred merely from name order. Worshiping/service has several lexically attested applications; no exact ritual or absence of all singing is inferred from the word. The narrative does not give a universal rule that every departure from every ministry must follow this exact ceremony or cannot be valid otherwise. No certainty about the men's private expectations or future career ambitions.

## Acts 13:2 legacy

## Acts 13:4–12
Plan section: included, because (b)/(c): Acts 9 supplies the labeled blindness-and-leading parallel; the witness reaches synagogue hearers and a Roman official.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Salamis is not equated with Soli as in the PDF. No independently unverified distances, temple history or job contract is asserted. Saul's double name is not presented as a new name bestowed at conversion, a reward from the governor or a universal mystical transition every believer must experience. No claim that every unbeliever can be personally accused of being a deceitful fraud on this specific account's authority. Luke says Paul was filled with the Spirit; it does not say he was waiting to avenge his own blindness. Elymas's blindness is temporary, but his later conversion is not narrated. The proconsul's response includes astonishment at teaching, not a miracle that removes any need for the message. No blanket assertion that almost all Jewish hearers everywhere reject the gospel or that all previous ministry was inferior.
