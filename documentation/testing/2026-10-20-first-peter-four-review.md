## 1 Peter 4:1–3

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Romans6:6–14 explicitly connects Christ's death/resurrection with freedom from sin's mastery and offering one's life to God.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline reverses or oversimplifies aorist/perfect tense meanings and uses this to argue an exclusively momentary pause in sin while suffering. No independent standard grammar was checked to support that claim; do not use it. Actual Thayer3973 does not supply that rule. Calvin's union-with-Christ/new-life reading and Wesley's inward conformity are checked directly. The text must not be rewritten as all pain automatically removes all sin. Christ's innocence is distinct from the believer's need to leave sin. Blanket assertions about Catholic penance/purgatory are not verified from Catholic documents here and are excluded. Generic television/fitness/hobby/working-hours examples do not create universal sinful-activity rules. No claim every non-Jew practiced every named vice; Calvin himself rejects that blanket inference. No racial inferiority implied by Gentiles.

## 1 Peter 4:4–6

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). John5:24–29 explicitly joins hearing/believing and life with Christ's authority to judge and the future resurrection.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The spiritually-dead reading in the baseline is one possible reading, not established as the only sense; the actual BSB says now dead. Calvin's own departed-person reading, Wesley's preaching-before-death reading and Catholic634 descent teaching are checked in their own texts. Calvin's editor reports spiritually-dead readings of other teachers, but their own documents were not checked; these are not attributed as verified individual primary-source positions in public. No guarantee all observers will be converted or all unbelievers always personally hate every act of goodness. No fixed new timeline of judgment or universal second chance after death; the Catholic context expressly distinguishes freeing the righteous from freeing the damned. Common section does not force all traditions into one doctrine of when faith becomes possible.

## 1 Peter 4:7–9

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Matthew24:42–51 pictures readiness as faithful care of the household; Romans12:9–13 joins hope, prayer, love and hospitality in present community life.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: End-nearness not converted into a date or reduced exclusively to each individual's death; Calvin explicitly includes universal renewal, Wesley the end of wrongs/suffering. No invented prophecy schedule. Love's covering faults does not make Christian acts an independent payment for sin, nor turn concealment of harmful wrongdoing into a new site rule. No unsupported universal ancient hospitality customs repeated as history. Prayer is not a specialized gift that relieves everyone else of the instruction to pray. Baseline examples not treated as guaranteed career/family choices for every reader.

## 1 Peter 4:10–11

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). 1Cor12:4–7 explicitly describes varied gifts/services from the one God for the common good, giving the wider shared-purpose setting of stewardship.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Gifts are entrusted for service, not proof a speaker's every opinion is infallible or all claimants equally qualified to teach. Calvin emphasizes appointed teaching, Wesley broader speech especially teaching; difference not hidden. No new site position that every form of service must await certainty about a single permanent gift label. No comprehensive office, ordination, gender or charismatic-continuation policy inferred. Doxology pronoun referent differs among interpreters; no extra divine-person conclusion settled by pronoun alone. Related studies linked but not treated as owner interpretation of these precise verses.

## 1 Peter 4:12–16

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). John15:18–21 explicitly connects hostility to disciples with the hostility Christ Himself faced; Peter places that shared path beside future revealed glory.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baseline Roman legal liberties/syncretism generalizations, precise Nero pyres, local Asian readers facing the Colosseum and exact audience-wide imminent deaths lack independent verification here and are omitted. Fiery trial need not prove literal burning or exact date; USCCB notes real interpretive uncertainty about intensified language. Current comparisons of Western countries, Iran or Saudi Arabia are not researched contemporary facts and are not repeated. Christian literally little Christ and always originally a derogatory title not independently verified; no invented synagogue street scene. No inference the devil's personal attention proves a particular person's faith or lack of suffering proves false faith. The source's accidental omission of not before suffering as murderer is not the actual text; actual BSB retains none. Calvin's real grief/hope coexistence supports avoiding pressure to deny pain.

## 1 Peter 4:17–19

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). 2Thess1:4–10 explicitly relates present persecution, God's righteous judgment, relief for sufferers and accountability at Christ's revelation.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Judgment here is not narrowed into an owner-approved exclusive reward-seat sequence or exact end-times chart. Calvin's fatherly correction and present course, and Wesley's primary temporal deliverance with eternal application, attributed directly. Proverbs11:31 differs in BSB wording from the quotation in BSB Peter; both verified, not silently harmonized. No claim every illness identifies a specific personal sin, every victim deserves the human mistreatment or every apparent enemy lacks faith. Entrusting oneself while doing good does not prohibit honest lament or seeking help. Creator retained as BSB even though Calvin prefers possessor. Eternal destruction in the cross-reference is not used to settle every disputed final-punishment interpretation.
