## 1 Corinthians 12:1–3

Plan section: included, because (a): John 16:12–15 explicitly states that the Spirit glorifies the Son and makes His truth known to the disciples.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s assertion that all gift questions were settled for 1,900 years was not independently verified and is omitted. No exact delegation supplying this question is invented. Saying Jesus is Lord is understood as genuine confession, not physical inability to pronounce words or a mechanical verbal salvation test. The passage is not made to settle every dispute over baptism in the Spirit, the sequence of conversion and empowerment, or final perseverance. In particular, the baseline’s claim that 12:3 itself proves permanent salvation even after professed rejection is not imported; Paul’s stated test concerns speech by the Spirit. Acts exceptions are not declared the only possible cases in all subsequent history. Wesley’s claim that every Jew and pagan cursed Jesus is not adopted as verified history. Intense emotion is not in itself proof of either divine or demonic inspiration, and the passage is not used to reject every meaningful personal experience.

## 1 Corinthians 12:4–7

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Ephesians 4:7–16 explicitly connects gifts with the ascended Christ, equipping the saints and the body’s growth toward unity and maturity.
Position source: What Is the True Church? — shared gifts and every believer’s responsibility to serve, content/studies/true_church.md line 83.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The position is limited to the study’s actual common-good/shared-ministry conclusion. It does not establish a new owner view on which gifts continue, a gift-identification test, the exclusion of every kind of recognized ministry leadership, or an exact conversion chronology. The baseline’s categorical claim that gifts are never natural abilities put to new use is not established by the passage or lexicon and is omitted. No promise that one gifted speaker can guarantee a conversion, or that numerical response rates prove a gift, is adopted. The baseline’s spiritual gift of prayer is not added to Paul’s list as if he names it here. Percentage calculations of the Spirit are not used as textual claims. Difference of task is not equated with difference in human value or with exemption from ordinary Christian responsibilities.
