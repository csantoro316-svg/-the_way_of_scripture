## 2 Corinthians 1:1–2

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Acts 18:1–11 explicitly recounts gospel work and the congregation's beginnings in Corinth, connecting the letter's care with the continuing mission of forming Christ's people.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's precise visit/letter chronology, dates, sea distance, population range, claims of Corinth as Paul's last European city, compulsory universal trade-guild membership and temple prostitutes guarding Aphrodite's temple were not independently verified and are omitted. Acts' account of Corinth and Paul's earlier European travels/Rome work makes the last-city claim especially unsuitable. Lost-letter reconstruction is not given a consensus label or asserted as a proved four/five-letter numbering. Statements about 4,000 government-registered denominations and the exact number of regional churches are unchecked and omitted. Naming believers saints is explained in the address without resolving a new system of election or imposing an unsupported exclusive category of sustaining grace distinct from saving grace. The greeting's peace is not divided by an invented of/from rule; Romans 5:1 actually has peace with God. Manuscript-variant generalizations are omitted without independent textual research.

## 2 Corinthians 1:3–7

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Romans 8:17–25 explicitly relates suffering with Christ to future glory and bodily redemption, setting present help within God's larger saving purpose.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Comfort's range includes consolation and encouragement; a root-word story cannot exclude consolation or prove that only physically sharing an identical trauma counts as ministry. Exact vocabulary occurrence totals were not independently counted and are omitted. The baseline's claim lack of felt comfort always proves lack of intimate knowledge is not stated by Paul; the next paragraph itself describes despair beyond personal endurance. No formula guarantees immediate emotional relief, a successful business, or a particular financial outcome. Philippians 4's petition language is not asserted to be exclusively a legal pleading without checking that lexicon. Psalm 23's first-person valley sentence does not prove the speaker sinned or chose a wrong path; that proposed explanation is omitted. God's love is not excluded as a purpose by a claim about His name; both are expressed in Scripture. Christ's sufferings are not converted into a claim every believer's pain repeats His saving work. The baseline's personal tragedies and business experiences are not appropriated as our own. No new owner position is created from pastoral application.
