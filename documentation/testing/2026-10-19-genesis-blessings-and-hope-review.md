## Per-group decisions

### Genesis 48:1–22 — Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh

Context: Luz promise, adoption and two tribal shares, later children counted under brothers, Rachel, gratitude, poor sight/deliberate crossed hands, divine shepherd/redeemer, return and land. First Chronicles distinguishes birthright and ruler roles; John/Joshua identify the remembered land without asserting a detailed unrecorded battle.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Hebrews 11:21 explicitly calls the blessing faith. Romans 8:15–17/Ephesians 1:5 supply the Bible-wide adoption/heir theme, labeled as a comparison rather than a prediction of every detail.
- Position source: none. Relevant Angel study linked; its developed position does not directly settle this verse.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: baseline's claim later sons would be excluded as Egyptians conflicts with 48:6, which counts them under their brothers' inheritance; omitted. Its bed-to-staff correction in 48:2 does not follow from the separate 47:31 variant; BSB retained. Exact all-thirteen equal shares/no continuing prominence system conflicts with the roles/distinctions actually described; not adopted. Rachel/Ephrath/Ephraim wordplay does not prove an intended replacement for a hypothetical unborn child. Precise legal knee/adoption ritual, all unreported thoughts about senility, and citizenship requirements were not independently established; public text follows BSB movement. BSB specifically says father's knees; no need to reverse it. Redeem root does not alone prove Jacob possessed a full doctrine of incarnation or automatically identify every angel as the Son. The identity is attributed to actual Christian commentators; no invented binding denomination table. A later absence of named children does not prove Joseph had none. Shechem is Joseph's burial, not Jacob's; Jacob's grave is Machpelah. Sword-and-bow reference remains historically obscure; Wesley/Henry propose recovery after dispossession, while the USCCB note offers a link with chapter 34, but no unreported conquest is asserted as fact. All suffering as personal punishment/directly inflicted evil is not established by this blessing.

### Genesis 49:1–28 — Jacob Blesses His Sons

Context: all twelve tribal futures, rebukes connected to actual earlier acts, Judah's rule, brief sayings for all sons, LORD's salvation, Joseph's care and blessing, Benjamin's strength. Later Simeon/Levi biblical roles checked without imposing ethnic character stereotypes.

- Plan section: included, because (a). Revelation 5:5–10 explicitly takes up the Lion of Judah and Root of David in the slain Lamb who redeems people from all nations. First Chronicles 5:1–2 distinguishes Joseph's birthright and Judah's ruler line.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: a single compulsory end-times chronology for every line is not established by days-to-come language. The text addresses future tribes and includes later royal fulfillment without requiring every image to refer only to one future millennium. Unsupported perfect continuity of Judah's political office, exact dynasty dating, and blanket claims that every ancient version/DSS translates identically are omitted. BDB/USCCB show genuine textual/translation uncertainty; it is not hidden. Rashi's messianic reading is checked directly without converting his Jewish identification to Christian belief. Issachar's supposed laziness/joy in sin, all descendants genetically inheriting fathers' defects, a nonexistent absolute lack of Reubenite leaders, and exact motives of later tribes are not established here. Dan's first-in-all-idolatry claim and speculative reason for absence in Revelation 7 are not supplied by Genesis; no serpent = entire tribe eternally condemned claim. Naphtali's BSB beautiful fawns is not silently replaced with eloquent speech because the baseline uses another rendering. Zebulun's exact shoreline boundaries and a necessity to resolve them only in a future territory map were not independently established here. Joseph's Shepherd/Rock language does not explicitly state that every earlier scene was a prediction of Messiah; supported parallels in prior groups stand on their own evidence. Baseline/Wesley language about Joseph's blessings surpassing all forefathers reflects different wording from BSB's ancient mountains; the verified BSB is retained. No precise agricultural/vine strength, war history, or exhaustive list of tribal heroes is invented.

### Genesis 49:29–33 — The Death of Jacob

Context: exact family burial site and named burials, Leah's burial newly supplied, Rachel distinction, charge to sons, final death.

- Plan section: omitted, because none apply beyond the burial faith already explained in 47:27–31 and the funeral/hope connection developed in the next group. No repetitive placeholder.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the source's speculation that Jacob willed his own death or received an unreported exact death-date revelation is not narrated. Leah's burial does not establish that marital rivalry continued into death or that she won a competition; the personal comparison is not published. Gathered to his people is not made a full map of intermediate-state geography. The family grave and actual deaths are described without invented inner conversations.

### Genesis 50:1–14 — Mourning and Burial for Jacob

Context: real grief, physicians/embalming forty days, Egyptian mourning seventy, permission and promise to return, large procession, local naming and seven-day lament, Machpelah burial/return.

- Plan section: included, because (c). Hebrews 11:13–16 supplies faith beyond present inheritance; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–14 joins grief with resurrection hope through Jesus. Joseph's tears are not faulted as lack of faith.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: exact standard Israel forty-day and Pharaoh seventy-two-day mourning schedule plus seventy/one/one prestige ladder are unverified; Genesis says forty days for embalming, not a universal Israel mourning custom. Physician choice may avoid religious practices in some readings, but a direct order to exclude ritual is unreported; no independent claim of it is made. Greatest funeral in all Scripture was not exhaustively checked; not needed. Exact west-bank route, Pharaoh's Hyksos motives, Egypt's precise military control of Canaan, and officials as hostages/property ensuring Joseph's return are unverified; omitted. Lazarus' hypothetical annoyance and knowledge of how every deceased person feels are not established; omitted. Embalming is not presented as necessary for resurrection. Public text preserves across-the-Jordan wording without forcing a modern map solution.

### Genesis 50:15–21 — Joseph Comforts His Brothers

Context: renewed fear, reported fatherly message, tears, personal appeal/slavery offer, God/place question, evil intent/good purpose, specific life preservation, ongoing kindness and provision for children.

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Catechism 312 explicitly places the Joseph/Christ comparison together; Acts 2:22–24 supplies the cross/purpose/human-responsibility account. Romans 12:17–21 supplies the wider refusal-of-revenge/practical-care theme.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF asserts the brothers certainly invented Jacob's message. Genesis reports it without independently confirming or denying it; public explanation does not decide authenticity. Every powerful man always taking revenge, every inner fear or reason for tears, and Joseph's exhaustive private understanding are not narrated. BDB's Hiphil specifically supports preservation of life here, not the baseline's claim that the form literally means resurrection or healing in this context. God's good purpose does not approve the betrayal, collectively blame later Jewish people for the cross, or justify harming someone in expectation of a good result. A complete future national conversion timetable is not established by this passage; omitted. No new site-wide determinism/free-will position is originated from one teacher's reading.

### Genesis 50:22–26 — The Death of Joseph

Context: 110 years, family generations/knees, future divine care, oath concerning bones, embalming/coffin, distinction from Jacob's immediate burial.

- Plan section: included, because (a). Hebrews 11:22 explicitly names faith and the coming exodus/bones instruction; Exodus 13:19 and Joshua 24:32 directly show transport and burial fulfillment.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's exact twenty-seven ancient references to ideal 110-year life and God's precise reason for choosing that age were not independently checked; omitted. A visible public coffin display continuously accessible for four generations is not stated. Joseph never requesting a pyramid does not prove an offered pyramid/royal tomb invitation. Exact chronological scheme for every generation and Exodus interval is not needed to explain the oath. The list of Machir/Gilead generations is not expanded beyond what this passage names. No current burial practice or exact intermediate-state doctrine is made a condition for future resurrection.
