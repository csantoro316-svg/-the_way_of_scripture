## Passage decisions

### Genesis 42:1–24 — Joseph's Brothers Sent to Egypt

Context: famine, ten travelers, Benjamin withheld, bowing and remembered dreams, recognition concealed, accusations, altered confinement arrangement, explicit acknowledgment of guilt, interpreter, tears, Simeon bound.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Acts 7:11–13 explicitly recalls the first visit and eventual revelation; the page does not move the revelation prematurely into this visit. Second Corinthians 7:9–11 supplies the wider repentance-and-changed-action theme. The brothers' later behavior remains to be seen; their present guilt is not diagnosed as definitively false repentance.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's detailed end-times timetable, ten-as-witness code, famine-as-certain-tribulation prediction, and a demand that every modern Jewish individual satisfy the Benjamin condition are not established by Genesis or the checked NT recollection; omitted. The role of the famine in this narrative is retained without that system. Exact reasons for failure to recognize Joseph (all hair shaved, makeup, changed ethnicity/dynasty) were not independently verified; only recognition and interpreter are narrated. PDF says Joseph was sixteen at sale, contrary to Genesis 37:2's seventeen; not repeated. Exact ancient Egypt/Canaan war chronology and a certain espionage penalty are not established here. Simeon's selection because Joseph now heard Reuben defended him is plausible but unstated; Wesley offers different conjectures. No exhaustive certainty about Joseph's thoughts, a direct new divine command, or the reason for each test is claimed. A sympathetic interpretation of testing is not a general permission for coercive or abusive discipline. The brothers' statement that one is no more does not establish all their private beliefs about Joseph's fate.

### Genesis 42:25–38 — Joseph's Brothers Return to Canaan

Context: supplied grain/provisions, secretly returned silver, fear, report, every bag of silver, Jacob's distress/refusal, Reuben's rash guarantee.

- Plan section: omitted, because none apply beyond what the immediate explanation already supplies. The hidden care/limited knowledge theme has been developed in preceding groups; a new standalone connection would repeat that explanation or force the PDF's detailed end-times scheme.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: returned money as certainly intended only to deter a return and force a life-risking ransom is not explicitly explained. The brothers' fear is narrated; a guaranteed legal death sentence for them is not independently verified. Jacob's statement is not converted into a proven interrogative accusation or proof he knew the original sale price. The text does not say they showed Jacob the twenty shekels after selling Joseph. Their report and his fear suffice. Reuben's offer does not approve killing innocent grandchildren or establish a valid legal contract. Jacob's refusal is not certainty that he believed Simeon had died; the text records distress about his loss/absence. A general account of karma, religious polemics, and all victims' suffering as direct payback is outside what this passage establishes and was not independently verified; omitted.

### Genesis 43:1–15 — The Return to Egypt with Benjamin

Context: continuing famine, Judah's response, personal guarantee, contrast with sale proposal and Reuben's offer, gifts and double silver, prayer, acceptance of risk, return.

- Plan section: included, because (c). The promise family's preservation is accompanied by changed treatment of another brother. Luke 3:8 supplies the Bible-wide theme that Abrahamic ancestry does not replace the fruit of repentance. The page traces a developing commitment rather than declaring Judah completely mature before its later test.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's Jacob-name = disobedient / Israel-name = godly code is not established as an invariant rule; not used. Its assertion that the brothers carry triple the first amount conflicts with the BSB's double amount in 43:12, 15; no twenty-sack versus twenty-shekel ransom code is taught. Exact journey durations, two-month imprisonment, current food prices, and the low economic value of the gift are not independently established. Judah's promise certainly means forfeiting all inheritance or constitutes the culturally greatest possible guarantee is not stated. Jacob's prayer plus gifts do not prove God was his insincere backup strategy. The national-rejection/tribulation/return system is one teacher's detailed construction, not an explicit explanation of this passage by Jesus or Paul; omitted. No collective modern ethnic character judgment is drawn from Jacob's fear. The brothers' unreported absence of all fear is not inferred from Judah's resolve.

### Genesis 43:16–34 — Joseph's Hospitality to His Brothers

Context: feared enslavement, steward's reassurance/received payment, Simeon released, hospitality, bowing, concern for Jacob, Benjamin and tears, separate service, birth-order seating, large portion, shared meal.

- Plan section: included, because (c). Romans 2:4 explicitly gives the wider theme of kindness leading toward repentance. This limited connection follows the story's actual kindness without requiring each servant, room, payment, or portion to predict a distinct end-times event.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the steward says he received their silver; this does not explicitly prove Joseph separately paid another bill on their behalf. No cross-payment prediction is derived from that unverified reconstruction. The steward's private conversion or full education in Israel's God is not narrated. Shalom does not mean all their moral/spiritual incompleteness has already been resolved. Joseph's house = Jerusalem temple, steward = Holy Spirit as Christ's servant, invitation = required last-days retreat, and each detail = Antichrist timetable are not established by Genesis/checked NT; omitted. Unverified racial/Hamite/Hyksos dining reconstruction is replaced by the reason the verse actually gives: Egyptians find eating with Hebrews detestable. Exact combinatorial odds and claims that birth order was visually impossible to discern are unnecessary and not textually established. Benjamin's portion may test jealousy as the PDF suggests, but the narrator does not state that motive; the public page describes the observable outcome without diagnosing every man's heart. No completed reconciliation is declared before Joseph reveals himself.
