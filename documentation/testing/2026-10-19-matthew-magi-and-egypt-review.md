## Per-group decisions

### Matthew 2:1–12 — The Magi Seek the King

Context: Herod/visitors contrasted, term magi, limits of number/name/royal title, Bethlehem and Micah/shepherd wording, Herod's concealed intention, star/house/gifts/dream, meaning of bow. Common readings: Catholic official adoration/nations and Wesley's monarch-homage emphasis; Irenaeus gift symbolism versus Wesley's practical emphasis; two verified early star explanations, explicitly individual teachers rather than mandatory denominational views.

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Psalm72 and Isaiah60 offer well-established scriptural gift/nations parallels. Matthew8:10–11 and28:18–20 explicitly develop the Gospel's welcome and mission beyond Israel. John19:39–40 supports a labeled myrrh/burial comparison, without saying Matthew reveals the magi's intended prediction.
- Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It — Matthew2:11.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: exact origin Babylon/Persia/Arabia, a 700-mile route, power to appoint kings, splendid clothes, wealth/guards, 600-year inherited Daniel school/curriculum, and Daniel's calendar causing the trip are not established by Matthew or the checked lexicon. Daniel9 is Hebrew, not the claimed Aramaic; no calendar theory used. Matthew does not identify the star exclusively as returning Shekinah glory, date its departure in Ezekiel to create a verified six-century schedule, or give a physical mechanism; early interpretations are reported fairly as proposals. Claimed absence/reappearance chronology beyond what is narrated, day visibility, precise star movement assumptions, and astronomical identifications remain unestablished. Exact toddler age, rental-house tenure, year-plus Bethlehem stay, parents' deliberate delay for magi, diapers, nativity encounters, and exact names/count are not given; omitted. Three gifts do not require three men. The visitors' full knowledge of crucifixion, intercession, or sacrificial payment cannot be proved from the gifts; Irenaeus is a sourced interpretation, not a transcript of their confession. Spices funding the flight is plausible/Wesley-proposed but not narrated as fact; no modern dollar conversion, aureus/salary arithmetic, monetary amount, or three-month wage claim verified/used. Frankincense is one of equal incense ingredients in Exodus30, not proved the primary ingredient; myrrh's most-common use is not established by John19. No claim that deity implies the family had no financial needs. Baseline contemporary Christmas grievance, imagined words from dead Herod, all-world motives, racial purity or converts not being truly Jewish, and Esau as Jacob's son are excluded. Original Scripture does not support that genealogy error (Esau is Isaac's son). Exact reign/death dates, senate appointment date, disease diagnosis and hostage funeral story require independent historical checking and are not needed/used. Josephus web attempt did not supply read evidence; no historical assertion rests on it. Matthew's specific hostile actors do not prove all Jews rejected Jesus or permanently forfeit God's promises.

### Matthew 2:11 — The Magi Honor Jesus

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Same documented Psalm72/Isaiah60 honor/nations parallels and Matthew8/28 expansion; not a new prediction assigned to every gift. The focused legacy page remains a valid overlapping reference inside the coherent 2:1–12 group.
- Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It — Matthew2:11.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: all relevant visitor/gift/age/knowledge limits above apply. No altered translation is labeled BSB. The study's royal-homage conclusion is attributed as an existing site reading, while alternative/common views are independently described fairly. Lexical range alone cannot settle the visitors' inner knowledge; no additional conclusion originated.

### Matthew 2:13–18 — Escape to Egypt and Weeping in Bethlehem

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). Matthew itself applies Hosea11:1 to Jesus; Exodus4:22–23 gives Israel-as-son setting. Exodus1–2 supplies the clearly labeled Moses infancy/deliverance comparison. Jeremiah31's actual restoration context and Revelation21:3–4 support hope beyond real grief without explaining each death or promising escape to every believer.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: flight to Alexandria, exact 60-mile border/500-mile route/travel days, crossing the Nile, exact duration, immediate following-day massacre, particular night packaging or parental conversation are not established. Gifts paying exactly the budget/lasting precisely to return, wealth on arrival and poverty on departure, and spices more portable/spendable than cash are not independently verified. The Roman provincial legal arrangement and universal safe entry are not needed; no claim used without historical primary checking. Matthew does not identify the killers as Roman troops from a particular garrison, give a death count, or establish a particular disease/age/medical diagnosis for Herod. Two-and-under orders do not give Jesus' exact age. Modern fourfold PaRDeS terminology as a universally fixed first-century rabbinic system, all four methods prescribed by God, and a rigid rule that only one is appropriate are not verified; no technical-method readout published. SecondPeter1:20 does not simply establish every personal application a violation; no theory imported. Jerusalem's final destruction is not dated605BC; the PDF's precise Jeremiah chronology is not adopted. Jeremiah31's restoration is not proof every Israelite repented or that widespread idolatry never recurred. Bethlehem's mothers are not equated with mothers sacrificing infants or blamed for Herod's violence. The claim that the children's deaths were a necessary evil to satisfy Herod, stop his search and make salvation possible is NOT stated by Matthew and is omitted. Romans8:28–30 was checked but cannot supply that missing causal account. Exodus does not say an angel delivers4:19; it says the LORD. Moses' birth rescue and later adult flight are kept distinct. No guarantee everyone will later understand why every tragedy had to happen, and no new position on the children's eternal fate is invented.

### Matthew 2:19–23 — The Return to Nazareth

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Close Exodus4:19–20 return-with-family wording is a labeled comparison; Hebrews3:1–6 explicitly distinguishes Moses the servant and Christ the Son. Matthew4:12–16 connects subsequent Galilee ministry with promised light. These add the next stage of the Gospel's movement without pretending each route detail is predicted.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: PDF2B ends with return instructions2:20, next lesson starts chapter3. This source gap does not authorize invented commentary; the final verses were researched from Scripture and checked primary Christian sources. No exact OldTestament sentence corresponding to2:23 was found in checked sources. Netser is branch/shoot, not literally Nazareth. Jerome's wordplay, Wesley's rejected-Messiah summary, and Calvin's consecration proposal are different explanations, none made universal church dogma. No claim Jesus took a Nazirite vow, avoided all wine, or that Samson's exact biography dictates His life. Nazarene/Nazirite are not silently treated as identical words. A formal church decree selecting one derivation was not verified and is not asserted. No archaeological population estimate, exact ruler appointment/reign dates, or presumed return route is used. The next lesson's unsupported age13 bar-mitzvah preparation, age17 apprenticeship, Adam-descent denial and humanity/divinity power mechanisms have not been adopted; chapter3 work must investigate any relevant claims independently.
