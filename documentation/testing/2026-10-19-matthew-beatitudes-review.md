## Per-group decisions

### Matthew5:1–12 — The Blessings of the Kingdom

- Plan section: included, because(b)/(c). Psalm37's meek/land and Isaiah61's poor/mourning/comfort form well-supported parallels. Luke4:16–21 explicitly connects Jesus' mission with Isaiah's good news. Revelation7:13–17 shows suffering answered by God/Lamb's shelter, provision and wiped tears, developing future hope rather than repeating the list.
- Position source: The Gospel According to Jesus — The Fullness; Who Shepherds the Church? — Matthew5:4 conclusion.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: sweeping four-century Ezra-school genealogy, every-commandment ten added restrictions, all Jews unaware of Scripture, all Pharisees wealthy hypocrites, universal social rejection/blame for disability and exact Mishnah institutional chronology are not independently established. Written Mishnah cannot simply be projected into every first-century scene as a completed universal rulebook. No exact Arbel/Bethsaida sermon site or universal crowd medical status/motives verified. Matthew says Jesus sat; the sermon is not explained by a certain place where He stood. Count of eight/nine blessings is not a hidden numerical doctrine. All rewards only future is contradicted by present kingdom language; all promises automatically mean going to heaven at death is too reductive. Poverty/hunger terms are qualified here, but that does not justify claiming every listed suffering is entirely unrelated to actual affliction; Luke6 explicitly mentions poor/hunger/weeping. No blanket exclusion of all Pharisees or every member of another religion from God's mercy by a social label. Spiritual inheritance shares precisely proportional to ministry service, exact millennial governing appointments from sons-of-God wording, and peacemaking as only a future geopolitical post are not proved by this text. The mourning and meekness terms are not assigned a narrower meaning by etymology alone. No assertion seeking present justice is futile, every unbeliever lacks compassion, or mercy is simply replaced by violence in all Jewish teaching. The list is not copied into a new site salvation formula; developed Gospel study supplies position. Different mourning readings acknowledged fairly, without editorializing in Common Interpretations. Unsupported source historical/denominational caricatures remain excluded.

### Matthew5:4 — Comfort for Those Who Mourn

- Plan section: included, because(b)/(c). Isaiah61/Luke4 restoration connection, Psalm51 and2Corinthians7 concrete grief/repentance examples, Revelation21:3–4 completion of comfort. The word's scope is distinguished from one sourced contextual interpretation.
- Position source: Who Shepherds the Church? — explicit Matthew5:4 mourning-over-sin conclusion, The Honest Dilemma.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: word pentheō does not lexically mean only sorrow for sin; neither does it specify only bereavement. The site study's narrower reading is preserved as its position rather than hidden in an allegedly neutral dictionary definition. Primary Calvin and Francis demonstrate that a broader reading is not simply absent from historic Christianity. No claim every grieving person automatically receives salvation, every sorrow is repentance, or psychological tears earn the kingdom. Baseline David's exact future body/kingdom comfort explanation is an application beyond Psalm51's stated request; Psalm itself is used without the imagined timetable. No private current grief diagnosis or invented pastoral promise. The old leadership anecdote is removed from Common Interpretations, not asserted as a denomination's official interpretation. No study quotation mislabeled BSB; fresh top quotation uses actual wording.
