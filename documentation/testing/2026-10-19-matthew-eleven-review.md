## Matthew11:1–6 — John's question and Jesus' answer

Context follows the mission instruction, prison question, works, Isaiah references, and final blessing. Common Interpretations fairly distinguishes Wesley's disciples-benefit reading from the bishops' probable personal uncertainty reading. Neither private motive is presented as an established biography or universal denominational teaching. Plan moves beyond the immediate answer to Jesus' explicit Isaiah fulfillment in Luke4 and the wider restoration of Revelation21; present healings are not equated with completion of the new creation.

- Plan section: included, because (a) Jesus explicitly connects Isaiah's promise to His mission in Luke4, and (c) the Bible's restoration theme adds the promised future beyond the immediate works.
- Position source: none. Exact prescribed open notice. Existing studies checked for a developed passage-specific position; a broad faith or gospel topic match is insufficient. The Gospel According to Jesus is further reading only.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: baseline's assertion that John grew up believing in two distinct Messiahs (prophet/son of Joseph and king/son of David), and that this question asks which one Jesus is, is not established by Matthew11, Luke7, or John1's separate questions. No independent first-century evidence for John's private schooling or universal rabbinic scheme was found in the sources checked; leave off page. The alleged Isaiah1–39 suffering-servant versus40–66 ruling-Messiah mapping conflicts with the location of Isaiah53 and is not supported by the cited prophetic texts; leave off page. No prison-release expectation, conversion percentage, remembered childhood instruction, detailed change in John's emotions after the reply, or full omniscience at death is verified. Relationship does not establish first-cousin status or shared childhood. No fixed miracle count or claim that Elijah died normally is needed. These were checked against the recorded question and relevant Scripture rather than reconstructed psychology. Exact prison site/dates and a universal postponed-kingdom system are omitted rather than asserted without independent evidence.
