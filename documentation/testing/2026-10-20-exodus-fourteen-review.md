## Exodus 14:1–14

Plan section: included, because (c). Psalm106:6–13 explicitly recalls rebellion and rescue for God's name; Ephesians2:4–10 supplies the wider theme of grace that displays God's kindness and produces good works. The connection is labeled as a theme, not a detail-by-detail conversion scheme.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF proposes spies tracking Israel, an exact Aqaba beach, 210 miles in seven days and constant day/night marching. Scripture here reports the king was told, not the identity of the messengers, and does not establish that itinerary. No independent evidence has verified these additions; omitted. Modern borders do not settle ancient Egyptian territory or the crossing. The USCCB note explicitly leaves the named sites unidentified. No claim that all traditional maps are necessarily wrong, or all guesses equally evidenced. Pharaoh's personal motive in the text is loss of service; no invented biography or named dynasty. Six hundred select chariots plus others retained without speculative total, wheel technology or unsupported strongest-army ranking.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Moses' promise concerns this rescue; no guarantee that every believer avoids death, all anxiety means personal guilt, or every failed ministry program is God's disclosed intention. Standing firm does not forbid following the next command to go forward. No general ban on prayer; both Calvin and Wesley explicitly reject the idea that God disapproves of Moses praying in 14:15. Hardening difference supported by Calvin14:17 and Wesley4:21, presented as individual teachers' explanations, not all Protestants' uniform official teaching. No new owner predestination position. Psalm106 reports later forgetfulness; no blanket claim that nobody at the sea had faith (Heb11:29 explicitly says by faith).

## Exodus 14:15–31

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Hebrews11:29 directly interprets crossing by faith; 1Cor10:1–12 explicitly connects cloud/sea/baptism into Moses with instruction and warning for the church. Isaiah43:16–21 explicitly recalls sea/chariots before promising a new work. The plan section adds subsequent biblical use beyond the miracle/angel comparison in Common Interpretations.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline says yam suph cannot mean sea of reeds. Actual BDB5488 does give reeds/rushes and probable sea-of-reeds explanation, with applications to both arms of the Red Sea. The baseline categorical lexical claim is excluded; BSB Red Sea wording preserved. This lexical range cannot locate the crossing, prove a shallow-marsh crossing, or remove the text's walls/dry ground/judgment. Ancient Arabia always equals the present Saudi state is an unverified and misleading geographic assumption; not used to force Gal4:25 into a modern map. No exact twelve-mile width, four-hour crossing time, two-million-person logistics or walking-speed medical/generalization claims imported.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Calvin identifies the angel as the Son; USCCB note describes God's power rather than an independent spiritual being. Their actual readings are distinguished, with the note's limited authority made clear. No new site position from an unrelated study. No claim that cloud material is the divine essence, Son is part of Father, or all angels bearing divine language are automatically Christ. BSB14:25 says wheels wobble: no replacement with another translation's detached wheels as certainty. Full pursuing force that entered sea dies; no inferred pharaoh identity or claim every Egyptian throughout the nation died. No unverified Jannes/Jambres death tradition. God uses wind in the text; no physical simulation or wind speed offered as independently established mechanism.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baptism into Moses in Paul is described without inventing a settled doctrine about infant baptism, mode, efficacy or later church membership. The text says Israel crossed dry; not an account of Israel being physically immersed. Paul also warns about later failure; no claim physical rescue automatically means eternal salvation for each individual, and no denial of Hebrews' express faith statement. Moses' role does not authorize unquestioning obedience to every later leader or suppress testing teachings. No newly originated owner doctrine.
