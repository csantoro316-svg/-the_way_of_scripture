## Per-group decisions

### Matthew8:14–17 — Healing and the Promise of Isaiah

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Matthew explicitly applies Isaiah53:4 to Jesus' healings; Isaiah53:4–12 and1Peter2:21–25 connect the servant's work with bearing sins on the cross. Romans8:18–25 and1Corinthians15:42–55 add the promised bodily redemption/resurrection, beyond temporary relief. Full redemption is not postponed in every respect, and present suffering is not denied.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: exact fever diagnosis, all ancient physicians treating fever as a separate disease, and the woman nearly dying are not recorded/independently checked. No claim every apostle had a wife:1Cor9:5 is not evidence for that universal inference. Peter's mother-in-law is stated without converting the passage into a complete rule about clerical marriage. “Conservation of Miracles” and the99.9% statistic are not established sources or measurements. The baseline's first-by-word-only/middle-without-word comparison does not fit Matthew8:3's touch and8:13's spoken response; not copied. No universal claim every present sickness is healed by correct faith, nor a claim all physical healing has ceased. No soul-as-only-real-self/rental-car anthropology, erasure of bodily continuity, requirement to disregard health, or sweeping atheist worldview claim is derived.1Cor15 describes the dead raised and changed; bodily hope is retained without the rental-car picture. The exact date and universal first-century authority of the Mishnah's healing rules are not independently checked. Mark/Luke supply Sabbath/sunset context, but no blanket ban on all healing is stated. Matthew's healing fulfillment is not explained away as entirely spiritual; BDB and actual text checked. No claim every NT quotation automatically imports every detail of its original chapter; the wider servant connection has actual supporting Scripture. Wesley, Catholic, and Assemblies explanations are accurately attributed, with Pentecostal present/future qualifications kept.

### Matthew8:18–22 — What Following Jesus Will Cost

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Matthew16:24–27 joins following, cross, life, and coming glory; Hebrews11:8–16 supplies the explicitly labeled Abraham/pilgrimage/promise parallel. Adds the Savior's path and lasting hope beyond a rule of self-denial.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Luke9:57 places the related exchange along a road, not a confirmed Magdala departure after a particular parable. Exact travel chronology, crowd size, boat position, and motive for Matthew's arrangement are unverified and omitted. Scribe's insincerity, flattery, desire for glamorous travel, and hoped-for reward are not narrated. No blanket self-enriching description of all rabbis or universal rabbinical-school practices is supported. Son of Man does not always mean only humility/suffering: Matthew16 and Daniel7 checked. Father already dead versus future death remains unspecified. Universal oldest-son residence obligation, years of delay, later burial, and the man's exact household history are not independently established. Wesley acknowledges uncertainty. No new categorical two-tier doctrine of saved non-disciples versus disciples, universal lifelong-believer statistics, or automatic reward schedule is adopted from one teacher. The passage does not cancel every family responsibility. No assertion Abraham never entered cities or claimed land; Genesis has already recorded land purchase. His tents/city hope is used as Hebrews describes. “God cannot steer a stationary object” is neither a textual limitation on God nor a rule for private revelation and is omitted.
