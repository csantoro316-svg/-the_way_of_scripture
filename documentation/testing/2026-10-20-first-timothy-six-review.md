## 1 Timothy 6:1–2

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Philemon 1:15–17 is an explicit related household setting, not the same household; Galatians 3:26–29 locates both slave and free among God's children and heirs in Christ.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baseline claims that Roman slavery was almost always voluntary indenture, that the yoke implies the person's own financial mistakes, that the slave was usually responsible for the condition and that slavery can be reduced to a modern mortgage are not established. Gaius 1.82 independently shows inherited slave status. No 60-million estimate, demographic reconstruction, confirmed Ephesian slave revolt or motive of newly believing slaves is made public. The letter's actual call to service is retained without declaring slave ownership God's ideal or treating people as ordinary freely employed workers. No present-day civil or employment policy is originated. The spiritual family connection does not assert that the passage itself abolished the institution.

## 1 Timothy 6:3–10

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Luke 12:15–21 directly parallels accumulating possessions while unprepared for God; Matthew 6:19–24 connects treasure, the heart and serving God.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's exact reconstructed prosperity message, donation scheme and deliberate motives in Ephesus are not established beyond the text's actual greed and corrupt teaching. The insistence that every contrary teacher must be knowingly deceptive is not generalized to all mistaken teachers. The claims that Jesus spoke about money more than any other subject, that it is wrong to seek relief of poverty, and that the text promises every faithful person permanent material security are not adopted. Jesus' poverty sayings do not cancel chapter 5's care or 6:18's sharing. No unverified ancient economic percentages or medieval modern demographic parallels are included.

The baseline's restriction of these verses to slaves is not certain: the letter moves from slaves to the wider problem of corrupt teaching and greed. The suggested literal punishment-piercings behind verse 10 are not verified; the text's sorrow language remains. Thayer confirms contentment and love of money, not a special word proving that shelter or medical needs should be dismissed. The BSB food-and-clothing wording remains. No owner position about permanent kingdom wealth ranks, automatic financial removal by God, final apostasy or a universal Christian budget is originated.

## 1 Timothy 6:11–16

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). The text itself names Christ's testimony before Pilate and future appearing; fresh John 18:33–37 supplies its direct Gospel setting. Second Timothy 1:8–10 explicitly connects endurance in suffering with God's grace, Christ's appearing and victory over death.
Position source: Who is The Angel of the Lord?, lines 107–117 and 139–144.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Timothy's precise confession occasion is not specified. Wesley's possible baptism and Calvin's broader ministry witness are accurately distinguished. The charge does not independently establish that Timothy was poor or primarily tempted by a slave rebellion. The qualities in verse 11 are not made a fixed sequence or six-step program, and faith is not lexically reduced to community attendance.

Calvin and Wesley's independent-life explanation does not erase actual confessional differences over human life after death. Catechism 366, Westminster 32 and Adventist Belief 26 are checked directly and fairly summarized. No new owner ruling on soul consciousness or conditional immortality is invented. The owner's identification of the Father is drawn narrowly from the existing study, with its broader visible-Son interpretation explicitly attributed to that study. The baseline's absolute claim that nobody in any heavenly vision has ever entered the Father's presence is not inferred from the light language as a new established fact.

## 1 Timothy 6:16

## 1 Timothy 6:17–19

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Matthew 6:19–21 supplies the treasure-in-heaven parallel; Acts 4:32–35 shows actual shared provision for people in need, without making its exact arrangement universally mandatory.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Calvin's own grace-based reward explanation and Wesley's own warning about withholding grace are accurately distinguished. Calvin's hostile description of Catholic merit is not made the Catholic position. This section names those two teachers' reasoning without manufacturing an official single Catholic, Wesleyan or Reformed verdict on every detail of reward. No statement that almsgiving independently purchases eternal life is attributed to either writer.

The baseline's promise of a literal reversed economic rank for rich believers in the kingdom is not verified from this text. Its certainty that God will remove someone's provision to teach them a lesson is not a guaranteed sequence in the passage. Job's saying is not mislabeled as Paul's quotation. The command to share and the value of future treasure remain; the public text does not supply a divine investment-return schedule, compulsory gift amount or an owner position about all earthly enjoyment.

## 1 Timothy 6:20–21

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Second Timothy 1:13–14 returns to the entrusted message, with the Holy Spirit's help; 2:1–2 connects its faithful transmission to people who will teach others.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Thayer's deposit/trust entry and the context support responsibility for received teaching. Wesley's charge and Calvin's gifts/grace readings are individually attributed. The baseline's expansion to flock, spiritual gift and testimony is not treated as the only lexical meaning. USCCB's possible gnostic connection is not an established identification of a later mature named movement; unnecessary historical taxonomy is omitted. No blanket ban on answering sincere questions, all apologetics, disagreement or respectful discussion is invented. No unverified final biography of Timothy's success or exact motive of every opponent is supplied. The BSB plural you all is retained; no main-text Amen is added.
