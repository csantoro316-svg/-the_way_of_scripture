## Per-group decisions

### Matthew 1:1–17 — The Genealogy of Jesus Christ

Context: David/Abraham, title Christ, three periods/fourteens, demonstrated selection of generations, women/family history, Joseph identified as Mary's husband, transition to birth explanation. Common readings: shared promise emphasis, then two primary-sourced explanations of differences with Luke, neither presented as the Gospel's explicit full reconstruction.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Second Timothy 2:8 identifies the risen Jesus as David's descendant; Galatians 3:8,16 directly connects Abraham's blessing with Christ and the nations. Second Samuel 7 supplies the royal promise and Matthew 28:18–20 the Gospel's outward mission. These add the larger promise-to-mission movement beyond a name list.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: baseline's exact Gospel interviewing/sourcing (Joseph or Jesus' siblings supplying Matthew, Mary supplying Luke, Paul's visions supplying Luke's birth account) is not established; omitted. Luke's own stated investigation is not reduced to a single secret source. Universal detective-conspiracy rules, exact Gospel audience/date/memory theories, Matthew knowing nothing before baptism, and all other authors never meeting Jesus are not independently verified here; not published. Full temple archives open like a modern national library and no historical dispute ever occurring are not established; Africanus/Eusebius itself reports disputes and a different archive tradition. Neither is made unquestionable history. All four women being Gentiles and three sexually guilty is unsupported: Tamar's ethnicity is unspecified; marriage to Uriah does not prove Bathsheba's ethnicity; women are not singled out as a uniformly immoral class. Ruth's Moabite origin is actually verified. Jesus-name total 749 and a universal numeric proof system are unverified; not used. David=14 is a plausible publisher-note explanation, but the public passage need not depend on that hypothesis. Exact counts of omitted kings and a settled solution to every grouping/count problem are not claimed. Matthew's Joseph line versus Luke's Mary line is a sourced interpretation, not uncontested fact; the actual Luke wording and early alternative are preserved. The Jeconiah curse in Jeremiah 22 is real, but a compulsory genetic/legal bypass through one proven Marian genealogy is not demonstrated by Matthew; omitted. No claim that royal authority or God's grace depends on racial purity. No invented Jewish/Gentile rejection timetable is inferred solely from the order David/Abraham. The private conversion anecdote and insulting claims about what most Jews believe are not independently verified or relevant to this passage; excluded.

### Matthew 1:18–25 — The Birth of Jesus Christ

Context: binding pledge, reader told divine origin, Joseph's intended quiet divorce, angel/dream and son-of-David address, saving name, Matthew's Isaiah explanation in its Ahaz/Davidic setting, obedience, birth and naming. Shared Christian conception teaching and distinct official lifelong-virginity teaching are fairly separated from a commentator's limits on until.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Galatians 4:4–7 directly connects the Son born of a woman with redemption/adoption. Matthew 26:27–28 connects the announced saving from sin with forgiveness through His self-giving; Romans 5:15–19 gives the Adam/Christ contrast without a genetic theory. Matthew 28:18–20 carries the Immanuel/presence theme into mission to the nations.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: exact universally arranged betrothal price, groom/family buying an unblemished woman, a mandatory one-year pregnancy-detection interval, both partners necessarily virgins, exact relocation/writ procedure for private divorce, and inevitable execution if Joseph complained are not independently established. Matthew and the independent publisher note establish a binding bond and planned divorce without that reconstruction. The source's imagined kitchen conversation, Mary's every inner response, universal family/friend ostracism, and Joseph's exclusively selfish motive are not narrated; omitted. Joseph and all Jews never hearing of the Holy Spirit is unsupported by Scripture and cannot be established by Acts 19's particular group; omitted. The angel's reported words are distinguished from Matthew's explanatory fulfillment comment in verses22–23. Biological manufacture/insertion of a male component, sin passed through male DNA, and Jesus not descending from Adam are not stated here; Luke3:38 and Westminster8.2 affirm genuine humanity from Mary. Genesis2:7 uses neshama, so the baseline's direct ruah word claim is not repeated. The Greek/Hebrew word ranges are not a substitute for reading Matthew's explicit conception statements or Isaiah's original Ahaz setting. Almah alone does not settle every question about Isaiah's near/future reference; that detailed Isaiah study is not invented here. Jesus' sinlessness is not reduced to one speculative mechanism or made a denial of His humanity. The text does not prove Joseph fixed a particular wedding date. Until alone does not settle later sexual relations; no single Protestant doctrine against lifelong virginity was confirmed in the checked confession, and no teacher is made to speak for all members. Catholic/OCA claims are verified from their own sources; Wesley/Gill's narrower comments are reported accurately. No later married-life conclusion is originated for the site. A false firstborn reading is not inserted into the BSB, which says Son here. Assurance of God's power is not turned into a guarantee every current marriage/illness/financial trouble ends as a person wants. None of the proposed miracle biology, Mary sinlessness mechanisms, or precise intermediate-state details is needed to explain the announced Savior.
