## 1 Corinthians 14:20–25

Plan section: included, because (c): Zechariah 8:20–23 explicitly describes nations seeking the Lord because God is with His people, a labeled parallel to the visitor’s recognition in verse 25.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Paul’s direct quotation is identified as Isaiah 28:11–12, not as a direct quote of Deuteronomy 28:49; the latter is a related warning. The baseline’s assertion that Israel had never heard Akkadian, a fixed first fulfillment chronology and an invariant rule that any foreign speech in Jerusalem always announces irreversible destruction were not independently established and are omitted. No inference every actual tongue requires unbelieving Jews in the room is imposed; verse 22 does not state that condition. No claim every pre-70 church gathering was mainly in Jerusalem, or that Acts 2:16 is only an analogy denying any Pentecost fulfillment, is imported. Fixed tribulation/kingdom timelines and the baseline’s reading of all Israel are not originated as a site position. Calvin’s two possible sign readings, Wesley’s attention/conviction emphasis and USCCB’s judgment emphasis are sourced, with no pretended unanimity on details. No universal verdict of deception against modern churches is made. Prophecy can affect unbelievers according to Paul’s own example; the stated believers/unbelievers contrast is not flattened into mutually exclusive possible audiences. Visitors’ exact responses and access to their private thoughts are not guaranteed. The Zechariah parallel preserves its own national/Jerusalem setting without declaring every detail fulfilled or replacing Israel by assertion.

## 1 Corinthians 14:26–33

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Ephesians 4:11–16 explicitly makes Christ’s gifts serve the body’s growth in unity, maturity, truth and love.
Position source: Worship as the Early Church Knew It — content/studies/worship.md lines 285–297, direct 14:26 interpretation.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Paul says two or three prophets; the baseline’s two or four wording is a mistake and is not copied. No modern stage, pulpit, classroom or detailed service schedule is retrojected into Corinth. The stated turn-taking, interpretation and weighing are retained without making a particular current service format mandatory. The private-hearer distinction does not prove every local congregation must have all gifts at each meeting. The baseline’s idea that an absent contribution makes a person purposeless is not adopted. A claim to inspiration cannot excuse refusing Paul’s stated order, but no sweeping account that every Old Testament prophet was always in full ordinary consciousness is independently established. Disorder is not proof that no believer present has the Spirit. The study position does not require each person to speak at each meeting or remove discernment from participation. Broad descriptions of all current Pentecostal practice are not adopted from one teacher’s experience.
