## Ephesians 6:1–4

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Deuteronomy 6:4–9 supplies a concrete, supported parallel of teaching God’s words through ordinary family life and passing on His knowledge across generations.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s rule that parental obedience universally ends only at marriage is not established by the verse. The public page follows the distinction supported by actual CCC 2217–2218 and does not prescribe a marriage-only threshold. The numbering of the command varies by tradition, so the text names the command rather than declaring the teacher’s numbering universal.

No automatic guarantee of a particular child’s longevity, conversion, obedience or future character is given. The baseline’s use of Proverbs 22:6 as an unconditional outcome promise and its detailed attribution of parents’ blame require more careful review and are not imported. No particular discipline method, no-questions rule or medical/developmental diagnosis is deduced. The instruction against provocation remains beside the command to obey.

The broad claim that all ancient Roman and Jewish fathers had identical absolute legal powers was not independently checked and is omitted. The Dobson anecdote and generalizations about pastors’ children or modern parenting are not independently verified and are not used as historical facts. The family teaching parallel does not claim all Mosaic covenant provisions are transferred unchanged.

## Ephesians 6:5–9

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Galatians 3:26–29 connects common belonging and inheritance across slave/free status; Philemon 15–17 supplies the concrete appeal to receive Onesimus as a beloved brother.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s population of 180 million and one-third slave estimate was not independently verified; neither is published. Its claim that Roman slavery consisted simply of indentured servants is not established and is not substituted for the actual text’s slaves and masters. No universal assertion that slavery is absent today, that all ancient slaves had the same legal situation, or that employment/prison/military relationships are identical to slavery is added.

The common section addresses the actual paired responsibilities and checked commentaries. It does not pretend all historical Christian responses to slavery were identical or that these verses announce an immediate legal abolition program. Nor does it turn Paul’s commands into a new owner endorsement of owning people. No instruction to remain in danger, waive legal rights or treat an earthly authority as equal to Christ is inferred. The promise that the Lord sees good is retained without inventing an exact reward system or guaranteed earthly benefit. The plan’s direct brotherhood connection is presented without an unverified account of Onesimus’s complete legal history or an exact reconstruction of how he left Philemon.
