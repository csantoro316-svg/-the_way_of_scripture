## 1 Corinthians 9:15–18

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Matthew 10 places freely giving what was received alongside provision for kingdom workers, giving the wider mission setting for Paul's choice.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's claim that commanded obedience can never receive a reward goes beyond this passage; Matthew 6 itself speaks of reward for prayer and giving. That general rule is omitted. Paul's commission is not described as lifelong unwillingness or God's approval of grudging service. There is no claim that accepting support makes all other ministers inferior, or that Barnabas certainly worked in Corinth. No new site doctrine of merit or rewards is originated.

## 1 Corinthians 9:19–23

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Ephesians 2 explicitly describes Jews and Gentiles gathered into one body and household with access to the Father through the Spirit.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Becoming all things is not permission for deception, idolatry or any behavior that attracts an audience; Paul explicitly remains under Christ's law. No imagined examples of Paul's discomfort, diet or appeasement are presented as history. No method guarantees conversion or replaces God's saving work. Sharing the gospel's blessings is not restricted by the assistant to an exact heavenly reward scheme. Cultural accommodation does not require erasing Jewish identity or making one culture the universal Christian pattern.

## 1 Corinthians 9:24–27

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Hebrews 12 explicitly directs the race toward Jesus' endurance and exaltation; 2 Timothy 4 connects finishing the race with the Lord's promised crown for all who long for His appearing.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Adokimos does not lexically mean loss of rewards only; the actual Thayer entry does not settle the warning's final scope. Wesley, Catholic study notes and Storms are represented accurately, with formal doctrines identified separately. No claim all Reformed readers share Storms' preferred interpretation, or that he excludes every perseverance reading. The athletic picture is not proof only one Christian can be saved or rewarded. Exact Olympic/Isthmian identification, winner rules and ancient training practices were not independently checked and are omitted. Bodily discipline is explained as purposeful self-control, not a command to injure oneself. No fixed thousand-year duration or mathematical account of rewards is imported. Calvin's assertions about ancient monks are not adopted as verified history.
