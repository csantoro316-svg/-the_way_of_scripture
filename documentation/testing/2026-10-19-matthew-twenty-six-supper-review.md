## Matthew 26:20–25

Plan section: included, because (a). John 13:18–20 explicitly applies Psalm 41:9 and connects advance warning with the disciples' faith and continuing mission. Retains the PDF's insight that the betrayal does not defeat God's purpose while keeping Judas responsible.

Position source: none. Exact open-position wording.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The detailed seating chart, rectangular table, mandatory honor order, youngest-on-right rule, Peter's lowest seat and Judas' self-chosen highest seat lack independent historical verification. John's actual text locates the beloved disciple at Jesus' side but does not supply the full chart or exact ages. No speculative biography follows from dipping into a shared bowl. The claim only Judas spoke up is contradicted by Matthew's account that each asked. John records Judas' departure, but Matthew does not precisely place it relative to bread/cup; no universal timetable is imposed. No claim every nonbeliever must leave a faithful church or that every person who leaves proves the sermon faithful. No unsupported ranking of Judas as receiving the uniquely worst punishment or blanket predictions that every human under pressure must deny Christ. Ezekiel 28's disputed identification is not needed here.

## Matthew 26:26–30

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). Exodus 24 and Jeremiah 31 provide supported covenant connections; Luke 22:20 and 1 Corinthians 11:25 explicitly relate Christ's blood to the new covenant. Paul's whole rebuke in 1 Corinthians 11:17–34 adds the shared-life implications, alongside remembrance, proclamation, and future coming.

Position source: none. Exact open-position wording.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF projects a full later Seder order, hidden/striped/pierced bread, four named cups, door for Elijah, and precise seating rules into this meal without independent first-century documentation. None is taught as established history. Matthew numbers no cups and identifies no hymn; the actual future kingdom promise is preserved without a mandatory missing-fourth-cup explanation. Absence of an explicit roast-lamb mention does not establish absence of lamb from the meal; the alleged missing supplies in John 13:29 are not proof. Walvoord himself proposes a lamb present, illustrating the difference of readings. No claim fruit of the vine technically excludes all wine mixtures or that the whole meal is literally suspended through history. The useful remembrance/until-He-comes connection rests on 1 Corinthians 11, not an invented ritual script. Communion is not made a new site position, and the public comparison does not quote the non-BSB English institution wording in catechisms. Orthodox teaching was not added as a separate paragraph because no further distinctive claim was researched for this group; do not infer all Orthodox share a Catholic account of how the change occurs.
