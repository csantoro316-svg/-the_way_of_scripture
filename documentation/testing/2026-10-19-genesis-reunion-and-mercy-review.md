## Passage decisions

### Genesis 44:1–17 — Benjamin and the Silver Cup

Context: planted cup and money, daybreak departure, knowingly false theft charge, brothers' rash offer and steward's narrower consequence, Benjamin's apparent guilt, all returning, Judah's statement, Joseph retaining only Benjamin. Earlier God-given interpretation is distinguished from the claim about divination; no ritual is invented.

- Plan section: omitted, because none apply beyond the immediate explanation and connections already developed. The next group gives the meaningful substitution parallel. This group does not need a separate hidden cup/ritual prediction.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: precise water/metal divination procedures and demon mechanics are not independently verified; omitted. The narrative does not show Joseph performing a ritual or explicitly explain every part of his claim. Wesley and Henry offer a search-thoroughly rendering; it is not silently substituted for the verified BSB. A certain universal death penalty, a legal entitlement to choose one's binding test/punishment throughout the ancient East, and specific rules for modifying it are not established by the text or independent legal source; only the steward's narrated terms are used. The PDF's same-afternoon reconstruction conflicts with the explicit daybreak departure in 44:3; omitted. Its fifteen-years-since-sale assertion conflicts with the prior ages/years; omitted. Imagined conversations, Benjamin's private pleas, Joseph's exact inner strategy, and an explicit divine command for all the deception are not narrated. Clearing themselves concerns their immediate apparent guilt; the page does not import a complete later doctrine of justification from one root. It distinguishes false theft charge from the real earlier betrayal.

### Genesis 44:18–34 — Judah Pleads for Benjamin

Context: father's attachment and grief, command to bring Benjamin, personal guarantee, offered enslavement, reversal of sale proposal. No claim Judah legally confesses that Benjamin stole the cup or proves his innocence.

- Plan section: included, because (b). Henry explicitly supports the historic Judah/Christ intercession-and-guarantee comparison. Hebrews 7:14, 22–25 supplies Christ's ancestry, better-covenant guarantee, and continuing intercession; Mark 10:45 supplies His life given for many. Judah's offered loss of freedom is distinguished from Christ's greater saving work, not called an atoning sacrifice itself.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the longest-speech claim was not checked against every speech in Genesis and adds nothing; omitted. All private thoughts of Judah/Joseph, exact semiprivate delivery, legal equivalence of exchanged slaves, and biological strength comparisons are not given. A detailed Sheol-compartment doctrine is not established by Judah's mention of death. The PDF's historical fifteen-year interval and suggestion the brothers enjoyed their father's grief are unverified; omitted. The proposed exact moment the Spirit regenerated each brother is not explicitly narrated. Changed actions are visible without diagnosing every unseen spiritual stage.

### Genesis 45:1–8 — Joseph Reveals His Identity

Context: attendants dismissed, loud tears, disclosure, terror, invitation near, sale named, two famine years/five remaining, God sending ahead to preserve life, Joseph's influential place. Human responsibility and God's saving purpose both retained.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(b). Acts 7:9–14 explicitly recalls the rejected brother's rescue/authority and disclosure on the second visit. Wesley 45:1–3 supplies an established divine-compassion comparison with Luke 15:20–24. The page identifies that as a comparison rather than making each attendant a prediction about future Gentile armies.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's rewriting of the father's-alive question as necessarily a declaration is not established by an independent grammatical source; the actual BSB question remains intact. The source's exact year-long testing and all secret thoughts/reasons are not narrated. Genesis 15 does not state that Judah's later marriage genetically endangered Messiah and therefore caused the migration; not adopted. The Canaanite-marriage/genetic-purity and exact modern ethnic guilt claims are unsupported. A complete future-tribulation system assigning each test, Egyptian, private room, and tear a specific prophetic event is not established by Acts' direct recollection or the Genesis text; omitted. Joseph does not become higher than Pharaoh's reserved throne. BDB 662/926 confirm restraint/terror senses but do not establish a secret end-times code. God turning evil to saving good does not approve betrayal, blame victims, or give a revealed identical explanation for every present hardship.

### Genesis 45:9–15 — Joseph Sends for His Father

Context: urgency, Goshen invitation, complete household and livestock, five years of need, visible assurance of identity, embrace and conversation.

- Plan section: included, because (b). Wesley 45:12 expressly compares Joseph's desire to have the family with him with Christ's desire for His people. John 14:1–3 supplies Jesus' promise to prepare and welcome. Egypt's immediate famine refuge and Christ's greater promise are distinguished.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's Goshen as northwest Egypt adjacent to Memphis is not independently verified and is not needed to explain the invitation; no precise geography published. Exact long-prepared sales strategy for persuading Jacob and every unspoken motive are not narrated. The great deliverance in 45:7 may look further ahead within the broader story, but the public page does not claim an explicit Moses prophecy in each word. The PDF mistakenly points to Genesis 47 for Joseph's coffin instructions; those are in Genesis 50, with Jacob's instructions in 47–49. Its wording is not repeated. Hebrews 11:22 will be used with the actual death instructions later. Pharaoh's acceptance is not made dependent on a proven Hyksos dynasty/ethnicity.

### Genesis 45:16–24 — Pharaoh Invites Jacob to Egypt

Context: royal welcome, wagons, gifts, clothes, Benjamin's 300 shekels/five outfits, twenty loaded donkeys, practical provisions, no quarrelling.

- Plan section: included, because (c). Ephesians 4:29–32 explicitly joins received forgiveness with changed speech, removal of bitterness, and kindness. Wesley 45:24 applies precisely that broad theme to Joseph's parting words.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: BDB 7264 supports a wider range including fear in 45:24, while Wesley reads quarrelling. Lexical range alone does not prove the PDF's exclusive fear translation or justify replacing the BSB. A commentator's reading can be discussed without declaring all others mistranslations. Exact Hyksos immigration policy, Pharaoh's two secret motives, and later dynasty chronology were not independently verified; omitted. The clothing does not explicitly predict incorruptible bodies, the ten-donkey pairs are not a demonstrated testimony code, and Pharaoh is not thereby a complete representation of the Father. A precise judgment scheme in which words never count is not established by this travel narrative; not imported. Personal gifts do not establish a universal promise of earthly wealth to forgiven people.

### Genesis 45:25–28 — The Revival of Jacob

Context: unbelievable news, stunned/numb response, reported words and visible wagons, revived spirit, resolve to see living son, transition to the departure after God's chapter-46 reassurance.

- Plan section: omitted, because none apply beyond the immediate narrative/previously developed hope and reunion connections. No forced modern Israel/resurrection conversion timetable is appended to this short response.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: BDB 6313 supports numbness but does not diagnose a specific cardiac condition, prove a heart stopped, or establish spiritual deadness/modern Jewish unbelief as the word's meaning. The PDF's fixed modern-national conversion scenario is not explicitly drawn by the NT from these verses; omitted. Jacob/Israel names are not used as an invariant sin/godliness code. The text does not describe all later questions about the brothers' deception or give license to invent their answers.
