## Luke 10:1–12

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c): John's harvest picture and Paul's account of God's field connect workers' shared labor with God's work and growth.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: PDF treats seventy/seventy-two as an ambiguity in translating one Greek number and claims only NIV has seventy-two. BibleHub BSB main text and manuscript footnote contradict this; use manuscript distinction. No public claim about a universal days-long greeting custom, ancient power-of-attorney practice, or a host already having the Spirit before hearing the message: baseline leads not independently established. The reason for sending pairs is not explicitly identified as a Deuteronomy 19 legal witness procedure; omit that inference as fact.

## Luke 10:13–16

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c): John explicitly connects Jesus' words with the Father and final judgment; Acts shows the continuing mission and dust testimony amid rejection.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No claim that every resident of Capernaum is unsaved or all Jews reject Jesus. The passage addresses towns without identifying every individual's final state. Hades not silently equated with every other Scriptural term for judgment. No unattested historical caricature of Jewish attitudes toward Gentiles.

## Luke 10:17–20

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c): Revelation's related picture of Satan's defeat is labeled without forcing identical chronology; Hebrews connects enrollment in heaven with the people gathered to Jesus.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: PDF's primordial-fall interpretation is not presented as consensus; directly checked Wesley, Calvin and USCCB connect this saying with the mission's defeat of evil. No attempt to settle Satan's whole history or identify the exact Revelation timeline. No claim that every messenger was later martyred; no blanket promise of present-day physical immunity or a site verdict about cessation of gifts. Distinction between Father, Son and Spirit retained; no identification of Spirit as merely another name for Jesus.

## Luke 10:21–24

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): 1 Peter explains prophetic expectation of Christ's suffering and glory; John explicitly identifies the Son as the revealer of the Father.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No claim that learning itself is condemned, that every Pharisee is proud, or that none of the disciples had recognized Jesus before the resurrection. Those broad claims exceed the passage and conflict with earlier recognition. Catechism used only for the directly checked thanksgiving emphasis.

## Luke 10:25–37

Plan section: included, because (c): the Law's neighbor and foreigner commands continue into Romans' account of love and 1 John's practical care under Christ's self-giving example.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: PDF cites Leviticus 19:16 for love of neighbor; actual command is 19:18. Corrected. No invented private thoughts or public humiliation of the lawyer. No claim that the victim is explicitly Jewish, that priest and Levite passed because of purity rules, or that all Jews despised mercy. John 4 supplies checkable Scripture background for the division. No unverified road robbery statistics, mileage, medical efficacy or precise inn cost. No coded assignment of each story detail to Christ/church without textual support. Calvin and Wesley's distinct salvation emphases attributed to the individual teachers; no owner conclusion adopted.

## Luke 10:38–42

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c): Deuteronomy's nourishment by God's word is labeled a connection; James joins receiving the word with practical care.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No identification of Mary as Mary Magdalene or Luke 7's unnamed woman. No inferred lifelong personality diagnosis of Martha, invented gestures of Jesus or claim that household duties and service are worthless. BSB's one necessary thing retained without substituting another manuscript reading.
