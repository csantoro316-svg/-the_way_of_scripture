## Genesis 2:4–25

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(b). Ephesians 5:31–32 explicitly quotes the marriage text and applies it to Christ/church. The further bride/sleep comparison is labeled a parallel, not an assertion that Adam died or that every detail predicts the cross.
- Position source: Returning to the Word / Is the Bible the Inerrant Word of God? Both developed studies compare the recorded command with Eve's added touching restriction. Position remains limited to that developed claim; other questions are not silently declared settled.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's claim that no rain fell anywhere until the flood is not established by Genesis 2's local description; Genesis 2/7/9 were identified as the offered evidence, but 2:5 does not establish the entire later interval, so excluded. The claim that Adam was moved east so he could watch God make the garden is not stated in the checked text and is excluded. The baseline's literal gloss servant in front for suitable helper is not established by BDB 5828 and is excluded. Exact counts for the rib/side word and deductions about the amount of tissue removed are not established by BDB and are excluded. Adam's original language, five-hour animal-naming estimate, and exact timing of animals versus man are not verified by the checked texts/lexicons; excluded. Sleep as definite death, bride-from-side as an explicit prediction, one theological theory of the tree's necessity, ability of incarnate God to sin, and loss-proof salvation deduced from the first marriage are not established by Genesis/Ephesians and are not asserted. Hosea 6:7's disputed reading was not separately researched and is not used to claim consensus on an Adam covenant.

## Genesis 3:1–7

- Plan section: included, because (a). Romans 5 explicitly compares Adam and Christ; 2 Corinthians 11 explicitly applies Eve's deception to Christian faithfulness.
- Position source: Returning to the Word / Is the Bible the Inerrant Word of God? Same narrow word-handling position as above. Existing studies' guesses about Eve's intentions are not copied as fact.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's claim that Eve remained innocent and acquired sin only through Adam's act conflicts with the checked wording of 1 Timothy 2:14; excluded. Her feelings after eating, the psychological reason she offered Adam fruit, the chronology of Satan's fall, universal original snake speech, and detailed serpent possession mechanics are not stated by the checked Scripture; excluded. Whether Adam heard the full conversation is not settled by being with her when he ate; no assertion beyond that verse. The source of the added touching restriction is not supplied by Genesis. The baseline's cross-generational physiological account of shared flesh is not established by Romans 5 and is excluded. The OCA Q&A's depiction of Catholic inherited guilt is not imported into the comparison; direct Catechism 404–405 is used instead.

## Genesis 3:8–13

- Plan section: included, because (c). Hiding/exposure/confession/restored approach adds the Bible-wide movement into Hebrews 4 and 1 John 1; not presented as a direct NT citation of these particular verses.
- Position source: none. No developed site study establishes an interpretation of the question scene beyond the related word-handling passage. Exact approved open-position wording.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's denial of walking and proposed translation as God entering like wind is not established by BDB 1980/7307; excluded. The proposed reason God made noise (so sinners could hide and avoid automatic destruction), and precise claims about what Adam internally felt or refused, are not supplied by the checked text and are excluded. Its identification of naked with nagad is not supported by the checked naked entry; not used. Questions as an invitation to acknowledge wrongdoing are attributed to Wesley, rather than declared a mechanically necessary explanation of God's knowledge or a site conclusion.

## Genesis 3:14–15

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Revelation's ancient-serpent/woman/offspring conflict and Romans 16's crushing echo support the parallel; Hebrews 2 states Christ's victory through death. Neither is misrepresented as a verbatim quotation of Genesis 3:15.
- Position source: none. The site's Angel of the Lord study was searched in the broader Genesis research context but supplies no developed reading settling this passage's offspring/Marian questions. Exact open-position wording.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's assertion that every curse always means an irreversible sentence to hell is not supported by BDB 779 and its applications to ground/creatures; excluded. Original snake anatomy/legs and chronological claims were not verified in independent historical/scientific sources and are not used. Virgin birth is not a definition of offspring in BDB 2233; no lexical proof is claimed. Calling Christ's death a small enemy victory is not the explanation given in Hebrews 2; excluded. Traditions' Christ and Mary readings are fairly attributed, not adjudicated by the assistant.

## Genesis 3:16–20

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). 1 Corinthians 15 explicitly discusses Adam/dust and the life given in Christ. Romans 8's creation/future freedom and Revelation 22's removed curse add the broader theme.
- Position source: none. No supplied owner position or developed site study settles the desire/relationship/creation-decay questions. Exact open-position wording.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's two-occurrence count for desire is contradicted by BDB's three settings; the verified count/range is used instead. Its conclusion that all these consequences are grace and the woman goes largely unpunished is not presented as the text's own conclusion; narrative consequences remain real. Childbirth as a specific memorial of God's own labor at the cross is not established by the quoted John 16 illustration and is excluded. The claim that the fall introduced the second law of thermodynamics or that shared dirt physically transmitted sin to the earth is not established by Genesis/Romans 8 or an independent scientific source; excluded. Adam's naming Eve as proof of the exact moment of his conversion is an inference, not stated; excluded. Competing meanings of desire are not settled by the dictionary alone. Wesley's historical comments are attributed and not universalized into a current Methodist decree.

## Genesis 3:21–24

- Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). Guarded tree/river/life/curse imagery is compared with Revelation 22's open access and God/Lamb-centered life, without claiming a direct Genesis quotation or establishing a timetable.
- Position source: none. Exact open-position wording; existing study backlinks remain empty rather than inventing one.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the baseline's definite claim of a first atoning animal sacrifice and a particular atonement doctrine from the skins is not stated in Genesis; Wesley independently proposes a related sacrifice parallel, but the public text labels it a suggestion. Exact conversion/forgiveness chronology, the guard's 1,630-year duration, and identifying the flaming sword with the Shekinah are not established by the checked Scriptures/lexicons and are excluded. The plural in verse 22 is not used as a stand-alone proof of all details of the Trinity. No historical claim about the garden surviving until the flood is asserted.

## Editorial review

Read for an interested family member without theological training: explain covenant, sacrament, inherited condition, and parallels in ordinary language; no unexplained scholarly category labels. Necessary Hebrew terms include script, transliteration, familiar pronunciation, plain meaning, and an actual BDB link. No teacher/ministry names appear in the public prose. No historical/cultural reconstruction was needed. The two developed positions stay confined to existing site studies; four other groups use the exact no-position text.

Quotation corrections: Genesis 2:4–25 and 3:1–7 had pre-existing BSB wording differences. Rebuilt affected verse groups from the directly fetched BibleHub BSB verses, preserving coherent paragraph starts. The other four quotation blocks were already exact after normalization. All six are rechecked directly before publication. Supporting Scripture is paraphrased, not silently quoted from the baseline's translation.
