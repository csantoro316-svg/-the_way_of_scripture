## Genesis 19:1–11

Context identifies the two angels, welcome, coercive demand, Lot's wrong offer, threat, and rescue/blindness. Sexual meaning follows actual BDB and the narrative threats, not an invented cultural claim.

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Jude 7 explicitly uses Sodom's sexual immorality as a judgment example. Ezekiel 16:49–50 adds pride, complacency, and neglect of the poor to the wider biblical account, without erasing the sexual violence narrated here.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No independent historical proof obtained for the PDF's precise gate architecture, Lot's judicial appointment, how/why he received office, exact motives for sitting there, or the claim he spent twenty-five years in Sodom. Genesis gives location and the crowd's accusation but not that reconstruction, omitted. Bread kneading is not proof of leavened bread or a particular number of preparation hours; no comparative custom claim included. No unsupported reconstruction of bargains Lot made to avoid assault or of his daughters' precise age. His appeal as brothers is not made proof he abandoned every spiritual identity. The narrative shows his harmful offer; it does not reveal every inward calculation about reputation. The fact that people oppose his rebuke is not a universal rule that unbelieving friends can never show loyalty. Blindness is not given a definite neurological explanation or said to occur only through angels on the basis of the source's unverified claim. The page does not use the assault scene alone to settle every modern ethical question outside its stated action.

## Genesis 19:12–23

Context retains warning, sons-in-law's response as BSB renders it, hesitation, compassion, command, Zoar request/grant, and sunrise. It distinguishes rescue and required movement without a hidden theory of soul rewards.

Plan section: included, because (a). 2 Peter 2:6–9 explicitly explains rescue of righteous Lot amid judgment; Luke 17:28–33 expressly uses his departure for readiness at the Son of Man's revelation.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The source's two angels/four hands as a fixed number chosen solely for exactly four people is not stated by Genesis, omitted. No exact body count for all of Lot's relatives inferred from the invitation to gather sons/sons-in-law. Why he hesitated is not exclusively assigned to wealth, family, or materialism; the text gives hesitation and mercy without all inward motives. The invitation to escape for life is not lexically converted into a specific scheme of preserving eternal rewards. No exact east/west mountain escape map, destination thought, or claim Zoar was always excluded from intended judgment; Genesis explicitly records the request and agreement not to destroy it. The source's rigid Gentile-church/pretribulation-rescue system is not proved by Lot's story or 1 Thessalonians 4; Luke/Peter connections are used in their stated scope. The prayers' relation to God's sovereignty is not settled by an invented complete causal account.

## Genesis 19:24–29

Context holds together destruction, wife's look, Abraham's limited sight, and God's explicit remembering/rescue. It does not invent Abraham's knowledge after the event.

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Jesus explicitly recalls Sodom and Lot's wife in Luke 17:28–33, with readiness and warning against turning back for possessions. Mercy within judgment remains visible through the Genesis ending.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF says brimstone is unknown on account of the proposed gopher relationship. BDB 1614 identifies brimstone/sulfur; public text follows verified BSB sulfur, with no chemical mechanism invented. Specific excavation claims about five cities, a million tomb skeletons, present-day continuity of Zoar, and particular sulfur formations were not independently verified; omitted. No fixed modern archaeological site or geological reconstruction. The passage says the plain is destroyed, so the public explanation does not repeat the source's conflicting assertion that only two cities and no wider valley were affected. Lot's wife is not assigned a definite birthplace, whole prior religious biography, or medical mechanism for becoming salt. Salt symbolism from Matthew 5 is not treated as an explicit explanation of why she became a salt pillar. The Son/Father interpretation of LORD from LORD is a documented historic option (seen in earlier research), but no new full identity decision is originated here. Whether Abraham learned of rescue immediately/later/never remains unstated, rather than used to construct a guaranteed answer-to-every-prayer rule.

## Genesis 19:30–38

Context follows stated fear, cave, daughters' concern/plan, intoxication and unawareness, pregnancies, named peoples. Lack of awareness is preserved rather than turning the account into a consensual agreement of all three. Common reading briefly includes wrongdoing and neighboring peoples' origins.

Plan section: included, because (c). Deuteronomy 2:9, 19 gives divine protection to Lot's descendants' lands; Ruth 1/4 and Matthew 1 connect a Moabite woman with David and Jesus. Matthew does not quote Genesis 19, so this is the Bible-wide grace/ancestry connection, not falsely classified as an explicit NT exposition of the cave episode.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Why Zoar made Lot afraid, how its people regarded him, and whether they expelled him are not given; compare Genesis 19 and Wesley's alternative suggestions, none independently proves the source's reconstruction. Omitted. The daughters' exact belief about every surviving man cannot be fully recovered; their stated concern is described without insisting they thought the whole world had ended. Their actions are not made a genetic or permanent moral description of every Moabite or Ammonite. No claim that every resulting child bears God's anger solely for the parent's wrongdoing. The Noah/Lot drunkenness resemblance was considered as a supported comparison; the public plan chose the less repetitive, scripturally clear later treatment of the descendants and Ruth's inclusion. No unsupported legal assignment of complete guilt/innocence beyond the account's stated unawareness.

## Genesis 20:1–18

Context follows repeated deception, divine warning/restraint, Abimelech's rebuke, half-sister explanation, return, vindication, prophet/prayer, and healing. Prophet note uses actual BDB, not an etymological theory. Existing Egypt study is related reading, not a direct owner position on this episode.

Plan section: included, because (c). God's protection of the promised mother connects with Isaac's next-chapter birth and Hebrews 11:11–12's faithfulness theme. James 5:16–18 supplies the wider prayer/confession/healing theme; it does not explicitly identify this Gerar scene, and is not presented as doing so.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The reason Abraham moved, Sarah's precise emotions, the king's motives, Abimelech as invariably a title rather than personal name, and exact local topography were not independently confirmed; omitted. The warning that he is as good as dead is not expanded into a second hidden meaning of reproductive death in his body. Genesis states closure of wombs and later healing; no physiological mechanism or individual diagnosis asserted. Abraham's dishonesty is not used to decide every conceivable case of deception beyond the passage. God's specific reasons for each method of warning, public rebuke, or prayer are not claimed as exhaustive. No conclusion that Sarah's cooperation in concealment is a universal model for obeying a husband's wrongdoing. No claim that a possible paternity dispute could actually defeat God's whole plan. God's faithfulness is not made approval of the deception, and gifts are not proof it was right.

## Genesis 21:1–8

Context follows the threefold emphasis on God's word/time, birth at Abraham's age one hundred, name, eighth-day sign, shared joy, weaning feast. It distinguishes the appointed promise to this family from an assumed promise to every family.

Plan section: included, because (a). Hebrews 11:11–12 expressly recalls Sarah's faith/conception; Galatians 4:22–28 explicitly uses the promised child when explaining believers as children of promise.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Exact weaning age and banquet customs were not independently checked; no age specified publicly. The source's assertion Isaac was the first infant ever circumcised on day eight is not established by the narrative and omitted. The birth at a promised time is not a general guarantee that every faithful infertile couple will conceive. No special medical explanation of conception, precise pregnancy date, or arbitrary symbolic rule for numbers. Laughter is observed across the actual Genesis narrative; no more is derived from the name's syllables than the wordplay supports.

## Genesis 21:9–21

Context preserves BSB mocking, inheritance dispute, Abraham's concern, God's distinction/promises, departure, fear and grief, divine hearing/water, and the boy's future. Care for Hagar remains part of the story even though the boy's voice is specifically mentioned.

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Galatians 4:21–31 explicitly quotes and explains the separation with the two women/sons and freedom, continuing in 5:1. The public page distinguishes Paul's stated use from invented symbolism for bread, shoulders, or water. The earlier Hagar promise and new rescue give a clear continuing-care theme.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF's all-Arab ancestry/general ethnic conflict claims are not independently established and omitted. It invents a complete symbolic legal code in the rations/shoulder gesture/disowning as death; Genesis and Galatians do not establish those details, omitted. No claim Hagar's life mattered less to God because He heard the boy, nor a claim she lacked a promise (Genesis 16 explicitly addresses her). The well's prior invisibility/creation and God's reason for waiting are not specified. No exact heat diagnosis, unconsciousness, distance in yards, or depletion time given. The age can be related to earlier ages, but no precise weaning-age calculation or toddler reconstruction is imposed. BDB 6711 gives several contextual senses; no specific sexual assault or other act beyond BSB mocking inferred from the root's use elsewhere. Galatians' persecution language is acknowledged in common reading without reconstructing all behavior. The page does not repeat a full Torah-abrogation or ethnicity-rejection system from the baseline as a site conclusion. No command that every father in a difficult household must copy this expulsion.

## Genesis 21:22–34

Context follows request for truth/kindness, complaint, denial of prior knowledge, covenant, seven lambs, well witness, oath, tree, and worship. Eternal title's word is checked under BDB's explicit divine-existence category.

Plan section: included, because (c). Hebrews 11:8–10 supplies the wider life-of-faith/stranger/waiting theme; Isaiah 40:28–31 connects the enduring Creator with strength for those who wait. The well agreement is not claimed to be the final fulfillment of the land promise or a direct NT prediction.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No independent confirmation obtained for the PDF's claim standing armies did not exist, Phicol was a deliberate show of force, precise military threat, or why the servants took the well. None is asserted. A universal treaty ritual, supposed unreported payment steps, full land-rights law, and exact length of permission to sojourn are not invented from the gift. Beersheba has oath/seven wordplay, but no numerical doctrine about seven or firm linguistic derivation required publicly; BSB's stated explanation is retained. Tamarisk planting is not made proof of a special future millennium. God Eternal is not defined as existing for just one finite age. No inference that Abimelech's recognition certifies every action of Abraham as good.
