## Genesis 14:1–9

Context follows the names, years of subjection/revolt, sequence of conquest, and coming danger to Lot. No speculative dates, king identifications, or modern military map.

Plan section: omitted, because none apply. These verses set up the war and capture. The later rescue and Melchizedek scenes have their own supported connections; no need to duplicate them before they occur.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF claims the campaign cleared lands specifically for later Moabites, Ammonites, Edomites, and Ishmaelites. Genesis names conquered peoples; it does not state that precise purpose or identify this campaign as the means of the later settlements. No independent proof obtained; omit the causal claim rather than force a plan section. The assertion that these are the first non-Hebrew personal names and therefore prove the pre-Babel language was Hebrew is not established by this passage, omitted. Specific modern route, hidden military approach, king identifications, and original motives beyond stated rebellion were not independently researched, omitted.

## Genesis 14:10–16

Context gives capture, news, 318 household men, allies, night pursuit, and rescue of the wider group. Does not invent army size or motives for the escapee.

Plan section: included, because (b)/(c). The PDF's loving pursuit/rescue parallel is retained in ordinary language and checked against the Father's concern in Matthew 18:12–14 and rescue in Colossians 1:12–14. Clearly a comparison, not an NT identification of Abram as God or guarantee of every captive's release.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The passage does not give the opposing army's troop count, odds, exact mileage, or the stage at which Lot was recovered. “Only logical reason” for the pursuit distance is not a revealed explanation; omitted. Scripture does not say the escapee came because of a particular reputation, that Lot publicized the relationship for status, or that the Hebrew designation was a newly invented local nickname; no independent corroboration, omitted. Lot-as-Gentile-church through Abram's alleged disobedience is not directly established by Romans 11 or Genesis; not included. No claim that all unbelievers feel no guilt, nor a general psychological rule for all believers. God's permanent indwelling and irrevocable rescue are not deduced as a full salvation system from this military comparison.

## Genesis 14:17–24

Context contrasts two kings and Abram's responses while respecting allies' shares. The BSB does not require asserting that the king of Sodom died and was replaced. Common section represents the figure-of-Christ reading, documented identity proposals, immediate refreshment, and the Catholic Eucharistic connection without deciding that latter question in the common section.

Plan section: included, because (a). Hebrews 7 explicitly explains Melchizedek, Abraham's tenth, blessing, Levi, oath, and Christ's permanent priesthood. It is a direct NT connection rather than a newly invented symbolic scheme.

Position source: Who Shepherds the Church?, What Changed at the Cross. Retain the original link. Position states the existing study's direct-access/church-leader conclusion; it does not invent a separate Melchizedek identity decision or a new rule for tithing.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF's confident identification as a Jebusite and its reconstruction of a dead prior king/new king are not established by the text; omitted. No independent source checked for precise valley location or travel mileage. The blank genealogy is not used as biological proof that Melchizedek literally had no parents, nor does the page assert he was definitely Shem or definitely Christ Himself. Genesis' gift of a tenth is not converted into a universal church tax. The bread/wine connection is identified as Catholic teaching with its own source, not represented as the only reading or collapsed into the site's sourced priesthood position.

## Genesis 15:1–7

Context explains childlessness, heir, stars, trust, and God's reminder of the land. Lexical note follows BDB's actual construction. Common section distinguishes acceptance and renewal fairly using the traditions' own confessions/catechism, with Methodist agreement on merit/faith and fruit of works rather than an invented works-salvation contrast.

Plan section: included, because (a). Romans 4 quotes Genesis 15:6, explains the pre-circumcision timing, and explicitly applies the words to believers in the God who raised Jesus. James 2 also quotes the verse and links faith with later action; this use is acknowledged rather than hidden.

Position source: none. The related Gospel study is not a direct developed exposition of Genesis 15:6, and the assistant does not settle the listed doctrinal differences on the owner's behalf.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The PDF's claim that counting stars and crediting righteousness are a single Hebrew wordplay is not verified: BDB 2803 identifies the latter verb; do not conflate English wording. The supposed legal rule that the oldest household-born servant necessarily inherits was not independently confirmed; Genesis states Abram's current expectation, not that universal rule. Specific claims that the vision's sole fear was returning armies, a specific first conversion date, or that a Hebrew grammatical form necessarily means a past completed conversion were not independently established. Hebrews 11 already shows earlier faith, but does not require a precise conversion chronology here. No unverified lesson that Adam's sole inward motive was disbelief or that every generation's saving-faith content can be exhaustively reconstructed. Existing study citations are not copied as verified BSB text.

## Genesis 15:8–21

Context follows the stated animals, wait, deep sleep, darkness, oppression, judgment, return, Amorite wrongdoing, and covenant. Jeremiah 34 is Scripture independent of the PDF explaining another divided-animal covenant scene; no extra-biblical claim that every culture always used one ritual. Abram is not described as passing through the pieces.

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Acts 7:6–7 explicitly recalls this prophecy; Exodus 12 records judgment/departure with goods. Hebrews 6:13–20 concerns God's later oath to Abraham, clearly identified as later rather than falsely treated as a direct quotation of this covenant ceremony. Its hope/anchor theme supplies the wider encouragement through delay.

Position source: none.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Exact 215/400/430-year reconstructions and a fixed beginning at Isaac's weaning involve questions not resolved by this passage; omitted pending a dedicated chronology review. The source's animals=five=grace, vultures=Egypt, delay=church age, and darkness=future tribulation system lacks explicit support in the cited texts; not included. Genesis does not report a shared cooked covenant meal, exact spacing of carcasses, blood depth, stained robes, or reason birds were uncut. No independent historical confirmation obtained, omitted. The assertion that deep sleep leaves the person consciously informed of every event is not a lexicon conclusion, omitted. God withholding Egypt's name to avoid thwarting His plan is speculative. The river of Egypt is not conclusively mapped here, nor are the listed boundaries used to decide modern territorial claims. Future-land interpretations remain attributed in the chapter-13 page; no invented owner position on final timetable.
