## 1 Corinthians 11:17–22

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): James 2 places treatment of rich and poor within faith in Christ and the promised kingdom; 2 Corinthians 8:9 explicitly roots generosity in Christ’s grace.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s account of all ancient religious meals as lavish, attendees primarily seeking food, mandatory food contributions and a precise rich/poor banquet system was not independently verified and is omitted. Paul records selfish eating and humiliation; no floor plan, staggered arrival schedule, seating scheme or universal personal motive is invented. Approved in verse 19 is not uncritically explained as wealthy self-approval; the surrounding rebuke and checked primary notes distinguish revealed character from permission to create divisions. The passage does not forbid all shared meals or imply the poor are spiritually inferior. The baseline’s opening recap about women outside male spiritual cover being vulnerable to sinful angels, including a Genesis 6 reconstruction, is not established by Paul’s brief reference and is not added to the earlier public pages.

## 1 Corinthians 11:23–26

Plan section: included, because (a): Jesus identifies the cup with the new covenant; Hebrews 8 explicitly quotes Jeremiah 31 and names Jesus the covenant’s mediator.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Paul’s received-from-the-Lord language is not expanded into a precise private-revelation mechanism. No absolute dating claim that this letter predates every Gospel is made without independent historical verification. The baseline’s proposed interruptions of exact Passover ritual steps were not independently checked and are omitted. Its assertion that every divine covenant requires blood is too broad and not established here. No fixed modern frequency is prescribed, and no assertion about precisely what ceases at Christ’s return is imported beyond proclaiming His death until He comes. The baseline’s single symbolic explanation is not labeled consensus. Jeremiah’s named Israel and Judah remain visible; no new owner ruling about Israel’s future or replacement is originated. Owner-study historical assertions about the development of sacrifice, priesthood and architecture are not copied as independently verified history.

## 1 Corinthians 11:27–34

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Hebrews 12:5–11 explicitly gives the Father’s purpose in discipline as sharing His holiness and bearing righteousness and peace, consistent with Paul’s stated distinction between discipline and condemnation.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline excludes reflecting on sins in general and insists body means church body alone; neither restriction is imposed as a settled textual or site conclusion. The actual primary readings show sacramental and community emphases. An unworthy manner is not rewritten as a requirement to have earned the gift by sinlessness. Conversely, free grace is not used to excuse deliberate wrongdoing or disregard for others. Paul identifies particular Corinthian sickness and deaths as discipline; this is not grounds for diagnosing every illness or treating the suffering as guilty. John 9:1–3 was checked in prior complete-book work and supplies the corrective example. The passage is not used to advise avoiding medical care, infer exact causes of a modern death, prescribe a penance policy, or originate a new account of final salvation. No imported lexical claim equates every use of judgment with eternal condemnation.
