## Passage decisions

### Genesis 46:1–6 — Jacob's Journey to Egypt

Context: Beersheba sacrifice, vision and personal reassurance, nation/presence/return promises, Joseph closing eyes, wagons and complete migration. Return is explained through later burial and descendants' deliverance, not an invented living return by Jacob.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Acts 7:14–15 explicitly recalls summons/migration. Exodus 3:7–12 supplies the later deliverance/presence assurance, showing promise and presence across both movements.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the exact animal, uncut-rock altar construction, throat-cutting sequence, blood application, and private consultation process are not narrated or independently established; not published. No rule that God is unable to speak to anyone before an animal sacrifice is inferred. The PDF's five = grace code and exact universal number pattern are unverified; omitted. Jacob/Israel names do not function as an invariant obedience code. Exact sixth-appearance numbering and every fear are unnecessary and not checked. The PDF first says Jacob will be entombed in Egypt, conflicting with its own later acknowledgment of Genesis 50:13's burial in Canaan; public explanation follows the checked Scripture. Personal resurrection into one precise future land timetable is not imposed as the only meaning of the return promise. No promise every believer can obtain an equally specific personal vision on demand is drawn from this scene.

### Genesis 46:7–27 — Those Who Went to Egypt

Context: grouped household, women remembered, deceased sons distinguished, Egyptian-born sons, sixty-six/seventy, Greek/Acts seventy-five. The list is not treated as a complete wagon passenger manifest including all spouses.

- Plan section: included, because (a). Acts 7:14–17 explicitly situates the migration and subsequent growth within the promise to Abraham. Exodus 1:1–7 continues the names/count/growth sequence.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: fixed seventy = sovereign works code across every occurrence is not established; omitted. USCCB's possible nations/Israel representation is a study-note proposal, not an explicit NT explanation, and not needed for the plan section. The PDF's claim Shaulites disappear under a Canaan curse is contradicted by Numbers 26:13 explicitly naming the Shaulite clan; not repeated. Benjamin's exact age and multiple wives as a necessary explanation of the genealogy are not given. Neither forced identical passenger counts across all textual forms nor an exhaustive solution to every name/count discrepancy is claimed. The ancient Greek list's additional descendants and total are actually visible in the primary text. Exact thirty-nine-year family chronology and speculative population multiplication calculations are omitted. Divine punishment for ethnicity is not established by a mother's identification as Canaanite. The list includes Shelah, so the PDF's stray omission when summarizing Judah's surviving sons is not repeated.

### Genesis 46:28–34 — Jacob Arrives in Egypt

Context: Judah sent for directions, emotional reunion, honest occupation answer, Egyptian detestation of shepherds, distinct settlement and next audience.

- Plan section: included, because (c). Exodus 1:1–7 follows the preserved family's growth; John 17:14–19 supplies the larger theme of life among others while remaining faithful to God. Ethnic isolation is not equated with holiness.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: exact unverified Midrash/rabbinic reconstruction of two Messiahs, conflation with John the Baptist's prophet question, and a full second-coming conquest code are not established from cited primary Jewish documents; omitted. The claim Pharaoh must be made to personally loathe the family as Joseph's exact strategy goes beyond the passage. Precise Memphis capital/route and all Egyptian cultural causes for shepherd prejudice are not independently verified; only the text's stated attitude is used. The source's blanket claims that all Christians will be hated by most people, particular modern scientific positions prove holiness, or outward appearance can never matter are outside the supported comparison. Scripture's call to faithfulness does not require racial purity, hostility, or manufactured social isolation. The family is not said to conquer Egypt at entry.

### Genesis 47:1–12 — Jacob Settles in Goshen

Context: five brothers, occupation, pasture need, royal welcome and employment, Jacob's age and hard travels, two blessings, settlement/provision. The BSB's 130 years is retained.

- Plan section: included, because (c). Genesis 12:2–3 supplies the blessing-through-Abraham theme; Hebrews 11:9–16 explicitly includes Jacob among heirs living as strangers with hope beyond present circumstances.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF mistakenly says Abraham was 125 at Isaac's birth; checked Genesis 21 states 100, and the mistake is not repeated. Pharaoh's surprise, age question as a secret honor challenge, every ancient audience's expectations, and Jacob's exact intention to claim superior rank are not narrated. Hebrews 7:7 concerns a particular blessing setting; no universal social-status proof is imposed on every blessing or greeting here. The word for hard/evil does not by itself prove Jacob is confessing moral inferiority to Abraham/Isaac; public explanation retains the BSB's hard-life sense. Theories of Rameses naming direction and exact pharaoh/dynasty identity are not independently verified; no chronological harmonization is asserted. An alleged permanent-departure sense proving Jacob never again met Pharaoh is not lexically established; omitted. Goshen/Rameses identification is supported by the own-publisher note without speculative capital geography.

### Genesis 47:13–26 — The Famine Continues

Context: money then livestock then land/servitude, royal concentration of property, priest exception, seed/fifth/four-fifths, expressed gratitude, ongoing law. The public explanation preserves both survival and loss of freedom.

- Plan section: omitted, because none apply without forcing an unsupported policy-to-salvation correspondence beyond the narrative explanation.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's confident universal ancient economic norm, exact efficiency/fairness of each price, automatic economic dignity in enslavement, worldwide livestock ownership, and Egypt being proven richest country on earth are not independently verified. Claims about free food necessarily exhausting reserves and no possible charitable alternative are economic conjecture, not stated divine instruction. The BSB says reduced to servitude; it is not softened into ordinary voluntary employment or merely relocated cities because another textual form/commentator uses that wording. The starving people's proposal and gratitude do not establish unrestricted free choice or a modern policy mandate. A precise seven-year retrospective chronology is not directly supplied by this section. Joseph buying people and land is not made a required detailed model for Jesus acquiring believers or a price to purchase grace. The text's particular rescue can be explained without adopting all the source's economic judgments or inventing a site position against/for the whole policy.

### Genesis 47:27–31 — The Israelites Prosper in Goshen

Context: growth/property, seventeen further years/147 total, deathbed burial oath, under-thigh gesture recalling Genesis 24, worship, promise beyond current comfort. Bed/staff explanation supported by a directly read publisher note and ancient Greek text.

- Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Hebrews 11:21 directly remembers Jacob's dying faith, blessing Joseph's sons, and worship; public explanation keeps the blessing connection rather than claiming Hebrews explicitly narrates the burial oath. Hebrews 11:13–16 supplies the larger faith-and-future-home theme.
- Position source: none.
- FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: the PDF's exact genital oath reconstruction, automatic life/posterity execution penalty, and universal ritual symbolism were not independently established here; the BSB gesture itself and earlier narrative parallel suffice. The request does not prove a precise intermediate-state geography or that resurrection requires burial in the correct place. Exact post-famine twelve years as a son-number code is unsupported. Staff/bed difference can arise through vowel reading of the same consonants; the source's certain copyist error is not established. The BSB is not silently replaced. Hebrews 11:21 explicitly mentions the sons' blessing, so it is not cited as a direct statement that Jacob's burial request itself is its sole subject. No particular future kingdom chronology is imposed on Hebrews' hope of a better country. A partial Greek vocabulary aside about downpayment is not needed here and is not published without checking that separate lexicon.
