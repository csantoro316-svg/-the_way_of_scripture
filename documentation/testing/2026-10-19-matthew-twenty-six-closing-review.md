Romans12:17–21 and Luke22:51 retain the baseline's useful connection between nonretaliation, concrete kindness, and overcoming evil with good.

Position source: none. Exact required open wording.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: No independent evidence establishes Judas' testimony to Pilate, a mandatory Roman arrest warrant, exactly200or600soldiers, or the precise size of this contingent; omit. No unsupported rule that a high priest could never leave his house before Passover or that his slave was the one necessarily unarmed person. His recorded act and Jesus' rebuke stand without them. Jesus' healing is reported by Luke, but erasing legal evidence is not given as its motive. The public victory connection instead rests directly on Hebrews2. No forced assertion that every contemporary temptation is a specific Satanic assignment, or that trial always reveals a known private divine script.

## Matthew 26:57–68

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). Jesus' answer invokes Psalm110andDaniel7; Isaiah50is a clearly labeled servant-suffering parallel. Hebrews12:1–3 explicitly joins shame/endurance to enthronement and encouragement. Retains the lesson's valuable contrast of humiliation with vindication without unrecorded physical detail.

Position source: none. Exact required open wording.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline gives a broad Roman/Judean administrative history and says Mishnah governed every rule before Rome conquered in66BC. These historical claims lack independent checking and are excluded; a later written Mishnah cannot simply be projected back as a complete trial manual. Universal rules about nighttime arrest, self-incrimination, three days between verdict and sentence, necessity of one dissenting vote, permissible priestly robe-tearing, and temple territorial exemptions are not taught. USCCB57–68and65explicitly acknowledge uncertainty about later legal standards. Matthew's own false-testimony account establishes wrongdoing without an invented legal audit. Annas' removal reason, Peter's family access through John, precise guard identities and coaching of two witnesses are not supplied as facts. John18identifies another disciple but does not name him there. No claim Orthodox Jews generally spit at Jesus' name or all Jewish people share the conduct of these named opponents. No blanket inherited guilt; public prose keeps agency with participants named in the scene. No use of mental-illness language as a diagnosis of unbelief. Isaiah50does not authorize inserting handfuls of plucked beard into Matthew's account or inferring every physical movement. John18:23records Jesus challenging an unjust blow, so His nonretaliation is not turned into a prohibition on truthfully questioning mistreatment.

Later courage rests on the actual Acts accounts and the Holy Spirit's work, not a speculative psychological biography.
