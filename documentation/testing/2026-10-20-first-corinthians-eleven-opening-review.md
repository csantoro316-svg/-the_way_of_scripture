## 1 Corinthians 11:1–2

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Jesus explicitly gives His own service as the disciples’ pattern in John 13:12–17, illuminating Paul’s imitation of Christ and the community formed by that example.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Tradition is not lexically equivalent to error or optional cultural ritual. No full doctrine of Scripture and tradition is deduced from this one use. The baseline’s statement that Corinth was the first Greek church is contradicted by Acts’ earlier Philippian and Thessalonian ministry and is omitted. No invented source of every Corinthian question is asserted. The rules of baptismal mode or precise supper ingredients are not decided from the baseline’s illustrations.

## 1 Corinthians 11:3–6

Plan section: omitted, because none apply in a way that adds beyond the interpretation already given.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Aner and gyne can mean man/woman or husband/wife; neither form proves a household-only reading by itself. The baseline’s statement that Jewish men never covered their heads in worship is not independently verified and is omitted. Claims that every uncovered woman intentionally rejected her husband, every prostitute wore short hair, or the Law commanded shaving adulteresses were not established and are omitted. Paul treats the conduct as dishonoring, but the exact motives and comprehensive local dress history are not supplied. Bullock’s alternative emphasizes interdependence; her historical claims about legal rights to veils, assault and class are not adopted without independent checks. Thayer does not prove her entire metaphorical reading, and no dictionary-only ruling is made. The Father/Son reference is not converted into a new site account of eternal divine hierarchy; Calvin and Wesley’s mediator explanations are attributed to them. The baseline’s advice about when spouses may resist authority is not turned into personal legal or safety guidance.

## 1 Corinthians 11:7–12

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c): Genesis explicitly gives both sexes God’s image and calling; Galatians 3 explicitly makes both heirs of the promise through Christ.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Paul does not deny women bear God’s image; Genesis 1:27 explicitly affirms it. Adam’s creation is not described as from nothing when Genesis 2:7 states dust. No inference that a woman requires a male intermediary to approach God is made. BSB verse 10’s sign-of-authority main text and alternative authority-over wording are distinguished. Angels as witnesses are Calvin/Wesley’s interpretations, not an explanation supplied by Paul in this verse. The USCCB note’s possible Aramaic mistranslation and suggested harmful cosmic powers were not independently verified and are omitted as factual claims. No Genesis 6 seduction reconstruction, clergy-as-angels certainty or universal demonic threat is imported. Bullock’s authority interpretation is attributed and kept separate from accepting all of her historical or lexical premises. Shared inheritance is not used to originate an owner position about every ministry role.

## 1 Corinthians 11:13–16

Plan section: omitted, because none apply in a way that adds beyond the interpretation already given.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s claim of one hair custom continuously from Adam and Eve is unsupported and omitted. No required measurement or universal hairstyle is invented. The text’s hair/covering relationship has competing readings; it is not settled by assertion. The Head Covering Movement’s continuing-practice case and the Catholic declaration’s nonbinding-disciplinary conclusion are sourced and distinguished. Catholic veil practice is not inaccurately equated with its ordination teaching. No claim all Protestants, Mennonites, Catholics or women share one practice. An inaccessible official Mennonite page and unread denominational documents are not cited as support. No contempt for those who cover or do not cover is introduced.
