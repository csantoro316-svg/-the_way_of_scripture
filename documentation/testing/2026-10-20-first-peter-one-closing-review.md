## 1 Peter 1:13–16

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Leviticus 19 makes holiness concrete in honest dealings, fair judgment and care for the poor; Matthew 5:43–48 explicitly calls Jesus' followers to reflect their Father's character in love for enemies.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baseline garment description and exact linen/ankle-length universality need independent historical evidence; the public page explains readiness without asserting that costume. A useful scriptural readiness parallel is available in Exodus 12, but no need to turn every clothing detail into typology. Sober-minded is not confined to literal alcohol abstinence, nor does its range justify an invented alcohol policy. Fasting physiology/flesh has a separate literal will is not established science or a lexical claim here; omitted. No claim every charitable act of every unbeliever is necessarily a secret selfish act without a direct text establishing that application. Comparative counts that only Revelation quotes more OT than 1 Peter are unverified and not published. Holiness does not mean the person has already achieved faultless conduct; no owner verdict on perfection or loss of salvation invented. Christian hope is not a formula for material or institutional success.

## 1 Peter 1:17–21

Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c). Exodus 12's unblemished Passover lamb supplies background; John 1:29 explicitly calls Jesus the Lamb of God who takes away sin. The connection is explained without making every Exodus detail a prediction.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Baseline krino means only assessing to find good is contradicted by the actual Thayer range; public text retains God's serious impartial judgment. The passage does not provide a precise judgment-at-instant-of-death/Bema chronology or say it concerns only optional reward loss. Calling God Father is not permission for careless conduct; fear is also not a proof He abandons those needing His care. Foreknown does not lexically mean existed beforehand. No denial of Christ's preexistence is introduced; the point is that this word alone is not that proof. Empty inherited way of life is not expanded into every ancestral practice, all Judaism, or all tradition being worthless. The precious price is not a financial transaction with a specified recipient inferred from ransom's possible range. No modern medical or political illustration substituted for Christ's saving work. Owner studies are not made to choose a new judgment/atonement position here.

## 1 Peter 1:22–25

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Isaiah 40 places the enduring word within God's comfort and shepherding promise; John 13:34–35 explicitly connects mutual love with Jesus' disciples and His own love as the pattern.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Calvin's ongoing-purification interpretation and his editor's preference for the past tense are distinguished; actual BSB past wording retained. Through the Spirit appears in older source translations but is not added to the BSB quotation. A grammar difference does not establish a new owner conclusion about salvation's order. Calvin's editor distinguishes imperishable seed from word as means; public wording says new life comes through the word without declaring one exclusive metaphor analysis. Living word is not automatically a claim every mention refers directly to the personal Word rather than the preached gospel; verse 25's identification remains central. Isaiah is not stripped of its own comfort/restoration setting. No claim ordinary mortality proves creation has no value, nor that love removes any need for truthful correction. The new-life/genuine-love connection is not reduced to generic church busyness.
