## James 2:1–7

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). 1 Corinthians 1:26–31 places the calling of the lowly within Christ as God's wisdom, righteousness and redemption, excluding human boasting.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Assembly is a real synagogue-related term but does not by itself prove Christian worship in a particular Jewish-owned synagogue building. Thayer explicitly recognizes a Christian gathering. No literal two visitors' identity, membership or precise usher motives are invented. The text condemns favoritism, not all morally relevant distinctions or every security/accommodation decision. Not every rich person is a blasphemer, nor every poor person faithful. Modern political/entertainment celebrities are not used as a universal class judgment. Baseline Archimedes anecdote not independently verified; the physical displacement direction as printed also raises an accuracy problem. It is unnecessary and omitted. No claim a trial is literally nondestructive in its earthly effects. The warning about worldly rank does not produce a new site position on church seating policies.

## James 2:8–13

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Leviticus 19:15–18 places impartial judgment beside love of neighbor; Matthew 18's forgiven servant connects received mercy with the treatment of others.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Royal law is not the Golden Rule's exact wording; the public page distinguishes the actual neighbor-love command. The baseline's wholly separate new law of precisely two rules replacing every Mosaic moral command is not lexical consensus; actual Leviticus is quoted by James, and traditions distinguish continuity differently. The public passage follows the command without imposing that scheme. Guilty of all does not assert all sins have equal consequences. The baseline restricts judgment exclusively to reward loss; Calvin/Wesley/USCCB speak more broadly and severely. No heavenly rank system or merciless merely means God prefers other believers is treated as established. Hebrews 10:26–31 is not silently equated with 1 Corinthians 3's work-testing fire; those differing readings have their own evidence. Mercy is not made a payment that earns God's pardon.

## James 2:14–17

Plan section: included, because (a)/(c). Ephesians 2:8–10 and Galatians 5:6,13–15 join saving grace with God's purpose for loving service.
Position source: none.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline's exclusive Bema/reward-loss meaning is not made consensus or a site verdict. An independently checked Free Grace source supplies a related but specifically present-deliverance interpretation, attributed accurately rather than made identical to every baseline detail. Reading save as rescue is lexically possible; lexicon alone does not prove that reading here. Thayer explicitly includes James 2:14 in its salvation category, so no false lexical consensus is asserted. Baseline says James already introduced judgment fire, but the actual James 2 text contains no fire image; omitted. BSB such faith retained despite Pesce's criticism of that translation choice. Reformed fruit/evidence is not described as earning initial salvation; Catholic grace-enabled love is not described as unaided purchase. The needy person's bodily situation remains central, rather than reducing the example to an abstract doctrinal puzzle. Claims all other readings compromise the gospel are not repeated.
