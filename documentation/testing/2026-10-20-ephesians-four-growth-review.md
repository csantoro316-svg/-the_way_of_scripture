## Ephesians 4:11–16

Plan section: included, because (c). Romans 8:29 places likeness to Christ within God’s purpose; 1 Corinthians 12:21–27 adds concrete care for weaker members and shared suffering and joy to the body picture.
Position source: What Is the True Church?, lines 79–89, especially 87.
FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: The baseline’s claim that no believer may ever desire or request a gift was not adopted; previously checked 1 Corinthians 12:31, 14:1 and 14:13 require greater care. No unverified claim that every apostle personally saw the risen Christ in exactly the same way, performed every miracle, or wrote Scripture is added. Exact dates for John’s death or the recognition of the complete canon were not independently researched here and remain off the page. Revelation 22’s warning alone does not establish that chronology.

The baseline’s allowance for prophecy as insight into Scripture is not identical to the Assemblies of God account of prophetic messages. Neither is presented as universal consensus. No claim that every pastor and teacher is necessarily the same role is deduced from Greek grammar without independent grammatical investigation. The BSB list is preserved; no ranking of divine affection or a numerical quota of gifts is inferred.

The baseline’s description of matching Christ’s knowledge is not used to promise omniscience or equality with Him. The public page follows the passage’s growth toward Christ. The examples involving famous teachers, particular modern books and church programs, universal lack of meaning in unbelievers’ lives, and exact psychology of every immature believer are not independently verified and are omitted. An admonition is not treated as proof that every recipient committed every listed problem.

The stone-fitting and poetic-meter explanations in PDF pages 180–182 were considered but not used as verified word meanings or historical facts. No independent evidence was checked for that specific ancient mortar practice. The page follows the body image that the actual passage supplies. Nor is numerical church growth promised as an automatic result of maturity.
