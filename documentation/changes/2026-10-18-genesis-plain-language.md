# Plain Language for Passage Explanations

Date: 2026-10-18
Status: Published

## Result

The owner found the first Genesis batch well researched but too academic to read comfortably. The two opening Genesis pages now explain their context, common readings, and connections to God's plan in ordinary language. Unnecessary labels such as analogical, framework, confessional, and chronology were removed from public prose. The underlying differences between readings remain, with the same primary-source reference URLs and no new site position.

The Passage Editorial Standard now requires an explanation that an interested reader with no theological training can follow. Explain the idea before naming a category, leave unnecessary academic labels in the source, explain essential unfamiliar words naturally, and keep repeated research procedure out of public teaching. This is a manual clarity review, not a word blacklist or a requirement to shorten writing for mobile. The master language standard points to the same expectation.

Scripture quotations, exact no-position wording, existing deeper-study relationships, and the optional plan-section basis are preserved.
