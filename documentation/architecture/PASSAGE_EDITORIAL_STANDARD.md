# Passage Editorial Standard

Effective: 2026-10-18. Authority: the owner's passage-writing prompt supplied in this conversation. This standard governs new editorial work and supersedes looser legacy interpretation/position guidance. Existing placeholders are not reviewed content.

## Passage groups and sections

Write about a coherent group of verses in sequence, grouped by context. Keep these sections in order after the full BSB text:

1. Context (required): immediate passage flow, surrounding verses, and the book's movement.
2. Common Interpretations (required): descriptive and fair, without deciding which reading is right.
3. Where does this fit in God's plan? (optional under the tests below).
4. Our Position (required under the authority rule below).
5. Deeper Study (required): retain links to related developed studies on this site when applicable. Do not invent a study or link an unrelated one merely to fill the section.

Public headings should be plain, conversational questions. Write explanatory prose. Use whatever length the content needs; do not compress it for mobile or turn every verse into a separate exposition.

## Clear writing is part of accuracy

The owner clarified this requirement after reading the first Genesis batch: scholarly care in the research must be delivered in simple, direct language. A reader should not need academic or church training to understand the explanation. This applies to every section, not just the introduction.

- Explain what a reading actually means before naming a school of interpretation. When the name adds nothing, leave it in the linked source. For example, explain that some readers see God's week as the pattern for ours without assuming His days are the same length; the reader does not need the label `analogical` to understand that view.
- Use familiar verbs and concrete statements. Replace phrases such as `continuing moral appointment` with what they mean: a belief that God still requires one day in seven for worship and rest.
- Keep necessary distinctions. Make the language clearer without turning different positions into the same view, overstating agreement, weakening an explanation, or choosing a site position.
- Explain necessary unfamiliar terms where they appear. Hebrew and Greek words remain useful where they help answer a real question and are immediately explained. Do not add a technical term merely to display scholarship, or retain an unnecessary term by attaching a dictionary definition.
- Let the prose teach the passage. Keep research procedure, source classifications, and repeated cautions in the internal evidence unless a reader needs them to understand a claim. When a distinction matters (such as a church's teaching versus one person's explanation), say it briefly in ordinary language.
- Keep primary-source links beside the claims they support. Introduce a church document naturally as a statement of belief or guide to its teaching when that helps the reader. Avoid opening with a catalog of documents and academic categories.
- Before publication, read the explanation as if speaking to an interested family member with no theological training. Can that person explain the different views in their own words without looking up the vocabulary? Revise sentences that sound like a report about research rather than a helpful explanation of Scripture.

This is a manual editorial check, not a word blacklist or an instruction to shorten content for a phone. Preserve the reasoning, supporting Scripture, fair comparisons, and source links. The goal is a full explanation that is easy to follow.

## Sources in order of authority

1. Scripture. Quote BSB only and verify every quotation directly against `biblehub.com/bsb` before use, including the top passage text. Read the passage itself, not BibleHub's commentary or generated summaries.
2. Original-language meaning from standard Hebrew/Greek lexicons. Check the actual lexicon entry; a word's range of meaning does not by itself decide its meaning in this passage. Follow the master doctrine's script, transliteration, pronunciation, and explanation rules when introducing a significant original-language term.
3. Independent, checkable historical and cultural sources.
4. Each tradition's own primary documents.
5. The Verse By Verse Ministry International teaching by Stephen Armstrong as a baseline, including the owner-supplied PDF under `content/assets/reference/stephen_armstrong/`. This is one teacher's reading, not consensus.

Never name the baseline teacher or ministry on public passage pages. Never quote or closely follow the baseline's wording or structure. Independently verify every original-word claim in a lexicon and every historical/cultural claim in an independent source. Synthesize in original prose alongside the other research. Leave unverifiable claims off the page and record what was tried under `FLAGGED FOR REVIEW` in the internal evidence file. The local PDF is a research asset, not a website download.

## Read the baseline for connections and lessons

The owner emphasized on 2026-10-18 that the PDFs should be read for more than passage summaries. For each passage, actively examine their explanations of significant Hebrew/Greek words, examples, practical lessons, and connections across Scripture. In particular, look for supported connections to God’s purposes, including parallels between the patriarchs’ actions and God’s dealings with His people. Do not skip this material merely because the section is optional.

Treat each proposed connection as a research question. Check the passage and the supporting Scriptures directly; independently check original-language claims against lexicons. Retain a useful connection when it meets test (a), (b), or (c) below, explain it in everyday language, and clearly distinguish an explicit New Testament explanation from a suggested parallel. A resemblance alone does not establish a prediction, a hidden meaning, or a detail-by-detail correspondence. Never force every person or action into a picture of Christ, and never use a parallel to establish a new site position.

Include practical lessons where the passage supports them, woven into the explanation rather than copied from a teacher’s outline. The PDF is a strong research starting point alongside Scripture and independently checked sources, not the whole source of truth.

## Books without an owner-supplied PDF

Owner authorization (2026-10-21): complete the remaining 29 books in the next session using directly researched sources; no additional PDFs will be supplied. A baseline teaching PDF is not a prerequisite for this work. The source hierarchy and every verification, clarity, position-authority and publication requirement above still apply.

Start with the passage and its surrounding book, then investigate significant original-language questions, historical context, relevant primary tradition documents, and supported connections across Scripture. Consult directly checked commentaries where useful, distinguish each commentator’s reading from official church teaching, and compare material disagreements fairly. Do not invent a replacement baseline, claim to have read unavailable teaching, or treat one commentary as consensus. Record sources actually read and their relevant boundaries in the evidence. Unsupported claims stay unpublished and are flagged with the attempted verification. Do not manufacture a missing-PDF flag when the passage can be adequately researched without one.

## Common Interpretations

If the major traditions read the passage alike, say so plainly in one or two sentences without a denomination breakdown. If they differ, represent each reading in terms its adherents would recognize and cite its own documents directly: Catholic Catechism/councils, Reformed Westminster and Baptist confessions, and Wesley/Methodist Articles for Arminian/Wesleyan claims. Include Orthodox and Lutheran readings only where they add a distinct reading. Include another tradition where its distinct position is materially relevant to the passage.

Check the primary document itself, not secondhand summaries or memory. Distinguish a binding confession, a church committee report, a historical teacher's commentary, and individual beliefs. Do not imply that every member or every denomination in a broad family holds the same view. If the document does not establish the claim, flag it and omit it. Show a question or note about differences only where actual differences are documented. Use neutral words such as position, reading, case, or reasoning; do not use combative language or editorialize about which view is correct.

## Optional plan section

Include it only where at least one test applies:

- (a) The New Testament itself connects the passage to Christ, the church, or God's larger purposes. Cite it.
- (b) A well-established parallel or typology can be supported from Scripture.
- (c) A clear Bible-wide theme adds something beyond the interpretation already given.

Otherwise omit the section entirely, including its heading; never write a placeholder or stretch a connection. Separate what the text/NT states from a proposed parallel and label parallels as such. Explain God's plan as Scripture states it (for example Ephesians 1:9-10), without imposing one tradition's system. Do not repeat material already covered in Common Interpretations.

## Our Position

The assistant must not originate a site position. Use only an existing site study that addresses the passage (name/link it), an owner-provided position, or an owner/AI conclusion already developed in conversation. A new proposed conclusion is flagged for the owner, not promoted to a position by the assistant.

When no authorized source exists, use exactly:

> We don't have a position on this passage yet. When our study reaches a conclusion the text supports, it will appear here.

Do not name the owner in public prose. A site study cannot be used to claim a broader conclusion than it actually develops.

## Review output and evidence

For each passage group, keep its drafted sections and these three internal review lines:

- `Plan section: included, because (a)/(b)/(c)` or `Plan section: omitted, because none apply.` Include the supporting references/reason.
- `Position source: [study name] / owner-provided / none.` An established conversation source can be identified in the evidence detail.
- `FLAGGED FOR REVIEW`: every unverified item, why it was excluded, and what verification was attempted. Say none when no unresolved claims remain.

The public page carries the teaching and source citations; the review flags and production metadata stay in repository evidence. Preserve the original source prompt's distinctions and do not hide unresolved claims in public prose.

## Implementation and publishing

Reviewed JSON records use `editorial_review.standard: passage-v1`, the review date, a BibleHub BSB `quote_source`, the internal `evidence_file`, `plan_basis` (a/b/c or an empty list), and `position_source`. Optional `god_plan_lines` precede `current_position_lines`; the existing position field name is retained for compatibility. Blank lines in the arrays separate paragraphs. Inline HTTPS Markdown links cite primary sources. Existing `deeper_study` relationships remain applicable.

Run `python3 tools/verify_bsb_passages.py <slug> ...` to compare the full source quotations directly with BibleHub. Compare exact numbered or unnumbered verse text; preserve all numbers that belong to Scripture itself (ages, counts, and so on). Never strip all numerals as a shortcut for removing verse labels. A changed markup structure or wording mismatch must fail verification rather than silently pass.

Use `python3 tools/generate_study_pages.py --passages-only <slug> ...` for selected reviewed records. It prepares the entire batch before writing, regenerates only those passage pages and index entries, and refreshes the shared reader. It preserves other published prose and does not run the legacy translation audit or derive new commentary from studies. Validate the source records, source citations, optional-section behavior, exact position wording, links, reader/standalone consistency, and preservation of unrelated files before deployment.


### Adding missing groups through focused publication

The same `--passages-only <slug> ...` route can admit new reviewed source records. The source filename must match its canonical book/citation, and it must have passage-v1 review metadata and a BSB-only quotation. Run the normal independent source checks and repository validator before publication; admission does not replace editorial review or quotation verification.

New groups are inserted in Bible order. Existing neighbors whose previous/next destination changes receive navigation-only updates; their prose is preserved. All output is prepared before writes, so missing or malformed affected neighbors fail without publishing a partial prepared batch. Existing supplemental book entries are preserved. For insertion batches, include the exact affected neighbor pages in the allowed file-change set, verify their prose is byte-preserved after removing navigation, and check both reader and standalone routes. Do not run the full generator to fill missing coverage.

Existing destinations may gain or change a descriptive title during review. The focused publisher refreshes adjacent standalone navigation when its rendered destination or description changes, even without adding a passage group. Audit the full rendered old/new navigation when identifying affected neighbors; comparing only adjacent IDs misses description changes. Neighbor prose and nonselected index records remain preserved.
