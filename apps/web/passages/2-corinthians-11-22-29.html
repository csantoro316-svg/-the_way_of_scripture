Passage

2 Corinthians 11:22-29

What has serving Christ actually cost Paul?

Berean Standard Bible

22 Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they descendants of Abraham? So am I. 23 Are they servants of Christ? (I am speaking as if I were out of my mind.) I am so much more: in harder labor, in more imprisonments, in worse beatings, in frequent danger of death. 24 Five times I received from the Jews the forty lashes minus one. 25 Three times I was beaten with rods, once I was stoned, three times I was shipwrecked. I spent a night and a day in the open sea.

26 In my frequent journeys, I have been in danger from rivers and from bandits, in danger from my countrymen and from the Gentiles, in danger in the city and in the country, in danger on the sea and among false brothers, 27 in labor and toil and often without sleep, in hunger and thirst and often without food, in cold and exposure.

28 Apart from these external trials, I face daily the pressure of my concern for all the churches. 29 Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is led into sin, and I do not burn with grief?

What is the context?

Paul can answer the claims about Jewish identity: he too is a Hebrew, an Israelite and a descendant of Abraham. When he turns to service of Christ, his account centers on what he has endured in that service.

He names hard labor, imprisonment, beatings, danger of death, shipwrecks and a night and a day in the open sea. His journeys have exposed him to danger in many places and from many people. Hunger, thirst, sleeplessness, cold and exposure also belong to the account.

The list gives us more of his experience than Acts records in detail. It should be read as his own account here, with each reported event left within what he actually tells us.

Alongside those external troubles is his daily concern for the churches. Another person’s weakness or being led into sin affects him deeply. His care for people is part of the weight of ministry, as real to him as the dangers he has faced.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly see this as Paul’s account of costly service, ending with his concern for people. Wesley’s notes on verses 23–29 discuss both hardship and shared concern; the USCCB notes on verses 23–29 likewise connect the sufferings with service of the crucified Christ.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Philippians 3:7–11 describes Paul valuing the knowledge of Christ above what he once counted as gain. His hope includes knowing Christ, the power of His resurrection and participation in His sufferings, looking toward resurrection from the dead.

That stated hope helps place these hardships within the larger purpose of belonging to Christ. The list records the cost of carrying His message, while Paul’s goal reaches through suffering toward life with Him.
