Passage

Romans 6:23

Why is eternal life called a gift rather than wages?

Berean Standard Bible

23 For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

What is the context?

This verse closes Paul’s contrast between service to sin and belonging to God. The former ends in death; the latter bears the fruit of holiness. The final words identify eternal life as God’s gift in Christ Jesus our Lord.

The contrast between wages and gift matters. Sin pays what it produces, while life comes from God’s generosity in Christ. Read Romans 6:15–23 to keep the gift together with the new life Paul describes.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this verse as contrasting sin’s deadly outcome with the life God gives through Christ. The gift is received from Him; the surrounding call to holiness describes the life of those who belong to Him, rather than a price that purchases it.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 2:8–10 likewise connects salvation as a gift with the good works for which believers are created in Christ. God’s giving and the changed life that follows are held together without turning eternal life into earned wages.

What is our position?

The Gospel According to Jesus uses Romans 6:23 to distinguish the wages sin earns from the gift God gives. Our position is that eternal life comes through Jesus Christ as a gift received in faith, rather than a wage for religious effort or moral achievement. The life He gives also calls the believer into repentance and following Him.
