Passage

1 Corinthians 6:1-8

Why are their lawsuits already a defeat?

Berean Standard Bible

1 If any of you has a grievance against another, how dare he go to law before the unrighteous instead of before the saints! 2 Do you not know that the saints will judge the world? And if you are to judge the world, are you not competent to judge trivial cases? 3 Do you not know that we will judge angels? How much more the things of this life!

4 So if you need to settle everyday matters, do you appoint as judges those of no standing in the church? 5 I say this to your shame. Is there really no one among you wise enough to arbitrate between his brothers? 6 Instead, one brother goes to law against another, and this in front of unbelievers!

7 The very fact that you have lawsuits among you means that you are thoroughly defeated already. Why not rather be wronged? Why not rather be cheated? 8 Instead, you yourselves cheat and do wrong, even against your own brothers!

What is the context?

Paul is addressing disputes in which believers take one another before outside judges. He asks why a community destined to share in God’s judgment cannot find someone wise enough to settle its everyday disagreements.

The failure runs deeper than the choice of a courtroom. The Corinthians are cheating and wronging their own brothers. Paul challenges their determination to win against one another, even asking why they would not rather accept a loss than continue the conflict. The person causing the loss is also directly rebuked, not excused.

The immediate subject is everyday disputes between members of the church. Paul’s own appeal to legal protection in Acts 25:10–12 shows that he recognizes a place for civil authority. Here his concern is the church’s failure to act with wisdom and justice toward its own members.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a rebuke of destructive litigation and wrongdoing among believers, with a call to wise settlement and willingness to bear loss. Calvin’s comment on verse 1 distinguishes avoidable lawsuits used to harass a brother from appearing and defending a case when summoned; his reading does not make every involvement in a civil court the same situation Paul condemns.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Daniel 7:18–27 describes God overturning oppressive rule and giving the kingdom to His holy people. Jesus also promises His disciples a share in judgment in Matthew 19:28. These passages give a wider setting for Paul’s reminder about the saints’ future role.

Paul uses that future responsibility to challenge present conduct. People called to share in God’s just rule should learn to deal justly with one another now. He does not explain here every detail of judging the world or angels, and the practical appeal does not depend on supplying a complete timetable.
