Passage

Jonah 2:8-10

What does Jonah acknowledge about salvation?

Berean Standard Bible

8 Those who cling to worthless idols forsake His loving devotion. 9 But I, with the voice of thanksgiving, will sacrifice to You. I will fulfill what I have vowed. Salvation is from the LORD!” 10 And the LORD commanded the fish, and it vomited Jonah onto dry land.

What is the context?

Jonah contrasts clinging to worthless idols with receiving the Lord’s loving devotion. He promises thanksgiving, sacrifice and fulfillment of his vows, ending with the acknowledgment that salvation comes from the Lord.

The language recalls the sailors, who also turned to the Lord, offered sacrifice and made vows after being rescued. The prophet and the sailors alike depend on mercy they cannot produce for themselves.

God then commands the fish, and it releases Jonah onto dry land. His confession of who saves is followed by the deliverance that makes a renewed response to God possible.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Cambridge understand the Lord as the source of the mercy that idols cannot provide. Henry sees the release as a demonstration of God’s command over His creatures and His mercy toward the praying prophet.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

A parallel in praise appears in Revelation 7:9–12. A multitude from every nation, tribe, people and language gives the credit for salvation to God and the Lamb.

Jonah’s thanksgiving concerns his own rescue. Revelation opens the view to a people gathered from across the world through the Lamb. The shared confession gives salvation’s Author the glory, while the wider scene brings into view the mercy for many peoples that Jonah himself struggles to welcome.
