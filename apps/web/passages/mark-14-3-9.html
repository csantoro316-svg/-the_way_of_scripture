Passage

Mark 14:3-9

Why does Jesus call this costly gift beautiful?

Berean Standard Bible

3 While Jesus was in Bethany reclining at the table in the home of Simon the Leper, a woman came with an alabaster jar of expensive perfume, made of pure nard. She broke open the jar and poured it on Jesus’ head. 4 Some of those present, however, expressed their indignation to one another: “Why this waste of perfume? 5 It could have been sold for over three hundred denarii and the money given to the poor.” And they scolded her.

6 But Jesus said, “Leave her alone; why are you bothering her? She has done a beautiful deed to Me. 7 The poor you will always have with you, and you can help them whenever you want. But you will not always have Me. 8 She has done what she could to anoint My body in advance of My burial. 9 And truly I tell you, wherever the gospel is preached in all the world, what she has done will also be told in memory of her.”

What is the context?

Between the leaders’ plot and Judas’s agreement with them, Mark tells of a meal in Bethany at Simon the Leper’s home. A woman pours expensive perfume on Jesus’ head. Some present call it wasteful because it could have been sold for a large amount and the proceeds given to the poor. Jesus defends her and relates her act to His coming burial.

Mark leaves the woman unnamed. John describes an anointing at Bethany involving Mary, Lazarus’s sister, and identifies Judas as the person objecting to the cost. Reading the accounts together helps explain why the woman here is commonly identified as Mary, while keeping each account’s own details visible. Luke’s earlier account concerns a sinful woman at a Pharisee’s home; Luke does not name her Mary or call her a prostitute. (John 12:1–8; Luke 7:36–50)

Jesus’ words about the poor do not cancel care for them: He says His followers can help them whenever they want. Deuteronomy similarly joins the continued presence of poverty with a command to give generously. What is unique here is the approaching end of His life among them and this woman’s opportunity to honor Him before His burial. Mark does not tell us the full extent of her understanding. (Deuteronomy 15:7–11)

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readers broadly understand this as costly devotion that Jesus accepts in view of His death and burial. The Catholic study notes emphasize His defense of the woman and His promise that her deed will be remembered wherever the gospel is proclaimed. (USCCB, note on Mark 14:3)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus explicitly places the woman’s act alongside the future worldwide proclamation of the gospel. Paul’s summary of that gospel centers on Christ dying for our sins, being buried, and being raised. The burial she anticipates belongs to the saving events that will be announced far beyond this room. (1 Corinthians 15:1–8)

The contrast with the surrounding plot is striking: as some prepare to hand Him over, she gives something precious to honor Him. That is a contrast Mark’s arrangement invites us to notice, rather than a claim that her gift purchases forgiveness or explains every detail of the cross.
