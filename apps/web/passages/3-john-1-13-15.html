Passage

3 John 1:13-15

How does a difficult letter end with personal care?

Berean Standard Bible

13 I have many things to write to you, but I would prefer not to do so with pen and ink. 14 Instead, I hope to see you soon and speak with you face to face. Peace to you. The friends here send you greetings. Greet each of our friends there by name.

What is the context?

The elder has more to write, but hopes to visit soon and speak face to face. The letter closes with peace and greetings between friends. Asking that each friend be greeted by name gives the ending a personal warmth.

The BSB places the peace and final greetings in verse 14. Editions that number them as verse 15 cover the same closing material; the Scripture panel here follows the BSB numbering.

The writer does not explain why he prefers to finish the conversation in person. His closing expresses that preference without telling readers that letters are inadequate or that a specific threat prevented him from saying more.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley emphasizes greeting the friends individually, as though they had each been named. The USCCB note recognizes friends as members of the connected Christian community. The emphasis is personal fellowship and peace at the end of a letter that has addressed a painful conflict.
