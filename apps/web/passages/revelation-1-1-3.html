Passage

Revelation 1:1-3

Why are we given the revelation of Jesus Christ?

Berean Standard Bible

1 This is the revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave Him to show His servants what must soon come to pass. He made it known by sending His angel to His servant John, 2 who testifies to everything he saw. This is the word of God and the testimony of Jesus Christ.

3 Blessed is the one who reads aloud the words of this prophecy, and blessed are those who hear and obey what is written in it, because the time is near.

What is the context?

The book begins by naming its source and purpose. God gives the revelation to Jesus Christ to show His servants what must soon take place. Jesus makes it known through His angel to John, who bears witness to what he saw.

The word translated revelation means an uncovering or disclosure. The opening presents something God makes known. It calls the readers and hearers to respond: the blessing is attached to hearing and obeying the prophecy, because the time is near.

The reference to one person reading aloud and others hearing fits a message received by a community. The book is meant to shape their faithfulness, rather than merely supply material for speculation.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Readings differ over how the promised events relate to history. Wesley understands their fulfillment as beginning soon after the book was given and extending toward the end of the world. The USCCB introduction emphasizes the message to first-century Christians facing pressure while also affirming its continuing hope for the church.

John Walvoord’s preface to his commentary presents a reading that places the great final conflict and victory in the future, while finding encouragement for earlier generations awaiting it. These examples show why readers can agree on Christ’s final victory while differing over which events had begun in John’s time and which remain ahead. They are particular explanations, rather than one uniform position shared by every Protestant or Catholic.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 1:9–10 describes God making known His purpose to bring all things in heaven and on earth together in Christ. That gives a wider setting for this opening. The future God reveals belongs to His purpose centered on Christ, and is given to help His people live faithfully within that purpose.
