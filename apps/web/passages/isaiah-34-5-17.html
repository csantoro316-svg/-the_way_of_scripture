Passage

Isaiah 34:5-17

What becomes of a land set against God’s people?

Berean Standard Bible

5 When My sword has drunk its fill in the heavens, then it will come down upon Edom, upon the people I have devoted to destruction. 6 The sword of the LORD is bathed in blood. It drips with fat— with the blood of lambs and goats, with the fat of the kidneys of rams. For the LORD has a sacrifice in Bozrah, a great slaughter in the land of Edom. 7 And the wild oxen will fall with them, the young bulls with the strong ones. Their land will be drenched with blood, and their soil will be soaked with fat. 8 For the LORD has a day of vengeance, a year of recompense for the cause of Zion.

9 Edom’s streams will be turned to tar, and her soil to sulfur; her land will become a blazing pitch. 10 It will not be quenched—day or night. Its smoke will ascend forever. From generation to generation it will lie desolate; no one will ever again pass through it. 11 The desert owl and screech owl will possess it, and the great owl and raven will dwell in it. The LORD will stretch out over Edom a measuring line of chaos and a plumb line of destruction. 12 No nobles will be left to proclaim a king, and all her princes will come to nothing.

13 Her towers will be overgrown with thorns, her fortresses with thistles and briers. She will become a haunt for jackals, an abode for ostriches. 14 The desert creatures will meet with hyenas, and one wild goat will call to another. There the night creature will settle and find her place of repose. 15 There the owl will make her nest; she will lay and hatch her eggs and gather her brood under her shadow. Even there the birds of prey will gather, each with its mate.

16 Search and read the scroll of the LORD: Not one of these will go missing, not one will lack her mate, because He has ordered it by His mouth, and He will gather them by His Spirit. 17 He has allotted their portion; His hand has distributed it by measure. They will possess it forever; they will dwell in it from generation to generation.

What is the context?

The judgment now names Edom and Bozrah. A sword and a great sacrifice picture the slaughter, while the reason is stated as the Lord’s day of vengeance for Zion’s cause. The scene presents judgment as His answer to wrong, rather than an uncontrolled contest between equal powers.

Edom’s streams, soil and land are pictured as tar, sulfur and burning pitch. Nobles and princes come to nothing; thorns take over towers and fortresses; and creatures occupy the places where people had lived.

The measuring line and plumb line in verse 11 reverse the usual expectation of building. The Hebrew words tohu, pronounced TOH-hoo, and bohu, pronounced BOH-hoo, are also paired in Genesis 1:2. Brown–Driver–Briggs and its entry for bohu connect their use here with chaos and emptiness. The picture is of an ordered human place being reduced to waste.

The closing command is to search and read the Lord’s scroll. The creatures will have their allotted place because His mouth has commanded and His Spirit gathers them. The ruins are not beyond His rule.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley read Edom as both the named enemy and a picture of opposition to God’s people more broadly. They see its desolation as a warning that attempts to ruin God’s people end in the ruin of the oppressor.

For the night creature in verse 14, Gill discusses a night bird as well as the Jewish association of Lilith with a female demon. The Brown–Driver–Briggs entry and the USCCB note identify the name with that night-demon imagery. The BSB retains the broader wording night creature.

That difference concerns one part of the ruined-land picture. The passage’s main movement is clear: places of human power become uninhabited waste, and the Lord’s announced judgment stands.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The language of smoke rising forever appears again in Revelation 14:9–13, in the warning against worshiping the beast, and in Revelation 19:1–4, in praise after the judgment of the great prostitute. The latter passage explicitly joins God’s true and just judgment with His answer to the blood of His servants.

The New Testament thus carries this prophetic language into its larger account of God ending corrupt power and answering violence. Revelation also calls the saints to perseverance and faith in Jesus.

This connection follows the shared judgment imagery; it does not by itself identify Edom’s territory with every place in Revelation. The promise is that oppression does not have the final word before God.
