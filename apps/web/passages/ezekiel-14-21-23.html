Passage

Ezekiel 14:21-23

What will the survivors reveal about Jerusalem’s judgment?

Berean Standard Bible

21 For this is what the Lord GOD says: ‘How much worse will it be when I send against Jerusalem My four dire judgments—sword, famine, wild beasts, and plague—in order to cut off from it both man and beast? 22 Yet, behold, some survivors will be left in it—sons and daughters who will be brought out. They will come out to you, and when you see their conduct and actions, you will be comforted regarding the disaster I have brought upon Jerusalem—all that I have brought upon it. 23 They will bring you consolation when you see their conduct and actions, and you will know that it was not without cause that I have done all these things within it,’ declares the Lord GOD.”

What is the context?

The Lord applies the four judgments directly to Jerusalem: sword, famine, wild beasts and plague. The examples just given show how little basis there is for expecting the city to be spared merely because righteous people belong to it.

Yet some sons and daughters will survive and be brought out. They will join the exiles, who will see their conduct and actions.

The Lord says that this encounter will bring consolation concerning the disaster. What the exiles see will help them understand that His actions against Jerusalem were not without cause. The survivors are evidence to be considered, rather than proof that the city was innocent.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley understands the survivors’ conduct and punishment as showing why God’s judgment was just. He expects their account to help the exiles recognize both God’s justice and His purpose in what has happened.

Henry also emphasizes mercy in the preservation of some people amid devastation. The USCCB notes focus on the survivors’ continuing corruption as evidence of Jerusalem’s condition.

These emphases address different sides of the ending: some are preserved, and their conduct helps explain the judgment. Survival alone is not presented as a declaration that every survivor was righteous.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Revelation 6:7–8 brings together the same four means of death: sword, famine, plague and wild beasts. The Lamb opens the seal before the pale horse appears and the authority described is given.

This close biblical parallel carries the language of judgment beyond Jerusalem’s immediate crisis into Revelation’s vision of God bringing evil to account. It does not make every detail of the two visions identical or establish a timetable from Ezekiel’s list. Both place the threatened devastation under God’s authority rather than outside His rule.
