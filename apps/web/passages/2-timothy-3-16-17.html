Passage

2 Timothy 3:16-17

What is Scripture meant to do in a person’s life?

Berean Standard Bible

16 All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for instruction, for conviction, for correction, and for training in righteousness, 17 so that the man of God may be complete, fully equipped for every good work.

What is the context?

Paul is encouraging Timothy to continue in the sacred writings that point to salvation through faith in Christ. He describes Scripture’s origin in God and the uses that follow: instruction, conviction, correction and training in righteousness.

The purpose is a person equipped for every good work. Teaching is meant to bear fruit in faithful conduct and useful service. These verses complete an appeal to keep living by what Timothy has received, even amid deception and opposition.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Major Christian traditions receive Scripture as inspired by God and authoritative for faith and life. Wesley emphasizes the Spirit’s help both in the writers’ work and in readers being taught through it. The Catholic Church likewise teaches that God inspired real human authors who used their own abilities (Catechism 105–107).

Traditions differ over Scripture’s relationship to the church’s other received teaching. The Reformed Westminster Confession 1.6 holds that everything necessary for God’s glory, salvation, faith and life is stated in Scripture or follows from it. The Catholic Church teaches that Scripture and apostolic Tradition together transmit God’s revelation, distinguishing that Tradition from later changeable customs (Catechism 80–83). Both affirm Scripture’s divine source; they differ over whether Scripture alone supplies the church’s certainty about revealed truth.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus explains in Luke 24:25–27, 44–47 how the Scriptures point to His suffering, resurrection and the proclamation of repentance for forgiveness to the nations. That gives a larger setting for the wisdom leading to salvation through Christ in verse 15.

Romans 15:4–6 says earlier Scripture was written for instruction, endurance, encouragement and hope, within a people learning to glorify God together. God’s purpose in giving Scripture reaches into the shared life and faithful service it forms.

What is our position?

In Is the Bible the Inerrant Word of God?, we understand God-breathed as a claim about Scripture’s origin in God. Our study applies inerrancy to the original writings as God inspired them. It distinguishes those writings from later translations, which are human works to be examined carefully against the Hebrew and Greek. This verse does not declare every later translation free from error. In Should We Seek Signs From God?, we also understand verses 16–17 as teaching that Scripture equips believers for good work. We should let its teaching guide our judgment and conduct, rather than treat everyday events as personal omens that supply a new message.
