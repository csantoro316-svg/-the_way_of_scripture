Passage

Ezekiel 38:17-23

How does the Lord bring the attack to an end?

Berean Standard Bible

17 This is what the Lord GOD says: Are you the one of whom I have spoken in former days through My servants, the prophets of Israel, who in those times prophesied for years that I would bring you against them? 18 Now on that day when Gog comes against the land of Israel, declares the Lord GOD, My wrath will flare up. 19 In My zeal and fiery rage I proclaim that on that day there will be a great earthquake in the land of Israel. 20 The fish of the sea, the birds of the air, the beasts of the field, every creature that crawls upon the ground, and all mankind on the face of the earth will tremble at My presence. The mountains will be thrown down, the cliffs will collapse, and every wall will fall to the ground.

21 And I will summon a sword against Gog on all My mountains, declares the Lord GOD, and every man’s sword will be against his brother. 22 I will execute judgment upon him with plague and bloodshed. I will pour out torrents of rain, hailstones, fire, and sulfur on him and on his troops and on the many nations with him. 23 I will magnify and sanctify Myself, and I will reveal Myself in the sight of many nations. Then they will know that I am the LORD.

What is the context?

The Lord places the attack within what He has spoken through Israel’s prophets. The invasion will not catch Him unprepared or overturn His word.

His response is pictured on a vast scale. A great earthquake shakes the land; creatures and people tremble; mountains, cliffs and walls fall. The invaders turn their swords against one another, and the Lord brings plague, bloodshed, torrential rain, hailstones, fire and sulfur.

The account ends with the purpose of the judgment. The Lord will make His greatness and holiness known before many nations. The decisive action belongs to Him, rather than to a stronger army appearing on Israel’s side.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Cambridge understands the earlier prophetic warnings as describing this kind of attack without previously naming Gog. Henry emphasizes that the destruction makes God’s greatness and holiness known.

Wesley describes the great shaking as disturbance and tumult like an earthquake. The more direct reading takes the earthquake and other judgments as events God brings upon the invaders. Both readings place their defeat under His judgment rather than their opponents’ military skill.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

First Corinthians 15:20–28 places the defeat of God’s enemies within the larger hope secured by Christ’s resurrection. Christ reigns until His enemies are put under His feet, and the last enemy to be destroyed is death.

Ezekiel shows a hostile army brought to nothing. Paul takes the hope further: those who belong to Christ will be raised, and Christ will hand the kingdom to God the Father after every opposing power has been overcome. God’s purpose reaches beyond surviving one invasion to the removal of death itself.
