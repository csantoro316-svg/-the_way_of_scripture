Passage

Daniel 10:15-21

How is Daniel strengthened to hear what comes next?

Berean Standard Bible

15 While he was speaking these words to me, I set my face toward the ground and became speechless. 16 And suddenly one with the likeness of a man touched my lips, and I opened my mouth and said to the one standing before me, “My lord, because of the vision, I am overcome with anguish, and I have no strength. 17 How can I, your servant, speak with you, my lord? Now I have no strength, nor is any breath left in me.” 18 Again the one with the likeness of a man touched me and strengthened me. 19 “Do not be afraid, you who are highly precious,” he said. “Peace be with you! Be strong now; be very strong!” As he spoke with me, I was strengthened and said, “Speak, my lord, for you have strengthened me.”

20 “Do you know why I have come to you?” he said. “I must return at once to fight against the prince of Persia, and when I have gone forth, behold, the prince of Greece will come. 21 But first I will tell you what is inscribed in the Book of Truth. Yet no one has the courage to support me against these, except Michael your prince.

What is the context?

Daniel turns his face toward the ground and cannot speak. One resembling a man touches his lips, and he explains that the vision has left him without strength or breath.

He is touched again and strengthened. The words of reassurance bring peace and courage, and Daniel becomes ready to listen.

The messenger says he must return to the conflict with the prince of Persia, after which the prince of Greece will come. First he will reveal what is written in the Book of Truth. Michael is named as Daniel’s people’s prince and the messenger’s supporter. The coming account of kingdoms is framed by God’s knowledge and care, even amid conflict Daniel cannot fully see.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry emphasizes the assurance of God’s love as a source of strength. Cambridge understands the Book of Truth as the heavenly record of what is to come. The reassurance prepares Daniel to receive the difficult message that follows.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 1:1–5 and 13–14 places angelic service within God’s larger purpose. Angels serve those who will inherit salvation, while the Son is above them and has made purification for sins.

Daniel’s experience shows a servant being helped to receive God’s word. Hebrews carries the theme into the full revelation through the Son: God’s care includes ministering servants, but the center of His saving purpose is Christ Himself. The help given in weakness serves that greater hope.
