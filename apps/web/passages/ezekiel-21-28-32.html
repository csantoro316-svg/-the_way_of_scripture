Passage

Ezekiel 21:28-32

Does Jerusalem’s judgment mean Ammon will escape?

Berean Standard Bible

28 Now prophesy, son of man, and declare that this is what the Lord GOD says concerning the Ammonites and their contempt: ‘A sword! A sword is drawn for slaughter, polished to consume, to flash like lightning— 29 while they offer false visions for you and lying divinations about you— to be placed on the necks of the wicked who are slain, whose day has come, the time of their final punishment. 30 Return the sword to its sheath! In the place where you were created, in the land of your origin, I will judge you. 31 I will pour out My anger upon you; I will breathe the fire of My fury against you; I will hand you over to brutal men, skilled in destruction. 32 You will be fuel for the fire. Your blood will stain your own land. You will not be remembered, for I, the LORD, have spoken.’”

What is the context?

The warning turns toward the Ammonites and their contempt. A sword is drawn for slaughter, while false visions and lying divinations offer a very different expectation.

The Lord declares that judgment will reach them in their own land. They will be handed over to brutal men skilled in destruction, and the fire picture returns.

The sword’s direction toward Jerusalem earlier in the chapter was therefore no promise of permanent safety for Ammon. The chapter closes with the Lord’s own announcement against another people who have trusted false assurances.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry reads the false visions as promises of victory that cannot preserve Ammon. Wesley connects the contempt with Ammon’s response to Israel’s affliction and reads the warning as Ammon falling under judgment as well.

The reversal prevents gloating over a neighbor’s disaster. The fact that another community faces judgment is no proof that one’s own conduct or assurances are sound.
