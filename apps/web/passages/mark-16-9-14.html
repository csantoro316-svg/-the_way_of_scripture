Passage

Mark 16:9-14

Why do the disciples struggle to believe?

Berean Standard Bible

9 Early on the first day of the week, after Jesus had risen, He appeared first to Mary Magdalene, from whom He had driven out seven demons. 10 She went and told those who had been with Him, who were mourning and weeping. 11 And when they heard that Jesus was alive and she had seen Him, they did not believe it.

12 After this, Jesus appeared in a different form to two of them as they walked along in the country. 13 And they went back and reported it to the rest, but they did not believe them either. 14 Later, as they were eating, Jesus appeared to the Eleven and rebuked them for their unbelief and hardness of heart, because they did not believe those who had seen Him after He had risen.

What is the context?

Some early manuscripts end Mark at verse 8; others include these final verses. The BSB prints verses 9–20 and explains the difference in its chapter footnote. The question is whether these verses belonged to Mark’s original ending; the resurrection itself is already announced in verse 6 and is also described in the other Gospels.

This passage moves from appearances of Jesus to reports that the disciples refuse to believe. Mary Magdalene tells the grieving group that He is alive. Two disciples also report an encounter with Him. At last Jesus appears to the Eleven and confronts their refusal to believe those who saw Him.

John gives the fuller account of Mary meeting Jesus, and Luke describes two disciples recognizing Him on the road to Emmaus and at a meal. Mark’s brief account resembles these events, but does not supply every detail or settle the exact order of all the appearances. Luke says the travelers were kept from recognizing Jesus; Mark’s different-form wording does not itself explain how His appearance changed. (John 20:11–18; Luke 24:13–35)

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these appearances as testimony to Jesus’ real resurrection and the disciples’ difficulty accepting it. Jesus’ rebuke addresses their refusal to receive the witnesses’ reports; it does not portray the disciples as people who expected a resurrection and immediately accepted every report. (Wesley on Mark 16:9–14; USCCB notes)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The witnesses’ struggle becomes part of the story through which later readers hear the good news. In John’s account, Thomas moves from demanding to see Jesus to confessing Him as Lord and God. Jesus then speaks of blessing for those who believe without seeing, and John explains that his written account is meant to lead readers to faith and life. Faith after the first generation receives testimony about Jesus rather than requiring everyone to repeat the first witnesses’ experience. (John 20:24–31)

Luke also shows Jesus opening the Scriptures to explain why the Christ had to suffer and enter His glory. Resurrection faith is rooted in what God has done and what Scripture says about it. The witnesses are then sent to proclaim repentance and forgiveness to the nations. (Luke 24:25–27, 44–49)
