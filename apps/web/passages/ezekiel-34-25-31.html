Passage

Ezekiel 34:25-31

What kind of life does the covenant of peace promise?

Berean Standard Bible

25 I will make with them a covenant of peace and rid the land of wild animals, so that they may dwell securely in the wilderness and sleep in the forest. 26 I will make them and the places around My hill a blessing. I will send down showers in season—showers of blessing. 27 The trees of the field will give their fruit, and the land will yield its produce; My flock will be secure in their land. Then they will know that I am the LORD, when I have broken the bars of their yoke and delivered them from the hands that enslaved them.

28 They will no longer be prey for the nations, and the beasts of the earth will not consume them. They will dwell securely, and no one will frighten them. 29 And I will raise up for them a garden of renown, and they will no longer be victims of famine in the land or bear the scorn of the nations. 30 Then they will know that I, the LORD their God, am with them, and that they, the house of Israel, are My people,’ declares the Lord GOD. 31 ‘You are My flock, the sheep of My pasture, My people, and I am your God,’ declares the Lord GOD.”

What is the context?

The Lord promises a covenant of peace. The people will live securely, free from the beasts and powers that have made them prey. Even wilderness and forest become places where they can rest without fear.

The promise includes rain in season, fruitful trees, a productive land and freedom from famine. The yoke is broken, and the people are delivered from those who enslaved them.

The final assurance goes deeper than comfortable circumstances: they will know that the Lord is with them and that the house of Israel is His people. The flock picture ends by making the relationship plain. They are His people, and He is their God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry develops the promise through Christ and the flourishing of His church, including the fruit and nourishment of salvation. Cambridge understands the garden of renown in verse 29 as the land’s restored fertility, rather than the name of a single plant.

Walvoord places Israel’s restored national life under Christ’s future earthly rule. These readings give different accounts of the promise’s full expression. The passage itself joins the Lord’s presence, faithful care, freedom from oppression and secure life.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 13:20–21 brings together the God of peace, the blood of the eternal covenant, the resurrection of Jesus and His identity as the great Shepherd of the sheep.

Hebrews does not explicitly quote Ezekiel here or provide a calendar for this promised restoration. The shared themes add the New Testament’s saving center: God’s covenant care comes through the living Jesus and equips His people to do His will. Belonging to the Shepherd produces a renewed life in His care.
