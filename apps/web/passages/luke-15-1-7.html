Passage

Luke 15:1-7

Why does Jesus describe finding a lost sheep as a reason to celebrate?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Now all the tax collectors and sinners were gathering around to listen to Jesus. 2 So the Pharisees and scribes began to grumble: “This man welcomes sinners and eats with them.” 3 Then Jesus told them this parable:

4 “What man among you, if he has a hundred sheep and loses one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine in the pasture and go after the one that is lost, until he finds it? 5 And when he finds it, he joyfully puts it on his shoulders, 6 comes home, and calls together his friends and neighbors to tell them, ‘Rejoice with me, for I have found my lost sheep!’ 7 I tell you that in the same way there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous ones who do not need to repent.

What is the context?

Tax collectors and sinners gather to listen to Jesus. The Pharisees and scribes grumble that He welcomes sinners and eats with them. That complaint supplies the setting for all three stories in this chapter.

Jesus asks them to imagine losing one sheep from a flock of a hundred. The shepherd goes after the lost one, carries it home on his shoulders and calls others to share his joy.

He applies the picture to heaven’s joy over a sinner who repents. The answer to the complaint is an invitation to see the welcome from God’s side: recovering the lost calls for celebration.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly recognize God’s concern for the lost and His joy over repentance. The Catholic bishops’ notes place this story alongside the coin and the two sons as Jesus’ response to resistance against that welcome. (USCCB note on Luke 15:1–32)

Calvin and Wesley explain the contrast with the ninety-nine through the special joy of a dramatic return from a lost way of life. Calvin explicitly adds that believers still need repentance throughout life. That explanation does not require the ninety-nine to be people who have never sinned. (Calvin on Luke 15:7; Wesley on Luke 15:7)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ezekiel describes God Himself seeking His scattered flock, finding the lost, bringing back strays and caring for the injured. The shepherd’s search belongs within that larger Scriptural picture of God acting to restore His people. (Ezekiel 34:11–16)

In John, Jesus identifies Himself as the good shepherd who lays down His life for the sheep and gathers them into one flock. The wider purpose is more than retrieving something misplaced: God’s saving care comes through Christ’s own self-giving and the people He gathers around Himself. (John 10:11–16)
