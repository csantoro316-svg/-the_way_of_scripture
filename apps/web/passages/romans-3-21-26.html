Passage

Romans 3:21-26

How can God put sinners right while remaining just?

Berean Standard Bible

21 But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been revealed, as attested by the Law and the Prophets. 22 And this righteousness from God comes through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe. There is no distinction, 23 for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, 24 and are justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

25 God presented Him as an atoning sacrifice in His blood through faith, in order to demonstrate His righteousness, because in His forbearance He had passed over the sins committed beforehand. 26 He did this to demonstrate His righteousness at the present time, so as to be just and to justify the one who has faith in Jesus.

What is the context?

But now marks the turn from the exposure of sin to God’s answer. His righteousness has been revealed apart from the law, yet the Law and the Prophets bear witness to it. Paul is not announcing a rescue unrelated to the Scriptures he has been using.

All have sinned and fall short of God’s glory. The answer comes through faith in Jesus Christ: people are justified freely by grace through the redemption in Him. Justified concerns being put right before God; redemption describes a costly release. The emphasis is on what God gives through Christ, rather than a record of achievement people bring.

God presents Christ as an atoning sacrifice in His blood, received through faith. Atonement means dealing with sin so that people can be reconciled to God. Paul connects this with God’s patience toward sins previously committed and with the demonstration of His righteousness now.

The Greek word behind atoning sacrifice is hilastērion (hee-lahs-TAY-ree-on), ἱλαστήριον. Thayer’s entry discusses both an atoning sacrifice and the mercy seat, the cover of the ark of the covenant associated with atonement. These meanings help explain the sacrificial background without requiring a reader to know Greek.

The final statement holds two truths together: God is just, and He puts right the person who has faith in Jesus. Forgiveness is presented through Christ’s death, rather than as indifference toward evil.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand these verses as a central account of salvation through Christ, given by grace and received through faith. Where traditions differ is in how they describe justification and the effect of Christ’s sacrifice.

The Reformed Westminster Confession 11.1–2 and 1689 Baptist Confession 11.1–2 describe God pardoning sins and counting believers righteous because of Christ’s obedience, received through faith alone. Their inward renewal accompanies this acceptance but is distinguished from its basis.

The Catholic Church teaches that justification includes forgiveness and inward renewal through Christ’s grace, conferred in baptism. The Catechism, paragraphs 1987–1993, makes God’s initiative the source of the person’s response. The Methodist Article IX describes being counted righteous through faith because of Christ, rather than one’s own works.

There are also different emphases in explaining the sacrifice. The Catholic USCCB note on verse 25 favors wording that emphasizes the removal of sin. Wesley on verses 25–26 emphasizes God’s judgment against sin being answered through Christ’s death. These are explanations from a commentary and a named teacher; they should not be mistaken for complete statements of every Catholic or Wesleyan’s understanding.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The mercy-seat meaning invites comparison with Exodus 25:17–22, where the cover of the ark is the place above which God promises to meet with Moses. The same word is used for the mercy seat in Hebrews 9:5. This is a supported connection in the sacrificial language, rather than a claim that every interpreter translates Romans 3:25 identically.

Hebrews 9:7–15 makes the larger connection explicit. It moves from the high priest’s entry with sacrificial blood to Christ’s entry by His own blood, securing eternal redemption and cleansing the conscience. Christ’s death also redeems transgressions under the first covenant. That helps show how the mercy announced now belongs with God’s earlier dealings, rather than making the earlier story irrelevant.

What is our position?

The Gospel According to Jesus draws on Romans 3:23 to explain that everyone needs salvation and cannot earn it through moral effort or religious activity. Our position here is that God gives salvation through Jesus’ death for sins and resurrection, received through faith in Him. That study supplies this conclusion; it does not settle every difference about justification or the language of atonement described above.
