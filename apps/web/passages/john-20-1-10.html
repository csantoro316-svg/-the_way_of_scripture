Passage

John 20:1-10

What do the disciples find at the empty tomb?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Early on the first day of the week, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene went to the tomb and saw that the stone had been removed from the entrance. 2 So she came running to Simon Peter and the other disciple, the one whom Jesus loved. “They have taken the Lord out of the tomb,” she said, “and we do not know where they have put Him!”

3 Then Peter and the other disciple set out for the tomb. 4 The two were running together, but the other disciple outran Peter and reached the tomb first. 5 He bent down and looked in at the linen cloths lying there, but he did not go in. 6 Simon Peter arrived just after him. He entered the tomb and saw the linen cloths lying there. 7 The cloth that had been around Jesus’ head was rolled up, lying separate from the linen cloths.

8 Then the other disciple, who had reached the tomb first, also went in. And he saw and believed. 9 For they still did not understand from the Scripture that Jesus had to rise from the dead. 10 Then the disciples returned to their homes.

What is the context?

John has just described Jesus’s burial in a nearby, unused tomb. Early on the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene finds the entrance open. Her first explanation is that someone has moved His body. An empty tomb does not immediately lead her to expect a resurrection.

Peter and the disciple Jesus loved run to investigate. They find the burial cloths, with the head cloth lying separately. John does not explain exactly how the body left the cloths, and the cloths do not supply a detailed account of how resurrection works.

The other disciple enters, sees and believes. Verse 9 adds that they still do not understand from Scripture that Jesus must rise. John allows us to see their understanding develop rather than presenting them as people who had everything worked out in advance.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the empty tomb alongside the appearances that follow as testimony to Jesus’s bodily resurrection. The disciples’ initial confusion is part of the account.

Commentators differ over what the other disciple believes in verse 8. Calvin’s note on verses 8–9 understands a beginning of resurrection faith before a fuller grasp of Scripture. Wesley’s note on verse 8 understands belief in Mary’s report that the body has been taken. These are two readings of the disciples’ developing understanding; neither changes the resurrection announced in the rest of the chapter.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus’s resurrection belongs to the fulfillment of Scripture, even while the disciples are still learning to recognize it. In Acts 2:25–32, Peter explains David’s words about God’s Holy One not seeing decay and applies them to the risen Jesus. The same Peter who now examines an empty tomb will later announce publicly what God has done.
