Passage

Daniel 5:17-24

What had Belshazzar known but refused to learn?

Berean Standard Bible

17 In response, Daniel said to the king, “You may keep your gifts for yourself and give your rewards to someone else. Nevertheless, I will read the inscription for the king and interpret it for him. 18 As for you, O king, the Most High God gave your father Nebuchadnezzar sovereignty and greatness, glory and honor. 19 Because of the greatness that He bestowed on him, the people of every nation and language trembled in fear before him. He killed whom he wished and kept alive whom he wished; he exalted whom he wished and humbled whom he wished. 20 But when his heart became arrogant and his spirit was hardened with pride, he was deposed from his royal throne, and his glory was taken from him. 21 He was driven away from mankind, and his mind was like that of a beast. He lived with the wild donkeys and ate grass like an ox, and his body was drenched with the dew of heaven until he acknowledged that the Most High God rules over the kingdom of mankind, setting over it whom He wishes.

22 But you his son, O Belshazzar, have not humbled your heart, even though you knew all this. 23 Instead, you have exalted yourself against the Lord of heaven. The vessels from His house were brought to you, and as you drank wine from them with your nobles, wives, and concubines, you praised your gods of silver and gold, bronze and iron, wood and stone, which cannot see or hear or understand. But you have failed to glorify the God who holds in His hand your very breath and all your ways. 24 Therefore He sent the hand that wrote the inscription.

What is the context?

Daniel declines the offered gifts but agrees to explain the writing. Before doing so, he reminds the king that Nebuchadnezzar’s power came from the Most High God. When that ruler became proud, he lost his throne and ordinary human life until he acknowledged God’s rule.

Belshazzar knew this history. Daniel’s charge is that he nevertheless refused to humble himself. Instead, he used God’s vessels to honor powerless gods and failed to glorify the One who holds his breath and all his ways in His hand.

That is why the hand appeared. The message on the wall belongs to a moral reckoning: a ruler who had received a warning through another king’s experience chose to defy the God who sustained his own life.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Cambridge emphasize Belshazzar’s responsibility for what he already knew. Nebuchadnezzar’s humiliation should have warned him; his own conduct repeats the pride while adding the public dishonoring of God’s vessels.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Acts 17:24–31, Paul tells another audience surrounded by idols that God gives everyone life and breath. Gold, silver and stone cannot represent the One who made and sustains people.

Paul then carries the message forward: God calls everyone to repent and has appointed a day of just judgment through the Man He raised from the dead. Daniel’s confrontation with one king belongs to the wider truth that every person depends on God and is accountable to Him. The gospel identifies the risen Jesus at the center of that coming judgment.
