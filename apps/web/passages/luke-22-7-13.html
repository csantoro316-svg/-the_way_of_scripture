Passage

Luke 22:7-13

How is the Passover meal prepared?

Berean Standard Bible

7 Then came the day of Unleavened Bread on which the Passover lamb was to be sacrificed. 8 Jesus sent Peter and John, saying, “Go and make preparations for us to eat the Passover.” 9 “Where do You want us to prepare it?” they asked.

10 He answered, “When you enter the city, a man carrying a jug of water will meet you. Follow him to the house he enters, 11 and say to the owner of that house, ‘The Teacher asks: Where is the guest room, where I may eat the Passover with My disciples?’ 12 And he will show you a large upper room, already furnished. Make preparations there.” 13 So they went and found it just as Jesus had told them. And they prepared the Passover.

What is the context?

The day arrives for the Passover sacrifice, and Jesus sends Peter and John to prepare the meal. They ask where He wants it made ready.

He gives them a sequence to follow: meet a man carrying a water jug, follow him to a house and ask its owner for the guest room where the Teacher may eat with His disciples. The owner will show them a large, furnished upper room.

They go, find things as Jesus has described and prepare the Passover. Luke’s focus falls on Jesus’ directions and their fulfillment as the meal is made ready.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly see Jesus deliberately preparing for the meal and the suffering that follows. Calvin understands the detailed directions as a sign of His knowledge and willing action, and emphasizes the disciples’ readiness to follow His instruction. (Calvin on Luke 22:7–13 and Matthew 26:18–19)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Exodus makes the Passover a memorial of God’s deliverance, with the blood as a sign of protection and the feast carried forward through the generations. Preparing this meal places Jesus and His disciples within that remembrance of rescue from Egypt. (Exodus 12:12–17)

Paul explicitly identifies Christ as the sacrificed Passover lamb and uses the removal of leaven to call believers away from malice and wickedness toward sincerity and truth. That gives a New Testament connection between the remembered deliverance, Jesus’ saving death and the life His people are to live in response. (1 Corinthians 5:6–8)
