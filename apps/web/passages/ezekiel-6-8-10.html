Passage

Ezekiel 6:8-10

What hope remains for the people scattered among the nations?

Berean Standard Bible

8 Yet I will leave a remnant, for some of you will escape the sword when you are scattered among the nations and throughout the lands. 9 Then in the nations to which they have been carried captive, your survivors will remember Me—how I have been grieved by their adulterous hearts that turned away from Me, and by their eyes that lusted after idols. So they will loathe themselves for the evil they have done and for all their abominations. 10 And they will know that I am the LORD; I did not declare in vain that I would bring this calamity upon them.

What is the context?

The announcement of destruction includes a promise that some will escape. They will be scattered among nations, yet a remnant will remain.

In captivity they will remember the Lord and how their unfaithful hearts and desire for idols grieved Him. Their remembrance includes deep remorse for the evil they practiced.

They will recognize Him as the Lord and know that His warning was not spoken in vain. The preserved people do not simply resume their former life; they remember the relationship they had rejected and the truth of His word.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley connect the survivors’ remembrance with repentance. The people recognize both their obligation to the Lord and the seriousness of turning away from Him.

Wesley emphasizes that the Lord Himself preserves the remnant. Henry treats the return to remembering Him as a gracious outcome within the calamity. Both explanations keep the promise of preservation together with the change in how the people regard their sin.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The promise follows a pattern already stated in Leviticus 26:39–45: exile and confession appear alongside the Lord’s commitment to remember His covenant and not destroy His people completely.

Romans 11:1–6 develops the wider theme of a remnant preserved by grace. Paul rejects the claim that God has abandoned His people and points to His keeping of a people for Himself.

Romans does not explicitly quote Ezekiel 6 in that discussion. The connection is the continuing faithfulness of God within a history that includes rebellion and judgment. The remnant bears witness to His mercy, rather than to the people’s ability to secure themselves.
