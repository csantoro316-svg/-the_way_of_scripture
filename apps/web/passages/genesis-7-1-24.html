Passage

Genesis 7:1-24

The Great Flood

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then the LORD said to Noah, “Go into the ark, you and all your family, because I have found you righteous in this generation. 2 You are to take with you seven pairs of every kind of clean animal, a male and its mate; a pair of every kind of unclean animal, a male and its mate; 3 and seven pairs of every kind of bird of the air, male and female, to preserve their offspring on the face of all the earth. 4 For seven days from now I will send rain on the earth for forty days and forty nights, and I will wipe from the face of the earth every living thing I have made.”

5 And Noah did all that the LORD had commanded him.

6 Now Noah was 600 years old when the floodwaters came upon the earth. 7 And Noah and his wife, with his sons and their wives, entered the ark to escape the waters of the flood. 8 The clean and unclean animals, the birds, and everything that crawls along the ground 9 came to Noah to enter the ark, two by two, male and female, as God had commanded Noah.

10 And after seven days the floodwaters came upon the earth. 11 In the six hundredth year of Noah’s life, on the seventeenth day of the second month, all the fountains of the great deep burst forth, and the floodgates of the heavens were opened. 12 And the rain fell upon the earth for forty days and forty nights.

13 On that very day Noah entered the ark, along with his sons Shem, Ham, and Japheth, and his wife, and the three wives of his sons— 14 they and every kind of wild animal, livestock, crawling creature, bird, and winged creature. 15 They came to Noah to enter the ark, two by two of every creature with the breath of life. 16 And they entered, the male and female of every living thing, as God had commanded Noah. Then the LORD shut him in.

17 For forty days the flood kept coming on the earth, and the waters rose and lifted the ark high above the earth. 18 So the waters continued to surge and rise greatly on the earth, and the ark floated on the surface of the waters. 19 Finally, the waters completely prevailed upon the earth, so that all the high mountains under all the heavens were covered.

20 The waters rose and covered the mountaintops to a depth of fifteen cubits. 21 And every living thing that moved upon the earth perished—birds, livestock, animals, every creature that swarms upon the earth, and all mankind. 22 Of all that was on dry land, everything that had the breath of life in its nostrils died. 23 And every living thing on the face of the earth was destroyed—man and livestock, crawling creatures and birds of the air; they were blotted out from the earth, and only Noah and those with him in the ark remained.

24 And the waters prevailed upon the earth for 150 days.

What is the context?

The preparation becomes an entrance, then a judgment. God tells Noah to bring his household into the ark, specifies the animals to preserve, and announces the approaching rain. The repeated notices of Noah's obedience link the events to chapter 6. The larger number of clean animals also prepares for the offerings after the flood in chapter 8.

The account names water from below and rain from above, then describes it rising over the mountains. The repeated statements about creatures dying make the judgment's seriousness unmistakable. At the same time, the ark is lifted by the water rather than destroyed by it. The very event that brings death outside carries the preserved family through.

God shuts Noah in, and the chapter closes with the waters prevailing for 150 days. The forty days of rain are not the entire length of the family's stay. Chapter 8 will follow the gradual retreat of the water and the much later departure. Genesis supplies a sequence; it does not say that ordinary life resumes as soon as the rain stops.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians read the flood as judgment on evil and the preservation of Noah's family. Where do readings differ? Many take the repeated references to all the mountains and all life on dry land as describing a flood over the whole earth. Augustine, City of God 15.27, defends the reality of water rising above the highest mountains, alongside a further picture of the church's rescue.

Other Christian teachers understand the flood as covering the whole inhabited human world without covering every part of the globe. Hugh Ross's explanation of his reading makes that distinction: universal in its judgment of humanity, geographically limited in extent. That is his organization's interpretation, not an official position shared by all Christians of a denomination. The readings differ over extent while both affirming judgment and preservation.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Matthew 24:37–39 makes the flood a warning about the coming of the Son of Man. The point is the abrupt arrival of judgment upon people who continued ordinary life without recognizing what was coming. Noah's entrance belongs to the contrast between ignoring God's warning and responding to it.

1 Peter 3:20–22 also names the eight people preserved through water and connects this with baptism through Christ's resurrection. These passages give the flood a place in the story of rescue and judgment. They call readers to take God's warning seriously and recognize the rescue He provides.
