Passage

Exodus 36:20-34

What holds the dwelling together?

Berean Standard Bible

20 Next, he constructed upright frames of acacia wood for the tabernacle. 21 Each frame was ten cubits long and a cubit and a half wide. 22 Two tenons were connected to each other for each frame. He made all the frames of the tabernacle in this way. 23 He constructed twenty frames for the south side of the tabernacle, 24 with forty silver bases to put under the twenty frames—two bases for each frame, one under each tenon. 25 For the second side of the tabernacle, the north side, he made twenty frames 26 and forty silver bases—two bases under each frame.

27 He made six frames for the rear of the tabernacle, the west side, 28 and two frames for the two back corners of the tabernacle, 29 coupled together from bottom to top and fitted into a single ring. He made both corners in this way. 30 So there were eight frames and sixteen silver bases—two under each frame.

31 He also made five crossbars of acacia wood for the frames on one side of the tabernacle, 32 five for those on the other side, and five for those on the rear side of the tabernacle, to the west. 33 He made the central crossbar to run through the center of the frames, from one end to the other. 34 And he overlaid the frames with gold and made gold rings to hold the crossbars. He also overlaid the crossbars with gold.

What is the context?

The frames now provide the dwelling’s upright structure. Acacia frames stand in silver bases along the south and north sides and at the back, with special arrangements for the corners. Crossbars connect the frames, including a central bar that runs from one end to the other.

The frames and bars are overlaid with gold, and gold rings hold the bars. These are the supports beneath the curtains and coverings just described. The later inventory identifies the silver used for the bases as coming from those counted in the census (Exodus 38:25–28). The gifts are becoming a complete, joined structure.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin reads the repeated measurements and construction details as a record of the workers following God’s design faithfully. The supports are practical parts of the dwelling, and their craftsmanship belongs to the people’s obedience just as much as the more prominent furnishings.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The New Testament uses building language to explain God’s work among His people. In 1 Corinthians 3:10–17, Christ is the foundation, workers must take care how they build, and the community is God’s holy temple because His Spirit dwells among them.

As a broader biblical parallel, a carefully joined dwelling helps us picture a people being built together. Paul directs our attention to Christ as their foundation and to the responsibility of those who serve them.
