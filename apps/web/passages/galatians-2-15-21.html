Passage

Galatians 2:15-21

How can we be made right with God and live for Him?

Berean Standard Bible

15 We who are Jews by birth and not Gentile “sinners” 16 know that a man is not justified by works of the law, but by faith in Jesus Christ. So we, too, have believed in Christ Jesus, that we may be justified by faith in Christ and not by works of the law, because by works of the law no one will be justified.

17 But if, while we seek to be justified in Christ, we ourselves are found to be sinners, does that make Christ a minister of sin? Certainly not! 18 If I rebuild what I have already torn down, I prove myself to be a lawbreaker.

19 For through the law I died to the law so that I might live to God. 20 I have been crucified with Christ, and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I live in the body, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself up for me. 21 I do not set aside the grace of God. For if righteousness comes through the law, then Christ died for nothing.

What is the context?

Paul moves from the dispute over fellowship to its foundation. Even those who are Jews by birth know that a person is justified by faith in Jesus Christ, through whom they have believed, rather than by works of the law. Being made right with God depends on Christ.

He then addresses the question of sin. Seeking to be justified in Christ does not make Christ a servant of sin. Rebuilding what has been torn down would expose the person doing so as a lawbreaker.

Paul describes the purpose of dying to the law as living to God. He speaks of being crucified with Christ and of Christ living in him. His present life is lived by faith in the Son of God who loved him and gave Himself for him.

The final statement returns to grace. If righteousness could come through the law, Christ’s death would have been needless. Paul places both acceptance before God and the life that follows within Christ’s self-giving.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the passage as grounding our right standing with God in Christ and His grace, while calling us into a changed life. Traditions differ in how they explain justification and its relationship to that change.

The Catholic Church teaches that justification includes forgiveness and inward renewal, received through God’s grace and conferred in baptism. The Catechism, paragraphs 1987–1993, says grace comes first and enables a person’s free response in faith and love. This is understood as Christ’s gift, not something earned independently of Him.

The Reformed Westminster Confession, chapter 11, distinguishes God’s accepting someone as righteous from His work of inward renewal. It teaches that Christ’s obedience and saving work are credited to the believer, received through faith alone. The same chapter says this faith is living and works through love.

The Methodist Articles of Religion IX–X likewise teach justification through faith because of what Christ has done, with good works following as the fruit of living faith. Wesley’s notes on verses 16–21 emphasize both freedom from trying to be justified through the law and the new life of living to God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 6:1–11 describes believers sharing in Christ’s death and resurrection so that they can walk in newness of life. Christ’s death and risen life become the pattern for their freedom from sin and life toward God.

That explicit connection develops the purpose expressed here in Paul’s desire to live to God. The Son who loved and gave Himself for him is also the source of the new life he now lives.
