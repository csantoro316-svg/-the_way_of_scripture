Passage

Ezekiel 34:11-16

What will the Lord Himself do for His scattered sheep?

Berean Standard Bible

11 For this is what the Lord GOD says: ‘Behold, I Myself will search for My flock and seek them out. 12 As a shepherd looks for his scattered sheep when he is among the flock, so I will look for My flock. I will rescue them from all the places to which they were scattered on a day of clouds and darkness. 13 I will bring them out from the peoples, gather them from the countries, and bring them into their own land. I will feed them on the mountains of Israel, in the ravines, and in all the settlements of the land.

14 I will feed them in good pasture, and the lofty mountains of Israel will be their grazing land. There they will lie down in a good grazing land; they will feed in rich pasture on the mountains of Israel. 15 I will tend My flock and make them lie down, declares the Lord GOD. 16 I will seek the lost, bring back the strays, bind up the broken, and strengthen the weak; but the sleek and strong I will destroy. I will shepherd them with justice.’

What is the context?

The answer to the failed shepherds begins with the Lord’s own promise: He Himself will search for His flock. He will rescue them from the places to which they were scattered on a day of clouds and darkness.

He will gather them from the peoples and countries, bring them to their land and feed them in good pasture. The picture moves from exposure and hunger to nourishment and rest.

He will seek the lost, bring back strays, bind the broken and strengthen the weak. He will also judge the sleek and strong. His shepherding includes both the rescue of the vulnerable and justice against those who harm them.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry first relates the promise to the restoration of the Jews, then draws out the good Shepherd’s care for His people. Wesley understands the cloudy day as a time of general distress.

Walvoord places the regathering and restored national life within a future earthly reign of Christ. The readings differ over the extent and setting of fulfillment, while recognizing the Lord’s initiative and the faithful care that answers the failed shepherds.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

A supported connection appears in John 10:1–18. Jesus identifies Himself as the good shepherd, contrasts His care with the thief and the abandoning hired hand, and lays down His life for the sheep. He also speaks of bringing other sheep into one flock under one shepherd.

Luke 15:1–7 pictures a shepherd searching for the lost sheep and rejoicing over its recovery, explaining heaven’s joy over a sinner who repents. These passages show the promised kind of care in Jesus’ ministry and saving work. The connection does not require settling every question about the timing of Israel’s restoration.
