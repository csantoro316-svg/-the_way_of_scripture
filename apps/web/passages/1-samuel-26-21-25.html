Passage

1 Samuel 26:21-25

Why do David and Saul still go separate ways?

Berean Standard Bible

21 Then Saul replied, “I have sinned. Come back, David my son. I will never harm you again, because today you considered my life precious. I have played the fool and have committed a grave error!” 22 “Here is the king’s spear,” David answered. “Let one of the young men come over and get it. 23 May the LORD repay every man for his righteousness and faithfulness. For the LORD delivered you into my hand today, but I would not extend my hand against the LORD’s anointed. 24 As surely as I valued your life today, so may the LORD value my life and rescue me from all trouble.” 25 Saul said to him, “May you be blessed, David my son. You will accomplish great things and will surely prevail.” So David went on his way, and Saul returned home.

What is the context?

Saul confesses sin, says he has acted foolishly and promises not to harm David again. He recognizes that David valued his life when he could have taken it.

David offers the spear back through one of Saul’s young men. He asks the LORD to repay righteousness and faithfulness, and to value and rescue his life as he has valued Saul’s. Saul blesses him and speaks of his future success, but David goes on his way while Saul returns home.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley takes Saul’s confession here as a particularly sincere acknowledgment of wrong and notes that no further pursuit is recorded. Henry and Gill remain doubtful about lasting repentance. They agree about the words spoken while assessing their depth differently.

Henry directs attention to David’s hope for recompense from the LORD. Saul’s invitation does not become the foundation of his security. Their separate departure allows mercy and caution to remain together, rather than treating the confession as an automatic return to the earlier relationship.
