Passage

Revelation 22:8-9

Why does the angel refuse John’s worship?

Berean Standard Bible

8 And I, John, am the one who heard and saw these things. And when I had heard and seen them, I fell down to worship at the feet of the angel who had shown me these things. 9 But he said to me, “Do not do that! I am a fellow servant with you and your brothers the prophets, and with those who keep the words of this book. Worship God!”

What is the context?

John identifies himself as the one who heard and saw these things. He falls at the feet of the angel who showed them to him, but the angel immediately refuses his worship.

The angel calls himself a fellow servant with John, the prophets and those who keep the book’s words. His command is direct: worship God. The messenger shares the work of service; he does not receive the worship that belongs to the One whose message he brings.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The central reading is shared: the angel refuses worship and directs John to God. Wesley connects the passage closely with the similar scene in Revelation 19:10, and the USCCB notes also cross-reference that scene. The instruction gives God the worship while placing the angel alongside the other servants.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The scene follows the same pattern as Revelation 19:10, where the angel also refuses worship and points John toward God. Its repetition keeps the reader’s attention on the source of the revelation.

Jesus’ response to temptation in Matthew 4:8–10 likewise directs worship and service to the Lord God alone. The authority of a messenger never becomes a reason to replace God as the One worshiped.

What is our position?

Our study Who is The Angel of the Lord? uses this passage to distinguish ordinary angels from the Angel-of-the-LORD encounters it examines. Here the angel refuses worship immediately and identifies himself as a fellow servant. We understand his correction plainly: worship belongs to God.
