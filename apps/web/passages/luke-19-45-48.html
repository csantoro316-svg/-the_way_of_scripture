Passage

Luke 19:45-48

What has happened to God’s house of prayer?

Berean Standard Bible

45 Then Jesus entered the temple courts and began to drive out those who were selling there. 46 He declared to them, “It is written: ‘My house will be a house of prayer.’ But you have made it ‘a den of robbers.’”

47 Jesus was teaching at the temple every day, but the chief priests, scribes, and leaders of the people were intent on killing Him. 48 Yet they could not find a way to do so, because all the people hung on His words.

What is the context?

Jesus enters the temple courts and drives out those selling there. He explains His action with two passages of Scripture: the house of prayer from Isaiah and the den of robbers from Jeremiah.

Isaiah’s setting includes foreigners joining themselves to the Lord and being welcomed in His house of prayer. Jeremiah confronts people who oppress others and commit wrong, then treat the temple as a place where they can claim safety while continuing unchanged. These passages give Jesus’ action a setting deeper than disruption in a busy marketplace. (Isaiah 56:6–8; Jeremiah 7:3–14)

Jesus then teaches in the temple every day. The chief priests, scribes and leading people want to kill Him, but cannot find a way because the people are listening intently. The next chapter opens with a challenge to His authority in this same setting.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly understand this action as a defense of God’s purpose for His house and a confrontation with its misuse. The Catholic bishops’ notes also emphasize Jesus’ authority and the temple becoming the setting for His teaching in Jerusalem. (USCCB notes on Luke 19:45–48)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John’s temple account connects Jesus’ challenge to the temple’s misuse with a saying about raising the temple again in three days. John explicitly explains that Jesus means His body, and that the disciples understand after His resurrection. This is a connection supplied by another Gospel, rather than an extra saying inserted into Luke’s scene. (John 2:13–22)

Ephesians describes people once separated being brought near through Christ, with access to the Father by one Spirit. Together they become a holy temple and a dwelling place for God. This wider picture carries the purpose of fellowship with God into the shared life of His people, with Christ as their cornerstone. (Ephesians 2:13–22)
