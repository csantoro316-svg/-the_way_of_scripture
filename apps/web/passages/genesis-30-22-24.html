Passage

Genesis 30:22-24

Joseph

Berean Standard Bible

22 Then God remembered Rachel. He listened to her and opened her womb, 23 and she conceived and gave birth to a son. “God has taken away my shame,” she said. 24 She named him Joseph, and said, “May the LORD add to me another son.”

What is the context?

God now remembers Rachel, listens to her, and enables her to conceive. Remembering here leads to action on her behalf; it does not mean God had lost track of her while Leah bore children. After the rivalry and the mandrake bargain, the account clearly credits Him with this birth.

Rachel gives birth to Joseph and says that God has taken away her shame. She then asks that the LORD add another son. The explanation of Joseph's name joins gratitude for what she has received with hope for another child. Her words allow us to hear the relief after her earlier distress.

Joseph's birth also marks a transition. Immediately afterward, Jacob asks Laban to let him return to his own homeland. This child will later become central both to the family's conflict and to its survival, though none of that future is explained to Rachel in this scene.

How is this passage commonly understood?

A common Christian reading emphasizes God's answer after Rachel's long distress, together with her gratitude and continuing hope. Wesley's note on Genesis 30:22 connects the name's wordplay with both the removal of shame and the request that God add another son.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Joseph will later tell his brothers that the harm they intended against him became, through God's purpose, a means of preserving many lives (Genesis 50:19–21). His birth is the beginning of a life through which the promised family will be sustained during famine.

Acts 7:9–16 retells that later history: the brothers are jealous of Joseph, but God is with him, and food in Egypt becomes the means of bringing the family there. Rachel's relief is therefore part of a much larger story of God's care. The narrative will again show His purpose advancing through circumstances the family could not have planned or understood at the time.
