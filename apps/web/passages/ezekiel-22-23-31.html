Passage

Ezekiel 22:23-31

Who will stand in the gap when the leaders and people are corrupt?

Berean Standard Bible

23 And the word of the LORD came to me, saying, 24 “Son of man, say to her, ‘In the day of indignation, you are a land that has not been cleansed, upon which no rain has fallen.’ 25 The conspiracy of the princes in her midst is like a roaring lion tearing its prey. They devour the people, seize the treasures and precious things, and multiply the widows within her. 26 Her priests do violence to My law and profane My holy things. They make no distinction between the holy and the common, and they fail to distinguish between the clean and the unclean. They disregard My Sabbaths, so that I am profaned among them.

27 Her officials within her are like wolves tearing their prey, shedding blood, and destroying lives for dishonest gain. 28 Her prophets whitewash these deeds by false visions and lying divinations, saying, ‘This is what the Lord GOD says,’ when the LORD has not spoken. 29 The people of the land have practiced extortion and committed robbery. They have oppressed the poor and needy and have exploited the foreign resident without justice.

30 I searched for a man among them to repair the wall and stand in the gap before Me on behalf of the land, so that I should not destroy it. But I found no one. 31 So I have poured out My indignation upon them and consumed them with the fire of My fury. I have brought their ways down upon their own heads, declares the Lord GOD.”

What is the context?

The closing accusation reaches across the community. In the BSB, princes are compared to a roaring lion that devours people and multiplies widows. Priests violate the Lord’s law, blur the distinctions entrusted to them and disregard His Sabbaths.

Officials act like wolves, destroying lives for dishonest gain. Prophets whitewash what is happening through false visions, claiming that the Lord has spoken when He has not.

The people also share in the wrongdoing through robbery, extortion and exploitation of the poor and the foreign resident. Responsibility does not end with the leaders.

The Lord says that He sought someone to repair the wall and stand in the gap before Him on behalf of the land, but found no one. The chapter ends with the announced outpouring of wrath: the people’s own ways return upon their heads.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry understands the passage as the guilt of people across ranks and offices, each abusing the power available to them. Wesley reads standing in the gap as interceding for mercy between the sinful people and the God they have offended.

The picture also recalls Psalm 106:19–23, where Moses stands in the breach when Israel worships the calf. In Ezekiel, the failure to find someone exposes the community’s need for faithful leadership and an effective plea on its behalf.

The statement concerns the situation described in the land. It is not a claim that no faithful person, prophet or prayer existed anywhere in the world; Ezekiel himself is receiving and speaking the Lord’s word.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 7:23–28 explains why Jesus’ priesthood supplies a lasting answer to human weakness. He lives forever, intercedes for those who draw near to God through Him, and offered Himself for sin once for all.

Hebrews does not explicitly identify Ezekiel’s missing intercessor as a prediction of Jesus. The wider connection is the need for someone who can truly represent people before God. Where the chapter exposes the failure of human offices and the absence of an effective defender, Hebrews directs hope toward the living Son and His complete saving provision.
