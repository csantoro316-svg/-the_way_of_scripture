Passage

1 Kings 8:22-30

How can God hear prayer at a house that cannot contain Him?

Berean Standard Bible

22 Then Solomon stood before the altar of the LORD in front of the whole assembly of Israel, spread out his hands toward heaven, 23 and said: “O LORD, God of Israel, there is no God like You in heaven above or on earth below, keeping Your covenant of loving devotion with Your servants who walk before You with all their hearts. 24 You have kept Your promise to Your servant, my father David. What You spoke with Your mouth You have fulfilled with Your hand this day. 25 Therefore now, O LORD, God of Israel, keep for Your servant, my father David, what You promised when You said: ‘You will never fail to have a man to sit before Me on the throne of Israel, if only your descendants guard their way to walk before Me as you have done.’ 26 And now, O God of Israel, please confirm what You promised to Your servant, my father David.

27 But will God indeed dwell upon the earth? The heavens, even the highest heavens, cannot contain You, much less this temple I have built. 28 Yet regard the prayer and plea of Your servant, O LORD my God, so that You may hear the cry and the prayer that Your servant is praying before You today. 29 May Your eyes be open toward this temple night and day, toward the place of which You said, ‘My Name shall be there,’ so that You may hear the prayer that Your servant prays toward this place. 30 Hear the plea of Your servant and of Your people Israel when they pray toward this place. May You hear from heaven, Your dwelling place. May You hear and forgive.

What is the context?

Solomon begins his public prayer before the altar with his hands spread toward heaven. He praises the LORD’s unmatched faithfulness and asks Him to continue keeping His promise to David. The prayer also remembers the responsibility of David’s descendants to walk faithfully before God.

Then comes a question that explains how Solomon understands the temple: if even the highest heavens cannot contain God, how could this house do so? Yet Solomon asks God to watch over this place, where He has chosen to put His Name.

The king asks for himself and for the people who will pray toward the temple. God is to hear from heaven and forgive. The house gives their worship a place of focus, while the One they call on remains greater than the whole creation.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill both explain that Solomon does not imagine God confined within the temple. God graciously makes His presence known there while remaining beyond the limits of any place.

Henry sees the fulfilled promise to David as grounds for continuing prayer: what God has already done encourages Solomon to depend on Him for what comes next. Forgiveness is central to the request, alongside confidence in God’s faithfulness.

These readings hold together two features of the passage: the temple genuinely matters in Israel’s worship, and God hears from heaven. The direction of prayer expresses dependence on the God who made the promise.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Acts 7:44–50, Stephen recalls the tabernacle, David’s desire for a dwelling place and Solomon’s building of the house. He then makes the same essential point Solomon makes here: the Most High cannot be enclosed in something human hands have built.

Stephen supports that point from the prophets, describing heaven as God’s throne and earth as His footstool. The New Testament therefore carries this warning forward directly: honoring a place of worship must go together with recognizing the greatness of the God to whom it belongs.
