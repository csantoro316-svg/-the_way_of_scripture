Passage

Exodus 30:22-33

Why was the anointing oil reserved for God’s service?

Berean Standard Bible

22 Then the LORD said to Moses, 23 “Take the finest spices: 500 shekels of liquid myrrh, half that amount (250 shekels) of fragrant cinnamon, 250 shekels of fragrant cane, 24 500 shekels of cassia —all according to the sanctuary shekel—and a hin of olive oil. 25 Prepare from these a sacred anointing oil, a fragrant blend, the work of a perfumer; it will be a sacred anointing oil.

26 Use this oil to anoint the Tent of Meeting, the ark of the Testimony, 27 the table and all its utensils, the lampstand and its utensils, the altar of incense, 28 the altar of burnt offering and all its utensils, and the basin with its stand. 29 You are to consecrate them so that they will be most holy. Whatever touches them shall be holy. 30 Anoint Aaron and his sons and consecrate them to serve Me as priests.

31 And you are to tell the Israelites, ‘This will be My sacred anointing oil for the generations to come. 32 It must not be used to anoint an ordinary man, and you must not make anything like it with the same formula. It is holy, and it must be holy to you. 33 Anyone who mixes perfume like it or puts it on an outsider shall be cut off from his people.’”

What is the context?

God gives a particular mixture of spices and olive oil for anointing the sanctuary, its furnishings, Aaron and his sons. A perfumer is to prepare the fragrant blend. Its purpose is to set these people and things apart for His service.

The same formula must not be reproduced for ordinary personal use or applied to someone outside its appointed use. The command is consistent with Aaron and his sons being anointed: their priestly consecration is precisely the purpose God has authorized. The penalty underscores the difference between something dedicated to Him and something people may use as they choose.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The main reading is that the oil marks the people and furnishings as belonging to God’s holy service. Wesley and Calvin also connect anointing with the work and gifts of the Holy Spirit. Calvin emphasizes that the outward service depends on the Spirit’s work; Wesley stresses reverence for what God has appointed.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The title Christ comes from the Greek Χριστός, Christos (khrees-TOSS), meaning anointed (Thayer). Acts 10:34–43 explicitly says that God anointed Jesus with the Holy Spirit and power, then recounts His ministry, death, resurrection and the forgiveness received through Him. Luke 3:21–22 also brings together the Father’s declaration about His Son and the Spirit descending upon Him.

The comparison points beyond a sacred material to the person God appoints and empowers. God’s gift remains His to give. In Acts 8:14–24, Peter rebukes Simon for thinking that the power to give the Spirit could be bought. The theme is God’s provision for His work, received in obedience rather than brought under human control.
