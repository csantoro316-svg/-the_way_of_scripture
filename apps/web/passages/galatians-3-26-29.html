Passage

Galatians 3:26-29

Who belongs to the family and the promise?

Berean Standard Bible

26 You are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus. 27 For all of you who were baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ.

28 There is neither Jew nor Greek, slave nor free, male nor female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus. 29 And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham’s seed and heirs according to the promise.

What is the context?

After describing the law’s guardianship, Paul tells the believers who they are: God’s sons through faith in Christ Jesus. He connects their baptism into Christ with being clothed with Christ.

That belonging is shared. Jew and Greek, slave and free, male and female are all one in Christ Jesus. Paul includes people across these boundaries in the family and inheritance he is describing.

He then returns to Abraham’s promise. If they belong to Christ, they are Abraham’s seed and heirs according to the promise. Their place in God’s family rests in belonging to Christ.

The next chapter develops the inheritance picture through the comparison of a young heir with a son who has come into the freedom appointed by the father.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly see faith, belonging to Christ and shared life in God’s family at the center of these verses. They differ in how they explain the relationship between baptism and that belonging.

The Catholic Church teaches that baptism gives new birth, adoption and membership in Christ’s body. The Catechism, paragraphs 1265–1267, describes this as God’s gift of grace, enabling faith, hope and love.

The Reformed Westminster Confession, chapter 28, describes water baptism as a sign and pledge of being joined to Christ. It distinguishes the time of receiving the rite from the time the Spirit gives the grace promised. The 1689 Baptist Confession, chapter 29, describes baptism as a sign of sharing in Christ’s death and resurrection, given to those who profess repentance and faith.

Dallas Theological Seminary, articles XIII–XIV, distinguishes the Spirit’s work of joining believers into Christ’s body from water baptism as testimony. It describes water baptism as an act of testimony.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 8:14–17 connects being God’s children with the Spirit’s witness, calling God Father and being heirs with Christ. That shared inheritance reaches through present suffering toward glory with Him.

The family language here therefore carries a future as well as a present hope. Those who belong to Christ share the promise and a life directed toward what God has prepared for His children.

What is our position?

Our study Baptism reads verses 26–27 as distinguishing sonship through faith from being clothed with Christ in baptism. Its position is that faith establishes the saved relationship with God, and water baptism follows as the appointed act in which the believer puts on Christ for a new life of obedience. The study sums up that distinction as faith saving and baptism clothing the believer with Christ.
