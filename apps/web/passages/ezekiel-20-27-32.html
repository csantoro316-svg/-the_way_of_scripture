Passage

Ezekiel 20:27-32

Has the present generation left its fathers’ idolatry behind?

Berean Standard Bible

27 Therefore, son of man, speak to the house of Israel, and tell them that this is what the Lord GOD says: In this way also your fathers blasphemed Me by their unfaithfulness against Me. 28 When I brought them into the land that I swore to give them and they saw any high hill or leafy tree, there they offered their sacrifices, presented offerings that provoked Me, sent up their fragrant incense, and poured out their drink offerings. 29 So I asked them: ‘What is this high place to which you go?’ (And to this day it is called Bamah.)

30 Therefore tell the house of Israel that this is what the Lord GOD says: Will you defile yourselves the way your fathers did, prostituting yourselves with their abominations? 31 When you offer your gifts, sacrificing your sons in the fire, you continue to defile yourselves with all your idols to this day. So should I be consulted by you, O house of Israel? As surely as I live, declares the Lord GOD, I will not be consulted by you! 32 When you say, ‘Let us be like the nations, like the peoples of the lands, serving wood and stone,’ what you have in mind will never come to pass.

What is the context?

The account moves into the land the Lord had promised. The people used its hills and leafy trees as places for offerings that provoked Him. Receiving the land had not ended the earlier pattern.

The Lord then addresses the present generation directly. Their gifts and the sacrifice of their sons show that the same defilement continues to this day.

That is why the question of consulting Him returns. They approach His prophet while continuing to dishonor His name through their idols.

He also exposes their desire to become like surrounding nations, serving wood and stone. That plan will not succeed. The next passage explains how He will assert His rule over them.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley understand the passage as exposing continued rebellion after settlement in Canaan. The current generation cannot treat the problem as something belonging only to its ancestors.

Wesley reads the wish to resemble the nations as a hoped-for security through adopting their ways. The answer is that Israel cannot erase its responsibility before the Lord by choosing another religious identity.
