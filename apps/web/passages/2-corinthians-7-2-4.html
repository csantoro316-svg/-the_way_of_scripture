Passage

2 Corinthians 7:2-4

Why is Paul still asking them to make room in their hearts?

Berean Standard Bible

2 Make room for us in your hearts. We have wronged no one, we have corrupted no one, we have exploited no one. 3 I do not say this to condemn you. I have said before that you so occupy our hearts that we live and die together with you. 4 Great is my confidence in you; great is my pride in you; I am filled with encouragement; in all our troubles my joy overflows.

What is the context?

Paul returns to the personal appeal heard in 2 Corinthians 6:11–13. He asks the Corinthians to make room for him and his companions, saying they have wronged, corrupted and exploited no one.

He immediately explains that he is not speaking to condemn them. They are so firmly held in his heart that he describes their relationship in terms of living and dying together. His defense of the ministry is joined with affection for the people.

Confidence, pride in them, encouragement and overflowing joy now come to the foreground, even while troubles continue. The following account of Titus will explain why Paul can speak this way. Reconciliation is beginning to change the relationship that had caused so much anguish.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as a renewed appeal for trust and affection after conflict. Wesley’s notes on verses 2–4 emphasize Paul’s care and confidence. The USCCB note on verses 2–4 connects the appeal with 6:11–13 and treats its joy and encouragement as a bridge to the account that follows.
