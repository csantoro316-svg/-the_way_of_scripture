Passage

Matthew 23:29-36

Honoring Past Prophets While Rejecting Those God Sends

Berean Standard Bible

29 Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, you hypocrites! You build tombs for the prophets and decorate the monuments of the righteous. 30 And you say, ‘If we had lived in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partners with them in shedding the blood of the prophets.’ 31 So you testify against yourselves that you are the sons of those who murdered the prophets. 32 Fill up, then, the measure of the sin of your fathers. 33 You snakes! You brood of vipers! How will you escape the sentence of hell?

34 Because of this, I am sending you prophets and wise men and teachers. Some of them you will kill and crucify, and others you will flog in your synagogues and persecute from town to town. 35 And so upon you will come all the righteous blood shed on earth, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah son of Berechiah, whom you murdered between the temple and the altar. 36 Truly I tell you, all these things will come upon this generation.

What is the context?

The final woe challenges the leaders' claim that they would never have joined their ancestors in killing the prophets. They build and decorate the prophets' tombs, yet Jesus says their conduct continues the same pattern. Their treatment of the messengers He sends will reveal the gap between honoring faithful people of the past and receiving God's word in the present.

Jesus warns that some of His messengers will be killed, flogged, or pursued from town to town. The warning recalls what He told the disciples in Matthew 10:16–23. He then speaks of the righteous blood shed from Abel to Zechariah and says these things will come upon this generation. The charge concerns participation in a repeated course of wrongdoing; it gives no license to assign inherited guilt to every later person of the same ancestry.

Abel's murder is recorded in Genesis 4:8–12. A prophet named Zechariah is killed in the temple courtyard in 2 Chronicles 24:20–22, but that account calls him the son of Jehoiada, while Matthew names the son of Berechiah. Zechariah 1:1 names the prophet Zechariah as Berechiah's son without describing his death. Those details should remain visible rather than being silently treated as identical.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the warning as exposing people who honor earlier messengers while rejecting those God sends to them. Wesley on 23:29–35 connects their present conduct with their ancestors' violence; the US Catholic bishops' notes on 23:29–36 likewise emphasize that continuing pattern.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 7:51–60 shows the pattern continuing when Stephen charges his hearers with resisting God's messengers and is then killed. Yet his final prayer asks that the sin not be held against them. The witness to Jesus includes both the cost of faithfulness and mercy toward persecutors.

The reference to Abel also opens a striking New Testament connection. Hebrews 12:22–24 brings believers to Jesus, the mediator of the new covenant, and His sprinkled blood, which speaks a better word than Abel's. Alongside the warning about bloodshed stands God's saving work through the One whose death brings people near to Him.
