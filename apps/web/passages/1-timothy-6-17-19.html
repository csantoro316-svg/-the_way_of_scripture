Passage

1 Timothy 6:17-19

How should people with wealth use what God has given?

Berean Standard Bible

17 Instruct those who are rich in the present age not to be conceited and not to put their hope in the uncertainty of wealth, but in God, who richly provides all things for us to enjoy. 18 Instruct them to do good, to be rich in good works, and to be generous and ready to share, 19 treasuring up for themselves a firm foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of that which is truly life.

What is the context?

Paul has warned against wanting to become rich. He now addresses believers who already have wealth. They are not to become proud or place their hope in something so uncertain. Their hope belongs in God, who provides generously and gives good things to enjoy.

Their resources bring an opportunity to do good. Paul asks for generosity, a readiness to share and a life rich in good works. Present wealth is to serve a future that lasts, rather than become a substitute for trusting God.

This is not an instruction to despise every gift or refuse all enjoyment. Enjoyment, humility and generosity belong together under the God who gives.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley both understand the passage as a call to replace pride and trust in wealth with trust in God and practical generosity. Both connect the future treasure with God’s gracious reward, rather than treating donations as a purchase of eternal life.

They describe that connection in different terms. Calvin emphasizes God’s acceptance and reward of service by grace, without human merit. Wesley likewise denies that giving can obtain eternal life, while warning that failure to give can accompany a loss of grace and salvation. Those are their own explanations of how the warning and promise work.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus tells His disciples in Matthew 6:19–21 to store up treasure in heaven, connecting their treasure with their hearts. Paul’s instructions share that future direction: possessions can be used now in service of what lasts.

Acts 4:32–35 shows believers sharing their resources so that people in need receive help. It is a concrete example of the generous life described here, rather than a command that every believer must use that community’s exact arrangement. God’s gifts become a means of care for others.
