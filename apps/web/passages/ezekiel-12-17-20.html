Passage

Ezekiel 12:17-20

Why must Ezekiel eat and drink with trembling?

Berean Standard Bible

17 Moreover, the word of the LORD came to me, saying, 18 “Son of man, eat your bread with trembling, and drink your water with quivering and anxiety. 19 Then tell the people of the land that this is what the Lord GOD says about those living in Jerusalem and in the land of Israel: ‘They will eat their bread with anxiety and drink their water in dread, for their land will be stripped of everything in it because of the violence of all who dwell in it. 20 The inhabited cities will be laid waste, and the land will become desolate. Then you will know that I am the LORD.’”

What is the context?

The Lord gives another visible sign. Ezekiel must eat his bread with trembling and drink his water with fear, portraying the experience of those living in Jerusalem and the land of Israel.

Even ordinary meals will be overshadowed by anxiety. The land will be stripped and inhabited cities laid waste because of the violence of their people.

The act brings the coming disaster into daily life. A meal, normally a moment of provision and rest, becomes a picture of the dread that will accompany judgment. Once again, the stated outcome is that the people will know the Lord.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry understands the trembling meal as a portrayal of Jerusalem’s fear during the siege. Wesley connects the land’s desolation with the injustice and oppression practiced among the people.

The sign therefore joins the people’s treatment of one another with the loss of ordinary security. Its message concerns the announced judgment on Jerusalem; it does not diagnose every experience of anxiety as punishment for wrongdoing.
