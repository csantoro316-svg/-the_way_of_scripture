Passage

1 Samuel 26:1-12

Why does David refuse another chance to kill Saul?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then the Ziphites came to Saul at Gibeah and said, “Is not David hiding on the hill of Hachilah, opposite Jeshimon?” 2 So Saul, accompanied by three thousand chosen men of Israel, went down to the Wilderness of Ziph to search for David there. 3 Saul camped beside the road at the hill of Hachilah opposite Jeshimon, but David was living in the wilderness. When he realized that Saul had followed him there, 4 David sent out spies to verify that Saul had arrived. 5 Then David set out and went to the place where Saul had camped. He saw the place where Saul and Abner son of Ner, the general of his army, had lain down. Saul was lying inside the inner circle of the camp, with the troops camped around him.

6 And David asked Ahimelech the Hittite and Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother, “Who will go down with me to Saul in the camp?” “I will go with you,” answered Abishai. 7 That night David and Abishai came to the troops, and Saul was lying there asleep in the inner circle of the camp, with his spear stuck in the ground by his head. And Abner and the troops were lying around him. 8 Abishai said to David, “Today God has delivered your enemy into your hand. Now, therefore, please let me thrust the spear through him into the ground with one stroke. I will not need to strike him twice!” 9 But David said to Abishai, “Do not destroy him, for who can extend a hand against the LORD’s anointed and be guiltless?” 10 David added, “As surely as the LORD lives, the LORD Himself will strike him down; either his day will come and he will die, or he will go into battle and perish. 11 But the LORD forbid that I should extend my hand against the LORD’s anointed. Instead, take the spear and water jug by his head, and let us go.” 12 So David took the spear and water jug by Saul’s head, and they departed. No one saw them or knew about it, nor did anyone wake up; they all remained asleep, because a deep sleep from the LORD had fallen on them.

What is the context?

The Ziphites again report David’s location, and Saul brings three thousand chosen men into the wilderness to find him. David confirms the pursuit through spies, locates the camp and sees Saul lying within its inner circle, with Abner and the troops around him.

Abishai volunteers to go down with David at night. Finding Saul asleep with his spear by his head, he offers to kill him with one stroke. He interprets the opportunity as God delivering David’s enemy into his hand.

David refuses. Saul is still the LORD’s anointed, and David will not make himself the one who kills him. He leaves Saul’s end with the LORD, whether through a direct stroke, the ordinary coming of death or battle. They take the spear and water jug instead.

No one sees them or wakes. The account explains that a deep sleep from the LORD has fallen on the camp. Once again Saul is vulnerable and David spares him, now making especially clear that the timing and manner of Saul’s end belong to God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Henry read this as a refusal to force the promised kingdom into David’s hands through wrongdoing. Abishai’s confidence that the opportunity comes from God does not settle what they should do with it.

Gill understands David’s account of Saul’s possible deaths as reason to wait rather than hurry God’s judgment. It does not supply David with a private date or method of execution.

Henry emphasizes how helpless the powerful camp becomes under God’s hand. Gill likewise recognizes the sleep as the LORD’s action. The miracle leaves David with a choice about how he will treat the king whose life is within reach.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Across the cave, Abigail’s intervention and this camp, David faces repeated occasions to decide whether he will trust God or grasp at an outcome through violence. The story gives a concrete picture of faith becoming visible in decisions under pressure.

James 1:2–5 describes tested faith developing perseverance toward maturity and directs those who lack wisdom to ask God. This is a shared theme of growth, rather than a rule that every hardship is a repeated examination with a predictable reward. God’s purpose includes forming people who can act wisely while they wait.
