Passage

Matthew 21:12-17

A House of Prayer, Healing, and Praise

Berean Standard Bible

12 Then Jesus entered the temple courts and drove out all who were buying and selling there. He overturned the tables of the money changers and the seats of those selling doves. 13 And He declared to them, “It is written: ‘My house will be called a house of prayer.’ But you are making it ‘a den of robbers.’”

14 The blind and the lame came to Him at the temple, and He healed them. 15 But the chief priests and scribes were indignant when they saw the wonders He performed and the children shouting in the temple courts, “Hosanna to the Son of David!” 16 “Do You hear what these children are saying?” they asked. “Yes,” Jesus answered. “Have you never read: ‘From the mouths of children and infants You have ordained praise’?” 17 Then He left them and went out of the city to Bethany, where He spent the night.

What is the context?

After the entry into Jerusalem, Matthew brings us into the temple courts. Jesus drives out those buying and selling, overturns the money changers' tables, and removes the seats of the dove sellers. He explains His action through God's words about the purpose of His house.

His words bring together Isaiah 56:7, where God's house welcomes people in prayer, and Jeremiah 7:3–11, where God confronts people who practice injustice and then treat the temple as a place of safety. The phrase about robbers carries that serious warning into the scene.

The blind and lame then come to Jesus in the temple, and He heals them. Children continue the praise heard on the road, calling Him Son of David. The chief priests and scribes object, but Jesus answers with Psalm 8:2, which speaks of praise from children and infants. He then leaves for Bethany for the night.

The scene holds together His challenge to the temple's misuse and His welcome of those who come for help. His authority is shown through both the correction and the healing, while the children's praise brings the question of His identity before the leaders.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand Jesus to be defending God's house as a place of prayer and displaying His authority through His actions and the praise He accepts. Wesley on 21:12–16 connects the warning with Isaiah and Jeremiah, while the US Catholic bishops' notes on 21:12–17 emphasize the challenge His actions and healings present to the temple authorities.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The wider promise in Isaiah 56:3–8 includes foreigners and others who might fear exclusion. God promises to gather people and make them joyful in His house of prayer. The words Jesus uses belong within that purpose of bringing people into worship before Him.

The New Testament develops the temple theme around Christ. In John 2:19–22, in another temple scene, Jesus speaks about His body being raised. Ephesians 2:18–22 describes Jewish and Gentile believers having access to the Father through Him and becoming His household, a dwelling for God through the Spirit. God's gathering work builds a people around the risen Christ.
