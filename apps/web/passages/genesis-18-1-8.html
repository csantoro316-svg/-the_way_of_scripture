Passage

Genesis 18:1-8

The Three Visitors

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then the LORD appeared to Abraham by the Oaks of Mamre in the heat of the day, while he was sitting at the entrance of his tent. 2 And Abraham looked up and saw three men standing nearby. When he saw them, he ran from the entrance of his tent to meet them and bowed low to the ground.

3 “My lord,” said Abraham, “if I have found favor in your sight, please do not pass your servant by. 4 Let a little water be brought, that you may wash your feet and rest yourselves under the tree. 5 And I will bring a bit of bread so that you may refresh yourselves. This is why you have passed your servant’s way. After that, you may continue on your way.”

“Yes,” they replied, “you may do as you have said.”

6 So Abraham hurried into the tent and said to Sarah, “Quick! Prepare three seahs of fine flour, knead it, and bake some bread.”

7 Meanwhile, Abraham ran to the herd, selected a tender and choice calf, and gave it to a servant, who hurried to prepare it. 8 Then Abraham brought curds and milk and the calf that had been prepared, and he set them before the men and stood by them under the tree as they ate.

What is the context?

The chapter opens by saying that the LORD appears to Abraham near the Oaks of Mamre. Abraham looks up and sees three men. As the scene unfolds, he welcomes them, offers water and rest, and prepares a meal. The opening tells us that more is happening than an ordinary visit, while the arrival is described in the familiar form of guests at a tent.

Abraham's welcome becomes generous action. He hurries to Sarah about bread, selects a good calf, has it prepared, and sets food before the visitors. He stands nearby while they eat. The repeated haste and the care given to the meal show his eagerness to serve, without requiring us to reconstruct customs the passage does not explain.

The meal prepares for the promise concerning Sarah and then the conversation about Sodom. In the wider scene, two of the visitors go toward Sodom while Abraham remains before the LORD; Genesis 19:1 calls the two who arrive there angels. These details are central to the different Christian explanations of the visitors.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Where do Christian readings differ? One long-standing reading sees the Son of God appearing with two angels. Justin Martyr, Dialogue with Trypho 56, explains the visitor at Mamre as the divine One sent by the Father, distinguished from the two angels who go to Sodom.

Another reading sees God revealing Himself through angelic visitors rather than one visitor being the Son Himself. Augustine, On the Trinity 2.10–12, considers the three visitors as a visible sign of the one God in three persons and compares Abraham's address with Lot's later address to the angels. These are historic Christian readings, rather than a division that every member of a modern denomination follows.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 13:1–3 calls believers to continue in love and welcome strangers, remembering that some have entertained angels without knowing it. Abraham's meal is a memorable example of that kind of welcome: care takes the form of time, food, effort, and a place to rest. Hospitality becomes part of living before God.

The theme also reaches into service to Christ. In Matthew 25:35–40, Jesus speaks of receiving food, drink, and welcome through what is done for the least of His brothers. His teaching gives acts of care a significance beyond their visible size. Abraham's generous service helps us picture how ordinary provision can become an expression of honor and love.

What is our position?

Our Who is The Angel of the Lord? study understands the LORD's visit to Abraham as an appearance of the eternal Son before His human birth. It includes the meal in Genesis 18 among the encounters that show the Son making Himself known before the New Testament account of His birth.
