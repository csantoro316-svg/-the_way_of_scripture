Passage

Titus 1:1-4

Why does Paul join truth, godliness and hope in his greeting?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Paul, a servant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ for the faith of God’s elect and their knowledge of the truth that leads to godliness, 2 in the hope of eternal life, which God, who cannot lie, promised before time began. 3 In His own time He has made His word evident in the proclamation entrusted to me by the command of God our Savior.

4 To Titus, my true child in our common faith: Grace and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior.

What is the context?

Paul introduces himself as God’s servant and an apostle of Jesus Christ. His work serves the faith of God’s people and their knowledge of a truth that leads to godly living. From the beginning, knowing the message and living by it belong together.

That life rests on hope in the God who cannot lie. God’s promise and His timing come before Paul’s entrusted work of proclamation. Paul speaks as someone responsible for a message he has received, rather than one he has invented.

He addresses Titus as his true child in their common faith. Their relationship and shared message stand behind the work Titus has been left to do in Crete, which the next paragraph explains.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Calvin read the greeting as tying the apostolic message to godliness and the hope of eternal life. God’s truthfulness makes that hope dependable; the message is meant to shape a faithful life.

They explain the promise’s timing differently. Wesley understands the promise before the world began as made to Christ, the Head of His people. Calvin considers that reading but favors God’s promise given to people in ancient times, including the promise to Abraham. These are named interpretations of the timing; the displayed BSB keeps its before time began wording.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 16:25–27 likewise joins God’s purpose hidden for long ages with its present proclamation to the nations, so that they may respond in faith. Paul’s entrusted message belongs within that wider work.

Later in this letter, Titus 2:11–14 explains how saving grace trains people to live godly lives while they await Christ’s appearing. The greeting introduces that connection between God’s saving promise, present conduct and future hope.
