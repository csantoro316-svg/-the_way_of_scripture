Passage

Revelation 17:7-14

What does the angel explain about the beast, the kings and the Lamb?

Berean Standard Bible

7 “Why are you so amazed?” said the angel. “I will tell you the mystery of the woman and of the beast that carries her, which has the seven heads and ten horns. 8 The beast that you saw—it was, and now is no more, but is about to come up out of the Abyss and go to its destruction. And those who dwell on the earth whose names were not written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world will marvel when they see the beast that was, and is not, and yet will be.

9 This calls for a mind with wisdom. The seven heads are seven mountains on which the woman sits. 10 There are also seven kings. Five have fallen, one is, and the other has not yet come. But when he does come, he must remain for only a little while. 11 The beast that was, and now is not, is an eighth king, who belongs to the other seven and is going into destruction.

12 The ten horns you saw are ten kings who have not yet received a kingdom, but will receive one hour of authority as kings along with the beast. 13 These kings have one purpose: to yield their power and authority to the beast. 14 They will make war against the Lamb, and the Lamb will triumph over them, because He is Lord of lords and King of kings; and He will be accompanied by His called and chosen and faithful ones.”

What is the context?

The angel begins the explanation with the beast. It was, is no more, will come from the Abyss and will go to destruction. The world marvels at its reappearance. Its apparent return to power should be read alongside the destination the angel names: destruction.

The seven heads are explained as seven mountains and also seven kings. Five have fallen, one is, and another is still to come for a short time. The beast is also an eighth king who belongs to the seven. The passage gives a sequence, but does not supply the rulers’ names.

The ten horns are ten kings who have not yet received a kingdom. They receive a brief period of authority with the beast and unite to give it their power. These kings are described together, in contrast to the sequence associated with the heads.

The alliance makes war against the Lamb, but the outcome is stated in advance. The Lamb will conquer because He is Lord of lords and King of kings. His companions are called, chosen and faithful. The explanation leads beyond the difficulty of identifying the rulers to the certainty of His victory.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The USCCB notes connect the seven mountains with Rome’s hills and the kings with Roman emperors, while acknowledging little agreement over the exact sequence. They connect the beast’s return with expectations about Nero returning to power. Wesley instead places the sequence within his account of papal power and its different periods.

Walvoord reads the heads in terms of successive imperial kingdoms leading to a future world empire. He favors the revival of imperial power over the personal return of a dead ruler.

The angel explicitly identifies the horns as kings and gives the alliance’s purpose and outcome. A proposed list of rulers should therefore be distinguished from those stated explanations. The difficulty of the list does not make the Lamb’s promised triumph uncertain.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The horns, kings and war against God’s people recall Daniel 7:21–27. Daniel sees hostile rule prevailing for a time, followed by God’s judgment and an everlasting kingdom. Revelation develops that same conflict and names the Lamb as the victorious ruler.

The title King of kings and Lord of lords also appears in 1 Timothy 6:15–16, in praise of God’s supreme sovereignty. Revelation applies that royal authority to the Lamb as the reason He conquers. The vision’s larger purpose is to assure His people that no coalition of earthly rulers can overturn His rule.
