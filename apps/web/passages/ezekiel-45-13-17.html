Passage

Ezekiel 45:13-17

What are the people and the prince each responsible to provide?

Berean Standard Bible

13 This is the contribution you are to offer: a sixth of an ephah from each homer of wheat, and a sixth of an ephah from each homer of barley. 14 The prescribed portion of oil, measured by the bath, is a tenth of a bath from each cor (a cor consists of ten baths or one homer, since ten baths are equivalent to a homer). 15 And one sheep shall be given from each flock of two hundred from the well-watered pastures of Israel. These are for the grain offerings, burnt offerings, and peace offerings, to make atonement for the people, declares the Lord GOD.

16 All the people of the land must participate in this contribution for the prince in Israel. 17 And it shall be the prince’s part to provide the burnt offerings, grain offerings, and drink offerings for the feasts, New Moons, and Sabbaths—for all the appointed feasts of the house of Israel. He will provide the sin offerings, grain offerings, burnt offerings, and peace offerings to make atonement for the house of Israel.

What is the context?

The people must supply measured portions of grain, oil and sheep for the offerings. The grain contribution is a sixth of an ephah from each homer of wheat or barley; one sheep is taken from each two hundred.

All the people participate in the contribution for the prince. In turn, he is responsible to provide the offerings for the feasts, New Moons, Sabbaths and other appointed occasions.

The collection therefore has an assigned purpose. It supports the offerings made for the house of Israel, rather than being described as wealth for the prince to spend without responsibility.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Cambridge explains the grain due as one-sixtieth and the prince’s obligation as providing the public sacrifices in return for the contributions. Henry emphasizes provision for the worship and ministers of God.

The passage gives responsibilities to both the people who contribute and the ruler who receives what is supplied.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Second Corinthians 8:12–21 describes a different kind of collection: the churches helping people in need. Paul takes care that the gift is administered honorably before the Lord and before people, and he considers what the givers can actually provide.

The amounts and purpose are different from Ezekiel’s prescribed offerings. The connection is the responsibility that accompanies shared resources. Gifts entrusted for God’s work and people’s needs call for generosity, clear purpose and trustworthy handling.
