Passage

Romans 14:5-9

How can different practices be offered to the same Lord?

Berean Standard Bible

5 One person regards a certain day above the others, while someone else considers every day alike. Each one should be fully convinced in his own mind. 6 He who observes a special day does so to the Lord; he who eats does so to the Lord, for he gives thanks to God; and he who abstains does so to the Lord and gives thanks to God.

7 For none of us lives to himself alone, and none of us dies to himself alone. 8 If we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord. So whether we live or die, we belong to the Lord. 9 For this reason Christ died and returned to life, that He might be the Lord of both the dead and the living.

What is the context?

Paul adds the example of days: one person gives a particular day special regard, while another regards them alike. He calls each person to be convinced in their own mind and describes both eating and abstaining as accompanied by thanksgiving to God.

The question reaches beyond the meal or calendar. Believers live and die belonging to the Lord. Paul grounds that belonging in Christ’s death and return to life, so that He is Lord of both the dead and the living. The practices are to express service to Him, rather than become a way of ruling one another.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly recognize the call to act for the Lord with gratitude and a clear conscience. They differ over how this relates to a weekly day of rest and worship. Wesley’s note on verse 5 identifies the disputed days as Jewish festivals.

An official Grace Communion International teaching article reads Romans 14:5–6 as allowing Christians to observe a day or treat it like others, without requiring a particular weekly Sabbath.

Reformed churches following the Westminster Confession, chapter 21, paragraphs 7–8, retain Sunday as the Christian Sabbath. The Catholic Church requires Sunday worship centered on the Eucharist (Catechism 2174–2177). These traditions do not understand freedom over disputed days as removing every obligation of weekly worship.

The Seventh-day Adventist Church instead teaches observance of the seventh-day Sabbath as a continuing command (Fundamental Belief 20). These differences should be represented in their own terms while retaining Paul’s direct instruction to serve the Lord and avoid contempt.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

2 Corinthians 5:14–15 gives a related reason for a changed life: Christ died and was raised so that those who live would live for Him. His saving work creates a new center for their decisions.

This helps explain the movement in Romans from food and days to life and death. God’s purpose is larger than getting everyone to adopt one person’s habits. Those who belong to the risen Lord are called to direct their lives toward Him, including the ordinary choices through which they give thanks.
