Passage

Ezekiel 45:9-12

What must replace dishonest and oppressive rule?

Berean Standard Bible

9 For this is what the Lord GOD says: ‘Enough, O princes of Israel! Cease your violence and oppression, and do what is just and right. Stop dispossessing My people, declares the Lord GOD.’ 10 You must use honest scales, a just ephah, and a just bath. 11 The ephah and the bath shall be the same quantity so that the bath will contain a tenth of a homer, and the ephah a tenth of a homer; the homer will be the standard measure for both. 12 The shekel will consist of twenty gerahs. Twenty shekels plus twenty-five shekels plus fifteen shekels will equal one mina.

What is the context?

The Lord directly commands Israel’s princes to stop violence, oppression and dispossessing His people. They are to do what is just and right.

Honest scales and dependable measures are part of that justice. The ephah and bath must each be a tenth of a homer, and the weights used for the shekel and mina must follow the stated standard.

This brings the vision into everyday dealings. Worship is not separated from the way leaders handle property, payments and the people entrusted to their care.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Cambridge connects the warning with unjustly expelling people from their possessions and with arbitrary demands made possible by unreliable standards. Henry reads just rule as part of the life pictured in the restored order.

The emphasis is straightforward: the Lord’s people must not be prey for those who hold authority over them.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Luke 22:24–30 shows Jesus correcting His disciples when they dispute who is greatest. He contrasts rulers who dominate others with leadership that serves, presenting Himself as the One among them who serves.

The connection reaches beyond honest accounting to the purpose of authority. In God’s kingdom, greatness is joined to service rather than using people for personal gain. Jesus’ own conduct gives His followers their pattern.
