Passage

Acts 16:1-5

Why is Timothy circumcised after the council’s decision?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Paul came to Derbe and then to Lystra, where he found a disciple named Timothy, the son of a believing Jewish woman and a Greek father. 2 The brothers in Lystra and Iconium spoke well of him. 3 Paul wanted Timothy to accompany him, so he took him and circumcised him on account of the Jews in that area, for they all knew that his father was a Greek. 4 As they went from town to town, they delivered the decisions handed down by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem for the people to obey. 5 So the churches were strengthened in the faith and grew daily in numbers.

What is the context?

Paul returns to Derbe and Lystra and finds Timothy, a disciple with a believing Jewish mother and a Greek father. Believers in Lystra and Iconium speak well of him. Paul wants him to join the work and circumcises him because the local Jews know his father was Greek.

This happens while the travelers are carrying the Jerusalem council’s decisions for the churches to obey. Luke places the two actions alongside one another: Timothy is prepared to accompany the mission, and the council’s judgment about Gentile believers is being delivered.

The churches become stronger in faith and increase in number. Timothy is joining work that includes teaching and strengthening existing communities as well as carrying the message farther.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly distinguish Timothy’s circumcision for the sake of the Jewish hearers from requiring circumcision for salvation. Calvin on verse 3 treats the action as avoiding an obstacle to the mission rather than restoring circumcision as a condition of being accepted by God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In 1 Corinthians 9:19–23, Paul explains his willingness to adapt for the sake of reaching people while remaining under Christ’s authority. Timothy’s preparation provides a concrete connection with that concern.

The contrast with Titus in Galatians 2:1–5 is useful: Titus was not compelled to be circumcised when the freedom of Gentile believers was at stake. A voluntary step for a particular mission is different from making that step a requirement of salvation.
