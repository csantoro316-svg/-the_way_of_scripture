Passage

Isaiah 11:1-16

What kind of King can bring justice, peace and a people together?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then a shoot will spring up from the stump of Jesse, and a Branch from his roots will bear fruit. 2 The Spirit of the LORD will rest on Him— the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and strength, the Spirit of knowledge and fear of the LORD. 3 And He will delight in the fear of the LORD. He will not judge by what His eyes see, and He will not decide by what His ears hear, 4 but with righteousness He will judge the poor, and with equity He will decide for the lowly of the earth. He will strike the earth with the rod of His mouth and slay the wicked with the breath of His lips. 5 Righteousness will be the belt around His hips, and faithfulness the sash around His waist.

6 The wolf will live with the lamb, and the leopard will lie down with the goat; the calf and young lion and fatling will be together, and a little child will lead them. 7 The cow will graze with the bear, their young will lie down together, and the lion will eat straw like the ox. 8 The infant will play by the cobra’s den, and the toddler will reach into the viper’s nest. 9 They will neither harm nor destroy on all My holy mountain, for the earth will be full of the knowledge of the LORD as the sea is full of water.

10 On that day the Root of Jesse will stand as a banner for the peoples. The nations will seek Him, and His place of rest will be glorious. 11 On that day the Lord will extend His hand a second time to recover the remnant of His people from Assyria, from Egypt, from Pathros, from Cush, from Elam, from Shinar, from Hamath, and from the islands of the sea. 12 He will raise a banner for the nations and gather the exiles of Israel; He will collect the scattered of Judah from the four corners of the earth. 13 Then the jealousy of Ephraim will depart, and the adversaries of Judah will be cut off. Ephraim will no longer envy Judah, nor will Judah harass Ephraim.

14 They will swoop down on the slopes of the Philistines to the west; together they will plunder the sons of the east. They will lay their hands on Edom and Moab, and the Ammonites will be subject to them. 15 The LORD will devote to destruction the gulf of the Sea of Egypt; with a scorching wind He will sweep His hand over the Euphrates. He will split it into seven streams for men to cross with dry sandals. 16 There will be a highway for the remnant of His people who remain from Assyria, as there was for Israel when they came up from the land of Egypt.

What is the context?

Chapter 10 ended with lofty trees being cut down. Now a new shoot grows from Jesse’s stump. Jesse was David’s father, so the picture returns to the family from which David’s kingship began. Where the previous scene showed proud power brought low, this one presents new life and a different kind of ruler.

The Hebrew word for Branch here is נֵצֶר, netser, pronounced NAY-tser. Brown–Driver–Briggs explains it as a sprout or shoot. The image is of life growing from roots that remain, rather than another imposing tree already standing at full height.

The Spirit of the Lord rests on this ruler, giving wisdom, understanding, counsel, strength, knowledge and reverence for God. He judges fairly for the poor and lowly instead of deciding by appearances or rumors. His word has power to carry out judgment; righteousness and faithfulness belong to the way He rules.

The peace is pictured through animals and children living without danger from one another. The explanation reaches beyond the animals: the earth will be full of the knowledge of the Lord. The Root of Jesse stands as a banner whom the nations seek.

The Lord also gathers the scattered of Israel and Judah. Their jealousy ends, and they stand together against their enemies. Obstacles to their return are removed, with a highway compared to Israel’s way out of Egypt. The chapter holds together righteous rule, the end of destructive hostility, and the gathering of God’s people.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry, Wesley and Gill identify the promised ruler as Christ. They connect the shoot from Jesse with the house of David brought low and renewed through Him. The NABRE notes likewise describe the hope for a new Davidic king who fulfills the ideals the historical kings failed to embody.

Readers differ over the animals living in peace. Gill understands them as pictures of people changed by grace: former persecutors become gentle toward those they once harmed. Henry also emphasizes changed human character while looking toward a fuller peace still to come. Irenaeus, in Against Heresies 5.33.4, accepts the connection with changed people but also expects the named animals themselves to live peacefully in a restored creation.

The gathering and victories also receive different explanations. Wesley reads the conquest language in terms of the Messiah’s spiritual victory through the spread of the gospel. The NABRE notes emphasize God restoring the scattered people to their land and ending the hostility between Israel and Judah. These readings place different emphasis on how the promised restoration takes shape; they agree that the passage looks toward God overcoming division and opposition.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 15:7–13 directly quotes Isaiah 11:10 about the Root of Jesse ruling the nations and becoming their hope. Paul uses the promise to explain why Jewish and Gentile believers should welcome one another: Christ confirms God’s promises and brings the nations into praise for His mercy.

The ruler’s powerful word also finds a close New Testament echo in 2 Thessalonians 2:8, where the Lord Jesus destroys the lawless one with the breath of His mouth. The promised King is not powerless before the wickedness He judges.

The peace of creation belongs beside the wider hope described in Romans 8:18–25: creation waits to be freed from decay as God brings His children into glory. Paul does not directly quote Isaiah’s animals there, but both passages place God’s saving purpose beyond private relief, reaching toward a creation freed from destruction.
