Passage

Ephesians 4:25-32

What does the new life look like between us?

Berean Standard Bible

25 Therefore each of you must put off falsehood and speak truthfully to his neighbor, for we are all members of one another. 26 “Be angry, yet do not sin.” Do not let the sun set upon your anger, 27 and do not give the devil a foothold. 28 He who has been stealing must steal no longer, but must work, doing good with his own hands, that he may have something to share with the one in need.

29 Let no unwholesome talk come out of your mouths, but only what is helpful for building up the one in need and bringing grace to those who listen. 30 And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, in whom you were sealed for the day of redemption. 31 Get rid of all bitterness, rage and anger, outcry and slander, along with every form of malice. 32 Be kind and tenderhearted to one another, forgiving each other just as in Christ God forgave you.

What is the context?

Paul now makes the call to put on the new self concrete. Falsehood gives way to truth because believers belong to one another. Anger must be kept from becoming sin or taking root, and the devil must be given no foothold.

The person who stole is to work honestly and become able to share with someone in need. Speech is to help the person who hears it. The Greek word behind unwholesome is sapros (sah-PROSS). Thayer’s lexicon describes what is rotten, poor in quality or unfit for use. Paul contrasts such speech with words that build someone up.

The warning against grieving the Holy Spirit stands within these instructions about relationships. Paul reminds the readers that they were sealed in Him for the day of redemption. Their conduct toward one another matters to the Spirit who marks them as God’s people.

The paragraph ends by replacing bitterness, rage, slander and malice with kindness, tenderness and forgiveness. God’s forgiveness in Christ supplies the pattern. This continues the chapter’s concern for a body whose members care for one another.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand these commands as practical expressions of life in Christ: honesty, generosity, helpful speech and forgiveness. Wesley’s notes on verses 25–32 and the USCCB notes connect the instructions with the church’s shared life. The warning about anger calls for prompt attention rather than allowing resentment to settle.

Christians understand grieving the Spirit as a serious warning about conduct that harms the life God gives His people. They differ over how the seal relates to the possibility of finally falling away.

The Reformed Westminster Confession, chapter 17, teaches that those truly called and sanctified will finally be saved, while recognizing that serious sin grieves the Spirit and damages their comfort and others’ lives. The Wesleyan Methodist Articles of Religion, article XII, teach that people who have received the Holy Spirit can depart from grace and fall into sin, and can rise again through God’s grace.

The Catholic Catechism, paragraphs 1272–1274, teaches that baptism’s spiritual mark cannot be erased, while also teaching that sin can prevent it from bearing the fruits of salvation. An enduring mark is therefore understood differently from an unconditional assurance of final salvation. These teachings frame the warning in distinct ways; all take the call to faithfulness seriously.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The day of redemption points forward. Romans 8:11, 23 connects the Spirit’s presence now with God’s future giving of life to mortal bodies and the redemption believers still await. The community is learning to live together in light of that promised future.

Matthew 5:14–16 also connects the good works of Jesus’ followers with others giving glory to their Father. This provides a supported parallel to the practical change described here: honesty, generosity and care make the Father’s character visible in the lives of His people.

What is our position?

On verse 30, our study Baptism teaches that sin grieves the Holy Spirit without driving Him out or cancelling the believer’s salvation. It distinguishes that secure standing from the harm sin does to fellowship with God. The study calls for honest confession and a renewed walk with Him.
