Passage

Luke 22:35-38

Why does Jesus warn His disciples to prepare for danger?

Berean Standard Bible

35 Then Jesus asked them, “When I sent you out without purse or bag or sandals, did you lack anything?” “Nothing,” they answered. 36 “Now, however,” He told them, “the one with a purse should take it, and likewise a bag; and the one without a sword should sell his cloak and buy one.

37 For I tell you that this Scripture must be fulfilled in Me: ‘And He was numbered with the transgressors.’ For what is written about Me is reaching its fulfillment.” 38 So they said, “Look, Lord, here are two swords.” “That is enough,” He answered.

What is the context?

Jesus asks the disciples to remember their earlier mission. When He sent them without purse, bag or sandals, they lacked nothing. He then warns of changed circumstances, speaking of taking supplies and buying a sword even at the cost of a cloak.

The reason He gives centers on His own coming suffering. Isaiah’s description of the Servant being counted among wrongdoers must be fulfilled in Him. His followers need to understand the danger ahead as their Teacher is treated as a criminal.

The disciples respond by pointing to two swords, and Jesus ends the exchange. Later in this same chapter, when a disciple uses a sword during His arrest, Jesus stops the violence and heals the injured man. The warning must be read alongside that action. (Luke 22:49–51)

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley both read the sword language as a forceful warning of approaching danger, rather than an instruction to spread Jesus’ message by armed force. Calvin emphasizes the severe trials ahead; Wesley says the saying is not a literal requirement for each disciple to obtain a weapon. The USCCB’s notes likewise explain the exchange as preparing the disciples for hostility and regard their reply about two swords as taking the image literally. These sources broadly agree on that point, so a separate denominational breakdown would overstate their differences. (Calvin on Luke 22:35–38; Wesley on Luke 22:35–38; USCCB notes on Luke 22:36, 38)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus Himself makes the connection with God’s announced purpose by applying Isaiah 53:12 to His coming suffering. In Isaiah, the Servant is counted with wrongdoers while bearing the sins of others and pleading for them. Being treated as a criminal does not mean Jesus has abandoned His mission; He identifies that rejection as part of what Scripture said He would face. (Isaiah 53:4–12)

Paul later uses the picture of armor and a sword to describe believers standing firm through truth, faith, God’s word and prayer. He also distinguishes the church’s struggle from warfare with ordinary weapons. This is a useful parallel for spiritual readiness, rather than proof that every object Jesus mentions here has a hidden meaning. His followers face opposition while depending on God to remain faithful. (Ephesians 6:10–20; 2 Corinthians 10:3–5)
