Passage

Hebrews 2:1-4

Why must we pay attention to the salvation we have heard?

Berean Standard Bible

1 We must pay closer attention, therefore, to what we have heard, so that we do not drift away. 2 For if the message spoken by angels was binding, and every transgression and disobedience received its just punishment, 3 how shall we escape if we neglect such a great salvation? This salvation was first announced by the Lord, was confirmed to us by those who heard Him, 4 and was affirmed by God through signs, wonders, various miracles, and gifts of the Holy Spirit distributed according to His will.

What is the context?

The opening therefore connects this warning with the Son’s greatness in chapter 1. Because of who He is, the message heard from Him deserves close attention. The writer includes himself in the warning: we must attend, so that we do not drift away.

The word translated drift away describes flowing or slipping past. It gives a picture of failing to hold to what has been heard. The warning concerns neglect as well as open rejection; simply hearing the message does not make attention unnecessary.

The earlier message delivered through angels carried consequences for disobedience. The greater salvation announced by the Lord cannot safely be treated as unimportant. Those who heard Him confirmed it to others, and God bore witness through signs, wonders, miracles and gifts of the Spirit distributed according to His will.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley both read this as a serious warning against neglecting the gospel. Christ’s standing makes the warning more urgent, and the witnesses and signs confirm the message rather than replacing it.

Christians differ over how such warnings relate to continuing in salvation. The Reformed Westminster Confession, chapter 17, teaches that those truly called and sanctified will finally persevere, although they can fall into serious sin. The Methodist Articles of Religion, Article XII, teach that people who have received the Holy Spirit may depart from grace, while allowing repentance and restoration. These are different accounts of the danger; neither makes neglect harmless.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Deuteronomy 18:15–19 promises a prophet to whom God’s people must listen. Acts 3:22–26 connects that promise with Jesus and the blessing God brings through Him. Hebrews’ call to listen belongs within that purpose of God making His salvation known.

Acts 1:8 describes witnesses carrying the message outward by the Spirit’s power. 1 Corinthians 12:4–11 likewise describes gifts distributed by the Spirit’s will. Together these passages show God providing both the message and the means for it to reach and serve people.
