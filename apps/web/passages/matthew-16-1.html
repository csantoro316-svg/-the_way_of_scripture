Passage

Matthew 16:1

Testing Jesus with a Request for a Sign

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then the Pharisees and Sadducees came and tested Jesus by asking Him to show them a sign from heaven.

What is the context?

This verse opens a new confrontation after the feeding of four thousand. Pharisees and Sadducees come together to ask Jesus for a sign from heaven. Matthew identifies their purpose as testing Him, so the request must be read within that challenge.

Jesus' reply in verses 2–4 exposes their failure to recognize what is already before them and points to the sign of Jonah. The earlier conversation in Matthew 12:38–42 explains that reference through Jonah's experience, the Son of Man's coming death, and the call to respond.

The surrounding Gospel has already described Jesus teaching, healing, and feeding crowds. The question is therefore more than whether He has done anything that calls for attention. It concerns what these challengers will accept and how they are responding to Him.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the request as a challenge to Jesus' authority, with the following refusal exposing the challengers' resistance. Wesley's notes on Matthew 16:1–4 and the US Catholic bishops' notes on 16:1–4 read it within the demand for proof and the failure to recognize the Messiah's work.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

This demand is answered by pointing toward the death and resurrection that will stand at the center of the gospel (Matthew 12:40; 1 Corinthians 15:1–8). The significance of Jesus' work will be made known through those events and their witnesses.

John 20:30–31 also explains why Jesus' signs are recorded: to lead people to believe who He is and have life in His name. The Bible therefore gives signs a purpose within God's work. The challenge here is refusing that testimony while requiring another sign before responding.

What is our position?

In Should We Seek Signs From God?, our discussion of this verse treats the demand for proof as an expression of unbelief in its immediate setting. The leaders are testing Jesus after His works have already given them reason to listen. The study calls us to receive what God has made known rather than build a habit of demanding further signs.
