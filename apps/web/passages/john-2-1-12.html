Passage

John 2:1-12

What does the first sign reveal about Jesus?

Berean Standard Bible

1 On the third day a wedding took place at Cana in Galilee. Jesus’ mother was there, 2 and Jesus and His disciples had also been invited to the wedding. 3 When the wine ran out, Jesus’ mother said to Him, “They have no more wine.” 4 “Woman, what is that to you and to Me?” Jesus replied. “My hour has not yet come.” 5 His mother said to the servants, “Do whatever He tells you.”

6 Now six stone water jars had been set there for the Jewish rites of purification. Each could hold from twenty to thirty gallons. 7 Jesus told the servants, “Fill the jars with water.” So they filled them to the brim. 8 “Now draw some out,” He said, “and take it to the master of the banquet.” They did so, 9 and the master of the banquet tasted the water that had been turned into wine. He did not know where it was from, but the servants who had drawn the water knew. Then he called the bridegroom aside 10 and said, “Everyone serves the fine wine first, and then the cheap wine after the guests are drunk. But you have saved the fine wine until now!”

11 Jesus performed this, the first of His signs, at Cana in Galilee. He thus revealed His glory, and His disciples believed in Him. 12 After this, He went down to Capernaum with His mother and brothers and His disciples, and they stayed there a few days.

What is the context?

Jesus, His disciples and His mother are at a wedding in Cana. When the wine runs out, His mother tells Him about the need. He replies that His hour has not yet come, and she tells the servants to do whatever He says.

Six stone jars are there for Jewish washing rites, each holding twenty to thirty gallons. Jesus tells the servants to fill them with water and take some to the master of the banquet. The water has become wine, and the master praises its quality without knowing where it came from.

The servants know what happened, and the Gospel identifies this as Jesus’ first sign: He reveals His glory and His disciples believe in Him. The account then follows Jesus, His family and His disciples to Capernaum for a short stay.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readers commonly understand the sign as revealing Jesus’ glory and strengthening His disciples’ faith. Readers differ over how to understand His exchange with His mother.

The Catholic Catechism presents Mary’s request as intercession offered in faith for the needs of the wedding feast. It also sees that feast as pointing toward the wedding of the Lamb. (Catechism §2618)

Wesley reads Jesus’ reply as a gentle correction of an untimely intervention. Calvin values Mary’s concern for others but thinks she goes beyond her proper place in trying to direct Jesus’ work. These are their interpretations of the exchange; both keep Jesus’ authority over His own work at the center. (Wesley on John 2:3–5; Calvin on John 2:4)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Isaiah pictures God’s salvation as a lavish feast for all peoples, with fine wine, death swallowed up and tears wiped away. Revelation pictures the joy of the marriage supper of the Lamb. The wedding and abundance at Cana form a supported parallel with this wider scriptural hope of joy in God’s saving work. The sign reveals the glory of the One through whom that hope is brought to fulfillment. (Isaiah 25:6–9; Revelation 19:6–9)

The mention of His hour also looks ahead within the Gospel. Jesus later announces that the hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified, and connects it with His approaching death and its fruit. The glory first revealed at Cana belongs within the larger story of the Son giving Himself so that others may have life. (John 12:23–32)
