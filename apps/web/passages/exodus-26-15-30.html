Passage

Exodus 26:15-30

What held the tabernacle upright and together?

Berean Standard Bible

15 You are to construct upright frames of acacia wood for the tabernacle. 16 Each frame is to be ten cubits long and a cubit and a half wide. 17 Two tenons must be connected to each other for each frame. Make all the frames of the tabernacle in this way. 18 Construct twenty frames for the south side of the tabernacle, 19 with forty silver bases under the twenty frames—two bases for each frame, one under each tenon. 20 For the second side of the tabernacle, the north side, make twenty frames 21 and forty silver bases—two bases under each frame.

22 Make six frames for the rear of the tabernacle, the west side, 23 and two frames for the two back corners of the tabernacle, 24 coupled together from bottom to top and fitted into a single ring. These will serve as the two corners. 25 So there are to be eight frames and sixteen silver bases—two under each frame.

26 You are also to make five crossbars of acacia wood for the frames on one side of the tabernacle, 27 five for those on the other side, and five for those on the rear side of the tabernacle, to the west. 28 The central crossbar in the middle of the frames shall extend from one end to the other. 29 Overlay the frames with gold and make gold rings to hold the crossbars. Also overlay the crossbars with gold. 30 So you are to set up the tabernacle according to the pattern shown you on the mountain.

What is the context?

The acacia frames provide the upright support for the coverings. Each has two projecting tenons—pieces that fit into the silver bases below. Twenty frames stand on each of the north and south sides. The rear is closed by six frames and two corner frames, with the corner connections joining the structure together.

Gold-covered crossbars pass through gold rings to hold the frames in place, with the central bar extending from end to end. The instructions combine separate supports into a stable structure that can be set up according to the pattern shown to Moses.

Exodus 38:25–28 later identifies the source of the silver: the offering from those counted in Israel. It provides one hundred bases for the sanctuary and inner veil. Exodus 30:12–16 calls that collection atonement money, with the same half-shekel amount required of rich and poor.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The frames, bases and bars are generally understood as the supporting structure for the tent. Gill specifically connects the silver bases with the census collection described in Exodus 38. Calvin stresses following God’s pattern and cautions that the two bases under each frame should not be assigned speculative identities, such as two covenants or two natures of Christ.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The use of the atonement collection for the sanctuary’s bases provides a comparison with the wider biblical themes of redemption and foundation. 1 Peter 1:17–21 explicitly says that believers are redeemed through Christ’s precious blood, rather than perishable silver or gold. The saving provision God gives through His Son reaches beyond a monetary offering.

1 Corinthians 3:10–15 also uses a building image, identifying Jesus Christ as the foundation and calling for care in how people build upon Him. These are broader biblical connections, not claims that Peter or Paul directly interprets each silver base. They direct attention to the one on whom God’s people depend and to the responsibility of the work built upon Him.
