Passage

1 Kings 3:6-15

Why does Solomon ask for an understanding heart?

Berean Standard Bible

6 Solomon replied, “You have shown much loving devotion to Your servant, my father David, because he walked before You in faithfulness, righteousness, and uprightness of heart. And You have maintained this loving devotion by giving him a son to sit on his throne this very day. 7 And now, O LORD my God, You have made Your servant king in my father David’s place. But I am only a little child, not knowing how to go out or come in. 8 Your servant is here among the people You have chosen, a people too numerous to count or number. 9 Therefore give Your servant an understanding heart to judge Your people and to discern between good and evil. For who is able to govern this great people of Yours?”

10 Now it pleased the Lord that Solomon had made this request. 11 So God said to him, “Since you have asked for this instead of requesting long life or wealth for yourself or death for your enemies—but you have asked for discernment to administer justice— 12 behold, I will do what you have asked. I will give you a wise and discerning heart, so that there will never have been another like you, nor will there ever be. 13 Moreover, I will give you what you did not request—both riches and honor—so that during all your days no man in any kingdom will be your equal. 14 So if you walk in My ways and keep My statutes and commandments, just as your father David did, I will prolong your days.” 15 Then Solomon awoke, and indeed it had been a dream. So he returned to Jerusalem, stood before the ark of the covenant of the Lord, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. Then he held a feast for all his servants.

What is the context?

Solomon begins by thanking God for the loving devotion shown to David and for placing David’s son on the throne. He then admits how unequal he feels to the task of governing so many people.

His request is for an understanding heart that can judge rightly. The Hebrew description draws on shama, pronounced shah-MAH, meaning to hear or listen. Brown–Driver–Briggs specifically lists this verse under attentive listening. Solomon needs a heart that listens so he can discern good and evil and serve the people well.

God is pleased that he asks for justice rather than wealth, long life or the death of enemies. He grants wisdom and adds riches and honor. The promise to prolong Solomon’s days is expressly connected with walking in God’s ways.

Solomon awakes, returns to Jerusalem and offers sacrifices before the ark. He then gives a feast for his servants.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Gill emphasizes the request’s practical setting: Solomon needs to hear cases, distinguish right from wrong and govern justly. His wisdom is to benefit the people entrusted to him.

Henry and Wesley draw attention to Solomon’s humility. Calling himself a little child expresses his sense of inexperience and dependence, rather than claiming that the work of a king is easy.

The dream is presented as an encounter in which God answers Solomon. The gifts are real, but receiving wisdom does not remove his responsibility to remain faithful. Verse 14 keeps that responsibility in view.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Solomon’s request belongs with the Bible’s wider invitation to seek wisdom from God. James 1:5–8 tells those who lack wisdom to ask the God who gives generously, and to ask in faith.

The connection concerns dependence on God for the wisdom needed to live and serve faithfully. Solomon asks for help with the responsibility before him; Christian readers are likewise invited to bring their need for wisdom to God.

James does not promise every believer Solomon’s wealth or position. The encouragement is to ask for wisdom rather than pretend we already have all we need.
