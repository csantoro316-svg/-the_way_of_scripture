Passage

Ezekiel 20:18-26

What does it mean that God gave them over to statutes that were not good?

Berean Standard Bible

18 In the wilderness I said to their children: ‘Do not walk in the statutes of your fathers or keep their ordinances or defile yourselves with their idols. 19 I am the LORD your God; walk in My statutes, keep My ordinances, and practice them. 20 Keep My Sabbaths holy, that they may be a sign between us, so that you may know that I am the LORD your God.’ 21 But the children rebelled against Me. They did not walk in My statutes or carefully observe My ordinances—though the man who does these things will live by them—and they profaned My Sabbaths. So I resolved to pour out My wrath upon them and vent My anger against them in the wilderness. 22 But I withheld My hand and acted for the sake of My name, so that it would not be profaned in the eyes of the nations in whose sight I had brought them out.

23 However, with an uplifted hand I swore to them in the wilderness that I would scatter them among the nations and disperse them throughout the lands. 24 For they did not practice My ordinances, but they rejected My statutes and profaned My Sabbaths, fixing their eyes on the idols of their fathers. 25 I also gave them over to statutes that were not good and ordinances by which they could not live. 26 And I pronounced them unclean through their gifts—the sacrifice of every firstborn in the fire—so that I might devastate them, in order that they would know that I am the LORD.

What is the context?

The Lord addresses the children in the wilderness and tells them not to repeat their fathers’ ways. They are to follow His statutes and keep His Sabbaths as a sign of belonging to Him.

They too rebel. Once again, He withholds the destruction He had resolved upon, for the sake of His name. But He also swears that they will be scattered among the nations because they reject His ways and fix their attention on inherited idols.

The BSB then says that He gave them over to statutes that were not good, followed by a charge concerning the sacrifice of firstborn children. This stands in sharp contrast with the life-giving ordinances described earlier.

The difficult statement must be read alongside Scripture’s explicit rejection of child sacrifice. Leviticus 18:21 prohibits giving children to Molech, and Jeremiah 7:30–34 says that burning children was something the Lord never commanded.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley understands the bad statutes as harmful practices God permitted the people to make for themselves after rejecting His good laws. Henry likewise explains judgment through people being given up to their own sinful desires, while denying that God is the author of sin.

The Cambridge discussion of verses 25–26 considers a misuse of the firstborn laws: the requirement concerning offerings was wrongly extended to children, although firstborn sons were to be redeemed. It reads the resulting delusion and destruction as judgment. The USCCB notes likewise connect the words with destructive child sacrifice and discuss the claim that the Lord accepted or required it.

These readings explain the relation between God’s judgment and the people’s practices differently. They do not make the earlier description of His life-giving laws disappear, and the explicit prohibitions prevent treating child sacrifice as an acceptable way to worship Him.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 7:37–43 recalls Israel receiving living words through Moses, refusing to obey and turning back toward Egypt in their hearts. Stephen says that God then gave them over to worship the host of heaven.

Acts is not explicitly quoting Ezekiel 20. It develops the same theme of rejected revelation and judgment through being handed over to the worship people choose. The history warns against confusing the possession of God’s word with a faithful response to it.
