Passage

1 Timothy 1:3-7

What should sound teaching produce?

Berean Standard Bible

3 As I urged you on my departure to Macedonia, you should stay on at Ephesus to instruct certain men not to teach false doctrines 4 or devote themselves to myths and endless genealogies, which promote speculation rather than the stewardship of God’s work, which is by faith.

5 The goal of our instruction is the love that comes from a pure heart, a clear conscience, and a sincere faith. 6 Some have strayed from these ways and turned aside to empty talk. 7 They want to be teachers of the law, but they do not understand what they are saying or that which they so confidently assert.

What is the context?

Paul reminds Timothy why he urged him to stay in Ephesus: certain people must stop teaching false doctrines and devoting themselves to myths and endless genealogies. These pursuits produce speculation rather than furthering God’s work by faith.

The goal Paul names is love flowing from a pure heart, a clear conscience and sincere faith. Teaching is meant to serve that life. Confidence and a wish to be recognized as a teacher do not replace understanding.

Some have turned from these qualities into empty talk. They want to teach the law but do not understand the matters about which they speak so confidently. Paul’s next paragraph explains that the problem is its misuse, rather than the law being bad.

The passage does not identify the full content of the myths or genealogies. It tells us their effect and gives a positive test of the instruction the community should receive.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a charge to protect the church from teaching that distracts from faith and love. Wesley’s notes on verses 3–7 contrast fruitful faith and love with empty religious disputes. The USCCB note likewise emphasizes the community’s growth in love. The warning does not condemn careful study simply because it involves difficult questions.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 3:8–12 describes God’s wisdom being made known through the church according to His eternal purpose in Christ. The community is meant to bear witness to that wisdom through its shared life.

Ephesians 4:11–16 connects teaching with a body growing toward maturity in Christ, stable in the truth and active in love. This is a supported parallel to Paul’s concern here: teaching serves God’s formation of a people who live together under His Son.
