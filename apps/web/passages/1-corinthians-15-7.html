Passage

1 Corinthians 15:7

Why is James named among the resurrection witnesses?

Berean Standard Bible

7 Then He appeared to James, then to all the apostles.

What is the context?

Paul names James within 1 Corinthians 15:5–11, a list of appearances supporting the gospel’s claim that Christ rose from the dead. James is mentioned individually before the reference to all the apostles.

The verse does not describe their meeting, identify its location or record a conversation. Its immediate purpose is to identify another witness. Elsewhere Paul names James as the Lord’s brother (Galatians 1:19); the related church-leadership study connects the James mentioned here with the leader seen in Jerusalem.

Questions about James’s later role should be kept distinct from what this sentence itself says. Paul is recounting an appearance, not recording the creation of a church office or a process for appointing a successor.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as one of the appearances testifying to Christ’s resurrection. The USCCB note on verses 3–7 places the named appearances alongside His death, burial and resurrection in the gospel summary. The verse itself leaves the details of the encounter unstated.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John 20:29–31 connects the testimony to the risen Jesus with faith among people who have not personally seen Him. The account is written so readers may believe in Him and have life in His name.

Naming witnesses such as James belongs within that wider purpose of making Christ known through testimony. Their experience serves people beyond those present at the appearance.

What is our position?

In Who Shepherds the Church?, we understand James’s standing as recognized personal authority likely connected with his witness to the risen Jesus and his service among other elders. We do not take this appearance as establishing a transferable office of institutional headship. The study distinguishes respect for a person’s maturity and knowledge from creating a title others must inherit.
