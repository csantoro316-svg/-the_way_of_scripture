Passage

Genesis 35:27-29

The Death of Isaac

Berean Standard Bible

27 Jacob returned to his father Isaac at Mamre, near Kiriath-arba (that is, Hebron), where Abraham and Isaac had stayed.

28 And Isaac lived 180 years. 29 Then he breathed his last and died and was gathered to his people, old and full of years. And his sons Esau and Jacob buried him.

What is the context?

Jacob comes to his father Isaac at Mamre near Hebron, where Abraham and Isaac had lived. The notice brings him back to his father's household after the long account of his departure, years with Laban, and return to Canaan. It does not give a complete record of every visit that may have happened along the way.

Isaac dies at 180, described as old and full of years, and Esau and Jacob bury him. Their shared action recalls Isaac and Ishmael burying Abraham in Genesis 25. The two brothers who once faced a threat of murder around their father's anticipated death now stand together at his burial.

This closes Isaac's family account before Genesis turns more fully toward Jacob's sons. The promises have passed to another generation, while Isaac's life ends in the land where he lived as an heir still awaiting the larger fulfillment.

How is this passage commonly understood?

A common Christian reading sees an honorable close to Isaac's life, with the brothers cooperating in his burial and the family promise continuing. Wesley's notes on Genesis 35:27–29 emphasize that peaceful ending after the earlier threat and estrangement.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Isaac's death does not mark the end of his relationship with God. When Jesus teaches about resurrection in Luke 20:37–38, He recalls God's identification as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and says that God is the God of the living.

Jesus is explaining the burning-bush passage, not commenting directly on this funeral. His teaching nevertheless gives the wider hope in which Isaac's life can be read. The promise extends beyond the years one patriarch lives to see, and the God who has called His people remains their God beyond death.
