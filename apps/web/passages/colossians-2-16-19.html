Passage

Colossians 2:16-19

Why does Paul point from the shadow to Christ?

Berean Standard Bible

16 Therefore let no one judge you by what you eat or drink, or with regard to a feast, a New Moon, or a Sabbath. 17 These are a shadow of the things to come, but the body that casts it belongs to Christ.

18 Do not let anyone who delights in false humility and the worship of angels disqualify you with speculation about what he has seen. Such a person is puffed up without basis by his unspiritual mind. 19 He has lost connection to the head, from whom the whole body, supported and knit together by its joints and ligaments, grows as God causes it to grow.

What is the context?

Because of what God has done in Christ, Paul tells the believers not to let others judge them over food, drink or religious dates. He calls these a shadow and identifies Christ as the reality to which the shadow belongs.

He also warns about false humility, angel worship and claims about visions. The person he describes is proud and disconnected from the head, Christ. The details of the teachers’ practices are not fully explained.

The alternative is a body nourished and held together through its connection with Christ. Growth comes from God. The warning protects that relationship from demands that displace the One who gives the church life.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the shadow-and-reality comparison as directing attention to Christ. They differ over what this means for continuing Sabbath observance.

Wesley’s note on verses 16–17 includes the weekly Jewish Sabbaths among the ceremonial observances Paul names. In the Reformed tradition, the Westminster Confession, 21.7–8, nevertheless teaches an enduring one-day-in-seven command, observed on Sunday as the Christian Sabbath.

The Catholic Catechism, paragraphs 2175–2176, distinguishes Sunday from the Sabbath and describes Sunday worship as fulfilling its spiritual truth and moral call to worship.

The Seventh-day Adventist Church’s Fundamental Belief 20 affirms continued seventh-day Sabbath observance. A Biblical Research Institute explanation reads the Sabbaths in this passage as annual ceremonial rest days, rather than the weekly Sabbath. That is a named reading within the tradition; the official belief statement itself does not give this detailed interpretation of Colossians.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 10:1–14 also uses the picture of a shadow for earlier worship, then points to Christ’s once-for-all offering. It is a supported parallel in how earlier patterns direct attention to His accomplished work; it does not by itself settle every dispute about the calendar.

Ephesians 4:15–16 describes the church growing from Christ its head as every part contributes in love. The purpose goes beyond freedom from another person’s judgment: God is forming a people whose growth comes from their union with His Son.
