Passage

Revelation 5:11-14

Why does all creation join in praising the Lamb?

Berean Standard Bible

11 Then I looked, and I heard the voices of many angels encircling the throne, and the living creatures and the elders. And their number was myriads of myriads and thousands of thousands. 12 In a loud voice they were saying: “Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and riches and wisdom and strength and honor and glory and blessing!”

13 And I heard every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and in the sea, and all that is in them, saying: “To Him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be praise and honor and glory and power forever and ever!” 14 And the four living creatures said, “Amen,” and the elders fell down and worshiped.

What is the context?

The circle of praise widens. John hears a vast company of angels surrounding the throne, the living creatures and the elders. Their praise centers on the Lamb who was slain. Power, riches, wisdom, strength, honor, glory and blessing are declared His due.

The wording recalls the great heavenly company before God’s court in Daniel 7:9–10. John then hears praise from every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth and in the sea. The vision brings the whole created order into the acknowledgment of God and the Lamb.

The One seated on the throne and the Lamb receive praise together. The creatures answer Amen, and the elders fall down in worship. This closes the chapter’s movement from John’s grief over the sealed scroll to the shared celebration of the One worthy to open it. The first seal is opened in the next chapter.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill both understand the shared praise as honoring the Lamb together with God. They include the acknowledgment of Christ by His enemies within the universal scope of the scene, while distinguishing that from the willing joy of His people. The passage’s central emphasis is His worthiness and the breadth of the praise He receives.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Philippians 2:9–11 looks toward every knee bowing and every tongue acknowledging Jesus Christ as Lord, to the Father’s glory. Revelation’s widening song gives a corresponding picture of that universal acknowledgment.

The contrast within the chapter is striking: no one else can open the scroll, yet the Lamb who was slain receives the worship of creation. His death is not a detour from His victory. It is the reason the song gives for His worthiness. God’s purpose moves forward through the crucified and risen Christ, and the vision places the coming events beneath His authority.
