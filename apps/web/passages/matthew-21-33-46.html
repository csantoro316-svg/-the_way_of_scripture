Passage

Matthew 21:33-46

The Rejected Son and the Cornerstone

Berean Standard Bible

33 Listen to another parable: There was a landowner who planted a vineyard. He put a wall around it, dug a winepress in it, and built a tower. Then he rented it out to some tenants and went away on a journey. 34 When the harvest time drew near, he sent his servants to the tenants to collect his share of the fruit. 35 But the tenants seized his servants. They beat one, killed another, and stoned a third. 36 Again, he sent other servants, more than the first group. But the tenants did the same to them. 37 Finally, he sent his son to them. ‘They will respect my son,’ he said. 38 But when the tenants saw the son, they said to one another, ‘This is the heir. Come, let us kill him and take his inheritance.’ 39 So they seized him and threw him out of the vineyard and killed him.

40 Therefore, when the owner of the vineyard returns, what will he do to those tenants?” 41 “He will bring those wretches to a wretched end,” they replied, “and will rent out the vineyard to other tenants who will give him his share of the fruit at harvest time.” 42 Jesus said to them, “Have you never read in the Scriptures: ‘The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone. This is from the Lord, and it is marvelous in our eyes’?

43 Therefore I tell you that the kingdom of God will be taken away from you and given to a people who will produce its fruit. 44 He who falls on this stone will be broken to pieces, but he on whom it falls will be crushed.” 45 When the chief priests and Pharisees heard His parables, they knew that Jesus was speaking about them. 46 Although they wanted to arrest Him, they were afraid of the crowds, because the people regarded Him as a prophet.

What is the context?

Jesus tells the leaders another vineyard story. Its planting, wall, winepress, and tower recall Isaiah 5:1–7, where Israel is God's vineyard and He seeks justice and righteousness. Here the owner entrusts the vineyard to tenants and sends servants to collect his share of the fruit.

The tenants attack the servants and finally kill the owner's son, hoping to take his inheritance. Jesus asks what the owner will do, and His hearers answer that he will judge the tenants and entrust the vineyard to others who give him its fruit.

Jesus then quotes Psalm 118:22–23: the rejected stone becomes the cornerstone through the Lord's doing. The warning about rejecting the son is followed by a promise that rejection will not have the last word. God gives the rejected stone its place.

The people who receive the kingdom are described by their fruit. People is ἔθνος, ethnos (ETH-noss), a people or nation. Thayer's entry lists uses for the Jewish people as well as other nations. Here the emphasis is on a people producing what God seeks.

The stone also carries a warning of judgment, with both falling on it and its falling on someone described as destructive. By the end, the chief priests and Pharisees understand that Jesus is speaking about them. They want to arrest Him but fear the crowds.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the owner as God, the servants as His messengers, and the son as Jesus, with judgment falling on those who reject Him. Wesley on 21:33–44 connects the cornerstone with the church gathered around Christ, while the US Catholic bishops' note on 21:43 identifies the fruit-bearing people as believing Israelites and Gentiles.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The New Testament directly identifies the rejected cornerstone with Jesus. In Acts 4:10–12, Peter proclaims His resurrection, applies the same psalm to Him, and announces salvation in His name. The rejection described in the parable leads into the saving work God brings through His risen Son.

1 Peter 2:4–10 develops the building picture further. Those who come to Christ, the living stone, are themselves built into a spiritual house and become a people who proclaim God's goodness. Ephesians 2:19–22 likewise places Christ at the foundation of God's household as its cornerstone. God gathers His people around the One others rejected, so that their shared life bears witness to Him.
