Passage

1 Samuel 25:1

How does Israel respond to Samuel’s death?

Berean Standard Bible

1 When Samuel died, all Israel gathered to mourn for him; and they buried him at his home in Ramah. Then David set out and went down to the Wilderness of Paran.

What is the context?

Samuel dies, and Israel gathers to mourn and bury him at his home in Ramah. The man who served through the end of Eli’s household, the appointment of Saul and the anointing of David is gone. His death marks a major loss in the middle of the unresolved conflict between Saul and David.

David then moves into the Wilderness of Paran. The verse places national mourning beside the continued movement of the fugitive king. It does not say whether David attended the burial or explain his precise reason for leaving.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Henry emphasize Israel’s reason to grieve for someone who had prayed for them and taught them God’s way. Their reading treats the mourning as recognition of faithful service, rather than a passing notice between adventures.

The brief report also closes Samuel’s personal part in the story while leaving the outcome of his work still unfolding. David has been anointed, but he has not yet become king.
