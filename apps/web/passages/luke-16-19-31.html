Passage

Luke 16:19-31

What should the rich man and his brothers have heard already?

Berean Standard Bible

19 Now there was a rich man dressed in purple and fine linen, who lived each day in joyous splendor. 20 And a beggar named Lazarus lay at his gate, covered with sores 21 and longing to be fed with the crumbs that fell from the rich man’s table. Even the dogs came and licked his sores.

22 One day the beggar died and was carried by the angels to Abraham’s side. And the rich man also died and was buried. 23 In Hades, where he was in torment, he looked up and saw Abraham from afar, with Lazarus by his side. 24 So he cried out, ‘Father Abraham, have mercy on me and send Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue. For I am in agony in this fire.’ 25 But Abraham answered, ‘Child, remember that during your lifetime you received your good things, while Lazarus received bad things. But now he is comforted here, while you are in agony. 26 And besides all this, a great chasm has been fixed between us and you, so that even those who wish cannot cross from here to you, nor can anyone cross from there to us.’

27 ‘Then I beg you, father,’ he said, ‘send Lazarus to my father’s house, 28 for I have five brothers. Let him warn them, so that they will not also end up in this place of torment.’ 29 But Abraham replied, ‘They have Moses and the Prophets; let your brothers listen to them.’ 30 ‘No, father Abraham,’ he said, ‘but if someone is sent to them from the dead, they will repent.’ 31 Then Abraham said to him, ‘If they do not listen to Moses and the Prophets, they will not be persuaded even if someone rises from the dead.’”

What is the context?

Jesus describes a rich man living in daily splendor while Lazarus lies hungry and covered with sores at his gate. Their closeness makes the contrast immediate: abundance is on one side of the gate, need on the other.

Both die, and their conditions are reversed. Lazarus is comforted at Abraham’s side, while the rich man is in torment and asks for relief. Abraham describes the reversal and the fixed chasm that cannot be crossed.

The BSB calls the place of the rich man’s torment Hades. The Greek word is Ἅδης, Hadēs (HAY-deez), which Thayer describes as the realm of the dead. The story presents the rich man’s suffering and Lazarus’ comfort without giving a complete description of every question about life after death. (Thayer, Greek 86)

The rich man then asks that Lazarus be sent to warn his five brothers. Abraham directs them to Moses and the Prophets. When the man insists that someone returning from the dead would bring repentance, Abraham says that those who refuse that existing witness will not be persuaded even by a resurrection.

The ending brings the warning back to people still living. The opportunity before the brothers is to hear God’s word and respond while they can.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly recognize a warning about neglected need, the use of wealth and judgment, together with the call to listen to God’s word. The Catholic bishops’ notes emphasize the reversal of the men’s conditions and the ending’s connection with rejection even after resurrection; Wesley connects the rich man’s fate with making worldly goods his happiness and stresses the power of God’s word to bring repentance. (USCCB notes on Luke 16:19–31; Wesley on Luke 16:25–31)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The Law already commands an open hand toward the poor rather than a hardened heart. James later connects living faith with meeting a brother’s or sister’s need for food and clothing. The care missing at the rich man’s gate belongs within a Scriptural call for God’s people to make mercy visible in their common life. (Deuteronomy 15:7–11; James 2:14–17)

John records a resurrection sign that leads many to believe, while some leaders respond by plotting Jesus’ death. That is a concrete parallel to the warning that an extraordinary sign does not guarantee a willing response from every person who receives its witness. (John 11:43–53)

At Luke’s conclusion, the risen Jesus explains what Moses and the Prophets say about Him and commissions the proclamation of repentance and forgiveness. The witness Abraham tells the brothers to hear belongs within the larger revelation fulfilled in Christ and carried to the nations. (Luke 24:25–27, 44–47)
