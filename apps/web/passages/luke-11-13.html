Passage

Luke 11:13

What gift does Jesus name when He speaks of the Father’s goodness?

Berean Standard Bible

13 So if you who are evil know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!”

What is the context?

This verse concludes Jesus’ teaching about prayer in Luke 11:1–13. After giving the disciples a prayer and the midnight-friend story, Jesus compares the Father’s giving with the ordinary care of earthly parents.

Even sinful people can recognize a child’s need and give something good. Jesus directs the disciples’ confidence toward their Father in heaven, whose goodness is greater, and identifies the Holy Spirit as His gift.

The verse is best read with the whole exchange, including the call to ask, seek and knock. Read Luke 11:5–13.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly recognize the Father’s generous care and the importance of the Holy Spirit as His gift to those who ask. Wesley emphasizes the greatness of that gift, and the Catholic bishops’ note likewise places the gift of the Spirit at the conclusion of Jesus’ teaching on prayer. (Wesley on Luke 11:13; USCCB note on Luke 11:13)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Galatians describes the Father sending His Son to redeem and then sending the Spirit of His Son into the hearts of His children. Romans likewise connects the Spirit with belonging to God’s family. The gift named here is part of God bringing people into a life of relationship with Him through Christ. (Galatians 4:4–7; Romans 8:14–17)

What is our position?

Prayer and Faith and What Does Prayer Look Like? use this verse to emphasize what Jesus names as the Father’s gift: the Holy Spirit. They understand mature prayer as seeking God’s purposes, His presence and His work of forming us, with trust in His goodness governing what we ask.
