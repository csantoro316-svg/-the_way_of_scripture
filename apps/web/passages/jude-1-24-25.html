Passage

Jude 1:24-25

Who can bring God’s people into His presence with joy?

Berean Standard Bible

24 Now to Him who is able to keep you from stumbling and to present you unblemished in His glorious presence, with great joy— 25 to the only God our Savior be glory, majesty, dominion, and authority through Jesus Christ our Lord before all time, and now, and for all eternity. Amen.

What is the context?

The letter ends in praise to the God who is able to keep His people from stumbling and present them unblemished in His glorious presence. After urging the readers to guard themselves and help others, Jude directs their confidence toward God’s power.

The praise is offered to the only God our Savior through Jesus Christ our Lord. Glory, majesty, dominion and authority belong to Him before all time, now and forever. The closing gathers the letter’s warnings and instructions into worshipful dependence on God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin emphasizes that the readers’ efforts depend on God’s power working with them. Wesley likewise identifies God as the one able to keep people from the errors and sins described and present them faultless. The USCCB note recognizes the return to the opening themes of divine care and preservation. These comments share the passage’s emphasis on God’s keeping power, without treating the call to vigilance earlier in the letter as unnecessary.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 5:25–27 describes Christ loving the church and giving Himself for her so that He may present her holy and without blemish. That passage gives a wider setting for Jude’s closing hope. God’s work reaches toward a people brought into His presence, with Christ’s self-giving love at the heart of that purpose.

What is our position?

Our studies Prayer and Faith and What Does Prayer Look Like? place trust in God at the center of prayer. The latter highlights Jesus’ prayer that the Father would keep His people and sanctify them in the truth. We read Jude’s closing praise in that same dependence: confidence rests in the God who keeps and brings His people into His presence.
