Passage

1 Kings 2:10-12

What does David’s death mean for the promise to his house?

Berean Standard Bible

10 Then David rested with his fathers and was buried in the City of David. 11 The length of David’s reign over Israel was forty years—seven years in Hebron and thirty-three years in Jerusalem. 12 So Solomon sat on the throne of his father David, and his kingdom was firmly established.

What is the context?

David dies and is buried in the City of David. The account summarizes his forty-year reign: seven years in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem.

Solomon sits on his father’s throne, and his kingdom is described as firmly established. David’s life ends, but the story of the house God promised him continues.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley, in his comments on David’s approaching death, emphasizes that even kings and prophets share humanity’s mortality. Their position does not exempt them from death.

The short notice closes one reign and opens another. It asks the reader to hold together the death of the king and the continuation of his line.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The New Testament expressly draws attention to David’s death and burial. In Acts 13:32–39, Paul says that David served God’s purpose in his generation, died and underwent decay.

He contrasts David with Jesus, whom God raised from the dead and who did not undergo decay. Paul connects that resurrection with God’s promises and the forgiveness proclaimed through Jesus.

The lasting hope therefore reaches beyond a succession of kings who eventually die. David’s son Solomon continues the line; the risen Jesus brings the promised salvation that death cannot end.
