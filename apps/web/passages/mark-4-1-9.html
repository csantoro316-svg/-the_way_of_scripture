Passage

Mark 4:1-9

One Sowing, Four Kinds of Ground

Berean Standard Bible

1 Once again Jesus began to teach beside the sea, and such a large crowd gathered around Him that He got into a boat and sat in it, while all the people crowded along the shore. 2 And He taught them many things in parables, and in His teaching He said, 3 “Listen! A farmer went out to sow his seed. 4 And as he was sowing, some seed fell along the path, and the birds came and devoured it.

5 Some fell on rocky ground, where it did not have much soil. It sprang up quickly because the soil was shallow. 6 But when the sun rose, the seedlings were scorched, and they withered because they had no root. 7 Other seed fell among thorns, which grew up and choked the seedlings, and they yielded no crop.

8 Still other seed fell on good soil, where it sprouted, grew up, and produced a crop—one bearing thirtyfold, another sixtyfold, and another a hundredfold.” 9 Then Jesus said, “He who has ears to hear, let him hear.”

What is the context?

Jesus teaches beside the sea again. This time Mark says He gets into a boat and sits while the crowd stays on shore. He tells a story about seed scattered on four kinds of ground: the path, rocky ground, thorny ground, and good soil.

The outcomes differ. Birds eat the seed on the path. Seed in shallow soil springs up quickly but withers in the sun because it has no root. Thorns choke other seedlings so they produce no crop. The good soil receives seed that grows and produces a harvest, though the amounts vary.

Jesus begins by calling the crowd to listen and ends by calling those with ears to hear. The story asks for more than noticing familiar pictures. His own explanation in Mark 4:13–20 identifies the seed with the word and the different ground with ways people respond to it. His explanation gives us the starting point for understanding it.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly see the different soils as different responses to God's word, with lasting fruitfulness as the good outcome. The US Catholic bishops' note on Mark 4:1–34 and Calvin on the parallel Matthew 13:1–9 both follow the story into Jesus' own explanation.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The word-and-growth picture has an earlier parallel in Isaiah 55:10–13. Rain waters the earth and brings seed and food; God's word accomplishes the purpose for which He sends it. This is a scriptural parallel, rather than a statement that Isaiah describes these four soils or promises that every individual hearer responds alike.

The message is also meant to be shared. Romans 10:14–17 connects sending, preaching, hearing, and faith, while acknowledging that not everyone welcomes the good news. Jesus' story prepares His followers to understand both the importance of spreading the word and the different responses it meets.
