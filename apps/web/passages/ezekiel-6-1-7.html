Passage

Ezekiel 6:1-7

Can the high places protect those who worship there?

Berean Standard Bible

1 And the word of the LORD came to me, saying, 2 “Son of man, set your face against the mountains of Israel and prophesy against them. 3 You are to say: ‘O mountains of Israel, hear the word of the Lord GOD! This is what the Lord GOD says to the mountains and hills, to the ravines and valleys: I am about to bring a sword against you, and I will destroy your high places.

4 Your altars will be demolished and your incense altars will be smashed; and I will cast down your slain before your idols. 5 I will lay the corpses of the Israelites before their idols and scatter your bones around your altars. 6 Wherever you live, the cities will be laid waste and the high places will be demolished, so that your altars will be laid waste and desecrated, your idols smashed and obliterated, your incense altars cut down, and your works blotted out. 7 The slain will fall among you, and you will know that I am the LORD.

What is the context?

Ezekiel must turn toward Israel’s mountains and address the mountains, hills, ravines and valleys. The warning reaches across the land because the places of false worship are spread through it.

The Hebrew word בָּמָה, bamah (bah-MAH), means high place. Brown–Driver–Briggs explains its use for worship sites on hills and also on built platforms. Here the surrounding references to idols, altars and incense make the false worship at these sites the focus.

The Lord will bring the sword, demolish the altars and smash the idols. The bodies of worshipers will lie before the objects they trusted, and their bones will be scattered around the altars.

The destruction of cities and shrines ends with the declaration that the people will know He is the Lord. The idols cannot secure their own survival or the safety of their worshipers.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley emphasize the failure of false worship as a source of safety. Wesley connects the bodies before the altars with the exposure of the idols’ inability to help.

Henry points out that God can bring down idolatrous worship even through people who practice idolatry themselves. The passage’s outcome concerns His judgment and the failure of the false gods, rather than the religious faithfulness of the conquering power.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

First Corinthians 10:14–22 carries the call to undivided worship into the life of believers in Christ. Paul urges them to flee idolatry and explains that sharing the Lord’s cup and table cannot be joined with participation in idol worship.

Paul does not quote Ezekiel 6 there. The shared theme is allegiance to the living God, now explained through participation in Christ and the unity of His body. The Lord’s people are called to honor that relationship in their worship and conduct.
