Passage

Mark 8:1-10

Jesus Feeds the Four Thousand

Berean Standard Bible

1 In those days the crowd once again became very large, and they had nothing to eat. Jesus called the disciples to Him and said, 2 “I have compassion for this crowd, because they have already been with Me three days and have nothing to eat. 3 If I send them home hungry, they will faint along the way. For some of them have come a great distance.” 4 His disciples replied, “Where in this desolate place could anyone find enough bread to feed all these people?” 5 “How many loaves do you have?” Jesus asked. “Seven,” they replied.

6 And He instructed the crowd to sit down on the ground. Then He took the seven loaves, gave thanks and broke them, and gave them to His disciples to set before the people. And they distributed them to the crowd. 7 They also had a few small fish, and Jesus blessed them and ordered that these be set before them as well. 8 The people ate and were satisfied, and the disciples picked up seven basketfuls of broken pieces that were left over. 9 And about four thousand men were present. And when Jesus had dismissed the crowd, 10 He immediately got into the boat with His disciples and went to the district of Dalmanutha.

What is the context?

Another large crowd has been with Jesus for three days and has nothing to eat. He takes the initiative, telling the disciples that He has compassion for them and does not want them to collapse on their way home. Some have traveled a great distance.

The disciples ask where enough food could be found in this desolate place. Jesus asks how many loaves they have: seven. He gives thanks, breaks the bread, and has the disciples distribute it, along with a few small fish. About four thousand men eat and are satisfied, with seven baskets of leftovers.

This follows Jesus' travel through the Decapolis in Mark 7:31. That setting helps explain why readers connect the meal with His help reaching beyond His own people, although Mark does not identify every person in the crowd as Gentile.

The basket word here is σπυρίς (spyris, spoo-REES), a woven basket or hamper. Mark uses a different basket word in the earlier feeding. The change is a real detail in the accounts, but the word itself does not establish the crowd's ethnicity or explain the meaning of the number seven.

Mark presents this separately from the feeding of the five thousand. Jesus later recalls both meals and their different quantities in Mark 8:19–20. After dismissing this crowd, He and the disciples travel by boat to Dalmanutha.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly see Jesus' compassion, His power to provide, and the disciples' continuing need to depend on Him. Wesley on Mark 8:8 also connects the provision of bread with the life people receive from Christ.

Commentators differ over how the two feeding accounts arose. Calvin's discussion of the second feeding treats it as a further miracle; the USCCB note on Mark 8:1–10 reports the view that the accounts developed from two traditions about one event. That is a discussion of the accounts' history in an explanatory note, not a binding Catholic ruling on how many feedings occurred.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The generous meal fits a wider picture of God's provision and welcome. Isaiah 25:6–9 describes a banquet for all peoples and God's victory over death and tears. Jesus later speaks of people coming from every direction to the table in God's kingdom (Luke 13:28–30). These provide a supported parallel to a meal that meets the needs of a crowd beyond Jesus' usual Galilean setting; Mark does not declare every detail of Isaiah's vision completed here.

The broader purpose is stated plainly in Romans 15:7–13: Christ confirms God's promises to Israel, and Gentiles glorify God for mercy. Reading this meal after the Syrophoenician woman's request and the Decapolis healing helps us follow that growing picture of His generosity. His mission reaches people the disciples must learn to welcome and serve.
