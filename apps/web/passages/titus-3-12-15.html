Passage

Titus 3:12-15

How can the church put good works into practice?

Berean Standard Bible

12 As soon as I send Artemas or Tychicus to you, make every effort to come to me at Nicopolis, because I have decided to winter there. 13 Do your best to equip Zenas the lawyer and Apollos, so that they will have everything they need.

14 And our people must also learn to devote themselves to good works in order to meet the pressing needs of others, so that they will not be unfruitful. 15 All who are with me send you greetings. Greet those who love us in the faith. Grace be with all of you.

What is the context?

Paul closes with practical arrangements. When Artemas or Tychicus is sent, Titus is to come to him at Nicopolis, where Paul intends to spend the winter. He is also to equip Zenas and Apollos for their journey so that they have what they need.

The request becomes an instruction for the community as a whole: learn to devote themselves to good works that meet pressing needs. Fruitfulness takes a concrete form in providing help, rather than remaining an idea discussed in the gathering.

The letter ends with greetings from those with Paul, love shared in the faith and grace for them all. The teaching about God’s saving kindness has led back to a people learning to show kindness in practice.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin reads the request as an opportunity for believers to exercise generosity instead of remaining unfruitful. Wesley likewise connects their learning with Titus’s instruction and example and the need to help Zenas and Apollos. The practical provision is part of living out the teaching.

Wesley understands Artemas or Tychicus as taking over Titus’s work while he visits Paul. The passage names the alternatives but does not say which one was ultimately sent.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Philippians 4:14–20 describes practical giving as partnership in gospel work and fruit in the givers’ lives. The help requested here belongs within that same pattern of people using their resources to support others in the mission.

Ephesians 4:28 also connects honest work with having something to share with someone in need. God’s renewed people learn a different use for their resources: meeting needs becomes part of the life they share in Christ.
