Passage

2 Peter 1:20-21

Where does Scripture’s authority come from?

Berean Standard Bible

20 Above all, you must understand that no prophecy of Scripture comes from one’s own interpretation. 21 For no such prophecy was ever brought forth by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.

What is the context?

These verses explain why the prophetic word deserves attention. Prophecy was not produced by human will; people spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.

The word for interpretation concerns explanation. The contrast in the next verse helps explain the emphasis: the message is not simply the speaker’s own account of what God might mean. Its source is God’s Spirit.

The carried-along language describes the Spirit’s direction while people genuinely speak. Peter’s concern is confidence in that message within the larger call to attend to God’s word.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley understand the main point as divine origin, rather than a blanket prohibition on individuals reading Scripture. Calvin prefers the sense that prophecy did not arise from a person’s own suggestions; Wesley explains it as God making known what had been unknown.

The Catholic Catechism, 105–106, and the Reformed Westminster Confession, 1.4, both affirm God as Scripture’s author. Agreement on that point does not mean every tradition has the same account of how disputed passages should be interpreted.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Luke 24:44–49 shows Jesus opening the Scriptures in relation to His death, resurrection and the announcement of forgiveness to all nations. God’s speaking through the prophets leads toward that saving message.

2 Timothy 3:14–17 also connects Scripture with salvation through faith in Christ and a life equipped for good work. Its divine origin serves God’s purpose of bringing people to Christ and shaping their lives.

What is our position?

Our study Is the Bible the Inerrant Word of God? understands this passage as describing Scripture’s origin in God: His Spirit carried the human speakers as they spoke from Him. The study applies inerrancy to the original texts as God inspired them. English translations are human works that must be examined against those texts; this passage does not promise that every translation choice is without error.
