Passage

Mark 1:16-20

Four Fishermen Follow Jesus

Berean Standard Bible

16 As Jesus was walking beside the Sea of Galilee, He saw Simon and his brother Andrew. They were casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen. 17 “Come, follow Me,” Jesus said, “and I will make you fishers of men.” 18 And at once they left their nets and followed Him.

19 Going on a little farther, He saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John. They were in a boat, mending their nets. 20 Immediately Jesus called them, and they left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men and followed Him.

What is the context?

Walking beside the Sea of Galilee, Jesus calls two sets of brothers during their work. Simon and Andrew are casting a net. James and John are mending nets in a boat with their father Zebedee. Both pairs respond promptly, leaving their former setting to follow Him.

Jesus uses their familiar work as a picture of what He will make them: people who gather others. The promise puts their future service in His hands. He calls them to follow, learn from Him, and become able to share His work.

Mark includes the hired men who remain in Zebedee's boat. That is a concrete detail about what the brothers leave behind, rather than a statement that every disciple must abandon every trade or leave family members without support.

John 1:35–42 records Andrew following Jesus after the Baptist's testimony and bringing Simon to Him. Luke 5:1–11 tells the call alongside a remarkable catch of fish. These accounts can be read together, while Mark's own emphasis stays on Jesus' summons and their response.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly see costly discipleship and preparation for sharing Jesus' mission. The Catholic Catechism, 551, emphasizes participation in His work, while Wesley on Mark 1:18 stresses their prompt response; no denominational breakdown is needed for that shared understanding.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The promise begins to take shape within Mark's own story. In Mark 3:13–19, these men are among the twelve appointed to be with Jesus and to be sent out to preach. In Mark 6:7–13, He sends them in pairs, and they call people to repent and bring help to those in need. Learning at His side becomes service to others.

John 15:16 also speaks of Jesus choosing and appointing His disciples to bear lasting fruit. This is a connection with His wider teaching, not a claim that Mark records everything the fishermen already understood. Their beginning in ordinary work does not set the limit of what He can make of them.
