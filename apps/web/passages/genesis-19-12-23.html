Passage

Genesis 19:12-23

Lot Flees to Zoar

Berean Standard Bible

12 Then the two men said to Lot, “Do you have anyone else here—a son-in-law, your sons or daughters, or anyone else in the city who belongs to you? Get them out of here, 13 because we are about to destroy this place. For the outcry to the LORD against its people is so great that He has sent us to destroy it.”

14 So Lot went out and spoke to the sons-in-law who were pledged in marriage to his daughters. “Get up,” he said. “Get out of this place, for the LORD is about to destroy the city!” But his sons-in-law thought he was joking.

15 At daybreak the angels hurried Lot along, saying, “Get up! Take your wife and your two daughters who are here, or you will be swept away in the punishment of the city.” 16 But when Lot hesitated, the men grabbed his hand and the hands of his wife and his two daughters. And they led them safely out of the city, because of the LORD’s compassion for them.

17 As soon as the men had brought them out, one of them said, “Run for your lives! Do not look back, and do not stop anywhere on the plain! Flee to the mountains, or you will be swept away!”

18 But Lot replied, “No, my lords, please! 19 Your servant has indeed found favor in your sight, and you have shown me great kindness by sparing my life. But I cannot run to the mountains; the disaster will overtake me, and I will die. 20 Look, there is a town nearby where I can flee, and it is a small place. Please let me flee there—is it not a small place? Then my life will be saved.”

21 “Very well,” he answered, “I will grant this request as well, and will not demolish the town you indicate. 22 Hurry! Run there quickly, for I cannot do anything until you reach it.” That is why the town was called Zoar.

23 And by the time the sun had risen over the land, Lot had reached Zoar.

What is the context?

The angels tell Lot to gather his people because the city is about to be destroyed. He warns the men pledged to marry his daughters, but they think he is joking. At daybreak the danger becomes urgent. Even then Lot hesitates, and the angels take him, his wife, and his two daughters by the hand and lead them out. The account gives the reason: the LORD's compassion for them.

Outside the city, the family is told to flee, avoid looking back, and keep moving beyond the plain. Lot fears he cannot reach the mountains and asks to escape to a nearby small town. His request is granted, and that town is spared. He reaches Zoar as the sun rises.

The rescue includes both mercy and a direction to act. Lot's hesitation has not made the danger disappear, and being brought outside the city is followed by the command to keep going. The contrast with the sons-in-law is equally sharp: a warning they take as a joke is the warning that leads the others out.

How is this passage commonly understood?

A common Christian reading emphasizes God's compassion in bringing Lot out despite his hesitation, together with the urgency of responding to the warning. Wesley's notes on Genesis 19:14–17 hold those two themes together.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

2 Peter 2:6–9 explicitly recalls Lot's rescue and concludes that the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from trials while holding the unrighteous for judgment. Peter calls Lot righteous without treating his decisions as consistently wise. The deliverance shows God's care for someone whose weakness is visible in the account.

Jesus also returns to this escape in Luke 17:28–33. Ordinary buying, selling, planting, and building continued until the day Lot left. Jesus uses the event to call His hearers to readiness when the Son of Man is revealed. The warning is about a response that cannot be postponed indefinitely while people secure the life they already know.
