Passage

Mark 9:1-8

The Son Revealed in Glory

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then Jesus said to them, “Truly I tell you, there are some standing here who will not taste death before they see the kingdom of God arrive with power.” 2 After six days Jesus took with Him Peter, James, and John, and led them up a high mountain by themselves. There He was transfigured before them. 3 His clothes became radiantly white, brighter than any launderer on earth could bleach them.

4 And Elijah and Moses appeared before them, talking with Jesus. 5 Peter said to Jesus, “Rabbi, it is good for us to be here. Let us put up three shelters—one for You, one for Moses, and one for Elijah.” 6 For they were all so terrified that Peter did not know what else to say. 7 Then a cloud appeared and enveloped them, and a voice came from the cloud: “This is My beloved Son. Listen to Him!” 8 Suddenly, when they looked around, they saw no one with them except Jesus.

What is the context?

This scene follows Jesus' announcement of suffering and His call to follow Him at real cost. He promises that some standing there will see God's kingdom arrive with power. Six days later He takes Peter, James, and John up a high mountain, where His appearance changes before them.

His clothes become brilliantly white, beyond anything an earthly launderer could produce. Moses and Elijah appear and speak with Him. Mark names the men but does not identify the mountain or explain how the disciples recognize them.

Peter proposes three shelters, one for each of them. Mark explains that the disciples are terrified and Peter does not know what to say. The text gives us that explanation without revealing a detailed plan Peter has worked out about a feast or the kingdom's timing.

A cloud envelops them, and the heavenly voice identifies Jesus as the beloved Son and commands them to listen to Him. Then they see Jesus alone. The direction to listen matters especially after Peter has resisted what Jesus said about suffering and death.

The parallel Luke 9:28–36 adds that Jesus is praying and that Moses and Elijah discuss His departure at Jerusalem. Matthew 17:1–8 also describes His shining face and His reassuring the terrified disciples. Those fuller details belong to the parallel accounts.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly see the transfiguration as a revelation of the Son's glory, joined to the suffering He has announced. The Catholic Compendium of the Catechism, 110, describes glory coming through the cross and a preview of His resurrection and glorious return.

Interpreters differ over the particular event promised in verse 1. Scofield's note on Mark 9:1, with its reference to Matthew 17:2, connects it with the mountain scene as a preview of the kingdom. Wesley on Mark 9:1 points to the kingdom's power at Pentecost.

The USCCB note on verse 1 reports both the Church's establishment in power and an expectation of Jesus' near return, favoring the latter as an explanation of the saying. These are differing commentary readings of the promise; that note is not a binding Catholic ruling that excludes every other interpretation.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Luke's account makes the connection with Jesus' mission explicit: Moses and Elijah speak about His departure, to be accomplished at Jerusalem (Luke 9:31). The word is ἔξοδος (exodos, EK-soh-dos), meaning departure and used here for the end He will meet. With Moses present, it invites a parallel with Israel's departure from Egypt in Exodus 12:31–42. The parallel is supported by Paul's explicit identification of Christ as the Passover lamb in 1 Corinthians 5:7; the word by itself does not explain every part of the scene.

The command to listen also recalls Deuteronomy 18:15–19, where Moses speaks of the coming prophet whom God's people must hear. Acts 3:18–26 applies that promise to Jesus. Hebrews 1:1–3 joins God's earlier speech through prophets with His final speech through the Son, who reveals His glory and provides purification for sins.

2 Peter 1:16–18 directly recalls the mountain and the heavenly voice when speaking of Jesus' majesty and coming. This is the New Testament's own connection: the disciples' glimpse of glory belongs to the witness about the One who suffers, rises, and will come in power.
