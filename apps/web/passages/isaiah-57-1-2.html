Passage

Isaiah 57:1-2

What does the careless world miss when the righteous die?

Berean Standard Bible

1 The righteous perish, and no one takes it to heart; devout men are taken away, while no one considers that the righteous are taken away from the presence of evil. 2 Those who walk uprightly enter into peace; they find rest, lying down in death.

What is the context?

After the warning about negligent leaders, the death of righteous and devoted people receives little attention. No one takes the loss to heart or considers what their being taken away means.

The passage supplies the perspective others fail to see: they are taken away from the presence of evil and enter peace. Those who walked uprightly find rest in death. The contrast is between the world’s indifference and the peace granted to the departed.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley both understand these verses as the death of righteous people and their release from approaching troubles. Henry distinguishes being spared death from being spared its sting; Wesley explains the beds as graves.

Their reading gives comfort about those who have died while treating the surrounding indifference as a warning. The peace of the righteous does not make the loss of their presence unimportant.
