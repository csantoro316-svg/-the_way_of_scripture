Passage

Matthew 6:31-33

A Different First Priority

Berean Standard Bible

31 Therefore do not worry, saying, ‘What shall we eat?’ or ‘What shall we drink?’ or ‘What shall we wear?’ 32 For the Gentiles strive after all these things, and your heavenly Father knows that you need them. 33 But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things will be added unto you.

What is the context?

These verses draw together Jesus' teaching about birds, flowers, and the Father's care in Matthew 6:25–34. The repeated questions about food, drink, and clothing express a life taken up with its needs. Jesus directs His followers toward a different first priority.

His comparison turns on knowing the Father. He describes the Gentiles as striving after these things, then reminds His hearers that their Father already knows their need. The contrast asks them to live from that relationship, with His kingdom and righteousness shaping what they pursue.

The promise concerns the needs already named. It is not a blank check for every possession or lifestyle a person might desire. The surrounding chapter has warned against making earthly treasure a master, and verse 34 still speaks of trouble in the present day.

In Luke 12:22–34, the related teaching connects trust, seeking the kingdom, and generosity. The Father's care is meant to free people to follow His purposes and share with others.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this passage as calling for trust in the Father's care and giving His kingdom first place, rather than allowing material needs to dominate life. Wesley's notes on Matthew 6:25–34 and the Catholic Catechism, 305–306, both place the teaching within confidence in God's care; the Catechism also explains that He works through the actions of His creatures.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Seeking God's kingdom belongs within His work of bringing people into it. Colossians 1:12–14 describes the Father rescuing people from darkness and bringing them into His Son's kingdom, where they receive redemption and forgiveness. The One they are called to trust is also the One who has acted to rescue them.

The kingdom reaches beyond a person's immediate circumstances. Ephesians 1:7–10 places forgiveness through Christ within God's purpose of bringing all things together in Him. Giving that purpose first place directs a life toward what God is bringing to fulfillment.

What is our position?

Our studies Prayer and Faith and What Does Prayer Look Like? understand the central issue as the direction of a person's trust. They call for seeking God and His will, with needs entrusted to His care, rather than making a desired outcome the goal of faith. The companion study distinguishes that surrender from a rule that asking is sinful.
