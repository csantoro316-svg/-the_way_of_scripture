Passage

Luke 23:50-56

Who cares for Jesus’ body after His death?

Berean Standard Bible

50 Now there was a Council member named Joseph, a good and righteous man, 51 who had not consented to their decision or action. He was from the Judean town of Arimathea and was waiting for the kingdom of God. 52 He went to Pilate to ask for the body of Jesus. 53 Then he took it down, wrapped it in a linen cloth, and placed it in a tomb cut into the rock, where no one had yet been laid.

54 It was Preparation Day, and the Sabbath was beginning. 55 The women who had come with Jesus from Galilee followed, and they saw the tomb and how His body was placed. 56 Then they returned to prepare spices and perfumes. And they rested on the Sabbath, according to the commandment.

What is the context?

Joseph of Arimathea is a member of the council, but Luke carefully distinguishes him from its decision and action against Jesus. He describes him as good and righteous, waiting for God’s kingdom.

Joseph asks Pilate for Jesus’ body, takes it down, wraps it in linen and places it in a tomb cut into rock where no one has yet been laid. The burial takes place on Preparation Day as the Sabbath is beginning.

The women from Galilee follow and see the tomb and how His body is placed. They prepare spices and perfumes, then rest on the Sabbath according to the commandment. Their observation connects this burial directly with the tomb they will visit in the next chapter.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly understand this as Jesus’ actual burial following His actual death, and as the setting for the resurrection account that follows. The Westminster Confession includes burial between death and resurrection, affirming that He rises in the same body in which He suffered. (Westminster Confession 8.4)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Isaiah describes the Servant being assigned a grave with the wicked and being with a rich man in His death. Matthew identifies Joseph as a rich disciple and says he places Jesus in his own new tomb. Together, these passages provide a supported connection between this burial and Isaiah’s picture of the suffering Servant. (Isaiah 53:9; Matthew 27:57–61)

Paul includes Christ’s burial within the message he received and passed on: Christ died for sins, was buried and was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures. The care taken with Jesus’ body therefore belongs within the gospel’s account of death overcome through resurrection. (1 Corinthians 15:3–8)
