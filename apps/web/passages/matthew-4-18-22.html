Passage

Matthew 4:18-22

The First Fishermen Follow Jesus

Berean Standard Bible

18 As Jesus was walking beside the Sea of Galilee, He saw two brothers, Simon called Peter and his brother Andrew. They were casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen. 19 “Come, follow Me,” Jesus said, “and I will make you fishers of men.” 20 And at once they left their nets and followed Him.

21 Going on from there, He saw two other brothers, James son of Zebedee and his brother John. They were in a boat with their father Zebedee, mending their nets. Jesus called them, 22 and immediately they left the boat and their father and followed Him.

What is the context?

Jesus calls two pairs of brothers beside the Sea of Galilee. Simon Peter and Andrew are casting a net; James and John are mending nets with their father Zebedee. The invitation reaches them during their ordinary work. Matthew emphasizes the prompt response of both pairs: they leave their nets, boat, and former setting to follow Him.

The fishing language gives their future work a familiar picture. Jesus will make them people who gather others. He does not simply tell them to be useful on their own; He calls them to follow Him and promises to shape what they will become.

John 1:35–42 records Andrew meeting Jesus through the Baptist's testimony and bringing Simon to Him. Matthew's lakeside call can therefore be read alongside an earlier introduction.

This scene follows the announcement of the kingdom and comes before the growing public ministry. Following Jesus will involve learning His message and sharing His work. The call changes real relationships and livelihoods, rather than remaining an admiration from a distance.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the brothers' response as costly discipleship and preparation to share Jesus' mission. The Catholic Catechism, paragraph 551, emphasizes the disciples' participation in His work; Baptist commentator John Gill on Matthew 4:19–20 likewise explains the promise as Jesus equipping them to bring others to faith.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The promised work develops later in the Gospel. In Matthew 10:1–8, these men are named among the twelve and sent to proclaim the kingdom and bring help to others. At the end, Matthew 28:18–20 gives the mission its worldwide reach. The call beside the water begins training for that outward work.

The initiative belongs to Jesus. John 15:16 later speaks of His choosing and appointing the disciples to bear lasting fruit. This comparison highlights His purpose and provision: the people He calls must follow, and He prepares them to serve. Their ordinary starting point does not set the limit of what He can do through them.
