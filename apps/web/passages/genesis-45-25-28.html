Passage

Genesis 45:25-28

The Revival of Jacob

Berean Standard Bible

25 So the brothers went up out of Egypt and came to their father Jacob in the land of Canaan. 26 “Joseph is still alive,” they said, “and he is ruler over all the land of Egypt!”

But Jacob was stunned, for he did not believe them. 27 However, when they relayed all that Joseph had told them, and when he saw the wagons that Joseph had sent to carry him back, the spirit of their father Jacob was revived.

28 “Enough!” declared Israel. “My son Joseph is still alive! I will go to see him before I die.”

What is the context?

The brothers return to Jacob in Canaan with news he has long believed impossible: Joseph is alive and rules over Egypt. At first he cannot believe them. The shock comes after years of mourning the son whose bloodied robe seemed to establish his death.

The description of Jacob being stunned uses פּוּג, pûg (poog), to grow numb. Brown–Driver–Briggs specifically cites his heart's response in Genesis 45:26. The word helps convey how overwhelming the report is before he can take it in.

The brothers repeat Joseph's words, and Jacob sees the wagons sent to carry him. His spirit revives. The news is accompanied by a visible invitation and the means to make the journey, and he resolves to see his living son before he dies.

The chapter closes with renewed hope and a decision to go. The actual departure will follow God's reassurance in the next chapter. For the moment, the father who refused comfort over Joseph's supposed death is preparing for a meeting he had no longer expected to have.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the scene as a movement from stunned disbelief to renewed hope. Wesley's notes on Genesis 45:26–27 emphasize that Joseph's being alive matters to Jacob even more than the report of his high position. The father's desire is to see his son.
