Passage

Matthew 23:37-39

The King Who Longs to Gather Jerusalem's Children

Berean Standard Bible

37 O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and stones those sent to her, how often I have longed to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, but you were unwilling! 38 Look, your house is left to you desolate. 39 For I tell you that you will not see Me again until you say, ‘Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord.’”

What is the context?

The sharp warnings end in a lament over Jerusalem. Jesus speaks of its killing God's messengers, yet also of His repeated desire to gather its children as a hen gathers her chicks. The refusal is set beside His longing, so the judgment is heard together with grief over those He would shelter.

The image recalls the shelter of God's wings in Psalm 91:1–4. Jesus then announces desolation and speaks of a future recognition using Psalm 118:26, the blessing already heard at His entry into Jerusalem.

The next scene shows Jesus leaving the temple and predicting its destruction (Matthew 24:1–2). The house left desolate therefore leads directly into the disciples' questions about what is coming. Jesus' lament holds His desire to gather, their unwillingness, and the approaching judgment together.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly hear both grief over rejection and a warning of judgment. Readers differ over the future welcome in the closing verse. Wesley on 23:39 expects a later joyful reception of Jesus. The US Catholic bishops' note on 23:37–39 records two readings: eventual acceptance, or recognition of Him when He comes as judge. The words themselves do not give a complete timetable.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

God's purpose includes His faithfulness and mercy toward those to whom He made promises. Romans 11:1–6 rejects the claim that God has simply cast off His people and points to a remnant chosen by grace. Romans 11:25–32 then warns Gentile believers against pride, describes a hardening in part, and places Israel's future within God's continuing call and mercy.

That larger passage guards against treating the lament as permission to despise Jewish people or declare God's faithfulness finished. Jesus' desire to gather and His warning of desolation belong within the wider scriptural story of God's judgment, promises, and mercy. The connection does not require assigning a date or a complete end-times sequence to these three verses.
