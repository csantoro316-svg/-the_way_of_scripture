Passage

Exodus 20:18-21

Why did Moses say not to be afraid, yet speak of fearing God?

Berean Standard Bible

18 When all the people witnessed the thunder and lightning, the sounding of the ram’s horn, and the mountain enveloped in smoke, they trembled and stood at a distance. 19 “Speak to us yourself and we will listen,” they said to Moses. “But do not let God speak to us, or we will die.” 20 “Do not be afraid,” Moses replied. “For God has come to test you, so that the fear of Him may be before you, to keep you from sinning.” 21 And the people stood at a distance as Moses approached the thick darkness where God was.

What is the context?

The people have heard the words spoken amid thunder, lightning, smoke and the ram’s horn. They tremble and move away, asking Moses to speak to them instead. They fear that hearing God directly will bring death.

Moses reassures them and explains the purpose of the encounter: God has come to test them, and the fear of Him is to help keep them from sin. His reassurance does not treat God’s holiness lightly. He calls them away from expecting immediate destruction and toward a lasting seriousness about listening to Him.

The people remain at a distance while Moses approaches the thick darkness where God is. The one they have asked to speak for them now carries that responsibility. The scene connects the commands with the response God seeks from the people who have heard them.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Calvin both distinguish terror that overwhelms the people from a fear of God that humbles them and leads to obedience. They understand the signs as intended to bring Israel to heed His word, with Moses serving as the intermediary the people have requested.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 12:18–24 explicitly recalls the voice that made the people ask to hear no more. It places that encounter alongside believers’ approach to heavenly Zion and Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant. Moses’ role in the scene belongs to the larger story of how people come near to God.

Hebrews 10:19–25 describes the confidence now given through Jesus’ blood. Believers are called to draw near with sincere hearts, hold to their hope and encourage one another toward love and good deeds. God’s purpose includes a people who can approach Him in faith and help one another remain faithful.
