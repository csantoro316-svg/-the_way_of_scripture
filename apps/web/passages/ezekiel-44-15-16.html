Passage

Ezekiel 44:15-16

Why may Zadok’s descendants approach the Lord?

Berean Standard Bible

15 But the Levitical priests, who are descended from Zadok and who kept charge of My sanctuary when the Israelites went astray from Me, are to approach Me to minister before Me. They will stand before Me to offer Me fat and blood, declares the Lord GOD. 16 They alone shall enter My sanctuary and draw near to My table to minister before Me. They will keep My charge.

What is the context?

The sons of Zadok are set in contrast with those who went astray. They kept charge of the Lord’s sanctuary when the Israelites turned away.

They are permitted to approach Him, present the fat and blood, enter His sanctuary and serve at His table. Their responsibility is described as keeping His charge.

The contrast is about faithfulness in an entrusted calling. Service near the Lord belongs within obedience to Him, rather than being a privilege to use independently of His word.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley emphasizes the honor the Lord gives to those who remain faithful when others draw back. Cambridge understands the passage as reserving priestly service in the new temple to Zadok’s family.

These readings connect their permitted approach with the faithfulness named in the passage itself.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

First Corinthians 4:1–5 describes Christ’s servants as stewards who must be found faithful. Paul points beyond people’s assessments to the Lord, who will reveal hidden motives and give the praise that is due.

This carries the theme of entrusted service into the life of Christ’s people. Faithfulness matters, and the Lord’s judgment reaches deeper than a public title or reputation. Paul does not supply a chart assigning Ezekiel’s priestly offices to particular Christians.
