Passage

2 Timothy 4:1-5

What must Timothy keep doing when people resist the truth?

Berean Standard Bible

1 I charge you in the presence of God and of Christ Jesus, who will judge the living and the dead, and in view of His appearing and His kingdom: 2 Preach the word; be prepared in season and out of season; reprove, rebuke, and encourage with every form of patient instruction.

3 For the time will come when men will not tolerate sound doctrine, but with itching ears they will gather around themselves teachers to suit their own desires. 4 So they will turn their ears away from the truth and turn aside to myths. 5 But you, be sober in all things, endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist, fulfill your ministry.

What is the context?

Paul has described Scripture’s usefulness for equipping a person for good work. He now gives Timothy a solemn charge before God and Christ Jesus, the Judge: proclaim the word and remain ready to do so, whether the circumstances seem favorable or not.

The verb for preach means to announce or proclaim, like a herald (Thayer). The task includes reproving, rebuking and encouraging, with patient instruction. Firmness and patience belong together.

Paul warns that hearers can choose teachers who suit their desires and turn toward myths instead of truth. Timothy must keep a clear head, endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist and fulfill the ministry entrusted to him.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley read this as a call to persistent proclamation rather than waiting for an easy or popular moment. Calvin connects it directly with the previous chapter: Scripture is to be both read and taught. Wesley emphasizes readiness to speak even when an obvious opportunity is lacking.

Both read the teachers and hearers in verses 3–4 as mutually reinforcing a preference for pleasing messages. The answer is faithful teaching joined with patience, rather than reshaping the message to secure approval.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The charge itself places Timothy’s work before the coming Judge and His kingdom. Acts 10:39–43 likewise joins testimony to Jesus’ death and resurrection with His role as Judge and the promise of forgiveness through His name.

Romans 10:13–17 explains why proclamation matters: people hear the message, believe and call on the Lord. Timothy’s patient work belongs within God’s purpose of bringing the gospel to those who need to hear it.
