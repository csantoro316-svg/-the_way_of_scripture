Passage

Isaiah 48:12-22

What should a redeemed people do with the way God opens for them?

Berean Standard Bible

12 Listen to Me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I have called: I am He; I am the first, and I am the last. 13 Surely My own hand founded the earth, and My right hand spread out the heavens; when I summon them, they stand up together. 14 Come together, all of you, and listen: Which of the idols has foretold these things? The LORD’s chosen ally will carry out His desire against Babylon, and His arm will be against the Chaldeans. 15 I, even I, have spoken; yes, I have called him. I have brought him, and he will succeed in his mission. 16 Come near to Me and listen to this: From the beginning I have not spoken in secret; from the time it happened, I was there.” And now the Lord GOD has sent me, accompanied by His Spirit.

17 Thus says the LORD your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: “I am the LORD your God, who teaches you for your benefit, who directs you in the way you should go. 18 If only you had paid attention to My commandments, your peace would have been like a river, and your righteousness like waves of the sea. 19 Your descendants would have been as countless as the sand, and your offspring as numerous as its grains; their name would never be cut off or eliminated from My presence.”

20 Leave Babylon! Flee from the Chaldeans! Declare it with a shout of joy, proclaim it, let it go out to the ends of the earth, saying, “The LORD has redeemed His servant Jacob!” 21 They did not thirst when He led them through the deserts; He made water flow for them from the rock; He split the rock, and water gushed out. 22 “There is no peace,” says the LORD, “for the wicked.”

What is the context?

The Lord calls Jacob and Israel to listen as the first and last, the One who founded the earth and spread out the heavens. He will bring His chosen ally against Babylon and give success to the mission He announced.

Verse 16 speaks of being sent by the Lord God, accompanied by His Spirit. The passage then names the Lord as Redeemer, teacher and guide. He laments the peace and continuing righteousness His people would have known had they paid attention to His commands.

The command to leave Babylon is to be accompanied by joyful proclamation to the ends of the earth: the Lord has redeemed Jacob. The remembered picture of water coming from a split rock presents Him as the One who provides along the way.

The final warning keeps the promise from becoming a reason for complacency. The rescue calls for receiving the Lord’s direction; the wicked do not find peace simply by sharing the name or outward circumstances of His people.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Commentators differ over the speaker at the end of verse 16. Gill reads the words as Christ speaking of being sent, and connects the passage with the Father, Son and Spirit. Henry also applies the sending to Christ.

Wesley reads the immediate speaker as Isaiah, while also seeing a possible reference through him to Christ. The Cambridge commentary on verse 16 reads a change to the prophet’s voice and understands the Spirit as accompanying his mission. This difference concerns the voice in this verse, rather than a simple division between believing and denying the Trinity.

For the final scene, Henry and Gill connect the rock and water with the exodus account and with Christ’s provision. Wesley reads the account as reassurance for the coming journey from captivity. They join the call to leave with the Lord’s continued guidance and the warning against refusing it.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

First Corinthians 10:1–13 looks back to the exodus and the wilderness food and drink. Paul explicitly identifies the spiritual rock with Christ, placing God’s provision within the account of Christ’s care for His people.

Paul then warns believers by the example of people who received those gifts but fell into idolatry and other wrongdoing. The history is written for their instruction, so confidence in their standing must go with vigilance and trust in God’s faithfulness.

This connects with Isaiah’s return-from-captivity scene through the earlier exodus, rather than as a direct quotation of Isaiah 48. Rescue and provision lead into a life of listening to God. The purpose is restored people walking in His way, not treating His gifts as a reason to disregard Him.
