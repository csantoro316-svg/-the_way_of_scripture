Passage

John 3:22-30

Why is John glad when people go to Jesus?

Berean Standard Bible

22 After this, Jesus and His disciples went into the Judean countryside, where He spent some time with them and baptized. 23 Now John was also baptizing at Aenon near Salim, because the water was plentiful there, and people kept coming to be baptized. 24 (For John had not yet been thrown into prison.)

25 Then a dispute arose between John’s disciples and a certain Jew over the issue of ceremonial washing. 26 So John’s disciples came to him and said, “Look, Rabbi, the One who was with you beyond the Jordan, the One you testified about—He is baptizing, and everyone is going to Him.” 27 John replied, “A man can receive only what is given him from heaven. 28 You yourselves can testify that I said, ‘I am not the Christ, but am sent ahead of Him.’ 29 The bride belongs to the bridegroom. The friend of the bridegroom stands and listens for him, and is overjoyed to hear the bridegroom’s voice. That joy is mine, and it is now complete. 30 He must increase; I must decrease.

What is the context?

Jesus and His disciples spend time in the Judean countryside while John continues baptizing at Aenon near Salim. The Gospel notes that John has not yet been imprisoned. The next chapter clarifies that Jesus’ disciples carry out the baptizing. Aenon’s plentiful water is given as the reason for John’s activity there; this does not locate Jesus at that same spring. (John 4:1–2)

After a discussion about ceremonial washing, John’s disciples report that people are going to Jesus. John answers that a person receives only what heaven gives. He reminds them that he has always said he is sent ahead of the Christ, rather than being the Christ himself.

John compares his joy to that of a bridegroom’s friend who is delighted to hear the groom. His mission succeeds as attention turns toward Jesus. The account records his disciples’ concern; it does not tell us that every one of them is an unbelieving person seeking a career.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readers understand this as a period when John’s work and Jesus’ growing ministry overlap, with John gladly directing attention to the Christ. The plentiful water also enters discussions about how baptism should be performed.

Where traditions differ is whether immersion is required. The Baptist Confession requires immersion for the proper administration of baptism. The Westminster Confession permits pouring or sprinkling, while the Catholic Catechism calls triple immersion the most expressive form and also recognizes pouring. These documents explain their traditions’ broader teachings; the single detail about plentiful water does not state every part of those teachings. (Baptist Confession 29.4; Westminster Confession 28.3; Catechism §1239) Wesley and Calvin both understand the wedding comparison as John welcoming Christ’s rightful place rather than treating Him as a rival. Calvin applies the distinction to teachers who serve Christ without claiming the people as their own. (Wesley on John 3:27–30; Calvin on John 3:29)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The wedding comparison belongs within a wider scriptural picture of Christ’s love for His people. Ephesians describes Christ loving the church, giving Himself for her and preparing her to stand before Him. John’s joy as the bridegroom’s friend is a parallel with that larger picture: the people belong to Christ, and His servants rejoice in their being brought to Him. (Ephesians 5:25–32)

Paul later addresses rivalry around teachers by calling himself and Apollos servants with different tasks, while God gives the growth. That provides another scriptural parallel to John’s acceptance of his assigned work. God’s purpose brings people to Christ through servants who work together. (1 Corinthians 3:4–9)

What is our position?

Our study Baptism reads the plentiful water in John 3:23 as support for immersion as the intended, normal practice. Its conclusion also recognizes that Scripture does not explicitly declare every other mode invalid and allows for pouring when immersion is unavailable.
