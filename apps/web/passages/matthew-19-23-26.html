Passage

Matthew 19:23-26

What Is Impossible for People Is Possible with God

Berean Standard Bible

23 Then Jesus said to His disciples, “Truly I tell you, it is hard for a rich man to enter the kingdom of heaven. 24 Again I tell you, it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God.”

25 When the disciples heard this, they were greatly astonished and asked, “Who then can be saved?” 26 Jesus looked at them and said, “With man this is impossible, but with God all things are possible.”

What is the context?

After the rich young man leaves in sorrow, Jesus tells the disciples how hard it is for a rich person to enter the kingdom of heaven. He sets before them the picture of a camel passing through the eye of a needle. The enormous contrast shows a difficulty beyond human ability.

The disciples are astonished and ask who can be saved. Jesus' answer turns their attention to God: what is impossible with people is possible with Him. The question in this conversation is salvation, arising from the man's attachment to his wealth and the call to follow Jesus.

Peter's next question asks what will become of those who have left everything to follow Him. The chapter moves from the wealth someone cannot bear to leave toward the inheritance Jesus promises His followers.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as exposing both the danger of wealth and the need for God's saving power. Wesley on 19:24–26 takes the camel and needle as a picture of human impossibility, and the US Catholic bishops' notes on 19:23–26 likewise understand riches as an obstacle overcome by God's power.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

God's mercy supplies what people cannot produce for themselves. Titus 3:3–7 describes people once enslaved to their desires being saved through God's kindness, mercy, and renewal by the Holy Spirit, poured out through Jesus Christ. By His grace they become heirs of eternal life.

That purpose also changes how wealth is used. 1 Timothy 6:17–19 tells believers who are rich to put their hope in God and to be generous and ready to share. Following Christ gives possessions a place in serving others while directing hope toward the life He gives.
