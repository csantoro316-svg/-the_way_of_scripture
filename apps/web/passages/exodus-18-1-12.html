Passage

Exodus 18:1-12

How did Jethro respond to what God had done for Israel?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Now Moses’ father-in-law Jethro, the priest of Midian, heard about all that God had done for Moses and His people Israel, and how the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt. 2 After Moses had sent back his wife Zipporah, his father-in-law Jethro had received her, 3 along with her two sons. One son was named Gershom, for Moses had said, “I have been a foreigner in a foreign land.” 4 The other son was named Eliezer, for Moses had said, “The God of my father was my helper and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh.” 5 Moses’ father-in-law Jethro, along with Moses’ wife and sons, came to him in the desert, where he was encamped at the mountain of God. 6 He sent word to Moses, “I, your father-in-law Jethro, am coming to you with your wife and her two sons.”

7 So Moses went out to meet his father-in-law and bowed down and kissed him. They greeted each other and went into the tent. 8 Then Moses recounted to his father-in-law all that the LORD had done to Pharaoh and the Egyptians for Israel’s sake, all the hardships they had encountered along the way, and how the LORD had delivered them. 9 And Jethro rejoiced over all the good things the LORD had done for Israel, whom He had rescued from the hand of the Egyptians. 10 Jethro declared, “Blessed be the LORD, who has delivered you from the hand of the Egyptians and of Pharaoh, and who has delivered the people from the hand of the Egyptians. 11 Now I know that the LORD is greater than all other gods, for He did this when they treated Israel with arrogance.” 12 Then Moses’ father-in-law Jethro brought a burnt offering and sacrifices to God, and Aaron came with all the elders of Israel to eat bread with Moses’ father-in-law in the presence of God.

What is the context?

Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law and the priest of Midian, hears about the rescue from Egypt and comes to visit. He brings Zipporah and her two sons, whom he had received after Moses sent her back. Their names recall Moses’ life as a foreigner and God’s help in delivering him from Pharaoh.

Moses welcomes Jethro with affection and respect. Their conversation includes both the great rescue and the hardships along the way. Moses tells how the LORD has delivered Israel through those difficulties; the story has room for what they have suffered as well as what they have received.

Jethro rejoices, blesses the LORD and declares His greatness over other gods. He brings an offering and sacrifices to God, and Aaron and the elders share a meal with him in God’s presence. The deliverance has become something a man from outside Israel hears about, praises God for and celebrates with the people who were rescued.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The visit is read as a response of praise and recognition of the LORD’s power. Wesley understands Jethro’s statement as an existing faith being strengthened: he already knew God, and now knows His greatness more fully. The Catholic USCCB study note also sees his sacrifice as suggesting that he was already a worshiper of Israel’s God.

Calvin agrees that Jethro acknowledges the true God, but thinks the comparison with other gods may show that some belief in lesser gods still remains. He also allows that Jethro’s new knowledge could mean an earlier faith confirmed by experience. These readings differ over how much can be concluded about Jethro’s earlier beliefs; the text clearly records his praise and offering.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jethro’s joy gives an early example of someone outside Israel sharing in praise for God’s work among His people. That fits the wider promise in Genesis 12:1–3: God will make Abraham’s descendants a blessing, with all the families of the earth in view.

Romans 15:8–12 explains the purpose through Christ. He confirms the promises to the patriarchs so that the Gentiles may glorify God for His mercy; Paul calls on the nations to rejoice with God’s people. Jethro’s response is an earlier example of that shared praise, rather than a claim that Paul is specifically interpreting this visit.
