Passage

Exodus 18:13-27

Why did Moses need other people to share the work?

Berean Standard Bible

13 The next day Moses took his seat to judge the people, and they stood around him from morning until evening. 14 When his father-in-law saw all that Moses was doing for the people, he asked, “What is this that you are doing for the people? Why do you sit alone as judge, with all the people standing around you from morning till evening?” 15 “Because the people come to me to inquire of God,” Moses replied. 16 “Whenever they have a dispute, it is brought to me to judge between one man and another, and I make known to them the statutes and laws of God.” 17 But Moses’ father-in-law said to him, “What you are doing is not good. 18 Surely you and these people with you will wear yourselves out, because the task is too heavy for you. You cannot handle it alone.

19 Now listen to me; I will give you some advice, and may God be with you. You must be the people’s representative before God and bring their causes to Him. 20 Teach them the statutes and laws, and show them the way to live and the work they must do. 21 Furthermore, select capable men from among the people—God-fearing, trustworthy men who are averse to dishonest gain. Appoint them over the people as leaders of thousands, of hundreds, of fifties, and of tens. 22 Have these men judge the people at all times. Then they can bring you any major issue, but all minor cases they can judge on their own, so that your load may be lightened as they share it with you. 23 If you follow this advice and God so directs you, then you will be able to endure, and all these people can go home in peace.”

24 Moses listened to his father-in-law and did everything he said. 25 So Moses chose capable men from all Israel and made them heads over the people as leaders of thousands, of hundreds, of fifties, and of tens. 26 And they judged the people at all times; they would bring the difficult cases to Moses, but any minor issue they would judge themselves. 27 Then Moses sent his father-in-law on his way, and Jethro returned to his own land.

What is the context?

Moses spends the next day judging disputes from morning until evening. He explains that people come to inquire of God, and he makes God’s instructions known as he settles their cases. Jethro sees a problem for both the judge and the people waiting for him: the burden is too great for one person.

His proposal preserves Moses’ responsibility to represent the people before God and teach them the way to live. Other capable men can handle ordinary disputes, while the difficult cases come to Moses. The work will be shared so that he can endure and the people can receive judgment without the same exhausting wait.

The Hebrew word חַיִל, chayil (KHAH-yil), has a range that includes strength, ability and worth. Brown–Driver–Briggs places these judges under ability and efficiency, often joined with moral worth. The list in the passage makes that character concrete: they must fear God, be trustworthy and reject dishonest gain.

Jethro places his advice under God’s direction. Moses listens, appoints suitable men over groups of different sizes, and keeps the difficult cases. The change allows others to carry real responsibility while Moses continues the work entrusted to him. Jethro then returns to his own land.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared reading sees wise counsel about the limits of one leader and the need for trustworthy helpers. Wesley and Calvin both emphasize the character of those appointed and Moses’ willingness to receive advice. They also take seriously the condition that the arrangement must remain under God’s authority.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

A similar need appears in Acts 6:1–7, when some widows are being overlooked in the daily distribution. The apostles arrange for qualified people to oversee that care so that they can continue in prayer and teaching. Both accounts show work being shared so that people are served and essential responsibilities receive attention. Acts is a later parallel, not an instruction to reproduce Israel’s exact ranks of judges in a church.

Ephesians 4:11–16 gives the larger purpose for the church: Christ gives people to equip others for ministry, and the whole body grows as its members do their part. Shared service is part of how His people mature together. The lesson reaches beyond helping one tired leader; it concerns a community being prepared to carry out its work.
