Passage

Ezekiel 22:17-22

Why is Jerusalem compared to a furnace full of dross?

Berean Standard Bible

17 Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying, 18 “Son of man, the house of Israel has become dross to Me. All of them are copper, tin, iron, and lead inside the furnace; they are but the dross of silver. 19 Therefore this is what the Lord GOD says: ‘Because all of you have become dross, behold, I will gather you into Jerusalem.

20 Just as one gathers silver, copper, iron, lead, and tin into the furnace to melt with a fiery blast, so I will gather you in My anger and wrath, leave you there, and melt you. 21 Yes, I will gather you together and blow on you with the fire of My wrath, and you will be melted within the city. 22 As silver is melted in a furnace, so you will be melted within the city. Then you will know that I, the LORD, have poured out My wrath upon you.’”

What is the context?

The Lord describes the house of Israel as dross, listing copper, tin, iron and lead within a furnace. The comparison concerns a people whose condition has become unacceptable to Him.

The Hebrew term סִיג, sig (seeg), is explained in Brown–Driver–Briggs as dross, the material removed from metal. The image contrasts what is expected from silver with the unwanted material associated with it.

Jerusalem will become the furnace into which the people are gathered. The Lord will blow upon them with the fire of His wrath, and they will be melted within the city.

The repeated announcement leaves the purpose of this immediate warning clear: they will know that He has poured out His wrath. The city that seemed a place of refuge is pictured as the place where judgment concentrates.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley emphasizes the change from a people practicing mercy and justice to a people described as dross. He understands the gathering into Jerusalem as gathering into a furnace where they face destruction.

Henry also describes the degraded condition, then draws on the wider refining picture to emphasize the separation of dross and the purification of good metal. The USCCB notes connect the image with other prophetic descriptions of smelting and purification.

The emphasis differs between the immediate threat and the refining theme drawn from the broader picture. Ezekiel’s repeated words here announce a severe judgment; they do not describe an easy process or guarantee that every individual will respond faithfully.
