Passage

Genesis 17:1-8

Abraham to Father Many Nations

Berean Standard Bible

1 When Abram was ninety-nine years old, the LORD appeared to him and said, “I am God Almighty. Walk before Me and be blameless. 2 I will establish My covenant between Me and you, and I will multiply you exceedingly.”

3 Then Abram fell facedown, and God said to him, 4 “As for Me, this is My covenant with you: You will be the father of many nations. 5 No longer will you be called Abram, but your name will be Abraham, for I have made you a father of many nations.

6 I will make you exceedingly fruitful; I will make nations of you, and kings will descend from you.

7 I will establish My covenant as an everlasting covenant between Me and you and your descendants after you, to be your God and the God of your descendants after you.

8 And to you and your descendants I will give the land where you are residing—all the land of Canaan—as an eternal possession; and I will be their God.”

What is the context?

Abram is ninety-nine when God appears again, thirteen years after Ishmael's birth. God introduces Himself as God Almighty and calls Abram to walk before Him and be blameless. The promise of descendants has not been abandoned during the years of waiting, and Abram is still called to a life shaped by the God who has spoken.

Blameless translates תָּמִים, tāmîm (tah-MEEM). Brown–Driver–Briggs places Genesis 17:1 among its uses for soundness and integrity. The command calls for a faithful way of life before God. It does not turn Abram's earlier failures into events that never happened or declare that he has never sinned.

God gives Abram the name Abraham and connects it with becoming the father of many nations. The promise includes fruitfulness, kings, descendants, the land of Canaan, and an enduring relationship in which God will be their God. A new name announces the future God promises while Abraham is still waiting for the son through whom the covenant will continue.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as God reaffirming His covenant and calling Abraham to live faithfully within that relationship. Wesley's notes on Genesis 17:1, 5–8 connect the new name with the promised family and emphasize that the greatest blessing is God giving Himself to be their God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 4:16–18 explicitly quotes God's words about making Abraham a father of many nations. Paul explains that the promise reaches people who share Abraham's faith, and he describes the God Abraham trusts as the One who gives life to the dead. The new name points to a family God can bring into being when the visible circumstances offer little reason for hope.

The promise that God will be their God also reaches beyond having descendants or land. Hebrews 11:13–16 describes the patriarchs longing for a better country and says that God is not ashamed to be called their God. The lasting goal is life with Him, even when the people receiving His promise spend their present lives waiting.
