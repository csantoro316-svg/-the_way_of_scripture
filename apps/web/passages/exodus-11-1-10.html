Passage

Exodus 11:1-10

What would finally bring Israel’s release?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then the LORD said to Moses, “I will bring upon Pharaoh and Egypt one more plague. After that, he will allow you to leave this place. And when he lets you go, he will drive you out completely. 2 Now announce to the people that men and women alike should ask their neighbors for articles of silver and gold.” 3 And the LORD gave the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians. Moreover, Moses himself was highly regarded in Egypt by Pharaoh’s officials and by the people.

4 So Moses declared, “This is what the LORD says: ‘About midnight I will go throughout Egypt, 5 and every firstborn son in the land of Egypt will die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on his throne, to the firstborn of the servant girl behind the hand mill, as well as the firstborn of all the cattle. 6 Then a great cry will go out over all the land of Egypt. Such an outcry has never been heard before and will never be heard again. 7 But among all the Israelites, not even a dog will snarl at man or beast.’ Then you will know that the LORD makes a distinction between Egypt and Israel. 8 And all these officials of yours will come and bow before me, saying, ‘Go, you and all the people who follow you!’ After that, I will depart.” And hot with anger, Moses left Pharaoh’s presence.

9 The LORD said to Moses, “Pharaoh will not listen to you, so that My wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt.” 10 Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, but the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart so that he would not let the Israelites go out of his land.

What is the context?

God announces one more plague. After it, Pharaoh will drive the people out completely. Israel is also told to ask its Egyptian neighbors for silver and gold, and the account describes the favor God has given the people and the regard in which Moses is now held.

Moses warns Pharaoh that the LORD will go through Egypt about midnight and every firstborn son will die, from the royal household to the household of the servant girl grinding grain. The firstborn of the cattle are included. Rank and wealth will not shield a household from the coming grief.

The great outcry in Egypt is contrasted with the quiet promised to Israel, expressed through the image of not even a dog snarling at its people or animals. The distinction will make the LORD’s action known. Pharaoh’s own officials will urge Moses and the people to leave.

Moses leaves Pharaoh in anger. The final verses look back over the wonders and the continued refusal: the LORD has hardened Pharaoh’s heart, and the people have not yet been released. The warning now stands immediately before the instructions for Passover.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared reading sees the final warning as bringing the confrontation to its announced conclusion. Calvin and Wesley understand Moses’ speech as continuing the final interview described at the end of chapter 10, before he leaves Pharaoh’s presence.

The request for silver and gold receives different explanations. Wesley emphasizes asking rather than borrowing and understands the goods as compensation for the people’s labor. Calvin allows a request made as borrowing, justified by God’s authority to transfer the possessions, and limits his explanation to this particular divine command. Brown–Driver–Briggs notes that the verb can describe borrowing, while also observing that a pretext of temporary use is not clear here. The BSB uses ask.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The coming judgment is directly recalled in Hebrews 11:28. It describes Moses keeping the Passover and applying the blood by faith so that Israel’s firstborn would be spared. The instructions that follow in Exodus will show how the people are to trust and act on God’s word.

Jude 1:5 places the wider deliverance within the New Testament’s teaching about Jesus, recalling that He brought His people out of Egypt and later judged unbelief. Rescue and accountability remain together in that recollection. The Exodus becomes both a testimony to God’s saving action and a warning about how His people respond to Him.
