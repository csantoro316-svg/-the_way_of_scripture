Passage

Daniel 8:23-27

What brings the deceitful ruler’s success to an end?

Berean Standard Bible

23 In the latter part of their reign, when the rebellion has reached its full measure, an insolent king, skilled in intrigue, will come to the throne. 24 His power will be great, but it will not be his own. He will cause terrible destruction and succeed in whatever he does. He will destroy the mighty men along with the holy people. 25 Through his craft and by his hand, he will cause deceit to prosper, and in his own mind he will make himself great. In a time of peace he will destroy many, and he will even stand against the Prince of princes. Yet he will be broken off, but not by human hands.

26 The vision of the evenings and the mornings that has been spoken is true. Now you must seal up the vision, for it concerns the distant future.” 27 I, Daniel, was exhausted and lay ill for days. Then I got up and went about the king’s business. I was confounded by the vision; it was beyond understanding.

What is the context?

The explanation describes an insolent ruler skilled in intrigue. His power brings terrible destruction to the mighty and the holy people. Deceit prospers through him, and he attacks people while they are at peace.

He even stands against the Prince of princes, yet he is broken without human hands. His apparent success does not give him the final word.

Daniel is told that the vision is true and concerns the distant future. He is to seal it up. Exhausted and ill for days, he eventually returns to the king’s business, still overwhelmed by what he has seen. Receiving a true revelation does not mean that he immediately understands everything it contains.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Cambridge understand the ruler as Antiochus, whose end comes through God’s intervention. Gaebelein treats Antiochus’s persecution as a preview of a later attack on Israel near the end.

These readings differ over whether a further ruler is in view. They share the conclusion that arrogant power does not escape God’s appointed limit.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

A parallel appears in 2 Thessalonians 2:1–12. Paul describes a lawless ruler who exalts himself against God, works through deception and is destroyed by the Lord Jesus at His appearing.

The parallel is the defeat of self-exalting, deceitful power by God’s action, beyond what human strength can achieve. It does not require settling every question about the ruler in Daniel 8. Paul brings the wider hope into focus through Jesus: the power that deceives and oppresses will answer to Him.
