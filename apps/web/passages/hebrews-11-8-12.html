Passage

Hebrews 11:8-12

How did Abraham and Sarah trust a promise still unfolding?

Berean Standard Bible

8 By faith Abraham, when called to go to a place he would later receive as his inheritance, obeyed and went, without knowing where he was going. 9 By faith he dwelt in the promised land as a stranger in a foreign country. He lived in tents, as did Isaac and Jacob, who were heirs with him of the same promise. 10 For he was looking forward to the city with foundations, whose architect and builder is God.

11 By faith Sarah, even though she was barren and beyond the proper age, was enabled to conceive a child, because she considered Him faithful who had promised. 12 And so from one man, and he as good as dead, came descendants as numerous as the stars in the sky and as countless as the sand on the seashore.

What is the context?

Abraham obeyed God’s call without knowing where the journey would lead. Even after reaching the promised land, he lived there as a stranger. Isaac and Jacob shared the promise and the same life in tents. The promise was real before they possessed its fullness.

His expectation reached beyond a temporary campsite to a city whose builder is God. That expectation helps explain why living as a foreigner did not make God’s promise meaningless to him.

Sarah’s example brings the same trust into a different difficulty. Though barren and beyond the usual age for bearing a child, she considered the One who promised faithful. The account is grounded in Genesis 18:9–15 and 21:1–7, where her initial laughter and Isaac’s birth are both recorded.

The many descendants came from a beginning that seemed humanly impossible. Hebrews celebrates God’s faithfulness and the trust that received His promise. It does not give every couple the particular promise made to Abraham and Sarah.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley read Abraham’s journey and life in tents as faith continuing beyond a first act of obedience. Both connect the city with the lasting home God provides.

Both also recognize the question raised by Sarah’s earlier laughter. Calvin understands her faith as overcoming the unbelief exposed by God’s rebuke; Wesley likewise notes that she first laughed at the promise. They do not treat a moment of doubt as the whole story of her response to God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Galatians 3:6–9, 13–14 connects Abraham’s promise with the gospel reaching the nations through Christ. Romans 4:16–25 joins Abraham’s confidence in God’s life-giving power with faith in the God who raised Jesus.

The family’s unlikely beginning therefore belongs within a purpose larger than their own household. God’s blessing reaches others through Christ, and the promise teaches trust in His power to give life.
