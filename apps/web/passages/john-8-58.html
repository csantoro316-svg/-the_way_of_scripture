Passage

John 8:58

What does Jesus claim by saying He existed before Abraham?

Berean Standard Bible

58 “Truly, truly, I tell you,” Jesus declared, “before Abraham was born, I am!”

What is the context?

This verse answers the question raised by the whole discussion in John 8:48–59: how can Jesus speak of Abraham seeing His day when He is not yet fifty years old? Jesus is not claiming an unusually long human lifespan. He places His existence before Abraham’s beginning.

The wording matters. Thayer’s entry for the verb meaning to come into existence directly cites Abraham in this verse, while the entry for the verb meaning to be cites Jesus’ statement. The contrast supports reading more here than the memory of an ordinary man. The connection with God’s self-revelation in Exodus 3:13–15 also helps explain why Christians have read this saying as a claim to divine identity.

In the next verse His opponents pick up stones. John records Jesus leaving the temple rather than submitting to their attack at this point. The verse should be read in that setting, alongside the Father’s sending of Jesus and His promise of life, rather than as an isolated puzzle about grammar.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The major Christian traditions understand this saying as testimony to the Son’s existence before Abraham and His divine identity. This shared reading appears in the Catholic Catechism, paragraph 590, Calvin’s commentary and Wesley’s notes.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The One who comes to fulfill God’s promises is not a late arrival in God’s purpose. John 1:1–14 describes the eternal Word becoming flesh, and Galatians 3:8–9 connects the gospel with the blessing promised to Abraham. Jesus’ coming brings that long-promised saving purpose into view. His identity explains how Abraham’s hope and the life Jesus offers belong to one story.

What is our position?

Our study Who is The Angel of the Lord? reads this verse as Jesus identifying Himself as the eternal Son, personally present before Abraham. The study connects that claim with Genesis 18 and John 1:18 and concludes that the Son was the One who appeared to Abraham and made the Father known. That identification is the study’s developed conclusion; this verse itself does not name a particular earlier encounter.
