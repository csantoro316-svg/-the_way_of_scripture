Passage

Matthew 13:51-52

Bringing Out Treasures New and Old

Berean Standard Bible

51 Have you understood all these things?” “Yes,” they answered. 52 Then He told them, “For this reason, every scribe who has been discipled in the kingdom of heaven is like a homeowner who brings out of his storeroom new treasures as well as old.”

What is the context?

Jesus asks whether the disciples have understood these things, and they answer yes. He follows with a picture of a scribe trained as a disciple of the kingdom: a householder bringing out both new and old treasures from a storeroom.

The picture joins learning with the ability to give something useful to others. Understanding is not treated as private possession alone. A trained teacher has resources to bring out, with both what has long been known and what Jesus is now making clear.

The old and new belong together within the kingdom's teaching. Earlier in this chapter, prophets longed for what the disciples now see, and Jesus explained His work through Isaiah and the Psalms. The new revelation through Him gives greater understanding of the Scriptures already received.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as a call for teachers formed by Jesus to draw on the riches of God's revelation. Wesley on Matthew 13:52 emphasizes a prepared teacher's resources for instruction. The Methodist Articles of Religion, VI, affirm that the Old Testament is not opposed to the New and that both offer life through Christ. The US Catholic bishops' note on 13:52 similarly reads the old as the Law and Prophets and the new as Jesus' teaching, with the former understood through its fulfillment.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

After His resurrection, Jesus opens the disciples' understanding of the Law, Prophets, and Psalms, connecting them with His suffering, resurrection, and the message of repentance and forgiveness to all nations (Luke 24:44–49). The treasures of Scripture equip people to announce the Savior and His work.

2 Timothy 3:14–17 likewise describes the Scriptures as making a person wise for salvation through faith in Christ and preparing God's servant for every good work. Learning the whole story serves God's purpose of forming people who can live and teach it faithfully.
