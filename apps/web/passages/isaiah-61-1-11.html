Passage

Isaiah 61:1-11

Who brings good news and turns mourning into joy?

Berean Standard Bible

1 The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on Me, because the LORD has anointed Me to preach good news to the poor. He has sent Me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives and freedom to the prisoners, 2 to proclaim the year of the LORD’s favor and the day of our God’s vengeance, to comfort all who mourn, 3 to console the mourners in Zion— to give them a crown of beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, and a garment of praise for a spirit of despair. So they will be called oaks of righteousness, the planting of the LORD, that He may be glorified.

4 They will rebuild the ancient ruins; they will restore the places long devastated; they will renew the ruined cities, the desolations of many generations. 5 Strangers will stand and feed your flocks, and foreigners will be your plowmen and vinedressers. 6 But you will be called the priests of the LORD; they will speak of you as ministers of our God; you will feed on the wealth of nations, and you will boast in their riches. 7 Instead of shame, My people will have a double portion, and instead of humiliation, they will rejoice in their share; and so they will inherit a double portion in their land, and everlasting joy will be theirs.

8 For I, the LORD, love justice; I hate robbery and iniquity; in My faithfulness I will give them their recompense and make an everlasting covenant with them. 9 Their descendants will be known among the nations, and their offspring among the peoples. All who see them will acknowledge that they are a people the LORD has blessed.

10 I will rejoice greatly in the LORD, my soul will exult in my God; for He has clothed me with garments of salvation and wrapped me in a robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom wears a priestly headdress, as a bride adorns herself with her jewels. 11 For as the earth brings forth its growth, and as a garden enables seed to spring up, so the Lord GOD will cause righteousness and praise to spring up before all the nations.

What is the context?

The speaker is sent with the Spirit of the Lord upon Him to bring good news to the poor, bind up the brokenhearted and proclaim freedom. The announcement includes the Lord’s favor, His day of vengeance and comfort for those who mourn.

The Hebrew verb מָשַׁח, mashach (mah-SHAKH), means to anoint. Brown–Driver–Briggs includes setting someone apart for an office and specifically lists Isaiah 61:1. Here the anointing belongs to the speaker’s God-given mission.

For Zion’s mourners, ashes and despair give way to beauty, joy and praise. They become like oaks of righteousness planted by the Lord for His glory. Ruined places are rebuilt, and the people are called His priests and ministers.

Shame is replaced by a double portion and lasting joy. The Lord declares His love of justice and hatred of robbery and iniquity, promising a faithful recompense and an everlasting covenant. Their descendants will be recognized as a people He has blessed.

The chapter ends in rejoicing over garments of salvation and righteousness, pictured through wedding celebration. As a garden brings up its growth, the Lord will cause righteousness and praise to spring up before the nations.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley understand the opening mission chiefly through Christ and His work of freeing and comforting people. Wesley allows a reference to the prophet while emphasizing its fullest application to Christ.

The USCCB notes give attention to the prophet’s commission, the comfort of Zion and the people’s renewed identity and mission. Henry connects the rebuilding promises both with Jews returning from captivity and with lives restored through the gospel.

Henry and Wesley also relate the priestly calling to believers’ service to God. Their explanations emphasize that those who receive rescue are brought into a life that honors Him, and that salvation becomes a reason for joy and praise.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Luke 4:16–22 supplies the direct identification. Jesus reads from Isaiah in Nazareth and declares the Scripture fulfilled in His hearers’ presence. The good news, freedom and year of favor belong to His explanation of His own mission.

Luke’s recorded reading ends with the year of favor, before Isaiah’s mention of the day of vengeance. The passage itself connects saving favor and God’s judgment; Luke makes clear that Jesus has come carrying the promised good news.

The calling to serve also has a New Testament parallel in First Peter 2:9–10. Peter describes a royal priesthood and holy people called out of darkness to proclaim God’s virtues. This is a shared priestly theme, rather than an explicit quotation of Isaiah 61.
