Passage

Hebrews 3:1-6

Why does Jesus deserve greater honor than Moses?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Therefore, holy brothers, who share in the heavenly calling, set your focus on Jesus, the apostle and high priest whom we confess. 2 He was faithful to the One who appointed Him, just as Moses was faithful in all God’s house.

3 For Jesus has been counted worthy of greater glory than Moses, just as the builder of a house has greater honor than the house itself. 4 And every house is built by someone, but God is the builder of everything. 5 Now Moses was faithful as a servant in all God’s house, testifying to what would be spoken later. 6 But Christ is faithful as the Son over God’s house. And we are His house, if we hold firmly to our confidence and the hope of which we boast.

What is the context?

The writer asks his brothers, who share a heavenly calling, to focus on Jesus. The word translated set your focus means to consider attentively or fix the mind on something. This is an invitation to look carefully at who Jesus is.

Jesus is called both apostle and high priest: the One sent by God and the One who represents His people before God. The comparison now turns to Moses, whose faithfulness is affirmed rather than dismissed.

The house language comes from Numbers 12:6–8, where God calls Moses faithful in His house. Hebrews distinguishes the servant within the household from the Son over it. The builder has greater honor than the house, and God builds everything.

The household is people, not a building made of stone. The writer includes his readers among that household, with a call to hold firmly to their confidence and hope. Belonging to Christ is not presented as a reason to become careless.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin, Wesley and the USCCB notes agree that Moses is honored as faithful, while Jesus holds the greater place as Son. The comparison brings together continuity in God’s purpose and the Son’s greater authority.

They differ on one detail of the house picture. Calvin and Wesley identify Christ with its divine builder. The USCCB commentary emphasizes God as founder and Jesus as the Son placed over God’s household. Both explanations distinguish the faithful servant from the Son over the household.

Calvin and Wesley both take the call to hold firmly seriously. How that relates to final perseverance is explained more fully in the warning that follows in verses 12–15.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John 5:45–47 records Jesus saying that Moses wrote about Him. Hebrews likewise describes Moses’ service as testimony to what would be spoken later. The faithful servant’s work leads toward the Son.

Ephesians 2:19–22 describes believers as members of God’s household, built together with Christ as the cornerstone and becoming a dwelling for God by the Spirit. Hebrews’ house picture belongs within that purpose of bringing people together under the Son.
