Passage

Ezekiel 47:1-6

How does a trickle from the temple become a river?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then the man brought me back to the entrance of the temple, and I saw water flowing from under the threshold of the temple toward the east (for the temple faced east). The water was coming down from under the south side of the temple, south of the altar. 2 Next he brought me out through the north gate and led me around the outside to the outer gate facing east, and there I saw the water trickling out from the south side.

3 As the man went eastward with a measuring line in his hand, he measured off a thousand cubits and led me through ankle-deep water. 4 Then he measured off a thousand cubits and led me through knee-deep water. Again he measured a thousand cubits and led me through waist-deep water. 5 Once again he measured off a thousand cubits, but now it was a river that I could not cross, because the water had risen and was deep enough for swimming—a river that could not be crossed on foot. 6 “Son of man, do you see this?” he asked. Then he led me back to the bank of the river.

What is the context?

Water flows from beneath the temple’s threshold toward the east, passing south of the altar. Ezekiel is taken outside by the north gate and brought around to see it emerging near the closed east gate.

The guide measures successive stretches of a thousand cubits and leads him through the water. First it reaches his ankles, then his knees and waist, and finally it is too deep to cross on foot.

The increasing depth prepares for the explanation that follows: this water carries life and healing from the sanctuary into the land. The guide’s question calls Ezekiel to notice what he has been shown.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry reads the growing stream as the spread of Christ’s gospel and the life-giving work of the Spirit. He also draws a lesson about the increasing depth of understanding as people attend to God’s word.

Cambridge emphasizes the vision’s restoration of the land as well as its spiritual significance: God’s presence supplies what His people need. The distinction is whether the stream is treated chiefly as a picture of the gospel or as part of the vision’s renewal of the land itself.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In John 7:37–39, Jesus invites the thirsty to come to Him and drink, speaking of living water flowing from the believer. John explains that He is speaking about the Spirit whom believers would receive after Jesus was glorified.

This is a connection through the living-water promise, rather than a claim that John identifies Ezekiel’s river as the sole source of Jesus’ words. The wider story shows life coming through Christ and the gift of His Spirit, reaching beyond an outward supply of water.
