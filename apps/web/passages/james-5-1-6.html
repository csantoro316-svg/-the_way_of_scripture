Passage

James 5:1-6

What does God see when wealth is built on another person’s suffering?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Come now, you who are rich, weep and wail over the misery to come upon you. 2 Your riches have rotted and moths have eaten your clothes. 3 Your gold and silver are corroded. Their corrosion will testify against you and consume your flesh like fire. You have hoarded treasure in the last days.

4 Look, the wages you withheld from the workmen who mowed your fields are crying out against you. The cries of the harvesters have reached the ears of the Lord of Hosts. 5 You have lived on earth in luxury and self-indulgence. You have fattened your hearts in the day of slaughter. 6 You have condemned and murdered the righteous, who did not resist you.

What is the context?

The warning about proud plans now becomes a warning to the unjust rich. James pictures wealth decaying and testifying against its possessors. What had seemed to offer security instead becomes evidence of how they have lived.

The accusations are concrete: withheld wages, luxury and self-indulgence, condemnation and the killing of the righteous. The harvesters’ cries have reached the Lord of Hosts. Their lack of earthly protection does not mean their suffering has escaped His notice.

The warning concerns wealth joined with exploitation and disregard for God. It does not say that every possession is evil or that every rich person has committed each offense listed.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley both read the passage as a severe warning to oppressors and an encouragement to those suffering under them. Wesley sees the righteous person’s suffering as including Christ; Calvin emphasizes the innocent poor harmed by the powerful.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Deuteronomy 24:14–15 forbids withholding a needy worker’s wages and warns that the worker may cry to God. Malachi 3:5 names defrauding laborers among the wrongs the Lord of Hosts will judge. James continues that account of God’s justice: power and wealth do not place someone beyond His hearing or judgment.
