Passage

Genesis 45:9-15

Joseph Sends for His Father

Berean Standard Bible

9 Now return quickly to my father and tell him, ‘This is what your son Joseph says: God has made me lord of all Egypt. Come down to me without delay. 10 You shall settle in the land of Goshen and be near me—you and your children and grandchildren, your flocks and herds, and everything you own. 11 And there I will provide for you, because there will be five more years of famine. Otherwise, you and your household and everything you own will come to destitution.’

12 Behold! You and my brother Benjamin can see that I, Joseph, am the one speaking with you. 13 Tell my father about all my splendor in Egypt and everything you have seen. And bring my father down here quickly.”

14 Then Joseph threw his arms around his brother Benjamin and wept, and Benjamin wept as they embraced. 15 Joseph kissed each of his brothers as he wept over them. And afterward his brothers talked with him.

What is the context?

Joseph tells his brothers to hurry home and bring Jacob to Egypt. He offers a place in Goshen where the whole household, its children, grandchildren, and livestock can be near him. There are five more years of famine, and he intends to provide for them rather than leave them to be reduced to destitution.

He points to what the brothers, including Benjamin, can see and hear for themselves: Joseph is speaking with them. They are to tell their father about his position and everything they have witnessed, then bring him without delay. The news of his life comes with a practical invitation for the family's future.

Joseph embraces Benjamin, and both weep. He then kisses all his brothers and weeps over them. Finally they talk with him. The men who were unable to answer when he first disclosed his identity can now speak within an openly restored family relationship.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly see reconciliation expressed through affection and concrete care. Wesley's notes on Genesis 45:8, 12 emphasize Joseph's desire to comfort his father, assure the family of his identity, and provide for them during the remaining famine. His authority is being used to bring them near and sustain them.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Joseph's invitation offers a Christian parallel between being welcomed and being cared for by the one who has gone ahead. Wesley's note on Genesis 45:12 connects Joseph's desire to have his family with him with Christ's desire for His people to be with Him.

In John 14:1–3, Jesus promises to prepare a place and welcome His disciples into His presence. Joseph's invitation concerns a real household's survival in Egypt; Jesus' promise reaches beyond that immediate need. The shared pattern helps explain the warmth of salvation: rescue includes welcome, belonging, and life with the One who provides it.
