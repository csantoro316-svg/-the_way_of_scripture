Passage

1 Corinthians 12:4-7

Why does God give different gifts to different people?

Berean Standard Bible

4 There are different gifts, but the same Spirit. 5 There are different ministries, but the same Lord. 6 There are different ways of working, but the same God works all things in all people. 7 Now to each one the manifestation of the Spirit is given for the common good.

What is the context?

Paul repeats the contrast between variety and a common source. There are different gifts, ministries and ways of working, but the same Spirit, Lord and God. Differences in service do not require a divided church or competing spiritual loyalties.

The word for gift is charisma (KHAH-ris-mah), χάρισμα, a gift of grace; see Thayer’s entry. Paul’s language directs attention to what God gives, rather than to an achievement that makes its recipient superior to someone else.

Verse 7 states the purpose: what the Spirit makes evident through each person is given for the common good. A gift is not simply private property or a badge of importance. How it helps other people belongs to understanding what it is for.

These verses establish the source and purpose of the variety before Paul lists examples. They do not require every believer to perform the same task, or make a person with a conspicuous gift more valuable than someone serving less visibly.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the variety of gifts as serving the good of one community under God. Wesley’s notes on verses 4–7 distinguish gifts, service and the effects God produces, while the USCCB note on verses 4–6 connects all three with God’s gracious work. Their shared purpose is more than personal spiritual distinction.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 4:7–16 connects the giving of gifts with the ascended Christ and the building up of His body. People are equipped for service so the community grows toward unity, maturity and the knowledge of the Son of God.

That gives the gifts a direction beyond accomplishing isolated tasks. God is forming a people who grow in truth and love under Christ, with each part contributing to the whole. The differences are meant to help the body mature together, rather than keep some members permanently dependent on a few gifted people.

What is our position?

In What Is the True Church?, we understand the Spirit’s gifts as given for the common good, with every believer sharing responsibility to serve. We therefore read this passage as calling the whole community to use what God gives for one another’s benefit. This statement does not settle which particular gifts continue today or how each should be practiced.
