Passage

Ezekiel 43:10-12

Why must the people see the temple’s design?

Berean Standard Bible

10 As for you, son of man, describe the temple to the people of Israel, so that they may be ashamed of their iniquities. Let them measure the plan, 11 and if they are ashamed of all they have done, then make known to them the design of the temple—its arrangement and its exits and entrances—its whole design along with all its statutes, forms, and laws. Write it down in their sight, so that they may keep its complete design and all its statutes and may carry them out. 12 This is the law of the temple: All its surrounding territory on top of the mountain will be most holy. Yes, this is the law of the temple.

What is the context?

Ezekiel must describe the temple so that the people become ashamed of their sins. The vision is intended to change their response to the Lord, not simply to satisfy their interest in a building.

If they are ashamed of what they have done, he is to show them the whole arrangement and its laws, writing it down in their sight. They are to keep what they are shown and carry it out.

The central rule is holiness: the surrounding territory on the mountain is to be most holy. The ordered design teaches the people to take the Lord’s presence and instructions seriously.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry connects the showing of God’s gifts through Christ with shame for sin and readiness to understand His redeeming love more fully. Wesley emphasizes the people seeing the glory their wrongdoing had ruined.

Both readings recognize that the vision calls for repentance and a changed life, rather than admiration without obedience.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 2:1–11 explains that God’s kindness, patience and restraint are meant to lead people to repentance. They do not make persistent wrongdoing safe or excuse a person who judges others while doing the same things.

The connection helps explain why a vision of restoration can expose sin so deeply. God’s purpose includes turning people back to Him through His goodness as well as warning them through judgment. Receiving hope is a reason to respond faithfully to Him.
