Passage

Matthew 13:1-9

The Sower and the Different Soils

Berean Standard Bible

1 That same day Jesus went out of the house and sat by the sea. 2 Such large crowds gathered around Him that He got into a boat and sat down, while all the people stood on the shore. 3 And He told them many things in parables, saying, “A farmer went out to sow his seed.

4 And as he was sowing, some seed fell along the path, and the birds came and devoured it. 5 Some fell on rocky ground, where it did not have much soil. It sprang up quickly because the soil was shallow. 6 But when the sun rose, the seedlings were scorched, and they withered because they had no root. 7 Other seed fell among thorns, which grew up and choked the seedlings.

8 Still other seed fell on good soil and produced a crop—a hundredfold, sixtyfold, or thirtyfold. 9 He who has ears, let him hear.”

What is the context?

Jesus leaves the house and teaches a large crowd from a boat, with the people standing on the shore. He begins with a farmer scattering seed. The scene turns attention to what happens as the seed reaches different kinds of ground.

The Greek word for parable is παραβολή, parabolē (pah-rah-boh-LAY), a comparison or story that illustrates something. Thayer's entry includes these sayings about God's kingdom. Jesus uses familiar things to invite His hearers to consider a deeper question.

The same sowing has different outcomes. Seed on the path is eaten by birds. Seed on shallow rocky ground grows quickly but withers in the sun. Seed among thorns is choked. Seed on good ground produces a crop, with different amounts of fruit.

Jesus closes by calling people to hear. The story invites more than recognition of a farming scene. His explanation in verses 18–23 will connect the soils with how people receive the message of the kingdom. The question of listening runs through both the story and the conversation that follows.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a picture of different responses to God's message and the importance of fruitful hearing. Wesley on Matthew 13:3–8 emphasizes the obstacles that keep hearers from bearing fruit. The Catholic Catechism, 546, likewise treats the soil image as a question Jesus puts to His hearers about their response to His word. The good harvest matters alongside the warnings about what can prevent it.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The seed picture belongs within the Bible's confidence that God's word accomplishes His purpose. Isaiah 55:8–13 compares His word with rain that brings growth and provides seed and food. That is a related picture of God's effective word, rather than a claim that every hearer responds in the same way.

In 1 Corinthians 3:5–9, Paul describes himself and Apollos as servants who plant and water, while God gives the growth. The larger work belongs to God, even as people faithfully take part in it. Different responses do not make the message worthless or put its final outcome entirely in the messenger's hands.
