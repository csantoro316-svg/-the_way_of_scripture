Passage

Genesis 30:1-8

Dan and Naphtali

Berean Standard Bible

1 When Rachel saw that she was not bearing any children for Jacob, she envied her sister. “Give me children, or I will die!” she said to Jacob.

2 Jacob became angry with Rachel and said, “Am I in the place of God, who has withheld children from you?”

3 Then she said, “Here is my maidservant Bilhah. Sleep with her, that she may bear children for me, so that through her I too can build a family.”

4 So Rachel gave Jacob her servant Bilhah as a wife, and he slept with her, 5 and Bilhah conceived and bore him a son. 6 Then Rachel said, “God has vindicated me; He has heard my plea and given me a son.” So she named him Dan.

7 And Rachel’s servant Bilhah conceived again and bore Jacob a second son. 8 Then Rachel said, “In my great struggles, I have wrestled with my sister and won.” So she named him Naphtali.

What is the context?

Rachel sees Leah bearing children and becomes envious. Her demand that Jacob give her children provokes his anger, and he replies that he cannot take God's place. The scene shows how the sisters' unequal circumstances have become a painful conflict within the marriage.

Rachel gives Bilhah, her maidservant, to Jacob as a wife so that she can build a family through her. Bilhah bears two sons, but the account records Rachel naming them and explaining what they mean to her. Bilhah's own thoughts are not reported.

With Dan, Rachel says God has vindicated her and heard her plea. With Naphtali, she speaks of wrestling with her sister and winning. Her explanations show how strongly the rivalry shapes her response to the births. The narrator records what she believes God has done without declaring that God endorses her competition with Leah.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the scene as showing the harm envy brings into the household and the attempt to secure children through a servant, recalling Sarah and Hagar. Wesley's notes on Genesis 30:1–3 emphasize the rivalry expressed in Rachel's words and naming of the sons.
