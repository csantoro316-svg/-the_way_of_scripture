Passage

Daniel 6:25-28

What does Darius announce about Daniel’s God?

Berean Standard Bible

25 Then King Darius wrote to the people of every nation and language throughout the land: “May your prosperity abound. 26 I hereby decree that in every part of my kingdom, men are to tremble in fear before the God of Daniel: For He is the living God, and He endures forever; His kingdom will never be destroyed, and His dominion will never end. 27 He delivers and rescues; He performs signs and wonders in the heavens and on the earth, for He has rescued Daniel from the power of the lions.”

28 So Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius and the reign of Cyrus the Persian.

What is the context?

Darius writes to people of every nation and language in his dominion. He orders reverence for Daniel’s God and explains why: He is living and enduring, His kingdom cannot be destroyed, and His rule will never end.

The king points to the rescue from the lions as a demonstration of God’s power to deliver and to perform wonders in heaven and on earth. The chapter that began with a decree threatening prayer ends with a proclamation honoring the God to whom Daniel prayed.

Daniel continues to prosper under Darius and Cyrus. The human rulers change, but the testimony at the close centers on the God whose dominion remains.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry takes the proclamation as a public acknowledgment of God’s power while distinguishing an announcement about God from a life of faithfulness to Him. Cambridge emphasizes Daniel’s continuing prosperity after his deliverance.

The conclusion points to the enduring kingdom of the living God, beyond the changing fortunes of His servant or any earthly king.
