Passage

2 Corinthians 3:1-3

What makes the Corinthians a letter from Christ?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Are we beginning to commend ourselves again? Or do we need, like some people, letters of recommendation to you or from you? 2 You yourselves are our letter, inscribed on our hearts, known and read by everyone. 3 It is clear that you are a letter from Christ, the result of our ministry, written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts.

What is the context?

Paul has just described speaking sincerely before God. He now asks whether he needs to start recommending himself again or supply letters of recommendation to the people who already know his work.

The Corinthians themselves are his letter. He carries them in his heart, and their shared life is something others can see. He then takes the image further: they are a letter from Christ, brought about through the ministry but written by the Spirit of the living God.

The contrast moves from ink to the Spirit and from stone tablets to human hearts. Paul is not claiming to have produced changed people by his own skill. He points to God’s work among them as evidence of what the ministry has served.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the letter as a picture of people whose lives show Christ’s work through the Spirit. Wesley’s notes on verses 1–3 and the USCCB notes on verses 1–3 both connect the image with Paul’s relationship to the congregation and with God’s work in their hearts. The picture does not mean the Corinthians have replaced Christ Himself or become a new written Scripture.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ezekiel 36:22–28 promises cleansing, a new heart and God’s Spirit within His people. The promise is addressed to Israel and includes God making His holy name known among the nations.

Paul’s image of the Spirit writing on human hearts draws on that kind of promised inward renewal. The connection shows that God’s purpose involves more than outward identification with a community: He is at work changing His people so their life makes Him known. It does not require erasing the original promise’s address to Israel.
