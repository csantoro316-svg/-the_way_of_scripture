Passage

Revelation 2:1-7

Can a church defend the truth and still lose its first love?

Berean Standard Bible

1 “To the angel of the church in Ephesus write: These are the words of Him who holds the seven stars in His right hand and walks among the seven golden lampstands. 2 I know your deeds, your labor, and your perseverance. I know that you cannot tolerate those who are evil, and you have tested and exposed as liars those who falsely claim to be apostles. 3 Without growing weary, you have persevered and endured many things for the sake of My name.

4 But I have this against you: You have abandoned your first love. 5 Therefore, keep in mind how far you have fallen. Repent and perform the deeds you did at first. But if you do not repent, I will come to you and remove your lampstand from its place. 6 But you have this to your credit: You hate the works of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate.

7 He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches. To the one who overcomes, I will grant the right to eat from the tree of life in the Paradise of God.

What is the context?

Ephesus was a Roman port city with a renowned sanctuary of Artemis; see UNESCO’s account of the archaeological site. Acts 19:8–12, 23–41 records Paul’s ministry there and the conflict stirred by tradesmen whose livelihood depended on Artemis worship.

Jesus addresses the church as the One who holds the stars and walks among the lampstands. He knows its labor, perseverance and testing of people who falsely claim to be apostles. Its refusal to accept evil and its endurance for His name receive real praise.

His complaint concerns its abandoned first love. He calls for remembrance, repentance and a return to the deeds done at first. The warning about removing the lampstand concerns the church’s place as His witness. The letter does not report how this community later responded.

Jesus also commends its rejection of the Nicolaitans’ works. The group appears again in Revelation 2:14–15, beside the warning about idolatry and sexual immorality. The appeal then reaches everyone able to hear what the Spirit says to the churches, with a promise of the tree of life to the one who overcomes.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley understands the first love as the warmth and vigor of love the church had earlier. John Gill explicitly includes love for God, Christ and one another. Both see the remedy as a real return to faithful action, rather than simply remembering an earlier feeling.

Readings differ over whether the seven churches also represent successive periods of history. Gill reads Ephesus as representing the apostolic age. Wesley explicitly rejects a sequence of ages and understands the seven churches as examples of conditions found throughout the church in every age. The direct address to the historic church and its continuing relevance are recognized in both readings.

The Nicolaitans’ precise identity is uncertain. Wesley suggests a connection with Nicolas in Acts 6:5; the USCCB note cautions that there is little evidence for that connection. The letter gives little detail about the group’s origins.

Gill understands the tree of life as a picture of Christ giving life to His people. Wesley emphasizes its promise of eternal life with God. These explanations share the hope of life given by Christ, while explaining the picture in different ways.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The tree of life appears in Genesis 2:8–9, with access to it barred after human rebellion in Genesis 3:22–24. In Revelation 22:1–5, it appears again beside the river from God’s throne, where the curse is gone and His servants see His face. Christ’s promise to Ephesus belongs to that larger hope: the life with God lost through sin is the life He holds out to His people.

What is our position?

Our study The True Church According to Jesus places Christ at the foundation of the church and identifies self-giving love as the mark of His people. Alongside the call in The Berean Standard to test teaching against Scripture, this gives us the pattern for reading Ephesus: care for truth and lived love belong together in following Christ.
