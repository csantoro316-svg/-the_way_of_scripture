Passage

Acts 18:7

What makes Titus Justus’s home a place for the message?

Berean Standard Bible

7 So Paul left the synagogue and went next door to the house of Titus Justus, a worshiper of God.

What is the context?

After opposition in the synagogue, Paul moves next door to the house of Titus Justus, described as a worshiper of God. The location changes, but the teaching continues. The following verse reports belief by Crispus and his household and by many Corinthians.

Justus’s description points to reverence for God. The account makes a private home part of the setting in which people hear the message; it does not describe a special construction or ceremony that turns the building into a dwelling God needs.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this move as the opening of another place for teaching after synagogue opposition. Hospitality and reverence help make room for the message; the passage does not require that all believers meet in the same kind of building.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The house next door becomes part of the continuing witness in Corinth. The following vision promises the Lord’s presence and further work in the city, and Paul remains to teach. 1 Corinthians 3:5–9 later describes the Corinthians themselves as God’s field and building: the lasting work concerns the people He brings to faith and helps to grow.

What is our position?

Worship as the Early Church Knew It directly discusses Acts 18:7. It emphasizes Justus’s reverence for God and the way his home becomes a setting for Christian fellowship. The focus is on people who honor God, rather than on a building regarded as sacred in itself.
