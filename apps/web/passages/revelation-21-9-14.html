Passage

Revelation 21:9-14

Why does the city bear the names of Israel and the apostles?

Berean Standard Bible

9 Then one of the seven angels with the seven bowls full of the seven final plagues came and said to me, “Come, I will show you the bride, the wife of the Lamb.” 10 And he carried me away in the Spirit to a mountain great and high, and showed me the holy city of Jerusalem coming down out of heaven from God, 11 shining with the glory of God. Its radiance was like a most precious jewel, like a jasper, as clear as crystal.

12 The city had a great and high wall with twelve gates inscribed with the names of the twelve tribes of Israel, and twelve angels at the gates. 13 There were three gates on the east, three on the north, three on the south, and three on the west. 14 The wall of the city had twelve foundations bearing the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb.

What is the context?

One of the angels who carried the final bowls invites John to see the bride, the wife of the Lamb. He then shows John the holy city coming down from God. The city shines with God’s glory, with a radiance John compares to a clear and precious jewel.

The invitation recalls chapter 17, where an angel with one of the bowls showed John Babylon. The new scene sets another city and another relationship before him: the holy city belongs to the Lamb.

Twelve angels stand at twelve gates, arranged three on each side. The gates bear the names of Israel’s twelve tribes, and the city’s twelve foundations bear the names of the Lamb’s twelve apostles. The vision joins these names within one city; John does not list the individual names or identify a particular apostle’s place in the foundations.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The bride and city are closely linked in the main readings. The Catholic Catechism 1045 identifies the holy city and bride with the community redeemed in Christ. The USCCB notes likewise read the description as a picture of the church. Walvoord understands it as a real dwelling place for God’s people across the ages, described with the beauty and relationship of a bride.

Wesley connects the apostolic foundations with the faith delivered through the apostles. These readings give different weight to the city as a place and as a picture of its people, but they recognize the importance of its relationship with God and the Lamb.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ezekiel 48:30–35 also describes gates named after Israel’s tribes, three on each side, and ends by naming the city for the LORD’s presence there. The repeated pattern connects the city John sees with God’s earlier promise to dwell among His people.

Ephesians 2:19–22 describes believers as members of God’s household, built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets with Christ as the cornerstone. The purpose is a dwelling place for God in His Spirit. Revelation’s named foundations belong within that larger picture of God gathering a people around Christ.

The tribal names also recall the covenants, promises and human descent of the Messiah through Israel in Romans 9:4–5. The vision remembers that history as it presents the Lamb’s city.
