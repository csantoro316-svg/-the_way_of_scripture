Passage

Genesis 25:1-6

Abraham and Keturah

Berean Standard Bible

1 Now Abraham had taken another wife, named Keturah, 2 and she bore him Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah.

3 Jokshan was the father of Sheba and Dedan. And the sons of Dedan were the Asshurites, the Letushites, and the Leummites.

4 The sons of Midian were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah.

All these were descendants of Keturah.

5 Abraham left everything he owned to Isaac. 6 But while he was still alive, Abraham gave gifts to the sons of his concubines and sent them away from his son Isaac to the land of the east.

What is the context?

Before closing Abraham's story, Genesis records his children through Keturah. Six sons are named, followed by descendants of Jokshan and Midian. Abraham's family extends beyond Isaac and Ishmael, even though the main account will continue through Isaac.

Abraham gives gifts to the sons of his concubines while he is alive and sends them east, away from Isaac. Isaac receives the main inheritance. These different arrangements keep his place as heir distinct while showing that Abraham also provides something for his other sons.

This fits the distinction God had already made in Genesis 17:19–21: He would establish the covenant through Isaac. The additional children do not replace that promise or divide it into competing family lines. Genesis gives these arrangements without explaining every motive behind the marriage or the timing of each child's birth.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as distinguishing Isaac's inheritance from the provision made for Abraham's other sons. Wesley's notes on Genesis 25:5–6 hold those responsibilities together: preserving Isaac's place does not mean giving the other children nothing.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The distinction between family descent and the promised inheritance becomes important in the New Testament. Galatians 3:26–29 says that people who belong to Christ are Abraham's offspring and heirs according to the promise. The family story eventually reaches people from different nations through faith in Christ.

The Bible also speaks of that inheritance as something believers still await. Ephesians 1:13–14 describes the Holy Spirit as its pledge, and Hebrews 9:15 connects the promised eternal inheritance with Christ's death. These passages develop the wider inheritance theme; they do not turn each of Keturah's children into a hidden picture of someone rejected by God.
