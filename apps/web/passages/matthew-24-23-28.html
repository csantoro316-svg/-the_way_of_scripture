Passage

Matthew 24:23-28

Do Not Chase Claims of a Hidden Christ

Berean Standard Bible

23 At that time, if anyone says to you, ‘Look, here is the Christ!’ or ‘There He is!’ do not believe it. 24 For false Christs and false prophets will appear and perform great signs and wonders to deceive even the elect, if that were possible. 25 See, I have told you in advance.

26 So if they tell you, ‘There He is, in the wilderness,’ do not go out, or, ‘Here He is, in the inner rooms,’ do not believe it. 27 For just as the lightning comes from the east and flashes as far as the west, so will be the coming of the Son of Man. 28 Wherever there is a carcass, there the vultures will gather.

What is the context?

Jesus follows the warning about great distress with another warning about deception. Reports that the Christ is here or there must not draw His followers away. False christs and false prophets will offer great signs and wonders, with deception so powerful that even God's chosen people are named as its target, if that were possible.

Jesus emphasizes that He has warned them beforehand. Claims about the wilderness or hidden rooms are to be refused. His comparison with lightning then points in another direction: the coming of the Son of Man will not depend on someone's private report of a concealed savior.

The passage ends with the picture of vultures gathering where a carcass lies. A carcass is a dead body; the picture is stark, and readers differ over exactly how it completes the warning. Luke 17:23–24, 37 also contains the warning against chasing reports, the lightning comparison, and the carcass saying. Those connections matter more than turning the birds into an unverified code for a modern event.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the warning to mean that impressive wonders do not by themselves establish a claimant as Christ. Readers differ over the final picture.

The US Catholic bishops' note on 24:26–28 links the vultures with the clear, recognizable character of the coming. Wesley on 24:28 sees a warning of judgment on Jerusalem, associating the birds with Rome's eagles. Calvin on 24:28 takes the gathering as a comparison calling scattered believers to remain united to Christ, while Walvoord's explanation sees judgment following corruption as naturally as vultures gather at a dead body. These are different explanations of the picture; none requires accepting a private claim that Jesus has appeared in a hidden place.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The New Testament places deceptive wonders beside the final defeat of evil. 2 Thessalonians 2:8–12 describes powerful deception and the lawless one's destruction through Christ's appearing. God's purpose is not overturned by the strength of a false claim or the spectacle attached to it.

The birds gathering around the dead also have a parallel in Revelation 19:17–21, where the defeat of the beast and false prophet is followed by birds consuming those killed in judgment. 2 Thessalonians 1:6–10 likewise joins Christ's appearing with judgment on evil and relief for His afflicted people. These are related pictures of His victory, rather than proof that only one reading of Matthew's saying is possible.

The true Christ is also the center of God's gathering purpose. Ephesians 1:7–10 connects forgiveness through His blood with God's plan to bring all things in heaven and on earth together in Him. That wider purpose gives the warning its positive direction: His people belong to the Savior through whom God brings His work to completion.
