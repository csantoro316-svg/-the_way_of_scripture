Passage

Mark 3:31-35

Who Belongs to Jesus’ Family?

Berean Standard Bible

31 Then Jesus’ mother and brothers came and stood outside. They sent someone in to summon Him, 32 and a crowd was sitting around Him. “Look,” He was told, “Your mother and brothers are outside, asking for You.”

33 But Jesus replied, “Who are My mother and My brothers?” 34 Looking at those seated in a circle around Him, He said, “Here are My mother and My brothers! 35 For whoever does the will of God is My brother and sister and mother.”

What is the context?

Jesus' mother and brothers arrive and send for Him from outside. Their arrival resumes the family story introduced in verses 20–21. A crowd sits around Him, and He uses the message about His relatives to ask who belongs to His family.

Looking at those around Him, He calls the person who does God's will His brother, sister, and mother. Luke 8:19–21 describes that response as hearing God's word and carrying it out. Jesus offers a place of belonging that reaches beyond birth and ancestry.

The saying does not make ordinary care for relatives worthless. Its point is that a family connection to Jesus cannot replace responding to God. Nor does Mark tell us everything Mary or each brother privately believes at this moment. The emphasis falls on the open invitation to do God's will.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand Jesus' central saying as describing His spiritual family. They differ over the relationship of the brothers standing outside. The Catholic Church teaches in Catechism 499–500 that Mary remained a virgin and these brothers are close relatives, rather than other children of hers.

The Orthodox Church in America's account of Joseph presents the Orthodox tradition that they are Joseph's children from an earlier marriage. An Orthodox Presbyterian Church answer instead reads them as children born to Mary and Joseph after Jesus, and therefore His half-brothers. That is the explanation in this church resource, not a view held by every Protestant interpreter. These different family trees leave the central saying clear: Jesus welcomes those who do God's will.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus' family language points toward the relationship His saving work brings. Hebrews 2:10–18 says that He shares our humanity, brings God's children to glory, and is not ashamed to call them brothers. He comes close enough to suffer, make atonement, and help those who are tempted.

This family also crosses divisions between peoples. Ephesians 2:11–22 describes Jews and Gentiles brought near through Christ's blood, reconciled through the cross, and welcomed into God's household with access to the Father by one Spirit. The belonging Jesus describes grows into a people united through Him.
