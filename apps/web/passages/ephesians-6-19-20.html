Passage

Ephesians 6:19-20

Why does Paul ask others to pray for his courage?

Berean Standard Bible

19 Pray also for me, that whenever I open my mouth, words may be given me so that I will boldly make known the mystery of the gospel, 20 for which I am an ambassador in chains. Pray that I may proclaim it fearlessly, as I should.

What is the context?

Paul has just called for persevering prayer for all God’s people. He now asks the readers to include him, naming his need for words and boldness to proclaim the gospel.

He is an ambassador in chains: a messenger of Christ whose imprisonment is real. His request concerns how he will speak within that situation. The need for prayer is shared by the messenger and the community he serves.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as a call to persistent prayer for fellow believers and for the clear, courageous proclamation of the gospel. Wesley’s notes on verses 18–20 explain prayer in the Spirit through the Spirit’s influence and emphasize intercession. The USCCB note on 6:10–20 also gives prayer a central place in the call to stand firm. The passage names both the broad concern for all God’s people and Paul’s particular request.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 4:23–31 also connects opposition, shared prayer and bold gospel witness. The believers ask for courage as well as God’s work of healing and signs, and the account ends with Spirit-filled proclamation.

This provides a supported parallel to Paul’s request. God’s people take part in the spread of the gospel through their dependence on Him and their prayers for those who speak.

What is our position?

Our study What Does Prayer Look Like? uses Paul’s request here as an example of prayer for faithfulness under pressure. He asks for words and courage to make the gospel known while in chains. The study emphasizes seeking God’s strength to carry out His purposes in the circumstances we face.
