Passage

Daniel 1:17-21

Who gives the wisdom that prepares the four for service?

Berean Standard Bible

17 To these four young men God gave knowledge and understanding in every kind of literature and wisdom. And Daniel had insight into all kinds of visions and dreams. 18 Now at the end of the time specified by the king, the chief official presented them to Nebuchadnezzar. 19 And the king spoke with them, and among all the young men he found no one equal to Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. So they entered the king’s service. 20 In every matter of wisdom and understanding about which the king consulted them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters in his entire kingdom. 21 And Daniel remained there until the first year of King Cyrus.

What is the context?

The account names God as the source of the four young men’s knowledge and understanding. Daniel also receives insight into visions and dreams, preparing the reader for what follows in the book.

At the end of their training, the king finds none equal to Daniel and his three friends. They enter his service, and he finds them greatly superior to the magicians and enchanters he consults.

The chapter ends by looking ahead to Daniel remaining until the first year of Cyrus. The captive youth’s life of service will extend across the fall of the power that brought him to Babylon. His usefulness and the Lord’s favor coexist with a life spent far from home.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley emphasize God giving wisdom and making the four fit for useful service. Cambridge understands the final mention of Cyrus as marking Daniel’s continuance through Babylon’s change of rulers, rather than announcing his death then.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

James 1:5–8 directs those who lack wisdom to ask God for it. James 3:13–18 then describes wisdom from above by its fruit: humility, peace, gentleness, mercy and sincerity, in contrast with envy and selfish ambition.

Daniel’s gifts prepare him for a particular royal calling; James addresses the life of Christ’s people more widely. The connection shows that wisdom given by God is more than impressive knowledge or success. It should shape the way a person lives and treats others.
