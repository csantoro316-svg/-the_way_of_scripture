Passage

1 Corinthians 7:29-35

What should remain at the center when life is passing away?

Berean Standard Bible

29 What I am saying, brothers, is that the time is short. From now on those who have wives should live as if they had none; 30 those who weep, as if they did not; those who are joyful, as if they were not; those who make a purchase, as if they had nothing; 31 and those who use the things of this world, as if not dependent on them. For this world in its present form is passing away.

32 I want you to be free from concern. The unmarried man is concerned about the work of the Lord, how he can please the Lord. 33 But the married man is concerned about the affairs of this world, how he can please his wife, 34 and his interests are divided. The unmarried woman or virgin is concerned about the work of the Lord, how she can be holy in both body and spirit. But the married woman is concerned about the affairs of this world, how she can please her husband. 35 I am saying this for your own good, not to restrict you, but in order to promote proper decorum and undivided devotion to the Lord.

What is the context?

Paul places marriage, grief, joy, possessions and use of the world within a short time and a world whose present form is passing away. None should become the final center of a believer’s life. The words about living as though without a wife must be read alongside his earlier commands to honor marital responsibilities, rather than as an instruction to abandon them.

He describes the attention marriage requires: a husband thinks about pleasing his wife, and a wife about pleasing her husband. The unmarried person can have a freedom of attention for serving the Lord. Paul is identifying different responsibilities, not claiming every unmarried person automatically uses time faithfully or that caring for a spouse is wrong.

His purpose is stated in verse 35: their good and undivided devotion to the Lord, rather than placing a restraint on them. The advice invites them to order their lives around Him while being honest about what their commitments require.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as a call to give the Lord first place while recognizing the practical demands of marriage. Wesley’s note on verse 33 explicitly treats provision and care for one’s family as a duty, and Calvin’s comment on verse 35 commends singleness for undistracted devotion while refusing to impose it on everyone.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

A useful parallel appears in Luke 10:38–42, where Mary listens at Jesus’ feet while Martha is distracted by many concerns. Jesus directs attention to what is needed. This is a parallel about attention to Him, not a statement about either woman’s marital status.

In Matthew 6:19–21, Jesus connects lasting treasure with the direction of the heart. Together these passages place Paul’s counsel within a wider call to give one’s life to what endures with God. Present responsibilities matter, but they should be carried out with a heart centered on Him rather than ruled by passing circumstances.
