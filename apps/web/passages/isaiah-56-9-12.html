Passage

Isaiah 56:9-12

What happens when the watchmen care only for themselves?

Berean Standard Bible

9 Come, all you beasts of the field; eat greedily, all you beasts of the forest. 10 Israel’s watchmen are blind, they are all oblivious; they are all mute dogs, they cannot bark; they are dreamers lying around, loving to slumber. 11 Like ravenous dogs, they are never satisfied. They are shepherds with no discernment; they all turn to their own way, each one seeking his own gain: 12 “Come, let me get the wine, let us imbibe the strong drink, and tomorrow will be like today, only far better!”

What is the context?

The welcoming promises give way to a sharp warning. Beasts are invited to devour, while Israel’s watchmen are described as blind, unaware and silent when they should give warning.

The images become increasingly pointed. These guardians are like dogs that cannot bark but remain ravenously hungry. They are also shepherds without discernment, each pursuing his own gain.

Their invitation to drink heavily assumes that tomorrow will simply bring more of the same pleasures. The people charged with vigilance are absorbed in appetite and complacency while danger approaches.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley understand the watchmen and shepherds as leaders and teachers who fail the people entrusted to them. Their neglect, greed and false security belong together.

Henry also applies the warning to leaders in other times and places. The passage exposes what happens when people responsible for warning and care become preoccupied with their own comfort and gain.
