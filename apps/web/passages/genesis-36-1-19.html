Passage

Genesis 36:1-19

The Descendants of Esau

Berean Standard Bible

1 This is the account of Esau (that is, Edom). 2 Esau took his wives from the daughters of Canaan: Adah daughter of Elon the Hittite, Oholibamah daughter of Anah and granddaughter of Zibeon the Hivite, 3 and Basemath daughter of Ishmael and sister of Nebaioth. 4 And Adah bore Eliphaz to Esau, Basemath gave birth to Reuel, 5 and Oholibamah gave birth to Jeush, Jalam, and Korah. These were the sons of Esau, who were born to him in the land of Canaan.

6 Later, Esau took his wives and sons and daughters and all the people of his household, along with his livestock, all his other animals, and all the property he had acquired in Canaan, and he moved to a land far away from his brother Jacob. 7 For their possessions were too great for them to dwell together; the land where they stayed could not support them because of their livestock. 8 So Esau (that is, Edom) settled in the area of Mount Seir.

9 This is the account of Esau, the father of the Edomites, in the area of Mount Seir.

10 These are the names of Esau’s sons: Eliphaz son of Esau’s wife Adah, and Reuel son of Esau’s wife Basemath.

11 The sons of Eliphaz were Teman, Omar, Zepho, Gatam, and Kenaz. 12 Additionally, Timna, a concubine of Esau’s son Eliphaz, gave birth to Amalek. These are the grandsons of Esau’s wife Adah.

13 These are the sons of Reuel: Nahath, Zerah, Shammah, and Mizzah. They are the grandsons of Esau’s wife Basemath.

14 These are the sons of Esau’s wife Oholibamah (daughter of Anah and granddaughter of Zibeon) whom she bore to Esau: Jeush, Jalam, and Korah.

15 These are the chiefs among the sons of Esau. The sons of Eliphaz the firstborn of Esau: Chiefs Teman, Omar, Zepho, Kenaz, 16 Korah, Gatam, and Amalek. They are the chiefs of Eliphaz in the land of Edom, and they are the grandsons of Adah.

17 These are the sons of Esau’s son Reuel: Chiefs Nahath, Zerah, Shammah, and Mizzah. They are the chiefs descended from Reuel in the land of Edom, and they are the grandsons of Esau’s wife Basemath.

18 These are the sons of Esau’s wife Oholibamah: Chiefs Jeush, Jalam, and Korah. They are the chiefs descended from Esau’s wife Oholibamah, the daughter of Anah.

19 All these are the sons of Esau (that is, Edom), and they were their chiefs.

What is the context?

After Isaac's burial, Genesis records Esau's family before returning to Jacob's household. The repeated identification of Esau as Edom connects Jacob's brother with the people who will later appear as Israel's neighbors. The list names his wives, sons, grandsons, and the chiefs descended from them.

Esau moves his household and possessions away from Jacob because their livestock and other property have become too great for them to live together. The explanation recalls Abraham and Lot's separation in Genesis 13. Here, too, the practical needs of large households help explain a lasting division in where the families settle.

Esau settles in the region of Mount Seir. His descendants include Teman and Amalek, names that recur in later biblical history. This family record gives those later names a place in the story rather than treating the peoples around Israel as strangers with no connection to the patriarchs.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as the family history explaining Edom's relationship to Israel and its settlement in Seir. Wesley's introduction and notes on Genesis 36:1–10 also connect Esau's growing family with the earlier promise that Rebekah's sons would become two nations.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

God's choice of Jacob to carry the covenant promise does not mean that Esau's family is outside His concern. Deuteronomy 2:4–6 calls Esau's descendants Israel's brothers and says God has given Seir to Esau. Israel is told to respect that possession and pay for the food and water it uses.

That later command adds something important to this genealogy: belonging to the chosen family does not give Israel permission to take whatever it wants from its neighbors. God's purpose for His people includes recognizing His dealings with other families and respecting the limits He sets.
