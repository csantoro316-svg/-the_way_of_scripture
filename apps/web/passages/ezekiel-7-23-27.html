Passage

Ezekiel 7:23-27

What happens when peace and guidance cannot be found?

Berean Standard Bible

23 Forge the chain, for the land is full of crimes of bloodshed, and the city is full of violence. 24 So I will bring the most wicked of nations to take possession of their houses. I will end the pride of the mighty, and their holy places will be profaned. 25 Anguish is coming! They will seek peace, but find none. 26 Disaster upon disaster will come, and rumor after rumor. Then they will seek a vision from a prophet, but instruction from the priests will perish, as will counsel from the elders. 27 The king will mourn, the prince will be clothed with despair, and the hands of the people of the land will tremble. I will deal with them according to their conduct, and I will judge them by their own standards. Then they will know that I am the LORD.’”

What is the context?

The command to forge a chain follows the charge that the land is full of bloodshed and the city full of violence. The coming occupation will take houses, humble the mighty and profane places the people treated as holy.

They will seek peace and find none. Disaster and rumor arrive one after another, and the usual sources of guidance fail: the sought-for vision is absent, priestly instruction perishes, and elders have no counsel to give.

The collapse reaches rulers and ordinary people alike. The king mourns, the prince despairs and the people tremble. The Lord again declares that He will deal with them according to their conduct, and they will know that He is the Lord.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry connects the chain with the consequences of breaking God’s commands: those who refused His rule find themselves bound under judgment. Wesley understands it as the binding of captives.

Both emphasize the complete loss of confidence when God’s judgment arrives. Political position and the offices associated with guidance do not supply the peace that the people seek.

The final recognition of the Lord answers the refusal that brought the chapter to this point. His word, rather than their former confidence, establishes the outcome.
