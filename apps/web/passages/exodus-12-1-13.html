Passage

Exodus 12:1-13

How were the households to prepare for the Passover?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Now the LORD said to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, 2 “This month is the beginning of months for you; it shall be the first month of your year. 3 Tell the whole congregation of Israel that on the tenth day of this month each man must select a lamb for his family, one per household. 4 If the household is too small for a whole lamb, they are to share with the nearest neighbor based on the number of people, and apportion the lamb accordingly. 5 Your lamb must be an unblemished year-old male, and you may take it from the sheep or the goats. 6 You must keep it until the fourteenth day of the month, when the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel will slaughter the animals at twilight.

7 They are to take some of the blood and put it on the sides and tops of the doorframes of the houses where they eat the lambs. 8 They are to eat the meat that night, roasted over the fire, along with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. 9 Do not eat any of the meat raw or cooked in boiling water, but only roasted over the fire—its head and legs and inner parts. 10 Do not leave any of it until morning; before the morning you must burn up any part that is left over. 11 This is how you are to eat it: You must be fully dressed for travel, with your sandals on your feet and your staff in your hand. You are to eat in haste; it is the LORD’s Passover. 12 On that night I will pass through the land of Egypt and strike down every firstborn male, both man and beast, and I will execute judgment against all the gods of Egypt. I am the LORD. 13 The blood on the houses where you are staying will be a sign; when I see the blood, I will pass over you. No plague will fall on you to destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt.

What is the context?

God marks the coming rescue by making this month the beginning of Israel’s year. Each household is to select an unblemished year-old male from the sheep or goats on the tenth day and keep it until the fourteenth, when the animals are to be killed at twilight. Smaller households are to share with their nearest neighbor according to how much they can eat.

The Hebrew word for unblemished, tamim, describes something complete and sound. Brown–Driver–Briggs lists its use for sacrificial animals here. The required condition of the animal is part of God’s instructions for the meal.

The blood is to be put on the sides and top of the doorframe. Inside, the household is to eat the animal roasted, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, ready for travel. Anything left until morning must be burned. The meal prepares them to leave when God delivers them.

God says He will strike Egypt’s firstborn and execute judgment against its gods. The blood on the houses is the sign He appoints: when He sees it, He will spare those houses. The instructions join a meal, a promised protection and readiness for departure.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared Christian reading sees Passover as God’s appointed rescue and as pointing toward Christ. That connection is affirmed in the Catholic Catechism 1340, the Westminster Confession 7.5 and Wesley’s notes, while the New Testament itself provides the foundation for it.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 Corinthians 5:7 explicitly identifies Christ as the Passover lamb who has been sacrificed. The connection between this rescue and Jesus is the New Testament’s own explanation.

1 Peter 1:18–21 describes redemption through Christ’s precious blood, comparing Him with a lamb without blemish or spot. The required soundness of the Passover animal finds a larger connection in the purity of Christ and His saving death.

Hebrews 11:28 remembers Moses keeping Passover and applying the blood by faith. God gives the promise of protection, and faith acts on His instructions. That trust belongs to the account of rescue itself.

What is our position?

Our study Should We Seek Signs From God? understands the blood on the Passover doorposts as a sign whose meaning God had already given through His word. The instructions and promise came first. The people were called to trust that promise and act on what He had said.
