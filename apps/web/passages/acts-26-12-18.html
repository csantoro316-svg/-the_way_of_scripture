Passage

Acts 26:12-18

What is Paul sent to do after Jesus confronts him?

Berean Standard Bible

12 In this pursuit I was on my way to Damascus with the authority and commission of the chief priests. 13 About noon, O king, as I was on the road, I saw a light from heaven, brighter than the sun, shining around me and my companions. 14 We all fell to the ground, and I heard a voice say to me in Hebrew, ‘Saul, Saul, why do you persecute Me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.’ 15 ‘Who are You, Lord?’ I asked. ‘I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting,’ the Lord replied.

16 ‘But get up and stand on your feet. For I have appeared to you to appoint you as a servant and as a witness of what you have seen from Me and what I will show you. 17 I will rescue you from your own people and from the Gentiles. I am sending you to them 18 to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins and an inheritance among those sanctified by faith in Me.’

What is the context?

Paul recalls traveling to Damascus with authority from the chief priests when a light brighter than the midday sun surrounds him and his companions. They fall to the ground, and he hears Jesus ask why he is persecuting Him.

The saying about kicking against the goads uses an image from handling working animals. κέντρον (kentron, KEN-tron) can mean an iron goad used to urge an animal onward; Thayer’s entry explains the saying as dangerous and futile resistance. An animal kicking against the point injures itself. Jesus is confronting Paul’s resistance and the harm it is doing.

Jesus appoints him as a servant and witness, with further things to be shown to him. The mission reaches both his own people and the Gentiles. Its aim is to open eyes, turn people from darkness to light and from Satan’s power to God, and bring forgiveness and a share among those who belong to Him through faith in Jesus.

The word sanctified describes being made holy and brought near to God. Thayer’s entry for the word specifically includes Acts 26:18 in that setting. Paul is being sent to people who need this change; his testimony credits Jesus with the authority and purpose of the mission.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the commission as a work of salvation and changed allegiance, not simply a transfer of information. Paul bears witness, while the forgiveness and belonging offered through faith are God’s gifts. The promised rescue does not mean he will avoid every arrest or period of suffering; he is recounting it while in chains.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Colossians 1:12–14 gives a closely related account: the Father brings people into the inheritance of His people in the light, rescues them from darkness and brings them into the kingdom of His Son, in whom there is forgiveness. The mission entrusted to Paul serves that movement into life under Christ.

Ephesians 1:7–14 joins forgiveness, God’s purpose to bring all things together in Christ, and the inheritance secured by the promised Spirit. The horizon is larger than one encounter on a road: God is gathering a people in Christ who share the future He promises.
