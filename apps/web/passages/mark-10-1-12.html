Passage

Mark 10:1-12

Marriage and the Creator’s Purpose

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then Jesus left that place and went into the region of Judea, beyond the Jordan. Again the crowds came to Him and He taught them, as was His custom. 2 Some Pharisees came to test Him. “Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife?” they inquired. 3 “What did Moses command you?” He replied. 4 They answered, “Moses permitted a man to write his wife a certificate of divorce and send her away.” 5 But Jesus told them, “Moses wrote this commandment for you because of your hardness of heart.

6 However, from the beginning of creation, ‘God made them male and female.’ 7 ‘For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, 8 and the two will become one flesh.’ So they are no longer two, but one flesh. 9 Therefore what God has joined together, let man not separate.”

10 When they were back inside the house, the disciples asked Jesus about this matter. 11 So He told them, “Whoever divorces his wife and marries another woman commits adultery against her. 12 And if a woman divorces her husband and marries another man, she commits adultery.”

What is the context?

Jesus leaves the previous setting and teaches the crowds in the region of Judea beyond the Jordan. Some Pharisees test Him by asking whether a man may divorce his wife. Jesus asks them what Moses commanded, and they answer by referring to permission to give a certificate of divorce.

Deuteronomy 24:1–4 describes a divorce followed by another marriage and forbids the first husband from taking the woman back after that second marriage ends. Jesus explains the permission in relation to hard hearts, then takes His hearers further back to creation. The permission does not describe everything God intended marriage to be.

Jewish discussion of the grounds for divorce is recorded in Mishnah Gittin 9:10. It preserves a stricter reading associated with Shammai's school and a broader one associated with Hillel's, even including a spoiled meal. That recorded debate helps explain the subject's contested nature, without telling us the private motives of each person questioning Jesus here.

Jesus brings together Genesis 1:27 and 2:24: God made man and woman, and the two become one flesh. He grounds His answer in a union God establishes, rather than in ways to dismiss a spouse. His command not to separate that union expresses the seriousness of the bond.

In private, He tells the disciples that divorce followed by another marriage involves adultery against the spouse. Mark records the warning about both a husband and a wife. The account does not include Matthew's exception for sexual immorality, so that exception has not been inserted into the quotation above. Christians consider this passage alongside Matthew 19:1–9 and 1 Corinthians 7:10–16 when asking how the teaching applies.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian traditions commonly read this as a call to lasting faithfulness in marriage. They differ over how Mark's unqualified warning relates to other passages and the circumstances in which a later marriage can be accepted.

The Catholic Church teaches that a valid marriage between baptized spouses, once consummated, cannot be dissolved by human power. Its Catechism 2382–2386 distinguishes that bond from legitimate separation or a civil divorce needed to protect rights, children, or inheritance. It also distinguishes an unjustly abandoned spouse from one responsible for breaking the marriage.

Reformed churches following the Westminster Confession 24.5–6 allow an innocent spouse to divorce and remarry after adultery, and recognize deliberate desertion that cannot be remedied as another ground for ending the marriage. This reading considers Matthew's exception and Paul's teaching about an unbelieving spouse's departure alongside Mark.

The Orthodox Church in America's guidelines, section F, uphold lifelong marriage while allowing a bishop to consider remarriage after divorce with repentance and attention to the people involved. The United Methodist Social Principles, Marriage: Divorce, recognize that divorce can become necessary when a marriage is destructive or beyond repair, and do not rule out remarriage. These are the stated approaches of those churches; they do not imply that every member or other church shares every detail.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The creation text Jesus uses becomes an explicit picture of Christ's relationship with His people. In Ephesians 5:25–33, Paul repeats Genesis 2:24 and connects the one-flesh union with Christ and the church. Christ gives Himself for her, makes her holy, and nourishes and cares for her as His own body. The larger purpose reaches beyond defending an institution: marriage is called to reflect faithful, self-giving love.

A related picture appears in Hosea 3:1–5, where the prophet's renewed love for an unfaithful wife is explicitly compared with the LORD's love for Israel despite their turning to other gods. That is a parallel about God's faithfulness, rather than a separate rule settling every divorce case. Together these passages show why covenant faithfulness matters within the Bible's account of God's work to reclaim His people.
