Passage

Philippians 2:19-24

Why does Paul want to send Timothy?

Berean Standard Bible

19 Now I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, that I also may be cheered when I learn how you are doing. 20 I have nobody else like him who will genuinely care for your needs. 21 For all the others look after their own interests, not those of Jesus Christ. 22 But you know Timothy’s proven worth, that as a child with his father he has served with me to advance the gospel.

23 So I hope to send him as soon as I see what happens with me. 24 And I trust in the Lord that I myself will come soon.

What is the context?

Paul hopes in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy so he can learn how the Philippians are doing. This travel plan is an expression of care: he wants someone to attend to their needs and bring reliable news.

He describes Timothy as a person who genuinely cares for them and whose service they already know. He has worked alongside Paul to advance the gospel, like a child with his father.

The contrast with those pursuing their own interests continues the chapter’s earlier call to look to others’ interests. Timothy gives the community a concrete example of that concern.

Paul intends to send him when his own circumstances become clearer, and also expresses trust in the Lord that he himself will come. The plans are held within that dependence on Christ.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as both a practical travel plan and a commendation of faithful care. Wesley’s notes on verses 19–22 explain Timothy’s concern and service alongside Paul. The USCCB notes also place the notice within Paul’s arrangements to visit and encourage the community. The emphasis is on proven character, concern for others and the gospel work they share.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

2 Timothy 2:1–2 calls Timothy to be strong in Christ’s grace and entrust what he has learned to faithful people able to teach others. This adds a wider purpose to the pattern of Paul and Timothy serving together.

Careful teaching and faithful service can continue through others. Timothy’s mission to the Philippians belongs within that larger pattern of the gospel being passed on and communities being strengthened beyond one person’s immediate presence.
