Passage

Genesis 31:1-21

Jacob Flees from Laban

Berean Standard Bible

1 Now Jacob heard that Laban’s sons were saying, “Jacob has taken away all that belonged to our father and built all this wealth at our father’s expense.” 2 And Jacob saw from the countenance of Laban that his attitude toward him had changed.

3 Then the LORD said to Jacob, “Go back to the land of your fathers and to your kindred, and I will be with you.”

4 So Jacob sent word and called Rachel and Leah to the field where his flocks were, 5 and he told them, “I can see from your father’s countenance that his attitude toward me has changed; but the God of my father has been with me. 6 You know that I have served your father with all my strength. 7 And although he has cheated me and changed my wages ten times, God has not allowed him to harm me. 8 If he said, ‘The speckled will be your wages,’ then the whole flock bore speckled offspring. If he said, ‘The streaked will be your wages,’ then the whole flock bore streaked offspring. 9 Thus God has taken away your father’s livestock and given them to me.

10 When the flocks were breeding, I saw in a dream that the streaked, spotted, and speckled males were mating with the females. 11 In that dream the angel of God said to me, ‘Jacob!’ And I replied, ‘Here I am.’

12 ‘Look up,’ he said, ‘and see that all the males that are mating with the flock are streaked, spotted, or speckled; for I have seen all that Laban has done to you. 13 I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed the pillar and made a solemn vow to Me. Now get up, leave this land at once, and return to your native land.’”

14 And Rachel and Leah replied, “Do we have any portion or inheritance left in our father’s house? 15 Are we not regarded by him as outsiders? Not only has he sold us, but he has certainly squandered what was paid for us. 16 Surely all the wealth that God has taken away from our father belongs to us and to our children. So do whatever God has told you.”

17 Then Jacob got up and put his children and his wives on camels, 18 and he drove all his livestock before him, along with all the possessions he had acquired in Paddan-aram, to go to his father Isaac in the land in Canaan.

19 Now while Laban was out shearing his sheep, Rachel stole her father’s household idols. 20 Moreover, Jacob deceived Laban the Aramean by not telling him that he was running away. 21 So he fled with all his possessions, crossed the Euphrates, and headed for the hill country of Gilead.

What is the context?

Laban's sons accuse Jacob of gaining wealth at their father's expense, and Laban's attitude toward him changes. The LORD tells Jacob to return to his own land and relatives, renewing the promise to be with him. Jacob calls Rachel and Leah into the field to explain the situation and the direction he has received.

He recounts his hard work, Laban's changing wages, and God's protection. In his account of the dream, the angel of God identifies Himself as the God of Bethel, reminds Jacob of the pillar and vow, and tells him to return. The command joins the present move with the promise at the beginning of his journey.

Rachel and Leah agree. They describe their father's treatment of them as that of outsiders and say that what God has given Jacob belongs to them and their children. They tell him to do what God has directed. Jacob then gathers the family, livestock, and possessions and leaves while Laban is away shearing sheep.

Rachel secretly takes her father's household idols. The Hebrew word is תְּרָפִים, tĕrāp̄îm (teh-rah-FEEM); Brown–Driver–Briggs describes these as objects of reverence associated with divination, noting the portable figures here. Jacob does not know she has taken them. He also conceals the departure from Laban, creating the setting for pursuit and confrontation.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the return as obedience to God's direction amid growing hostility from Laban's household. Wesley's notes on Genesis 31:3, 19 distinguish Jacob's departure, which he regards as a prudent escape from danger, from Rachel's theft of the idols.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The return begins with God's promise at Bethel: He would remain with Jacob and bring him back. Now the God who has seen his treatment directs him to leave. The account shows faithfulness expressed through protection, provision, and a call to move when the time has come.

A later, larger departure will carry this same theme. In Exodus 3:7–10, God sees His people's affliction, hears their cries, and acts to bring them out toward the promised land. Jacob's departure is not a detailed prediction of every event in the Exodus, but both accounts reveal the God who sees oppression and keeps His promise to His people.
