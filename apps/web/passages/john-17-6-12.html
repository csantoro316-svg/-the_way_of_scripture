Passage

John 17:6-12

Why does Jesus ask the Father to keep His disciples?

Berean Standard Bible

6 I have revealed Your name to those You have given Me out of the world. They were Yours; You gave them to Me, and they have kept Your word. 7 Now they know that everything You have given Me comes from You. 8 For I have given them the words You gave Me, and they have received them. They knew with certainty that I came from You, and they believed that You sent Me.

9 I ask on their behalf. I do not ask on behalf of the world, but on behalf of those You have given Me; for they are Yours. 10 All I have is Yours, and all You have is Mine; and in them I have been glorified. 11 I will no longer be in the world, but they are in the world, and I am coming to You. Holy Father, protect them by Your name, the name You gave Me, so that they may be one as We are one. 12 While I was with them, I protected and preserved them by Your name, the name You gave Me. Not one of them has been lost, except the son of destruction, so that the Scripture would be fulfilled.

What is the context?

Jesus now speaks about the people who have received the Father’s words through Him. They have believed that He came from the Father and have kept the word entrusted to them. He places their life and calling within what the Father has given.

His prayer is directed toward their coming situation. He is returning to the Father, and they will remain in the world. He asks the Holy Father to protect them by His name so that they may be one. The unity He desires is grounded in the relationship between the Father and Son.

He also speaks of His care for them and of the loss of the son of destruction, the betrayer. The reference recalls Judas’s departure and betrayal in John 13:18–30. The prayer names the loss and the fulfillment of Scripture without treating the betrayal as a surprise that overturns Jesus’ work.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the prayer as expressing Jesus’ care for His followers as He departs. The reference to Judas raises a real difference about his place among those given to Jesus.

Calvin on verse 12 understands Judas as outwardly sharing the appointed role of an apostle, without belonging to God’s truly chosen flock. Wesley on verse 12 reads the exception as one of those given to Jesus being lost. The Reformed Westminster Confession, chapter 17, teaches final perseverance for those truly saved; the Methodist Articles of Religion, Article XII, allow for departing from grace and returning through repentance. These documents supply the broader doctrinal setting, while the individual notes address Judas directly.

The focus on the disciples in verse 9 should not be turned into a command never to pray for anyone else. 1 Timothy 2:1–4 explicitly calls for prayer for all people. Here Jesus is asking for what His followers need in the calling He has given them.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The disciples’ preservation serves the mission they will carry forward. In John 18:8–9, Jesus asks that His companions be allowed to go, and John connects their release with His word that He has not lost those given to Him. The narrative shows His care as the arrest approaches.

His continuing care is also described in Hebrews 7:23–25, where His permanent priesthood and intercession belong to His saving work. The disciples will lose His visible presence at their table, but they are not being left without His concern.

What is our position?

Our study What Does Prayer Look Like? treats Jesus’ request that His followers be kept in the Father’s name and united as a pattern for praying for others. It directs our attention to their life with God and faithful formation. Here the priority is that they remain His people and carry out their calling together, rather than making easier circumstances the main goal.
