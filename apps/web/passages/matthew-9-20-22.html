Passage

Matthew 9:20-22

Faith Reaches Out to Jesus

Berean Standard Bible

20 Suddenly a woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years came up behind Him and touched the fringe of His cloak. 21 She said to herself, “If only I touch His cloak, I will be healed.” 22 Jesus turned and saw her. “Take courage, daughter,” He said, “your faith has healed you.” And the woman was healed from that very hour.

What is the context?

This healing takes place while Jesus is on His way to the synagogue leader's daughter in Matthew 9:18–26. A woman with twelve years of bleeding comes up behind Him and touches His cloak. Matthew records what she says to herself, followed by Jesus' assurance that her faith has healed her.

Her condition has a place within the Law's cleansing requirements. Leviticus 15:25–30 describes a prolonged discharge as making a woman unclean until the required cleansing after it ends. This is a statement about her condition under the Law, rather than proof that she has committed a particular sin.

The fringe she touches is described by κράσπεδον, kraspedon (KRAS-peh-don), a garment's edge or tassel. Thayer's Greek Lexicon connects the word here with the tassels prescribed in Numbers 15:37–41, reminders of God's commandments. Matthew says she touches Jesus' garment; he does not describe the clothing as having power apart from Him.

The word translated healed comes from σῴζω, sōzō (SOH-zoh), which can mean rescue, save, or make well. Thayer's Greek Lexicon specifically lists Matthew 9:21–22 under restoration from disease. Here a real bodily healing is in view; the same word can carry a wider saving meaning in other settings.

Mark 5:25–34 and Luke 8:43–48 add that the bleeding stops when she touches Him and that she then tells Him what has happened. Her initial approach contains no spoken request to Jesus. His response is personal: He addresses her as daughter and sends her in peace in those fuller accounts.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the encounter as a response of faith to Jesus' power and mercy, rather than healing produced independently by a garment. The Catholic Catechism, 2616, includes her among those whose faith reaches Jesus without a spoken request, and Wesley on Matthew 9:22 places His encouragement within her fearful approach and cleansing.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The peace Jesus gives her in Mark 5:34 belongs alongside a wider theme of welcome through Him. Romans 5:1–2 describes peace with God and access to grace through Jesus Christ, received through faith. Her welcome gives a personal picture beside Paul's wider teaching about peace with God.

Titus 3:3–7 describes God's saving mercy through new birth and renewal by the Spirit, leading to the hope of eternal life. The woman's bodily restoration provides a helpful picture beside that larger rescue: people come in need, and the gift of restored life comes from God.

What is our position?

On the woman's healing in verses 20–22, Prayer and Faith emphasizes that she reaches toward Jesus in trust without first voicing a request to Him. Jesus credits her faith. The study's position is that the focus belongs on trust in Him, rather than on the wording, frequency, or eloquence of a request.
