Passage

Romans 16:17-20

How should the church respond to teaching that harms it?

Berean Standard Bible

17 Now I urge you, brothers, to watch out for those who create divisions and obstacles that are contrary to the teaching you have learned. Turn away from them. 18 For such people are not serving our Lord Christ, but their own appetites. By smooth talk and flattery they deceive the hearts of the naive. 19 Everyone has heard about your obedience, so I rejoice over you. But I want you to be wise about what is good and innocent about what is evil. 20 The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.

What is the context?

Paul interrupts the greetings with a warning about people creating divisions and obstacles contrary to the teaching the believers have learned. He tells them to watch for these people and turn away from them.

The danger includes self-serving motives hidden by smooth speech and flattery. Paul connects their discernment with the obedience he already rejoices over: they are to be wise about good and innocent about evil.

The warning ends in hope. The God of peace will crush Satan under their feet, and Paul speaks the grace of Jesus over them. Their vigilance belongs within confidence in God’s victory, rather than confidence in their own ability to outmaneuver every deceiver.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as a call to discern and resist destructive teaching. Paul’s standard concerns what is contrary to the teaching received and the harm caused through deception. The warning belongs alongside his earlier call for patience in disputed practices, so disagreement itself is not enough to identify someone as a deceiver.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The defeat of the serpent in Genesis 3:15 provides a biblical parallel to the promise of Satan’s defeat here. The shared theme is God overcoming the evil that threatens His people.

Hebrews 2:14–18 connects deliverance from the devil’s power with Christ sharing our humanity and dying for us. Romans assures a community facing deception that the God of peace will prevail. The promise rests in His work, giving them reason to remain faithful while resisting evil.
