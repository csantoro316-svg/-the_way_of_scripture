Passage

Ephesians 4:11-16

How does the whole church grow toward Christ?

Berean Standard Bible

11 And it was He who gave some to be apostles, some to be prophets, some to be evangelists, and some to be pastors and teachers, 12 to equip the saints for works of ministry and to build up the body of Christ, 13 until we all reach unity in the faith and in the knowledge of the Son of God, as we mature to the full measure of the stature of Christ.

14 Then we will no longer be infants, tossed about by the waves and carried around by every wind of teaching and by the clever cunning of men in their deceitful scheming. 15 Instead, speaking the truth in love, we will in all things grow up into Christ Himself, who is the head. 16 From Him the whole body, fitted and held together by every supporting ligament, grows and builds itself up in love through the work of each individual part.

What is the context?

These verses explain the purpose of the gifts introduced in verses 7–10. The risen Christ gives apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers so His people are prepared for ministry and His body is built up.

The word translated equip is katartismos (kah-tar-tis-MOSS). Thayer’s lexicon connects it with a word for strengthening or perfecting, also used for training and instruction. In this sentence, the preparation has a purpose: God’s people are to serve.

Paul describes the goal as all of us growing in faith and knowledge of the Son of God, with Christ as the measure of maturity. He then shows what that growth looks like. Believers become steadier when faced with misleading teaching and learn to speak truth with love.

The body picture brings the paragraph together. Christ is the head, and growth comes from Him as each part contributes. Paul is describing the health of the whole community, including what its members say, believe and do for one another.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as Christ providing people who teach, guide and prepare His church for service. The USCCB note on verse 12 explicitly understands the whole people of God as those being equipped for ministry.

Christians differ over how the apostles’ and prophets’ ministries continue. The Catholic Catechism, paragraphs 857–862, distinguishes the apostles’ unique role as witnesses and foundation from the pastoral care continued by bishops as their successors.

Among evangelical Christians, Dallas Theological Seminary’s doctrinal statement, article XVI, teaches that the foundational offices of apostle and prophet ended with the New Testament period, while evangelists, pastors and teachers continue. It also affirms that God can perform miracles today.

The Pentecostal Assemblies of God distinguishes the original apostles’ unique authority from ongoing apostolic work, such as missionary work and starting churches, without requiring an apostolic title or office. It also affirms prophecy as a continuing gift, with messages tested by the church against Scripture. These are distinct accounts of continuing ministry, rather than a single view shared by all Christians.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 8:29 describes God’s purpose for His people as being conformed to the image of His Son. The growth described here belongs to that larger purpose: God is shaping people to resemble Christ.

1 Corinthians 12:21–27 develops the same body picture by emphasizing the care owed to members who seem weaker and the sharing of suffering and joy. This parallel helps explain why maturity includes making room for the contribution and needs of others. A community’s growth is more than the knowledge or prominence of a few members.

What is our position?

Our study What Is the True Church? recognizes the leadership roles described here. Their purpose is to prepare believers to take part in ministry. Leaders serve and equip the community, while the whole community shares responsibility for its life and service. The study calls for active participation by believers and affirms the need for spiritually mature leadership.
