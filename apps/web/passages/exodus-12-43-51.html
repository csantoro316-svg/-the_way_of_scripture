Passage

Exodus 12:43-51

Who could share in the Passover meal?

Berean Standard Bible

43 And the LORD said to Moses and Aaron, “This is the statute of the Passover: No foreigner is to eat of it. 44 But any slave who has been purchased may eat of it, after you have circumcised him. 45 A temporary resident or hired hand shall not eat the Passover. 46 It must be eaten inside one house. You are not to take any of the meat outside the house, and you may not break any of the bones. 47 The whole congregation of Israel must celebrate it. 48 If a foreigner resides with you and wants to celebrate the LORD’s Passover, all the males in the household must be circumcised; then he may come near to celebrate it, and he shall be like a native of the land. But no uncircumcised man may eat of it. 49 The same law shall apply to both the native and the foreigner who resides among you.”

50 Then all the Israelites did this—they did just as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron. 51 And on that very day the LORD brought the Israelites out of the land of Egypt by their divisions.

What is the context?

The final instructions define participation in Passover. An outsider does not eat it simply by being present or employed in the household. Circumcision is required, including for a purchased servant who is to participate.

A foreign resident may join the observance when all the males of his household are circumcised. He is then to be treated like a native of the land, with the same law applying to both. The instructions establish a boundary for the meal and also a way for the foreigner to share in it.

The animal must be eaten within one house, with no meat carried outside and no bones broken. The whole congregation is to observe Passover. The chapter ends with Israel doing as commanded and the LORD bringing them out of Egypt by their divisions.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared historical reading understands circumcision as the covenant sign required for participation, while recognizing that a foreigner could enter and be received on the same terms. Calvin and Wesley both emphasize that joining the covenant people opens participation to the outsider.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John 19:31–36 draws attention to Jesus’ legs remaining unbroken and identifies this as fulfillment of Scripture. This gives a clear connection with the Passover requirement that the lamb’s bones remain unbroken, alongside Paul’s explicit description of Christ as our Passover lamb in 1 Corinthians 5:7.

The reception of the foreigner also belongs within a wider biblical story. Ephesians 2:11–19 describes Gentiles who were once outsiders being brought near through Christ’s blood and made members of God’s household. Colossians 2:11–14 speaks of a circumcision performed by Christ, joining it with burial and resurrection with Him, faith and forgiveness. The New Testament develops belonging to God’s people around the work of Christ.
