Passage

Ezra 6:13-18

How is the completed temple dedicated?

Berean Standard Bible

13 In response, Tattenai the governor of the region west of the Euphrates, Shethar-bozenai, and their associates diligently carried out what King Darius had decreed. 14 So the Jewish elders built and prospered through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo. They finished building according to the command of the God of Israel and the decrees of Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes, kings of Persia. 15 And this temple was completed on the third day of the month of Adar, in the sixth year of the reign of King Darius.

16 Then the people of Israel—the priests, the Levites, and the rest of the exiles—celebrated the dedication of the house of God with joy. 17 For the dedication of the house of God they offered a hundred bulls, two hundred rams, four hundred lambs, and a sin offering for all Israel of twelve male goats, one for each tribe of Israel. 18 They also appointed the priests by their divisions and the Levites by their groups to the service of God in Jerusalem, according to what is written in the Book of Moses.

What is the context?

Tattenai and his associates carry out Darius’s instructions. The elders continue building, encouraged by Haggai and Zechariah, and the temple is completed on the third day of Adar in Darius’s sixth year.

The account places God’s command alongside the Persian royal decrees. The people then celebrate the dedication with joy, offering bulls, rams and lambs, together with twelve male goats as a sin offering for all Israel, one for each tribe.

Priests and Levites are assigned to their groups for service according to the Book of Moses. Completion means the restoration of an ordered place of worship, not merely finishing a building.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley emphasizes that the success belongs to God, who both sends the prophets and moves the ruler to support the work. Gill reads the twelve goats as expressing concern for all Israel, including people from beyond Judah and Benjamin.

Readers also discuss why Artaxerxes is named in the summary of royal decrees before the completion date under Darius. Gill considers several possibilities and favors treating the name as another title of Darius; the Cambridge commentary understands it as a reference to later royal help included in the summary. The completion date itself is explicitly given as Darius’s sixth year.
