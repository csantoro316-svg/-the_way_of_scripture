Passage

Isaiah 52:13-15

How can the disfigured Servant be the One God exalts?

Berean Standard Bible

13 Behold, My Servant will prosper; He will be raised and lifted up and highly exalted. 14 Just as many were appalled at Him— His appearance was disfigured beyond that of any man, and His form was marred beyond human likeness— 15 so He will sprinkle many nations. Kings will shut their mouths because of Him. For they will see what they have not been told, and they will understand what they have not heard.

What is the context?

This begins the account of the Servant that continues through chapter 53. The first announcement is His success and great exaltation. The next line describes an appearance so disfigured that people are appalled.

The contrast is central: the One who suffers such humiliation will affect many nations, and kings will fall silent before Him. People who had never been told will see and understand. The suffering described next must be read alongside this announced outcome.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley identify the Servant with Christ and read the humiliation and exaltation together. His suffering does not mean His mission has failed.

They explain the sprinkling image somewhat differently. Henry connects it with Christ’s blood and its continuing saving power; Wesley connects it with His teaching reaching the nations like water. Both understand the closing words as the revelation of saving truth to people who had not previously received it.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 15:20–21 quotes the closing promise when Paul explains his aim to preach where Christ has not been named. He sees the announcement about those who had not heard as a reason to carry the gospel into new places.

This gives the worldwide reach of the Servant’s work a concrete New Testament connection. The One whose suffering astonishes people becomes the subject of good news carried to nations that had not known Him.
