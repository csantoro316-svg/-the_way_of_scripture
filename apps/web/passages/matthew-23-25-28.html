Passage

Matthew 23:25-28

A Clean Appearance Cannot Hide What Is Inside

Berean Standard Bible

25 Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, you hypocrites! You clean the outside of the cup and dish, but inside they are full of greed and self-indulgence. 26 Blind Pharisee! First clean the inside of the cup and dish, so that the outside may become clean as well.

27 Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, you hypocrites! You are like whitewashed tombs, which look beautiful on the outside but on the inside are full of dead men’s bones and every kind of impurity. 28 In the same way, on the outside you appear to be righteous, but on the inside you are full of hypocrisy and wickedness.

What is the context?

Two woes develop the difference between appearance and reality. First, Jesus compares the leaders to a cup and dish cleaned outside but filled inside with greed and self-indulgence. He calls for the inside to be cleaned first, so that the outside becomes clean as well.

Then He compares them to whitewashed tombs: attractive outside, but containing bones and uncleanness within. Numbers 19:11–16 explains the uncleanness associated with contact with a corpse or grave. A later Jewish text, Mishnah Shekalim 1:1, records the marking of graves in Adar. That supplies background for making graves visible; Matthew's own emphasis is the contrast between the tomb's appearance and its contents.

Jesus states the comparison plainly: outward righteousness conceals hypocrisy and wickedness. The two pictures expose a religious life concerned with how it appears while its inner character remains corrupt. Cleaning the outside cannot settle what is wrong within.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand these pictures as a call for inner character to agree with outward conduct. Wesley on 23:25 connects the inner corruption with unjust gain and uncontrolled desires; the US Catholic bishops' notes on 23:25–28 emphasize appearances hiding impurity.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Scripture gives a positive answer to the need for inward cleansing. Psalm 51:6–12 asks God for a clean heart and a willing spirit. In Ezekiel 36:24–28, He promises cleansing, a new heart, and His Spirit leading His people into obedience.

Titus 3:4–8 describes God's mercy bringing new birth and renewal through the Holy Spirit, poured out through Jesus Christ. It then calls those who believe to do good. The larger purpose reaches from the inward change God gives into the visible life of His people.
