Passage

1 Kings 8:12-21

Why does Solomon begin by remembering God’s promise?

Berean Standard Bible

12 Then Solomon declared: “The LORD has said that He would dwell in the thick cloud. 13 I have indeed built You an exalted house, a place for You to dwell forever.” 14 And as the whole assembly of Israel stood there, the king turned around and blessed them all 15 and said: “Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who has fulfilled with His own hand what He spoke with His mouth to my father David, saying, 16 ‘Since the day I brought My people Israel out of Egypt, I have not chosen a city from any tribe of Israel in which to build a house so that My Name would be there. But I have chosen David to be over My people Israel.’

17 Now it was in the heart of my father David to build a house for the Name of the LORD, the God of Israel. 18 But the LORD said to my father David, ‘Since it was in your heart to build a house for My Name, you have done well to have this in your heart. 19 Nevertheless, you are not the one to build it; but your son, your own offspring, will build the house for My Name.’ 20 Now the LORD has fulfilled the word that He spoke. I have succeeded my father David, and I sit on the throne of Israel, as the LORD promised. I have built the house for the Name of the LORD, the God of Israel. 21 And there I have provided a place for the ark, which contains the covenant of the LORD that He made with our fathers when He brought them out of the land of Egypt.”

What is the context?

Solomon responds to the cloud, then turns to bless the gathered people. He gives the LORD the credit for bringing this day about: what God promised to David, He has now carried out.

David wanted to build a house for the LORD’s Name. God welcomed that desire but appointed David’s son to do the building. Solomon now sits on David’s throne and has completed the house, with a place for the covenant ark.

The emphasis is on a promise kept. Solomon’s work has a place in the account because God spoke first. The tablets in the ark also keep Israel’s covenant with the LORD at the center of the dedication.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill read the completed temple as the fulfillment of the promise that David’s son would build it. Henry emphasizes the same direction of thought: Solomon points to what God has done, rather than treating the ceremony simply as a celebration of his own achievement.

Gill understands Solomon’s language about a lasting dwelling in contrast with the movable tabernacle. The words should also be read alongside Solomon’s acknowledgment in verse 27 that even the heavens cannot contain God. A settled place of worship does not make Him small enough to be enclosed in a building.
