Passage

Daniel 4:1-3

Why does the king announce what God has done for him?

Berean Standard Bible

1 King Nebuchadnezzar, To the people of every nation and language who dwell in all the earth: May your prosperity be multiplied. 2 I am pleased to declare the signs and wonders that the Most High God has performed for me. 3 How great are His signs, how mighty His wonders! His kingdom is an eternal kingdom; His dominion endures from generation to generation.

What is the context?

The chapter opens as a proclamation from Nebuchadnezzar to people of every nation and language. He wants them to know the signs and wonders the Most High God has performed for him.

Before telling the experience, he gives its conclusion: God’s kingdom is eternal, and His dominion lasts from generation to generation. The powerful human ruler is about to explain how he learned that his own authority was not ultimate.

The account therefore begins with praise before looking back on warning, humiliation and restoration. Its emphasis is not only what the king suffered, but what he came to acknowledge about God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley read the proclamation as the king making known the mercy and power he experienced. Both connect the opening praise with the changed acknowledgment that closes the chapter.
