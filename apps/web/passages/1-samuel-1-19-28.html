Passage

1 Samuel 1:19-28

What does Hannah do with the son she asked for?

Berean Standard Bible

19 The next morning they got up early to bow in worship before the LORD, and then they returned home to Ramah. And Elkanah had relations with his wife Hannah, and the LORD remembered her. 20 So in the course of time, Hannah conceived and gave birth to a son. She named him Samuel, saying, “Because I have asked for him from the LORD.”

21 Then Elkanah and all his house went up to make the annual sacrifice to the LORD and to fulfill his vow, 22 but Hannah did not go. “After the boy is weaned,” she said to her husband, “I will take him to appear before the LORD and to stay there permanently.” 23 “Do what you think is best,” her husband Elkanah replied, “and stay here until you have weaned him. Only may the LORD confirm His word.” So Hannah stayed and nursed her son until she had weaned him.

24 Once she had weaned him, Hannah took the boy with her, along with a three-year-old bull, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine. Though the boy was still young, she brought him to the house of the LORD at Shiloh. 25 And when they had slaughtered the bull, they brought the boy to Eli. 26 “Please, my lord,” said Hannah, “as surely as you live, my lord, I am the woman who stood here beside you praying to the LORD. 27 I prayed for this boy, and since the LORD has granted me what I asked of Him, 28 I now dedicate the boy to the LORD. For as long as he lives, he is dedicated to the LORD.” So they worshiped the LORD there.

What is the context?

The family worships before leaving Shiloh. Back at home, Hannah conceives and later gives birth to Samuel. The narrator explains the answer in terms of God remembering her. This describes His attention and action on her behalf, rather than suggesting that He had lost track of her.

Hannah connects Samuel’s name with having asked the LORD for him. The Hebrew verb שָׁאַל, sha’al (shah-AHL), means to ask or request. The same root appears again when she dedicates him in verse 28. Brown–Driver–Briggs notes the sense of handing someone over in that verse. What she asked to receive, she now gives into God’s service. The connection with Samuel’s name is wordplay, rather than a simple dictionary translation of the name as “asked of God”; see the name entry.

She first stays home to nurse Samuel, with Elkanah’s agreement. Once he is weaned, she brings him to Shiloh with the offerings. No exact age is given. She identifies herself to Eli and explains that this is the child for whom she prayed. Her dedication is lifelong, and the scene ends in worship.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry emphasizes that caring for the young child at home is itself a faithful responsibility. Hannah’s temporary absence from the annual visit does not cancel her commitment. He also draws out the order of giving: she offers to God a child she first received from Him.

Wesley explains that the older English language of lending Samuel does not mean Hannah plans to reclaim him after a short term. Her commitment is permanent. The BSB expresses this as dedication. Both readings recognize thanksgiving and fulfilled commitment at the center of the scene, rather than a payment that makes God owe her another favor.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The passage shows a parent giving a dearly wanted son into a purpose beyond her household. Christians can compare this costly giving with the much greater gift described in John 3:16–18: God gives His Son so that those who believe in Him may have eternal life.

The comparison has limits. Samuel is dedicated to service; this scene does not describe him dying for anyone’s sins. John identifies Jesus as the Son through whom God saves the world. The shared theme is a beloved son given for a purpose reaching beyond one family, while the saving work belongs to Jesus Himself.

What is our position?

Our study [Prayer and Faith](../studies/prayer-and-faith.html) reads Hannah’s request as an honest desire placed within God’s purposes. She asks for a son and promises his whole life to God’s service. The account says that God remembered her; it does not make the answer a reward for reaching a certain level of persistence or emotion. Samuel’s life will serve a purpose much larger than the relief of Hannah’s sorrow.
