Passage

Acts 13:1-3

How are Barnabas and Saul sent into their next work?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Now in the church at Antioch there were prophets and teachers: Barnabas, Simeon called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene, Manaen (who had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch), and Saul. 2 While they were worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, “Set apart for Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.” 3 And after they had fasted and prayed, they laid their hands on them and sent them off.

What is the context?

Luke names prophets and teachers in the Antioch church: Barnabas, Simeon called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene, Manaen and Saul. He notes that Manaen had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch. The list shows several people serving together without specifying that their order establishes a ranking.

While they are worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit directs them to set apart Barnabas and Saul for work to which He has already called them. The initiative comes from the Spirit, and the community responds through fasting, prayer, laying on hands and sending.

The word translated worshiping is λειτουργέω (leitourgeō, lay-toor-GEH-oh). Thayer’s entry describes service rendered to the Lord, including prayer, teaching and other service. Luke names fasting and prayer here but does not give a complete order of worship or identify every activity taking place.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly see the Spirit initiating the mission and the church responding through prayer and a public sending. Wesley on verse 2 understands this as commissioning people for their appointed work, rather than Saul first becoming an apostle at this moment.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

This sending moves forward the calling already described in Acts 9:15–16. Saul was chosen to carry Jesus’ name before Gentiles, rulers and Israel; the next account will show that witness reaching another place and another hearer.

Galatians 1:15–16 describes Paul’s calling by grace to proclaim God’s Son among the Gentiles. Acts places that personal calling within the praying life and practical support of a community. God’s direction and the help of fellow believers are working together in the mission.

What is our position?

Worship as the Early Church Knew It directly examines the word used in Acts 13:2 as sacred service directed toward the Lord. The study emphasizes active service rather than treating believers as spectators. Here that service is connected with fasting, prayer and responding to the Spirit’s call; Luke does not supply a full list of everything the gathering included.
