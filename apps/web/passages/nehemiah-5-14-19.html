Passage

Nehemiah 5:14-19

Why does Nehemiah decline the governor’s allowance?

Berean Standard Bible

14 Furthermore, from the day King Artaxerxes appointed me to be their governor in the land of Judah, from his twentieth year until his thirty-second year (twelve years total), neither I nor my brothers ate the food allotted to the governor. 15 The governors before me had heavily burdened the people, taking from them bread and wine plus forty shekels of silver. Their servants also oppressed the people. But I did not do this, because of my fear of God. 16 Instead, I devoted myself to the construction of the wall, and all my servants were gathered there for the work; we did not acquire any land.

17 There were 150 Jews and officials at my table, besides the guests from the surrounding nations. 18 Each day one ox, six choice sheep, and some fowl were prepared for me, and once every ten days an abundance of all kinds of wine was provided. But I did not demand the food allotted to the governor, because the burden on the people was so heavy. 19 Remember me favorably, O my God, for all that I have done for this people.

What is the context?

Nehemiah looks back over twelve years as governor and explains that he and his brothers did not take the food allowance assigned to the office. Earlier governors and their servants had burdened the people; he chooses differently because he fears God.

He continues with the wall work, gathers his servants to it and does not acquire land. He also hosts 150 Jews and officials, along with other visitors, without demanding the allowance because the people’s burden is already heavy.

The account gives both his reason and his appeal: consideration for the people shapes his use of office, and he asks God to remember him favorably for what he has done for them.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Gill understand the example as refusing to enrich oneself at the people’s expense. They read the closing prayer as dependence on God’s gracious recognition, rather than a claim that God owes salvation as payment for service. The stated reason for declining the allowance is the weight already resting on the community.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus describes leadership through service in Mark 10:42–45, grounding it in His own coming to serve and give His life as a ransom. The needs of those served take priority over personal advantage.

Paul also distinguishes a right to material support from the choice to set that right aside for the gospel in 1 Corinthians 9:3–18. The broader connection is freely accepting a cost for the good of others. It does not make receiving legitimate support inherently wrong; Paul expressly affirms that support while explaining his own choice.
