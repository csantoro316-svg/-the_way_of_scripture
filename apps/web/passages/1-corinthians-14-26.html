Passage

1 Corinthians 14:26

What should every contribution to a gathering accomplish?

Let all things be done for edification: a closing standard for the whole project.

Berean Standard Bible

26 What then shall we say, brothers? When you come together, everyone has a psalm or a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, or an interpretation. All of these must be done to build up the church.

What is the context?

This verse moves Paul’s discussion from the value of understandable speech to the practical conduct of a gathering. Different people have different contributions, but all are directed toward building up the church.

The following instructions in 1 Corinthians 14:26–33 show that participation includes taking turns, interpretation, testing and knowing when to be silent. Having something to offer does not mean insisting on offering it in any manner one chooses.

The list is not a requirement that every person perform each activity at every meeting. Paul’s concern is that the variety of what is brought should help the gathered people grow.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the verse as directing the church’s different contributions toward a shared benefit. Calvin’s note on verse 26 stresses using each gift for everyone’s good, while the USCCB note on verses 26–33 identifies building up as the governing test.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 Peter 4:8–11 similarly calls each person to use what has been received to serve others as a steward of God’s varied grace. Speaking and serving are to depend on what God supplies so He is glorified through Jesus Christ.

That places the gathering within a life of shared service. A contribution matters because it helps people and honors the God whose grace made it possible.

What is our position?

In Worship as the Early Church Knew It, we understand this verse as the framework for mutual participation in the assembly. Believers bring what God has given them to build one another up in Christ. The purpose is shared growth, rather than a gathering evaluated mainly as a performance or a personal emotional experience.
