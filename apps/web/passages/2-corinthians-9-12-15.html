Passage

2 Corinthians 9:12-15

How can practical help lead people to thank God?

Berean Standard Bible

12 For this ministry of service is not only supplying the needs of the saints but is also overflowing in many expressions of thanksgiving to God. 13 Because of the proof this ministry provides, the saints will glorify God for your obedient confession of the gospel of Christ, and for the generosity of your contribution to them and to all the others. 14 And their prayers for you will express their affection for you, because of the surpassing grace God has given you.

15 Thanks be to God for His indescribable gift!

What is the context?

Paul describes what the collection will accomplish. It supplies the saints’ needs and also leads to many expressions of thanksgiving to God. The material help and the response of worship belong together.

Those receiving the gift will see evidence that the Corinthians’ confession of the gospel is shaping their conduct. Their generosity becomes a visible expression of obedience to Christ and care for other people.

The relationship grows in both directions. The recipients will pray for the Corinthians and feel affection for them because of God’s grace at work among them. The gift does more than move resources: it strengthens the bond between believers.

Paul ends by thanking God for His indescribable gift. The final praise gathers up the whole account, which began with grace given by God and has led to willing service, needs met, prayer and thanksgiving.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly see this as generosity producing thanksgiving and closer fellowship. Wesley’s notes on verses 11–15 and the USCCB note on verses 11–15 both follow the movement from God’s gifts, through service to others, back to praise directed to Him.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 15:25–27 identifies the collection as help for poor believers in Jerusalem and describes Gentile believers sharing material resources after receiving spiritual blessings. That gives these prayers and expressions of affection a wider setting: the gift strengthens ties across communities.

Ephesians 2:14–18 describes Christ bringing Jews and Gentiles near to God in one body. The collection does not create that unity; it gives an everyday expression to it as people help one another and thank their shared Father.
