Passage

Romans 10:13

Who is invited to call on the Lord?

Berean Standard Bible

13 for, “Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.”

What is the context?

Paul quotes Joel after saying that there is no difference between Jew and Greek: the same Lord is Lord of all and gives richly to those who call on Him. Everyone in this promise makes its welcome clear.

The surrounding verses connect calling with believing in Christ and hearing the good news. Calling on the Lord is an expression of reliance on Him, within the message Paul has just described through belief and confession.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this verse as an invitation that crosses the distinction between Jew and Gentile. No ancestry gives someone a separate Lord or a different source of salvation. The verse calls people to trust Him; it does not say that salvation occurs without any response to Him.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Joel 2:28–32 places this promise within the outpouring of God’s Spirit and deliverance associated with the day of the Lord. Paul brings that promise into his announcement of the risen Jesus and the shared welcome to Jews and Gentiles. Calling on the Lord belongs to God’s promised rescue, reaching beyond inherited religious standing.

What is our position?

The Gospel According to Jesus uses this verse to invite people to turn to Jesus in faith and call on Him. The study emphasizes a response of the heart rather than a formula: trusting the Lord who saves, instead of trying to secure salvation through ancestry, moral achievement or religious activity.
