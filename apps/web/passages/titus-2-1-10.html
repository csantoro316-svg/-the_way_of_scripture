Passage

Titus 2:1-10

How should sound teaching shape everyday relationships?

Berean Standard Bible

1 But as for you, speak the things that are consistent with sound doctrine. 2 Older men are to be temperate, dignified, self-controlled, and sound in faith, love, and perseverance.

3 Older women, likewise, are to be reverent in their behavior, not slanderers or addicted to much wine, but teachers of good. 4 In this way they can train the young women to love their husbands and children, 5 to be self-controlled, pure, managers of their households, kind, and submissive to their own husbands, so that the word of God will not be discredited.

6 In the same way, urge the younger men to be self-controlled. 7 In everything, show yourself to be an example by doing good works. In your teaching show integrity, dignity, 8 and wholesome speech that is above reproach, so that anyone who opposes us will be ashamed, having nothing bad to say about us.

9 Slaves are to submit to their own masters in everything, to be well-pleasing, not argumentative, 10 not stealing from them, but showing all good faith, so that in every respect they will adorn the teaching about God our Savior.

What is the context?

Paul contrasts Titus’s teaching with the harmful teaching just described. He addresses older men, older women, younger women and younger men, then people living in slavery. Faithfulness is to become visible across the whole community, rather than remain a concern only for its leaders.

Older believers are called to maturity and self-control. Older women have an active teaching role in helping younger women care for their families and live faithfully. The household language in verse 5 concerns working at and caring for the home (Thayer); the BSB renders it managers of their households.

Titus must model the good work and trustworthy speech he teaches. Slaves are asked to serve faithfully rather than steal or be argumentative. They are enslaved people under masters, so the relationship should not be reduced to an ordinary freely chosen job.

The reasons Paul gives repeatedly concern witness: God’s word is not to be discredited, opponents should have no sound charge against their conduct, and their lives are to make the teaching about God our Savior attractive.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Calvin read the paragraph as practical teaching for the community’s different relationships, with the teacher’s own example included. Older women’s instruction helps younger women put faith into practice; age alone is not a substitute for a faithful life.

Traditions differ over how a wife’s submission relates to shared responsibility. The Southern Baptist Faith and Message, Article XVIII affirms equal worth together with distinct leadership and household roles. Christians for Biblical Equality instead teaches shared marital leadership and responsibility under mutual submission. These are stated approaches to applying household passages, rather than claims that every Christian in either tradition describes every household the same way.

Wesley qualifies obedience to husbands and masters by what is lawful and can be done without sin. His reading does not make a human authority’s every demand morally right. His account of care at home also allows going out for necessity, devotion and mercy. The passage’s instructions should be considered with their actual concern for faithful conduct and the reputation of the message.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In John 13:34–35, Jesus says His disciples’ love for one another will identify them as His followers. The cross-generational care and faithful conduct in this paragraph give that witness everyday form.

1 Peter 2:9–12 places honorable conduct among the nations within the calling of a people brought into God’s light. Paul likewise connects ordinary relationships with the way others hear and see the message about God.
