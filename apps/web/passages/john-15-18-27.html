Passage

John 15:18-27

How will the disciples bear witness when they face hatred?

Berean Standard Bible

18 If the world hates you, understand that it hated Me first. 19 If you were of the world, it would love you as its own. Instead, the world hates you, because you are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world. 20 Remember the word that I spoke to you: ‘No servant is greater than his master.’ If they persecuted Me, they will persecute you as well; if they kept My word, they will keep yours as well. 21 But they will treat you like this because of My name, since they do not know the One who sent Me.

22 If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not be guilty of sin. Now, however, they have no excuse for their sin. 23 Whoever hates Me hates My Father as well. 24 If I had not done among them the works that no one else did, they would not be guilty of sin; but now they have seen and hated both Me and My Father. 25 But this is to fulfill what is written in their Law: ‘They hated Me without reason.’

26 When the Advocate comes, whom I will send to you from the Father—the Spirit of truth who proceeds from the Father—He will testify about Me. 27 And you also must testify, because you have been with Me from the beginning.

What is the context?

The command to love one another is followed by a warning about the hatred the disciples will face. Their connection with Jesus will bring them into the opposition He has already encountered. A servant cannot assume exemption from the treatment given to his master.

Jesus connects the rejection of His words and works with rejection of the Father who sent Him. His statement about their guilt concerns the responsibility of those who have received this witness and rejected it. It does not mean that no one had sinned before He came.

He describes this hatred as fulfilling Scripture. The words have a parallel in Psalm 69:4, where the speaker suffers hostility without cause. The opposition will not put the disciples’ mission outside God’s purpose.

The answer to that opposition includes witness. The Spirit of truth will testify about Jesus, and the disciples must testify because they have been with Him from the beginning. Their firsthand knowledge of Him matters, and their testimony will be supported by the Spirit He sends from the Father.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the warning as preparation for opposition to Christ’s witness. Calvin on verses 20–22 and Wesley on verses 21–22 connect the rejection with ignorance of God and responsibility for rejecting Jesus’ revelation. The warning is not permission to treat every personal dispute as persecution. 1 Peter 4:14–16 distinguishes suffering for Christ from suffering for wrongdoing.

Traditions differ over the Spirit’s eternal relationship to the Father and Son. The Orthodox Church in America’s teaching on the Holy Spirit identifies the Father as the Spirit’s eternal source and rejects adding “and the Son” to that statement in the creed. Its account of spiritual life also affirms that the Spirit is sent into the world through Christ.

The Catholic Church’s Catechism, paragraphs 243–248, teaches that the Spirit is eternally from the Father and Son as one source, while still recognizing the Father as the first origin. The Reformed Westminster Confession, chapter 2.3, and Methodist Articles of Religion, Article IV, also confess the Spirit’s eternal relation to the Father and Son.

This disagreement concerns the Spirit’s eternal relationship within God, which is distinct from His being sent to the disciples in history. These traditions all affirm that the Spirit is divine and bears witness to Jesus. Neither reading treats Him as a creature or denies the sending described in this passage.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus’ appeal to Scripture places the rejection He faces within the biblical story of unjust hostility. The promised Spirit means that rejection will not end the witness to Him.

After the resurrection, Acts 1:6–8 connects the coming of the Spirit with the disciples becoming Jesus’ witnesses from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth. The mission grows beyond the room even as it encounters resistance. The Spirit and the witnesses serve the same purpose: making Jesus known.
