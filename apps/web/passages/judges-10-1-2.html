Passage

Judges 10:1-2

What are we told about Tola?

Berean Standard Bible

1 After the time of Abimelech, a man of Issachar, Tola son of Puah, the son of Dodo, rose up to save Israel. He lived in Shamir, in the hill country of Ephraim. 2 Tola judged Israel twenty-three years, and when he died, he was buried in Shamir.

What is the context?

After Abimelech’s violent rule, Tola rises to save Israel. He is from Issachar and lives in Shamir, in the hill country of Ephraim. He judges Israel for twenty-three years, then dies and is buried in Shamir.

The notice gives his family, location, purpose, and length of service. It records no particular battle, speech, or dramatic sign. His place in the account shows that the work of a judge can be summarized as years of service, rather than only a single military victory.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley understands Tola’s saving work chiefly as wise government that checks disorder and oppression. Henry reads the brief accounts of Tola and Jair as useful, peaceable service that provides fewer dramatic events to recount. These are explanations of the short notice; the text does not give a detailed record of Tola’s administration.
