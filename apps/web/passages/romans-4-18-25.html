Passage

Romans 4:18-25

How does Abraham’s hope point us toward the resurrection?

Berean Standard Bible

18 Against all hope, Abraham in hope believed and so became the father of many nations, just as he had been told, “So shall your offspring be.” 19 Without weakening in his faith, he acknowledged the decrepitness of his body (since he was about a hundred years old) and the lifelessness of Sarah’s womb. 20 Yet he did not waver through disbelief in the promise of God, but was strengthened in his faith and gave glory to God, 21 being fully persuaded that God was able to do what He had promised. 22 This is why “it was credited to him as righteousness.”

23 Now the words “it was credited to him” were written not only for Abraham, 24 but also for us, to whom righteousness will be credited—for us who believe in Him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead. 25 He was delivered over to death for our trespasses and was raised to life for our justification.

What is the context?

Abraham trusts God’s promise of descendants when ordinary expectations give him no reason to hope for a child. He recognizes the condition of his own aged body and Sarah’s womb. His faith does not require pretending those limits are absent.

Paul emphasizes Abraham’s confidence that God can do what He has promised. Genesis also records questions, laughter and missteps along the journey, including Abraham’s questions in Genesis 17:17–18. Paul’s emphasis on faith should be read with that fuller story, rather than turned into a claim that Abraham never faced difficulty or acted wrongly.

The words about righteousness being credited were written for others as well. Paul applies them to those who believe in the God who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead. Jesus was delivered over to death for our trespasses and raised for our justification, our being put right before God.

The connection is trust in the God who gives life. Paul does not give a general promise that every outcome a person strongly wants will happen. Abraham’s confidence answers a promise God actually made.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as faith resting on God’s ability to keep His word even where human resources fail. The USCCB note on verse 20 describes doubts resolved in commitment to the promise; Calvin on verse 20 likewise distinguishes Abraham’s astonished questions from rejecting what God says. These comments allow the strength of Paul’s emphasis to stand alongside the struggles Genesis records.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Paul makes the connection to Christ explicit: the same God who fulfilled His promise in the face of Abraham and Sarah’s inability raised Jesus from the dead. The story of Abraham’s faith is written for those who will trust Him in that greater announcement.

Hebrews 11:17–19 adds another connection from Abraham’s life. When tested with Isaac, he reasons that God can raise the dead, and he receives Isaac back in a figurative sense. That is a scripturally stated comparison, not a claim that Isaac literally died. Across these accounts, hope rests in the God for whom death cannot defeat His promise.
