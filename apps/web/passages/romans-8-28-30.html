Passage

Romans 8:28-30

What is the good God is working toward?

Berean Standard Bible

28 And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose. 29 For those God foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, so that He would be the firstborn among many brothers. 30 And those He predestined, He also called; those He called, He also justified; those He justified, He also glorified.

What is the context?

Paul continues speaking to people who suffer, wait and need help praying. The promise is addressed to those who love God and are called according to His purpose. It does not call every event good. It says God works through all things toward the good He intends for His people.

Verse 29 names that purpose: being made like His Son, who is firstborn among many brothers. The good in view reaches further than immediate comfort or a preferred outcome. It concerns belonging to Christ’s family and sharing His likeness and glory.

Paul links God’s foreknowledge, purpose, calling, justification and glory. The Greek word translated predestined is proorizō (proh-or-ID-zoh), προορίζω, meaning to decide or determine beforehand; see Thayer’s entry. The word identifies a prior purpose. How that purpose relates to human response is part of the difference between Christian readings below.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians broadly agree that God’s grace comes first and that His purpose is to bring His people into Christ’s likeness. Traditions differ over how election and the believer’s continuing response fit together.

Reformed churches following the Westminster Confession, chapter 3, teach that God’s choice of those brought to salvation is fixed and rests on His grace rather than foreseen faith or perseverance. The 1689 Baptist Confession, chapter 3, paragraphs 4–6, gives the same account of election. This reading understands the sequence as assurance that God brings those He chooses through to glory.

Wesley’s note on verse 30 reads the sequence as the way God leads people toward glory while allowing that a believer may turn away before its completion. This reflects the Wesleyan emphasis on continuing in God’s grace; it does not make grace unnecessary or faith a way of earning salvation.

The Catholic Church teaches that God’s eternal plan includes each person’s free response to grace (Catechism 600) and that God predestines no one to hell (Catechism 1037). These teachings set boundaries for a Catholic reading without settling every question about how foreknowledge and election work.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 1:3–14 places adoption, redemption and the promised inheritance within God’s purpose in Christ. Verses 9–10 describe His purpose to bring together all things in heaven and on earth in Christ. Romans adds the personal dimension: the people God calls are to become like His Son. The goal is larger than arranging convenient circumstances; it is a renewed people whose life is centered on Christ.
