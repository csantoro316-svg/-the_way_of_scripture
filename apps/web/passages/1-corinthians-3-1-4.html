Passage

1 Corinthians 3:1-4

What shows that the Corinthians still need to grow?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Brothers, I could not address you as spiritual, but as worldly—as infants in Christ. 2 I gave you milk, not solid food, for you were not yet ready for solid food. In fact, you are still not ready, 3 for you are still worldly. For since there is jealousy and dissension among you, are you not worldly? Are you not walking in the way of man? 4 For when one of you says, “I follow Paul,” and another, “I follow Apollos,” are you not mere men?

What is the context?

Paul has just spoken about receiving understanding through the Spirit. He now tells the Corinthians that he could not address them as spiritually mature. They are brothers and infants in Christ, yet their conduct still follows the world’s pattern.

Milk and solid food picture teaching suited to different stages of growth. Paul gave them what they were ready to receive, but their continuing unreadiness is now a problem. Having received gifts and knowledge has not automatically produced a mature way of living.

His evidence is concrete: jealousy, quarrels and rival claims to Paul or Apollos. Spiritual growth should affect how they treat one another. The issue is not merely whether they can discuss difficult subjects, but whether their shared life reflects what they have received from Christ.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a rebuke of believers whose jealousy and divisions reveal immaturity. Wesley’s notes on verses 1–4 distinguish having gifts from growing in grace, while treating Paul’s teaching as adapted to what his hearers can receive.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 4:11–16 describes Christ giving servants to equip His people so that the whole body grows toward maturity. Speaking the truth in love and each part contributing belong to that growth.

God’s purpose for teaching therefore reaches beyond increasing someone’s knowledge. He is building a people who grow together in Christ. Rival loyalties work against the shared maturity for which His gifts are given.
