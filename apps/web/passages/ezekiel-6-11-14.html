Passage

Ezekiel 6:11-14

Why is Ezekiel told to clap, stamp and cry out?

Berean Standard Bible

11 This is what the Lord GOD says: Clap your hands, stomp your feet, and cry out “Alas!” because of all the wicked abominations of the house of Israel, who will fall by sword and famine and plague. 12 He who is far off will die by the plague, he who is near will fall by the sword, and he who remains will die by famine. So I will vent My fury upon them. 13 Then you will know that I am the LORD, when their slain lie among their idols around their altars, on every high hill, on all the mountaintops, and under every green tree and leafy oak—the places where they offered fragrant incense to all their idols. 14 I will stretch out My hand against them, and wherever they live I will make the land a desolate waste, from the wilderness to Diblah. Then they will know that I am the LORD.’”

What is the context?

Ezekiel must express distress with his hands, feet and voice over the people’s abominations and the coming sword, famine and plague. The warning is to be delivered with visible and audible force.

The different dangers reach those far away, those nearby and those remaining under siege. Distance or another chosen refuge does not make the announced judgment disappear.

The bodies lying among the idols will expose the failure of worship on the hills, under green trees and around the altars. The Lord’s outstretched hand leaves the land desolate.

The repeated ending brings the purpose into focus: they will know that He is the Lord. The chapter has moved from the destruction of false worship, through the promise to preserve some, to a final declaration that His word will be made known.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley read the gestures as an expression of sorrow, indignation and compassion over sin and its consequences. They do not treat the scene as a celebration of suffering.

Both emphasize the reach of the judgment and the failure of the refuges on which the people depended. Henry also urges compassion toward people facing the ruin their wrongdoing brings.
