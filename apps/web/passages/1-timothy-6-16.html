Passage

1 Timothy 6:16

Why does Paul praise God as the source of immortal life?

Berean Standard Bible

16 He alone is immortal and dwells in unapproachable light. No one has ever seen Him, nor can anyone see Him. To Him be honor and eternal dominion! Amen.

What is the context?

This verse closes Paul’s charge to Timothy in 6:11–16. Timothy is to persevere until Christ appears at the time God appoints. Praise for God’s unmatched rule, immortal life and unapproachable light brings the charge to its conclusion.

The verse should stay connected with that encouragement. Timothy’s life and future rest with the God whose power is greater than the pressures he faces.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Major Christian readings agree that God is the source of life. Calvin and Wesley understand His unique immortality to mean that He has life in Himself; whatever life creatures have comes from Him.

Where traditions differ is what happens to a person at death. The Catholic Church teaches that the soul survives bodily death and will be reunited with the body at the resurrection (Catechism 366). The Reformed Westminster Confession 32.1–2 likewise teaches conscious life after death and a future bodily resurrection. Seventh-day Adventists instead teach that death is an unconscious state until resurrection, when God gives eternal life to His redeemed (Belief 26). These readings all depend on God giving life; they differ over the soul’s condition between death and resurrection.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John 1:18 connects the unseen God with the Son who makes Him known. Colossians 1:15–20 likewise describes the Son as the image of the invisible God and places creation and reconciliation through Him together.

These passages give a wider setting for knowing the God whom human sight cannot reach. God makes Himself known through His Son, and 2 Timothy 1:9–10 connects life and immortality with Christ’s appearing and the gospel.

What is our position?

In Who is The Angel of the Lord?, we understand the unseen One in this passage as the Father. Read alongside John 1:18, this helps explain how people in the Old Testament could encounter God visibly: our study identifies those appearances with the eternal Son, who makes the Father known. This is the position developed in that study, rather than a conclusion supplied by the word light on its own.
