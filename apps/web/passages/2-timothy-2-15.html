Passage

2 Timothy 2:15

What does it mean to handle the word of truth accurately?

Berean Standard Bible

15 Make every effort to present yourself approved to God, an unashamed workman who accurately handles the word of truth.

What is the context?

This instruction belongs to Paul’s warning in 2:14–19 about destructive disputes and false teaching. Timothy is to be a careful worker whose handling of the truth can stand before God.

The Greek verb orthotomeo (or-thoh-toh-MEH-oh) uses the picture of cutting straight. Thayer explains its use here as teaching the truth correctly and directly. Accuracy serves the people who hear; the surrounding verses show the damage caused when the message is distorted.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin emphasizes seeking God’s approval and explaining the message in a way that builds people up. Wesley emphasizes explaining and applying the whole of Scripture faithfully to those who hear it. Their shared concern is honest teaching, rather than verbal display or careless claims.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Paul earlier tells Timothy to entrust the teaching to reliable people who can teach others (2 Timothy 2:1–2). Accurate handling serves that continuing transmission of the gospel.

Ephesians 4:11–16 gives the wider goal: people equipped to serve, protected from misleading teaching and growing together toward maturity in Christ. Careful teaching matters because it helps build that shared life.

What is our position?

In The Berean Standard, we understand this verse as a call to honest, daily and humble engagement with Scripture. We should examine words in their context, test what we are taught and remain willing to be corrected by the text. The goal is a faith that knows what it believes and why, rather than correctness for its own sake.
