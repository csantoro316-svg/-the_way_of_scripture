Passage

2 Timothy 3:1-5

What makes these difficult times spiritually dangerous?

Berean Standard Bible

1 But understand this: In the last days terrible times will come. 2 For men will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boastful, arrogant, abusive, disobedient to their parents, ungrateful, unholy, 3 unloving, unforgiving, slanderous, without self-control, brutal, without love of good, 4 traitorous, reckless, conceited, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God, 5 having a form of godliness but denying its power. Turn away from such as these!

What is the context?

Paul has just instructed Timothy to teach patiently while refusing quarrels. He now warns of terrible times marked by people’s conduct: self-centeredness, greed, pride, broken relationships, cruelty and a love of pleasure that displaces love for God.

The warning reaches into religion itself. A person can maintain the appearance of godliness while denying its power. Timothy is told to turn away from such people, rather than be drawn into their pattern of life.

The description is meant to help him recognize danger. Verse 6 immediately points to people already acting this way, so Paul is not speaking only of a remote future Timothy could never encounter.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley understand the last days as the era of the gospel and the church, rather than only a brief final interval. Both apply the warning to people who can be found within the religious community. Outward claims to godliness do not excuse a life that contradicts it.

They read the list as a reason for vigilance and faithful conduct. It does not supply a date for Christ’s return or turn every troubling event into a separate prediction to decode.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Matthew 7:15–23, Jesus warns that impressive religious claims can conceal a life that does not do His Father’s will. The fruit of a life matters when claims of belonging to Him are tested.

Romans 12:1–2 describes the positive direction for God’s people: lives offered to Him and minds renewed so they can discern what pleases Him. Paul’s warning belongs within that purpose of forming people whose worship and conduct agree.
