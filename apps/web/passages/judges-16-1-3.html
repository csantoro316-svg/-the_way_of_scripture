Passage

Judges 16:1-3

Can great strength hide a serious weakness?

Berean Standard Bible

1 One day Samson went to Gaza, where he saw a prostitute and went in to spend the night with her. 2 When the Gazites heard that Samson was there, they surrounded that place and lay in wait for him all night at the city gate. They were quiet throughout the night, saying, “Let us wait until dawn; then we will kill him.” 3 But Samson lay there only until midnight, when he got up, took hold of the doors of the city gate and both gateposts, and pulled them out, bar and all. Then he put them on his shoulders and took them to the top of the mountain overlooking Hebron.

What is the context?

Samson goes to Gaza, sees a prostitute, and spends the night with her. When the townspeople learn he is there, they wait at the city gate, planning to kill him at dawn. The danger comes from people who already regard him as their enemy.

He gets up at midnight and removes the gate doors, both posts, and the bar. He carries them to the mountain overlooking Hebron. The narrator describes an extraordinary escape, without explaining how Samson learned of the ambush or how much the gate weighed. The man who can carry away a city’s defenses remains vulnerable in his own choices.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Henry both read the visit to the prostitute as sin. They distinguish Samson’s extraordinary gift from the condition of his heart. His ability to escape does not make the conduct that brought him there right.

Both suggest that his departure may have involved a renewed awareness of guilt or danger. They present possibilities rather than a confession recorded in the passage. The account itself reports what he does at midnight without describing a prayer or stating his motive for getting up.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The New Testament connects sexual conduct with belonging to Christ. In 1 Corinthians 6:12–20, Paul explains that believers’ bodies belong to the Lord, that they are joined to Christ, and that God will raise them as He raised Jesus. The call to flee sexual immorality rests on that relationship and future hope.

That adds something beyond the contrast between physical strength and personal weakness: God’s saving work concerns the whole person. Our bodies are part of a life bought for His service, so how we use them matters.
