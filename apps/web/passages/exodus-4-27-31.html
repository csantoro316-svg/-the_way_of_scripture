Passage

Exodus 4:27-31

How did Israel respond to the news that God had seen their suffering?

Berean Standard Bible

27 Meanwhile, the LORD had said to Aaron, “Go and meet Moses in the wilderness.” So he went and met Moses at the mountain of God and kissed him. 28 And Moses told Aaron everything the LORD had sent him to say, and all the signs He had commanded him to perform. 29 Then Moses and Aaron went and assembled all the elders of the Israelites, 30 and Aaron relayed everything the LORD had said to Moses. And Moses performed the signs before the people, 31 and they believed. And when they heard that the LORD had attended to the Israelites and had seen their affliction, they bowed down and worshiped.

What is the context?

God sends Aaron into the wilderness, where he meets Moses at the mountain of God. Moses tells him what the LORD has commanded him to say and explains the signs. The help God promised is now taking place.

The brothers gather Israel’s elders. Aaron delivers the message, Moses performs the signs, and the people believe. Their response brings the story back to the concern Moses raised at the beginning of the chapter: how would they know God had appeared to him?

What leads them to bow in worship is the news that the LORD has attended to them and seen their affliction. They have not yet left Egypt, but they have heard that their suffering has been seen and that God is acting.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared reading sees the meeting as the promised beginning of the mission: the brothers deliver God’s message, the signs confirm it, and Israel responds with belief and worship. Calvin and Gill both connect their thanksgiving with learning that God has taken notice of their suffering.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Rescue and worship belong together in this account. God has already called Israel His son and demanded that Pharaoh release them to worship Him. The people begin to worship while they are still waiting for that freedom.

The theme returns in Luke 1:68–75. Zechariah praises God for visiting and redeeming His people, remembering His covenant with Abraham, and granting deliverance so they may serve Him without fear. The purpose of rescue reaches beyond escape from an enemy into life with God.
