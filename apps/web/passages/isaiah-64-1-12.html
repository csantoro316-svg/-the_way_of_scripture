Passage

Isaiah 64:1-12

What can people plead when their own righteousness cannot cover them?

Berean Standard Bible

1 If only You would rend the heavens and come down, so that mountains would quake at Your presence, 2 as fire kindles the brushwood and causes the water to boil, to make Your name known to Your enemies, so that the nations will tremble at Your presence! 3 When You did awesome works that we did not expect, You came down, and the mountains trembled at Your presence. 4 From ancient times no one has heard, no ear has perceived, no eye has seen any God besides You, who acts on behalf of those who wait for Him. 5 You welcome those who gladly do right, who remember Your ways. Surely You were angry, for we sinned. How can we be saved if we remain in our sins?

6 Each of us has become like something unclean, and all our righteous acts are like filthy rags; we all wither like a leaf, and our iniquities carry us away like the wind. 7 No one calls on Your name or strives to take hold of You. For You have hidden Your face from us and delivered us into the hand of our iniquity. 8 But now, O LORD, You are our Father; we are the clay, and You are the potter; we are all the work of Your hand. 9 Do not be angry, O LORD, beyond measure; do not remember our iniquity forever. Oh, look upon us, we pray; we are all Your people!

10 Your holy cities have become a wilderness. Zion has become a wasteland and Jerusalem a desolation. 11 Our holy and beautiful temple, where our fathers praised You, has been burned with fire, and all that was dear to us lies in ruins. 12 After all this, O LORD, will You restrain Yourself? Will You keep silent and afflict us beyond measure?

What is the context?

The prayer asks the Lord to tear open the heavens and come down. Shaking mountains, kindled brushwood and boiling water picture a presence powerful enough to make the nations tremble. The people remember awesome works they had not expected Him to do.

They confess that no other God acts as He does for those who wait for Him. Yet their prayer acknowledges their sin and asks how they can be saved while remaining in it.

Their condition is pictured through uncleanness, filthy garments and withered leaves blown away by iniquity. They have failed to call on Him or take hold of Him. The prayer does not offer their record as a reason they deserve rescue.

They instead appeal to Him as Father and potter: they are His clay and the work of His hand. They ask that His anger and remembrance of sin not continue without measure.

The devastated cities and burned temple give the appeal its painful setting. What their fathers treasured lies in ruins. The closing questions ask whether the Lord will continue to hold back and remain silent.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley connect the opening plea with God’s mighty past acts and the people’s hope for renewed intervention. Both also recognize the confession as an acknowledgment that sin has left them unable to claim His favor on the strength of their deeds.

Henry emphasizes the failure of works offered as a way to earn mercy. Wesley emphasizes the depth of the community’s corruption and its need to call on the Lord. Their explanations of the potter and Father images stress dependence on the One who made and claimed them.

The prayer combines honesty about guilt with continuing hope in His compassion. It does not end by declaring themselves beyond His reach.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

First Corinthians 2:6–16 draws on Isaiah’s language about what no eye has seen or ear heard. Paul uses it in explaining God’s wisdom and what He has prepared for those who love Him.

Paul immediately adds that God has revealed these things through the Spirit. His point is therefore more than saying that God’s gifts remain unimaginable. Human wisdom did not discover His saving purpose, but His Spirit makes known what He has freely given.

The prayer’s dependence on the God who acts thus connects with the gospel’s dependence on the God who reveals. Understanding and receiving His saving work rests on His gracious initiative.
