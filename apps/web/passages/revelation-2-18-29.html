Passage

Revelation 2:18-29

What does Jesus see when good works and false teaching exist together?

Berean Standard Bible

18 To the angel of the church in Thyatira write: These are the words of the Son of God, whose eyes are like a blazing fire and whose feet are like polished bronze. 19 I know your deeds—your love, your faith, your service, your perseverance—and your latest deeds are greater than your first.

20 But I have this against you: You tolerate that woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess. By her teaching she misleads My servants to be sexually immoral and to eat food sacrificed to idols. 21 Even though I have given her time to repent of her immorality, she is unwilling. 22 Behold, I will cast her onto a bed of sickness, and those who commit adultery with her will suffer great tribulation unless they repent of her deeds. 23 Then I will strike her children dead, and all the churches will know that I am the One who searches minds and hearts, and I will repay each of you according to your deeds.

24 But I say to the rest of you in Thyatira, who do not hold to her teaching and have not learned the so-called deep things of Satan: I will place no further burden upon you 25 than to hold fast to what you have until I come.

26 And to the one who overcomes and continues in My work until the end, I will give authority over the nations. 27 He will rule them with an iron scepter and shatter them like pottery —just as I have received authority from My Father. 28 And I will give him the morning star. 29 He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches.

What is the context?

Jesus addresses Thyatira as the Son of God, with eyes like blazing fire and feet like polished bronze. As the letter unfolds, He says that He searches minds and hearts. His knowledge reaches beneath a church’s appearance and reputation.

This church receives genuine praise for love, faith, service and perseverance. Its later deeds exceed its earlier ones. Yet it tolerates a woman called Jezebel, who claims to speak for God and leads Christ’s servants into sexual immorality and eating food sacrificed to idols. The name recalls Ahab’s wife, whose influence led him further into idolatry; see 1 Kings 16:29–33 and 21:25–26.

Jesus has given her time to repent, but she refuses. He announces severe judgment on her and on those who continue in her deeds, while still calling her followers to repentance. This is a specific warning from Christ; the letter does not tell us the later events by which it was carried out.

He also distinguishes the people who have rejected her teaching. They are to hold fast until He comes. The promise then looks beyond the present struggle: the one who overcomes and continues in His work will share authority over the nations and receive the morning star. Good works, truthful teaching and continuing faithfulness all matter in His assessment.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Interpretations differ over the reach of the name Jezebel. Wesley understands a woman deceiving the church in Thyatira, named for her resemblance to the Old Testament queen. Gill also reads her as a picture of the Roman Catholic Church within his account of successive church ages. This is an identification made by Gill; Revelation does not name that later institution in this letter. Wesley explicitly rejects reading the seven churches as a sequence of historical ages.

Wesley and Gill both understand the bed as an image of affliction, but their wider applications differ with their identification of the woman. Her children can be understood as followers shaped by her teaching; Wesley also allows her actual children. The letter’s distinction between her adherents and those who rejected her teaching should remain visible in either reading.

The promise of authority is also explained differently. Wesley emphasizes sharing in Christ’s victory over the nations. Gill includes the spread of the gospel and the defeat of opposition through the Reformation and beyond. Both identify the morning star with Christ. These teachers share confidence in His final victory while proposing different ways the promised rule unfolds.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The promise of an iron scepter and shattered pottery directly takes up Psalm 2:7–9, where God gives the nations to His Son. Jesus says that He shares authority received from His Father with the one who overcomes and continues in His work. The future of His people is bound to His own victory.

The morning star becomes clearer when Jesus identifies Himself that way in Revelation 22:16. The hope offered here includes Christ Himself. The warning also echoes Jeremiah 17:9–10: God searches the heart and judges deeds. Together these connections show that hidden corruption will not escape His judgment, and that present faithfulness belongs within His larger purpose to establish His Son’s rule.
