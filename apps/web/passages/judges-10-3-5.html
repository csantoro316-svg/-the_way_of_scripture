Passage

Judges 10:3-5

What does Jair’s household tell us?

Berean Standard Bible

3 Tola was followed by Jair the Gileadite, who judged Israel twenty-two years. 4 He had thirty sons who rode on thirty donkeys. And they had thirty towns in the land of Gilead, which to this day are called Havvoth-jair. 5 When Jair died, he was buried in Kamon.

What is the context?

Jair follows Tola and judges Israel for twenty-two years. He is a Gileadite, and his household is described through thirty sons, thirty donkeys, and thirty towns in Gilead. The towns remain associated with his name. He dies and is buried in Kamon.

These details show the extent of his family’s place in the region. The account does not report his battles, name his wives, or tell us how his sons carried out their roles. The brief notice should be read on its own terms before the next passage introduces Israel’s renewed unfaithfulness.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill suggest that Jair’s sons served as local deputies who traveled to administer justice. That is a possible explanation of the family’s towns and donkeys, rather than a task explicitly assigned to each son in these verses. Henry assesses Jair’s leadership positively as part of a period of useful service. The account’s brevity leaves many details of his family life unknown.
