Passage

Luke 9:51-56

Why does Jesus refuse the call for fire on the Samaritan village?

Berean Standard Bible

51 As the day of His ascension approached, Jesus resolutely set out for Jerusalem. 52 He sent messengers on ahead, who went into a village of the Samaritans to make arrangements for Him. 53 But the people there refused to welcome Him, because He was heading for Jerusalem.

54 When the disciples James and John saw this, they asked, “Lord, do You want us to call down fire from heaven to consume them?” 55 But Jesus turned and rebuked them. 56 And He and His disciples went on to another village.

What is the context?

Luke marks a turning point: as the time of His ascension approaches, Jesus sets out resolutely for Jerusalem. He sends messengers ahead to make arrangements in a Samaritan village.

The people refuse to welcome Him because He is heading for Jerusalem. John’s Gospel records a Samaritan woman describing the disagreement over worship on her people’s mountain or in Jerusalem, which helps readers understand the significance of the destination. (John 4:19–24)

James and John ask whether they should call down fire to consume the village. Jesus turns and rebukes them, and the group goes to another village. His determined movement toward Jerusalem is accompanied by a refusal to let the disciples answer this rejection with destruction.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly see Jesus correcting the disciples’ desire to punish those who refuse Him. The Catholic bishops’ notes emphasize His rejection of that response, while Wesley places His determined journey within the suffering and glory ahead. (USCCB notes on Luke 9:51–55; Wesley on Luke 9:51)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The journey is directed toward the saving events that culminate in Jesus’ death, resurrection and ascension. At the Gospel’s end, He explains that repentance and forgiveness will be proclaimed in His name to the nations. Rejection in this village does not change the purpose of His mission. (Luke 24:44–49)

Acts later provides a striking contrast with the request for fire. Samaritans receive Philip’s proclamation of Christ, and Peter and John go to them and pray for them to receive the Holy Spirit. John, who asked about destroying a Samaritan village in the Gospel, now prays for Samaritan believers. The larger witness Jesus commissions explicitly includes Samaria. (Acts 8:4–17; Acts 1:8)
