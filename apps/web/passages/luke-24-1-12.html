Passage

Luke 24:1-12

Why is Jesus’ tomb empty?

Berean Standard Bible

1 On the first day of the week, very early in the morning, the women came to the tomb, bringing the spices they had prepared. 2 They found the stone rolled away from the tomb, 3 but when they entered, they did not find the body of the Lord Jesus. 4 While they were puzzling over this, suddenly two men in radiant apparel stood beside them. 5 As the women bowed their faces to the ground in terror, the two men asked them, “Why do you look for the living among the dead?

6 He is not here; He has risen! Remember how He told you while He was still in Galilee: 7 ‘The Son of Man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and on the third day rise again.’ ” 8 Then they remembered His words. 9 And when they returned from the tomb, they reported all these things to the Eleven and to all the others.

10 It was Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Mary the mother of James, and the other women with them who told this to the apostles. 11 But their words seemed like nonsense to them, and they did not believe the women. 12 Peter, however, got up and ran to the tomb. And after bending down and seeing only the linen cloths, he went away, wondering to himself what had happened.

What is the context?

On the first day of the week, the women return with the spices they prepared. These are the women who saw the tomb and how Jesus’ body was placed there. They find the stone rolled away and the body gone.

Two men in radiant clothing stand beside them and ask why they are looking for the living among the dead. They announce that Jesus has risen and remind the women that He had foretold His betrayal, crucifixion and resurrection on the third day.

The women remember His words and report what happened to the Eleven and the others. Luke names Mary Magdalene, Joanna and Mary the mother of James, along with other women. The apostles initially dismiss their report as nonsense.

Peter runs to the tomb, sees only the linen cloths and leaves wondering. Luke records the empty tomb, the announcement, the remembered promise and the first responses to the news; fuller recognition follows in the rest of the chapter.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian traditions commonly understand the empty tomb within the announcement that Jesus truly rose from the dead. The Catholic Catechism describes it as an essential sign leading toward recognition of the resurrection, and Westminster affirms His rising in the same body in which He suffered. (Catechism §§639–641; Westminster Confession 8.4)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The announcement calls the women back to Jesus’ own promise, including the suffering they had just witnessed. Acts later presents His burial and resurrection as the fulfillment of God’s promises, applying the promise that God’s Holy One would not see decay to Jesus. It joins that proclamation with forgiveness through Him. (Luke 9:22; Luke 18:31–33; Acts 13:29–39)

Paul also explains Christ’s resurrection as the beginning of the promised harvest: those who belong to Him will be raised at His coming. Romans connects being united with His death and resurrection with walking in newness of life now. The empty tomb therefore stands within both the hope of resurrection and the new life His people are called to live. (1 Corinthians 15:20–23; Romans 6:3–11)
