Passage

1 Corinthians 16:5-9

Can an open door for the gospel come with opposition?

Berean Standard Bible

5 After I go through Macedonia, however, I will come to you; for I will be going through Macedonia. 6 Perhaps I will stay with you awhile, or even spend the winter, so that you can help me on my journey, wherever I go. 7 For I do not want to see you now only in passing; I hope to spend some time with you, if the Lord permits.

8 But I will stay in Ephesus until Pentecost, 9 because a great door for effective work has opened to me, even though many oppose me.

What is the context?

Paul explains his travel hopes: he intends to pass through Macedonia before coming to Corinth, and he would like enough time with them for a substantial visit, perhaps through the winter. He hopes they can help him on the next stage of the journey.

His plans remain subject to the Lord’s permission. These are his intentions, not a promise that every detail of his itinerary has been revealed in advance.

For now he will remain in Ephesus until Pentecost because an important opportunity for work has opened there. There are also many opponents. Paul mentions opportunity and opposition together; difficulty has not automatically made the work pointless or closed the door.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand Paul to be weighing fruitful ministry, time with the congregation and the Lord’s permission as he makes plans. Wesley’s note on verse 9 explicitly observes that opposition can accompany opportunity. This is encouragement to discern faithfully, rather than a rule that every dangerous situation requires someone to remain.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 19:8–10 describes opposition to Paul’s teaching in Ephesus and his continuing work in another setting. The message of the Lord spread through the surrounding province of Asia.

This gives a concrete example of the mission continuing through resistance. God’s purpose to make Christ known is not measured simply by how comfortable a journey becomes. Paul’s plans serve that purpose while remaining open to the Lord’s direction.
