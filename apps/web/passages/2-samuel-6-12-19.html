Passage

2 Samuel 6:12-19

What changes when the ark’s journey resumes?

Berean Standard Bible

12 Now it was reported to King David, “The LORD has blessed the house of Obed-edom and all that belongs to him, because of the ark of God.” So David went and had the ark of God brought up from the house of Obed-edom into the City of David with rejoicing. 13 When those carrying the ark of the LORD had advanced six paces, he sacrificed an ox and a fattened calf. 14 And David, wearing a linen ephod, danced with all his might before the LORD, 15 while he and all the house of Israel brought up the ark of the LORD with shouting and the sounding of the ram’s horn.

16 As the ark of the LORD was entering the City of David, Saul’s daughter Michal looked down from a window and saw King David leaping and dancing before the LORD, and she despised him in her heart. 17 So they brought the ark of the LORD and set it in its place inside the tent that David had pitched for it. Then David offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the LORD. 18 When David had finished sacrificing the burnt offerings and peace offerings, he blessed the people in the name of the LORD of Hosts. 19 Then he distributed to every man and woman among the multitude of Israel a loaf of bread, a date cake, and a raisin cake. And all the people departed, each to his own home.

What is the context?

When David hears of the blessing on Obed-edom’s household, he resumes bringing the ark to Jerusalem with joy. This time the account speaks of people carrying it. After six paces, David sacrifices an ox and a fattened calf.

Wearing a linen ephod, David dances before the LORD with all his might, while Israel joins with shouting and the ram’s horn. Michal watches from a window and despises him in her heart. Her response introduces a conflict that will surface when he comes home.

The ark is placed inside the tent David has prepared. He offers burnt offerings and peace offerings, blesses the people in the LORD’s name, and distributes bread, date cakes and raisin cakes to men and women alike. The public celebration ends with people returning to their households.

How is this passage commonly understood?

1 Chronicles 15:1–15 gives the fuller account of the correction: David appoints Levites, calls them to consecrate themselves, and has the ark carried on shoulders with poles as Moses commanded. Joy and attention to God’s instructions now appear together.

Gill reads the sacrifice after six paces as thanksgiving at the safe beginning of the journey. He questions the idea that it was repeated at every six steps. The BSB describes a sacrifice after six paces; it does not explicitly say every six paces along the entire route.

Wesley and Henry understand David’s linen clothing as a setting aside of royal display in service before God. 1 Chronicles 15:27 also describes his robe of fine linen. The king joins the procession as a worshiper who serves the LORD.
