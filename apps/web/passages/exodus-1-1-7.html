Passage

Exodus 1:1-7

How did Jacob’s family become a people in Egypt?

Berean Standard Bible

1 These are the names of the sons of Israel who went to Egypt with Jacob, each with his family: 2 Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah; 3 Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin; 4 Dan and Naphtali; Gad and Asher. 5 The descendants of Jacob numbered seventy in all, including Joseph, who was already in Egypt.

6 Now Joseph and all his brothers and all that generation died, 7 but the Israelites were fruitful and increased rapidly; they multiplied and became exceedingly numerous, so that the land was filled with them.

What is the context?

Exodus begins by naming the sons of Israel who came to Egypt with Jacob. It picks up the family story told in Genesis, where Joseph was already living in Egypt and his relatives joined him during the famine.

The opening count is seventy, with Joseph already in Egypt. The story then moves beyond that generation: Joseph, his brothers and their contemporaries die, while their descendants become numerous and fill the land.

The repeated descriptions of fruitfulness and increase make growth the main event in these verses. The small family has become a people. That growth will soon trouble Egypt’s new king, setting up the conflict that follows.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared emphasis is God’s faithfulness to His promises about Abraham’s family. Wesley understands the increase as the result of the promise made to the fathers. The Catholic USCCB notes also connect it with those promises and with the language of blessing and multiplication in Genesis. These explanations share the passage’s movement from one family to a growing people.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Genesis 46:2–4, God told Jacob not to fear going to Egypt because He would make him a great nation there. The growth at the opening of Exodus belongs directly with that promise. Egypt is part of the story God has already begun with this family.

Stephen makes the connection explicit in Acts 7:17: the people increased as the time approached for God to fulfill His promise to Abraham. That promise reaches back to Genesis 12:1–3, where the making of a great nation belongs within God’s purpose to bless all the families of the earth.
