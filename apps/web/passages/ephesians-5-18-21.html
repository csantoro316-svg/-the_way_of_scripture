Passage

Ephesians 5:18-21

What does life filled with the Spirit look like together?

Berean Standard Bible

18 Do not get drunk on wine, which leads to reckless indiscretion. Instead, be filled with the Spirit. 19 Speak to one another with psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs. Sing and make music in your hearts to the Lord, 20 always giving thanks to God the Father for everything in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. 21 Submit to one another out of reverence for Christ.

What is the context?

Paul continues the call to wise living with a contrast: drunkenness leads to reckless conduct, while believers are to be filled with the Spirit. The verses that follow describe a shared life of song, thanksgiving and submission out of reverence for Christ.

The singing has two explicit directions. Believers speak to one another through psalms, hymns and spiritual songs, and sing and make music in their hearts to the Lord. The text keeps mutual encouragement and praise to Him together.

Thanksgiving is offered to God the Father in the name of Jesus Christ. The paragraph then calls believers to submit to one another. Paul places their treatment of each other within their reverence for Christ.

This last instruction also leads into the following discussion of households. The whole movement belongs within the letter’s concern for a community shaped by Christ’s love.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this paragraph as contrasting drunkenness with life directed by God’s Spirit, expressed through worship, gratitude and regard for others. Wesley’s notes on verses 18–20 connect the Spirit’s work with heartfelt singing and thanksgiving. The USCCB notes connect the following household instructions with mutual responsibility under Christ.

On the shared point, the passage itself names the practices rather than leaving Spirit-filled life as an undefined feeling. Its instructions include both the heart’s response to the Lord and what believers do for one another.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 2:13–18 records an accusation of drunkenness at Pentecost. Peter explains what is happening through God’s promised outpouring of the Spirit. This is a useful parallel to Paul’s contrast, rather than proof that the two passages describe an identical outward experience.

The shared life that follows in Acts 2:42–47 includes teaching, fellowship, prayer, practical care and praise to God. God’s gift forms a people who learn, give thanks and care for one another. That larger purpose helps connect the practices named here.

What is our position?

Our study Worship as the Early Church Knew It emphasizes the purpose of speaking to one another in song: communicating truth that helps others grow in faith. It distinguishes that service from a song’s emotional effect on an individual. Personal feeling by itself does not show that the gathering is being built up. The study also recognizes praise and thanksgiving directed to God.
