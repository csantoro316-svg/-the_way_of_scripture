Passage

Exodus 26:7-14

What covered the tabernacle’s inner curtains?

Berean Standard Bible

7 You are to make curtains of goat hair for the tent over the tabernacle—eleven curtains in all. 8 Each of the eleven curtains is to be the same size—thirty cubits long and four cubits wide. 9 Join five of the curtains into one set and the other six into another. Then fold the sixth curtain over double at the front of the tent. 10 Make fifty loops along the edge of the end curtain in the first set, and fifty loops along the edge of the corresponding curtain in the second set. 11 Make fifty bronze clasps and put them through the loops to join the tent together as a unit.

12 As for the overlap that remains of the tent curtains, the half curtain that is left over shall hang down over the back of the tabernacle. 13 And the tent curtains will be a cubit longer on either side, and the excess will hang over the sides of the tabernacle to cover it. 14 Also make a covering for the tent out of ram skins dyed red, and over that a covering of fine leather.

What is the context?

Eleven curtains of goat hair form a tent over the linen tabernacle. They are longer than the inner curtains and are joined into sets of five and six, with the sixth folded double at the front. Bronze clasps join the sets. The extra material hangs over the back and sides to cover the structure beneath.

Above these curtains are coverings of ram skins dyed red and fine leather. The different layers belong to the tent’s construction and protection. The BSB’s fine leather translates a Hebrew term whose exact material or animal source is uncertain; Brown–Driver–Briggs records several proposals. The instructions do not require us to settle on a particular species to understand how the layers cover the tabernacle.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Readers generally recognize these as the sanctuary’s outer coverings. They differ over spiritual meanings assigned to the materials. Gill sees the protective layers as illustrations of God’s care for His people and connects the covering with Christ’s righteousness and blood. Calvin questions the practice of interpreting the ram skins as Christ’s blood while having no sound explanation for every other layer. His emphasis is restraint in drawing meanings that the text does not state. These are different approaches among individual Christian commentators, not competing descriptions of how the tent was assembled.
