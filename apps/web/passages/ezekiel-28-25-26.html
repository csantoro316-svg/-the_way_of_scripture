Passage

Ezekiel 28:25-26

What does the Lord promise when He gathers Israel again?

Berean Standard Bible

25 This is what the Lord GOD says: ‘When I gather the house of Israel from the peoples among whom they have been scattered, I will show Myself holy among them in the sight of the nations. Then they will dwell in their own land, which I have given to My servant Jacob. 26 And there they will dwell securely, build houses, and plant vineyards. They will dwell securely when I execute judgments against all those around them who treat them with contempt. Then they will know that I am the LORD their God.’”

What is the context?

The chapter closes with a promise to the house of Israel. The Lord will gather His scattered people from the nations and show His holiness among them in the sight of those nations.

The promised life is concrete: dwelling in the land given to Jacob, building houses and planting vineyards. Security means being able to live and work without the contemptuous neighbors continuing to threaten them.

The Lord brings the gathering and the judgments together. The outcome is not simply possession of land but recognition of the One who has acted: they will know Him as the Lord their God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Jamieson–Fausset–Brown sees a partial fulfillment in the return from Babylon and a fuller future fulfillment for Israel under Christ. Wesley also regards the complete freedom from the hostile neighbors as still awaiting fulfillment.

Henry includes restored safety and prosperity, then carries the full promise toward the heavenly home of all God’s redeemed people. These readings differ in how they describe the final setting and scope, while agreeing that the Lord Himself brings His people into lasting security.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ezekiel 36:22–28 develops the same gathering and display of holiness by adding cleansing from idols, a new heart and the gift of God’s Spirit. The restoration reaches the people’s inner life and their obedience, as well as the land in which they dwell.

Hebrews 8:8–13 quotes the promise of a new covenant with the house of Israel and the house of Judah, including God’s law within them, knowledge of Him and forgiveness of sins. This wider covenant picture explains why knowing the Lord is more than the relief of surviving a threat: His purpose includes a renewed relationship with His people.
