Passage

Exodus 40:34-38

What does God’s glory filling the dwelling mean for His people?

Berean Standard Bible

34 Then the cloud covered the Tent of Meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle. 35 Moses was unable to enter the Tent of Meeting because the cloud had settled on it, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle.

36 Whenever the cloud was lifted from above the tabernacle, the Israelites would set out through all the stages of their journey. 37 If the cloud was not lifted, they would not set out until the day it was taken up. 38 For the cloud of the LORD was over the tabernacle by day, and fire was in the cloud by night, in the sight of all the house of Israel through all their journeys.

What is the context?

The cloud covers the Tent of Meeting and God’s glory fills the dwelling. In this moment, even Moses is unable to enter. The completion of the work is answered by God making His presence known among the people.

This brings the book’s rescue to its stated purpose. God had said He brought Israel out of Egypt so that He might dwell among them (Exodus 29:45–46). The calf threatened that relationship; now, after Moses’ pleas and the renewal of the covenant, God’s presence fills the dwelling prepared at His command.

The presence also guides the journey. When the cloud rises, the people set out; when it remains, they stay. The cloud by day and fire by night are visible to the whole house of Israel. Numbers 9:15–23 develops the same pattern of waiting and moving at God’s command. Exodus ends before arrival in the land, with God accompanying His people on the way.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Calvin both read the filling of the dwelling as God graciously taking His place among Israel and confirming His promise. They also understand the cloud as the continuing sign of His presence and guidance; Moses’ inability to enter highlights the greatness of God’s glory.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John 1:14–18 brings together God’s dwelling and glory in Jesus: the Word becomes flesh and dwells among us. The Greek verb is σκηνόω, skēnoō (skay-NOH-oh), which can mean living in a tent or dwelling; Thayer’s lexicon specifically includes this verse. God makes Himself known through the Son, full of grace and truth.

The New Testament then describes God’s people as His dwelling. Second Corinthians 6:14–18 calls them the temple of the living God, and 1 Corinthians 6:17–20 describes the body as a temple of the Holy Spirit, joining that gift to a life that honors God.

The final vision brings the purpose to its fullness: God dwells with His people, wipes away their tears, and removes death and mourning (Revelation 21:1–4). The book that began with Israel oppressed in Egypt ends with God among them, pointing onward to the lasting life with Him that Scripture promises.

What is our position?

As explained in The True Church According to Jesus, God now dwells in His people through His Spirit. The study connects His presence in the tabernacle with His indwelling in believers and calls for lives made holy by that presence.
