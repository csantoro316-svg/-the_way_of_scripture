Passage

Luke 3:1-6

How does John prepare the Lord’s way?

Berean Standard Bible

1 In the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, while Pontius Pilate was governor of Judea, Herod tetrarch of Galilee, his brother Philip tetrarch of Ituraea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias tetrarch of Abilene, 2 during the high priesthood of Annas and Caiaphas, the word of God came to John son of Zechariah in the wilderness. 3 He went into all the region around the Jordan, preaching a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins,

4 as it is written in the book of the words of Isaiah the prophet: “A voice of one calling in the wilderness, ‘Prepare the way for the Lord, make straight paths for Him. 5 Every valley shall be filled in, and every mountain and hill made low. The crooked ways shall be made straight, and the rough ways smooth. 6 And all humanity will see God’s salvation.’”

What is the context?

Luke places John’s public ministry within the reign of Tiberius and names the civil and religious leaders of the time. Then the focus moves to John, Zechariah’s son, receiving God’s word in the wilderness. The child promised in the opening chapter now begins his work.

John travels through the region around the Jordan, preaching a baptism of repentance for forgiveness of sins. Repentance is μετάνοια, metanoia (met-ah-NOY-ah): a change of mind that turns away from sin toward a changed life. John’s next words will make that change practical through the fruit it produces. (Thayer’s lexicon)

Luke identifies this ministry through Isaiah’s picture of preparing a road for the Lord: valleys filled, heights lowered, crooked places straightened, and rough ways made smooth. The picture belongs to the announcement of God’s coming salvation. John prepares people to receive the One who is coming, rather than organizing a road-building project. (Isaiah 40:1–11)

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian traditions commonly understand John as the promised messenger calling people to repentance and preparing them for the Lord. His baptism belongs to that call and points forward to Jesus, as Paul later explains to people who had received John’s baptism. (Catechism 718–720; Wesley on Luke 3:4–6; Acts 19:1–7)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Isaiah’s road picture comes within a larger message of comfort, forgiveness, and the Lord coming to care for His flock. Luke carries the quotation through its announcement that humanity will see God’s salvation. This continues the reach already heard in Simeon’s praise: light for the nations and glory for Israel. (Isaiah 40:1–11; Luke 2:29–32)

The same chapter of Isaiah contrasts human life’s frailty with God’s enduring word. Peter explicitly connects that enduring word with the gospel through which people receive new life. John’s call belongs to this movement from God’s promise to the good news of what He brings about in Christ. (Isaiah 40:6–8; 1 Peter 1:23–25)
