Passage

Luke 23:26-31

Why does Jesus tell the women to weep for themselves?

Berean Standard Bible

26 As the soldiers led Him away, they seized Simon of Cyrene on his way in from the country, and they put the cross on him to carry behind Jesus. 27 A great number of people followed Him, including women who kept mourning and wailing for Him.

28 But Jesus turned to them and said, “Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for Me, but weep for yourselves and for your children. 29 Look, the days are coming when people will say, ‘Blessed are the barren women, the wombs that never bore, and breasts that never nursed!’ 30 At that time ‘they will say to the mountains, “Fall on us!” and to the hills, “Cover us!”’ 31 For if men do these things while the tree is green, what will happen when it is dry?”

What is the context?

Simon of Cyrene is seized on his way in from the country and made to carry the cross behind Jesus. A large crowd follows, including women mourning for Him.

Jesus turns to the women of Jerusalem and directs their concern toward themselves and their children. He warns of coming days so terrible that having no children will be considered a blessing. Their present grief points toward a greater distress still ahead.

He uses Hosea’s language of people asking the mountains and hills to cover them, then adds the picture of green and dry wood. The question presses the seriousness of what is coming: if this is happening now, what will happen under conditions represented by the dry wood?

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly take Jesus’ words as a warning that reaches beyond His immediate suffering to coming judgment. Wesley explains the green wood in relation to the innocent Jesus and the dry wood in relation to those facing judgment; the USCCB’s notes connect this warning with Jesus’ earlier grief over Jerusalem. These readings agree on the warning’s seriousness. (Wesley on Luke 23:30–31; USCCB notes on Luke 23:26–32 and cross references)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Simon carrying the cross behind Jesus gives a visible parallel with Jesus’ earlier call for disciples to take up their cross and follow Him. Luke does not tell us whether Simon had already chosen discipleship, so the connection concerns the picture of following, rather than a claim about his conversion. (Luke 9:23–26)

Hosea’s original warning concerns Israel’s false worship, guilt and coming devastation, including exile to Assyria. Jesus uses its language to warn Jerusalem about approaching distress. Revelation later uses the same picture of people seeking shelter beneath mountains in the face of judgment. These are supported connections in the Bible’s wider warning that human power and hiding places cannot finally shield people from God’s judgment. (Hosea 10:1–15; Revelation 6:12–17)
