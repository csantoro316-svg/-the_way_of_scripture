Passage

Ezekiel 7:14-22

Can weapons, wealth or the sanctuary deliver the city?

Berean Standard Bible

14 They have blown the trumpet and made everything ready, but no one goes to war, for My wrath is upon the whole multitude. 15 The sword is outside; plague and famine are within. Those in the country will die by the sword, and those in the city will be devoured by famine and plague. 16 The survivors will escape and live in the mountains, moaning like doves of the valley, each for his own iniquity. 17 Every hand will go limp, and every knee will turn to water. 18 They will put on sackcloth, and terror will overwhelm them. Shame will cover all their faces, and all their heads will be shaved.

19 They will throw their silver into the streets, and their gold will seem unclean. Their silver and gold cannot save them in the day of the wrath of the LORD. They cannot satisfy their appetites or fill their stomachs with wealth, for it became the stumbling block that brought their iniquity. 20 His beautiful ornaments they transformed into pride and used them to fashion their vile images and detestable idols. Therefore I will make these into something unclean for them. 21 And I will hand these things over as plunder to foreigners and loot to the wicked of the earth, who will defile them. 22 I will turn My face away from them, and they will defile My treasured place. Violent men will enter it, and they will defile it.

What is the context?

The trumpet sounds and preparations are made, but no army goes out to fight. The sword threatens those outside, while plague and famine consume those inside. Even the survivors who reach the mountains mourn over their iniquity.

Limp hands, unsteady knees, sackcloth and shaved heads picture terror and shame. The people are losing the confidence and strength on which they relied.

Silver is thrown into the streets, and gold becomes unclean in their eyes. It cannot deliver them from the Lord’s wrath or fill their empty stomachs. The wealth that had drawn them into wrongdoing proves unable to supply what they most need.

The ornaments used in pride and idolatry become plunder for foreigners. The Lord turns His face away, and violent men enter and defile His treasured place. Wealth and a sacred building cannot shelter the city from the judgment it has been warned about.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley emphasize the failure of riches as a refuge. They connect wealth abused through pride and idolatry with its inability to rescue people in the day of judgment.

Wesley understands the treasured place as the temple and its innermost sanctuary. Henry likewise stresses that possession of the temple will not protect people who refuse to be governed by God.

The passage’s different losses therefore expose the same misplaced confidence. The people cannot use what they possess as a substitute for faithfulness to the Lord.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

First Peter 1:17–21 develops the contrast between silver and gold and the redemption God provides. Believers were redeemed from an empty way of life through Christ’s precious blood, rather than perishable wealth.

Peter connects that rescue with God’s purpose before the world’s foundation and with His raising and glorifying Jesus. Faith and hope are therefore directed toward God.

Peter does not explicitly quote Ezekiel 7. The shared theme helps distinguish the failure of wealth from the saving provision of God: what people cannot purchase for themselves is supplied through Christ.
