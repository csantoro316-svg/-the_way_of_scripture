Passage

Judges 6:11-24

What gives Gideon a reason to go?

Berean Standard Bible

11 Then the angel of the LORD came and sat down under the oak in Ophrah that belonged to Joash the Abiezrite, where his son Gideon was threshing wheat in a winepress to hide it from the Midianites. 12 And the angel of the LORD appeared to Gideon and said, “The LORD is with you, O mighty man of valor.” 13 “Please, my Lord,” Gideon replied, “if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened to us? And where are all His wonders of which our fathers told us, saying, ‘Has not the LORD brought us up out of Egypt?’ But now the LORD has forsaken us and delivered us into the hand of Midian.” 14 The LORD turned to him and said, “Go in the strength you have and save Israel from the hand of Midian. Am I not sending you?” 15 “Please, my Lord,” Gideon replied, “how can I save Israel? Indeed, my clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the youngest in my father’s house.” 16 “Surely I will be with you,” the LORD replied, “and you will strike down all the Midianites as one man.”

17 Gideon answered, “If I have found favor in Your sight, give me a sign that it is You speaking with me. 18 Please do not depart from this place until I return to You. Let me bring my offering and set it before You.” And the LORD said, “I will stay until you return.” 19 So Gideon went in and prepared a young goat and unleavened bread and an ephah of flour. He placed the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot and brought them out to present to Him under the oak. 20 And the angel of God said to him, “Take the meat and the unleavened bread, lay them on this rock, and pour out the broth.” And Gideon did so. 21 Then the angel of the LORD extended the tip of the staff that was in his hand and touched the meat and the unleavened bread. And fire flared from the rock and consumed the meat and the unleavened bread. Then the angel of the LORD vanished from his sight.

22 When Gideon realized that it was the angel of the LORD, he said, “Oh no, Lord GOD! I have seen the angel of the LORD face to face!” 23 But the LORD said to him, “Peace be with you. Do not be afraid, for you will not die.” 24 So Gideon built an altar to the LORD there and called it The LORD Is Peace. To this day it stands in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

What is the context?

Gideon is threshing wheat in a winepress to hide it from the Midianites when the angel of the LORD appears. The greeting names him a mighty man of valor, but his answer expresses the distress of the whole people: if God is with them, where is the rescue their fathers described? The preceding prophet’s message has already supplied the reader with an explanation for Israel’s condition.

The narrative then names the speaker as the LORD, who sends Gideon to save Israel. Gideon points to the weakness of his clan and his place as the youngest in his father’s household. God answers with the promise of His own presence. The call rests on the One sending him, rather than a new claim that Gideon has suddenly become powerful on his own.

Gideon asks for a sign and brings an offering. Fire from the rock consumes it, and the angel vanishes. Gideon becomes afraid that seeing the angel of the LORD will mean his death, but the LORD speaks peace and tells him not to fear. Gideon builds an altar called The LORD Is Peace. The Hebrew שָׁלוֹם, shalom (shah-LOHM), includes peace and well-being; Brown–Driver–Briggs includes the reassurance in verse 23 among its examples.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley, Henry, and Gill identify the angel in this encounter as the divine Son. They point to the movement between the titles angel of the LORD and LORD, together with the supernatural acceptance of the offering. This is their Christian reading of the messenger’s identity.

Henry emphasizes God strengthening someone whose faith needs confirmation. Wesley reads the promise of God’s presence as the source of Gideon’s strength. Their emphasis is on God’s initiative and reassurance, while taking Gideon’s questions seriously.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Gideon’s call recalls Moses’ earlier commission. In Exodus 3:7–12, God hears the cries of the oppressed, sends Moses, and answers his question about his own adequacy with the promise to be with him. Gideon’s encounter repeats that pattern of rescue and promised presence.

The theme continues when the risen Jesus sends His disciples to make disciples of all nations and promises to be with them always (Matthew 28:16–20). These servants have different assignments, but the shared assurance points to God accomplishing His purpose through people who depend on Him.

What is our position?

Our study Who is The Angel of the Lord? identifies the messenger in Gideon’s encounter as the eternal Son appearing before His birth as Jesus. The study draws attention to the account’s movement between the angel of the LORD and the LORD Himself, along with Gideon’s fear and God’s assurance of peace.
