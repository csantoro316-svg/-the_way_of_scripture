Passage

Exodus 38:1-7

Where will Israel bring its offerings?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Bezalel constructed the altar of burnt offering from acacia wood. It was square, five cubits long, five cubits wide, and three cubits high. 2 He made a horn at each of its four corners, so that the horns and altar were of one piece, and he overlaid the altar with bronze. 3 He made all the altar’s utensils of bronze—its pots, shovels, sprinkling bowls, meat forks, and firepans. 4 He made a grate of bronze mesh for the altar under its ledge, halfway up from the bottom.

5 At the four corners of the bronze grate he cast four rings as holders for the poles. 6 And he made the poles of acacia wood and overlaid them with bronze. 7 Then he inserted the poles into the rings on the sides of the altar for carrying it. He made the altar with boards so that it was hollow.

What is the context?

After the furnishings inside the tent, the account moves to the altar of burnt offering. Bezalel makes it from acacia wood overlaid with bronze, with horns at its corners, a bronze grate, utensils, rings, and carrying poles. It is larger than the gold incense altar described in chapter 37 and serves a different purpose.

The altar follows the directions in Exodus 27:1–8. It belongs in the courtyard, before the entrance to the tent. Its hollow construction and poles fit a dwelling that will travel with the people; the altar and its equipment are now ready for the appointed sacrifices.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley reads the altar in relation to Christ’s sacrifice and the offerings of thanksgiving made through Him. Calvin emphasizes that it is made according to God’s instructions so that the people can offer their sacrifices with confidence in the worship He has appointed.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 13:10–16 speaks of an altar, recalls the sacrifice whose body is taken outside the camp, and directs attention to Jesus suffering outside the city gate to sanctify His people by His blood. Through Him, believers are then called to offer praise, do good, and share with others.

First Peter 2:4–10 likewise describes God’s people as a holy priesthood offering spiritual sacrifices acceptable through Jesus Christ. The larger movement is toward a redeemed people whose whole life becomes grateful service through Him.
