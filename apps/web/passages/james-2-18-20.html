Passage

James 2:18-20

Is knowing that God exists the same as living by faith?

Berean Standard Bible

18 But someone will say, “You have faith and I have deeds.” Show me your faith without deeds, and I will show you my faith by my deeds. 19 You believe that God is one. Good for you! Even the demons believe that—and shudder. 20 O foolish man, do you want evidence that faith without deeds is worthless?

What is the context?

The discussion now takes the form of an exchange about showing faith. James places a claim that cannot be seen beside conduct that makes faith visible. The challenge is followed by an unsettling example: even demons recognize that God is one, and they shudder.

That example prevents agreement with a true statement from being treated as the whole response God seeks. The passage ends by pressing the question again: faith without deeds is worthless.

The example does not say the demons trust Christ for salvation. Nor does it give an account of their origin. James uses their recognition of God to make the reader examine what his or her own confession amounts to.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley both understand the example as showing the inadequacy of mere knowledge of God without a changed response to Him. They read the challenge to show faith through deeds as exposing a profession without living faith.

Readers who understand the wider passage as warning already-saved believers, such as Keith Pesce, frame the issue differently: whether faith is fruitful and protects a believer from sin’s consequences. The disagreement concerns the force of James’ warning, while the call to act rather than rest in religious claims remains serious.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Titus 2:11–14 describes Jesus giving Himself to redeem and purify a people eager for good deeds. Grace teaches a changed way of living as they await Him. That purpose goes beyond recognizing facts about God: it concerns the kind of people Christ is forming.
