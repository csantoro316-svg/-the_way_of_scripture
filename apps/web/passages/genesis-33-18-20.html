Passage

Genesis 33:18-20

Jacob Settles in Shechem

Berean Standard Bible

18 After Jacob had come from Paddan-aram, he arrived safely at the city of Shechem in the land of Canaan, and he camped just outside the city. 19 And the plot of ground where he pitched his tent, he purchased from the sons of Hamor, Shechem’s father, for a hundred pieces of silver. 20 There he set up an altar and called it El-Elohe-Israel.

What is the context?

Jacob arrives safely at Shechem in Canaan after his time in Paddan-aram. He camps outside the city and buys the plot where he pitches his tent from the sons of Hamor, Shechem's father. The brief notice places him back in the land God promised to bring him to.

He also builds an altar and names it El-Elohe-Israel, acknowledging God as the God of Israel. The new name he received during the wrestling now appears in his worship. His arrival is marked both by practical arrangements for the household and an acknowledgment of the God who has brought him safely through.

The purchase introduces Hamor's family before the painful events of chapter 34. For the moment, however, the account closes the journey with safe arrival and worship, rather than with Jacob claiming possession of the whole promised land.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a thankful acknowledgment of God's care after a difficult journey. Wesley's notes on Genesis 33:18, 20 join Jacob's safe arrival with worship of the God who has entered into covenant with him.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The safe arrival gives concrete expression to God's promise at Bethel to watch over Jacob and bring him back. The altar marks that faithfulness in a real place, even though the purchase is only a small part of the land his descendants are promised.

Hebrews 11:9–10, 13–16 describes the patriarchs as heirs who live as strangers while looking toward what God will provide. Jacob can give thanks for a completed journey while continuing to wait for the larger fulfillment. Gratitude for what God has already done and hope for what remains ahead belong together.
