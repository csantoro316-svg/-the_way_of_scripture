Passage

Ezekiel 47:21-23

What place do resident foreigners have in the inheritance?

Berean Standard Bible

21 You are to divide this land among yourselves according to the tribes of Israel. 22 You shall allot it as an inheritance for yourselves and for the foreigners who dwell among you and who have children. You are to treat them as native-born Israelites; along with you, they shall be allotted an inheritance among the tribes of Israel. 23 In whatever tribe a foreigner dwells, you are to assign his inheritance there,” declares the Lord GOD.

What is the context?

The division includes foreigners living among Israel who have children there. They are to be treated as native-born Israelites and receive an inheritance among the tribes.

Their portion is to be assigned in the tribe where they dwell. The Lord’s instruction gives them a lasting place in the community’s future rather than leaving them permanently without a share.

This sits alongside the earlier warnings about unauthorized and unfaithful sanctuary service. The vision distinguishes that responsibility from the place of foreigners who share life among the people.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley reads this in the light of Jews and Gentiles becoming one in Christ. Henry likewise sees room for believers united to Christ to share the blessings of the new covenant.

The immediate instruction concerns an inheritance for resident foreigners. Those Christian applications see the welcome here as pointing toward the wider inclusion made known through Christ.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 15:7–13 joins Christ confirming God’s promises to the patriarchs with Gentiles glorifying God for His mercy. Paul calls believers to welcome one another as Christ welcomed them.

This broader connection holds faithfulness to Israel’s promises together with mercy reaching the nations. Paul is not drawing the boundaries of Ezekiel’s tribal lands; he is explaining a shared life of worship and hope made possible through Christ.
