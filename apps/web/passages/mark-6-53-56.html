Passage

Mark 6:53-56

Healing at Gennesaret

Berean Standard Bible

53 When they had crossed over, they landed at Gennesaret and moored the boat. 54 As soon as they got out of the boat, the people recognized Jesus 55 and ran through that whole region, carrying the sick on mats to wherever they heard He was. 56 And wherever He went—villages and towns and countrysides—they laid the sick in the marketplaces and begged Him just to let them touch the fringe of His cloak. And all who touched Him were healed.

What is the context?

The boat lands at Gennesaret. People recognize Jesus and spread the news through the region, carrying the sick on mats to wherever they hear He is. Mark describes activity across villages, towns, and countryside rather than one isolated healing.

People lay the sick in marketplaces and ask that they be allowed to touch even the fringe of His cloak. All who touch Him are healed. The scene recalls the woman who touched His clothing in Mark 5:25–34, but Mark does not tell each person's story or explain what everyone understands about Jesus.

This summary follows the disciples' struggle to recognize what His works mean. The crowds recognize Jesus and seek His help; recognition and a desire for healing are not, by themselves, a full account of their faith or their later response to His teaching. Mark gives us the healings without supplying those missing details.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly see these healings as evidence of Jesus' compassionate power and the eagerness of people to bring others to Him. Calvin on Matthew 14:34–36 and Mark 6:53–56 emphasizes the many public witnesses to His works and the need to seek in Him more than bodily relief.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The healings belong to a larger scriptural picture of God's rescue. Isaiah 35:4–6 joins God's coming to save with restored sight, hearing, and movement. When asked whether He is the expected One, Jesus points to works of this kind in Matthew 11:2–6. Gennesaret provides another scene in that wider pattern; Mark does not label this summary a separate fulfillment quotation.

Scripture's promised end reaches beyond these local recoveries. Revelation 21:1–5 describes God dwelling with His people and the removal of death, mourning, and pain. These healings offer a glimpse of His restoring power, while that final renewal remains more than a promise that every illness will disappear immediately in the present.
