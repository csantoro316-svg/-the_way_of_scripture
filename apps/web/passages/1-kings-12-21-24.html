Passage

1 Kings 12:21-24

Why does Rehoboam call off the planned war?

Berean Standard Bible

21 And when Rehoboam arrived in Jerusalem, he mobilized the whole house of Judah and the tribe of Benjamin—180,000 chosen warriors—to fight against the house of Israel and restore the kingdom to Rehoboam son of Solomon. 22 But the word of God came to Shemaiah the man of God: 23 “Tell Rehoboam son of Solomon king of Judah, all the house of Judah and Benjamin, and the rest of the people 24 that this is what the LORD says: ‘You are not to go up and fight against your brothers, the Israelites. Each of you must return home, for this is My doing.’” So they listened to the word of the LORD and turned back according to the word of the LORD.

What is the context?

Back in Jerusalem, Rehoboam gathers a hundred eighty thousand chosen warriors from Judah and Benjamin. The plan is to fight Israel and restore his rule over the whole kingdom.

Before the campaign begins, God sends a word through Shemaiah. The king and people are not to fight their fellow Israelites. Each man is to go home, because the division has come from God.

This time they listen. The force turns back, and the attempt to reverse the separation by war stops at God’s command.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry understands the response as submitting to God even when His word crosses the king’s wishes. Gill likewise reads an attack to recover the kingdom as resisting the judgment God has just announced.

The command is specific to this threatened conflict between the two parts of Israel. Its force in the story is that Rehoboam’s claim to restore his power must yield to what God has said.
