Passage

Romans 16:1-2

Why does Paul ask the church to welcome Phoebe?

Berean Standard Bible

1 I commend to you our sister Phoebe, a servant of the church in Cenchrea. 2 Welcome her in the Lord in a manner worthy of the saints, and assist her with anything she may need from you. For she has been a great help to many people, including me.

What is the context?

Paul commends Phoebe as a sister and a servant of the church in Cenchrea. He asks the Roman believers to receive her in a way worthy of God’s people and to help her with whatever she needs.

The word translated servant is diakonos (dee-AH-koh-noss), διάκονος. Its range includes servant, minister and deacon; see Thayer’s entry. Paul identifies her service with a particular church and honors the help she has given to many, including himself.

The request makes mutual care practical. Someone who has supported others is now to receive support from another community. Paul’s commendation calls the church to recognize her service and act on that recognition.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly recognize this as a commendation of Phoebe’s important service and a call to welcome and assist her. Wesley’s note on verse 1 understands her role as that of a deaconess, and Calvin’s comment also treats it as recognized service in the church. The passage identifies her work and the response it deserves without describing an appointment ceremony or a complete set of duties.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 Peter 4:10–11 calls each believer to use God’s gifts in service to others, with the strength He provides and for His glory through Christ. Phoebe’s commendation gives a concrete example of service being recognized and supported within God’s people.

The movement between communities also matters: care is not confined to people already familiar to the receiving church. Shared belonging in the Lord becomes a reason to welcome and help a sister whose work has benefited others.
