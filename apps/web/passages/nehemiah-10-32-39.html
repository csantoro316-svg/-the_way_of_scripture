Passage

Nehemiah 10:32-39

What will it take to keep the temple’s service from being neglected?

Berean Standard Bible

32 We also place ourselves under the obligation to contribute a third of a shekel yearly for the service of the house of our God: 33 for the showbread, for the regular grain offerings and burnt offerings, for the Sabbath offerings, for the New Moons and appointed feasts, for the holy offerings, for the sin offerings to make atonement for Israel, and for all the duties of the house of our God. 34 We have cast lots among the priests, Levites, and people for the donation of wood by our families at the appointed times each year. They are to bring it to the house of our God to burn on the altar of the LORD our God, as it is written in the Law.

35 We will also bring the firstfruits of our land and of every fruit tree to the house of the LORD year by year. 36 And we will bring the firstborn of our sons and our livestock, as it is written in the Law, and will bring the firstborn of our herds and flocks to the house of our God, to the priests who minister in the house of our God.

37 Moreover, we will bring to the priests at the storerooms of the house of our God the firstfruits of our dough, of our grain offerings, of the fruit of all our trees, and of our new wine and oil. A tenth of our produce belongs to the Levites, so that they shall receive tithes in all the towns where we labor. 38 A priest of Aaron’s line is to accompany the Levites when they collect the tenth, and the Levites are to bring a tenth of these tithes to the storerooms of the treasury in the house of our God. 39 For the Israelites and the Levites are to bring the contributions of grain, new wine, and oil to the storerooms where the articles of the sanctuary are kept and where the ministering priests, the gatekeepers, and the singers stay. Thus we will not neglect the house of our God.”

What is the context?

The people undertake a yearly contribution of a third of a shekel for temple service, including the bread, regular offerings, appointed feasts and sacrifices for sin. Families are assigned times to bring wood for the altar.

They also promise firstfruits, firstborn offerings and the tithes supporting the Levites and priests. Numbers 18:8–32 explains the background: firstborn sons are redeemed, the Levites receive a tenth of the produce, and they in turn give a tenth of what they receive.

A priest is to accompany the Levites collecting the tithe. The goods go into the temple storerooms, supporting the service and those who carry it out. The closing promise brings all the arrangements together: the house of God is not to be neglected.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry emphasizes that turning from wrong must be accompanied by taking up neglected duties. Gill reads the provisions as practical support for worship and for the people who serve. The commitment includes money, produce, materials, schedules and oversight, making the promise concrete.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The repeated sacrifices supported here belong to the earlier worship described in Hebrews 10:1–25. Hebrews points to Christ’s one offering for sins and the access to God that comes through Him.

The passage then calls believers to draw near, hold to their hope, encourage love and good deeds, and continue meeting together. The wider connection is care for worship and for one another, now grounded in Christ’s completed offering. The New Testament gives that call its own foundation rather than simply transferring the temple’s supply arrangements to a church building.
