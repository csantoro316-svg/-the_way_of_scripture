Passage

Isaiah 62:1-12

Will Zion remain forsaken?

Berean Standard Bible

1 For Zion’s sake I will not keep silent, and for Jerusalem’s sake I will not keep still, until her righteousness shines like a bright light, her salvation like a blazing torch. 2 Nations will see your righteousness, and all kings your glory. You will be called by a new name that the mouth of the LORD will bestow. 3 You will be a crown of glory in the hand of the LORD, a royal diadem in the palm of your God. 4 No longer will you be called Forsaken, nor your land named Desolate; but you will be called Hephzibah, and your land Beulah; for the LORD will take delight in you, and your land will be His bride. 5 For as a young man marries a young woman, so your sons will marry you; and as a bridegroom rejoices over his bride, so your God will rejoice over you.

6 On your walls, O Jerusalem, I have posted watchmen; they will never be silent day or night. You who call on the LORD shall take no rest for yourselves, 7 nor give Him any rest until He establishes Jerusalem and makes her the praise of the earth. 8 The LORD has sworn by His right hand and by His mighty arm: “Never again will I give your grain to your enemies for food, nor will foreigners drink the new wine for which you have toiled. 9 For those who harvest grain will eat it and praise the LORD, and those who gather grapes will drink the wine in My holy courts.”

10 Go out, go out through the gates; prepare the way for the people! Build it up, build up the highway; clear away the stones; raise a banner for the nations! 11 Behold, the LORD has proclaimed to the ends of the earth, “Say to Daughter Zion: See, your Savior comes! Look, His reward is with Him, and His recompense goes before Him.” 12 And they will be called the Holy People, the Redeemed of The LORD; and you will be called Sought Out, A City Not Forsaken.

What is the context?

The opening voice will not be silent until Zion’s righteousness and salvation shine before the nations. Her coming glory is pictured as a crown held in the Lord’s hand, and He will give her a new name.

The names Forsaken and Desolate give way to Hephzibah and Beulah. The explanation in the passage is that the Lord delights in her and her land will be His bride. The marriage picture expresses His joy in the people and the reversal of abandonment.

Watchmen on Jerusalem’s walls will not be silent. Those who call on the Lord are told to persist until He establishes the city as a praise in the earth. His sworn promise is that the people will enjoy their own harvests and praise Him, instead of seeing enemies consume what they worked to produce.

The final summons is to prepare the way, remove stones and raise a banner for the nations. Salvation is announced to Daughter Zion with the coming Savior and His reward. The people are named holy and redeemed, and the city sought out rather than forsaken.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry understands the opening voice as the Son assuring the church of His love and continuing care. Wesley understands it as the prophet’s resolve to keep praying and proclaiming the promised deliverance. They therefore differ over the identity of the first speaker.

Both connect the chapter with the Lord’s faithfulness and the flourishing of His people. The new names express a changed condition and relationship, while the watchmen and repeated calls encourage persistence in prayer.

For Henry, that persistence rests on God’s willingness to receive earnest prayer. Wesley emphasizes a vigilant ministry that keeps the Lord’s promises before the people. Neither explanation treats the command as a need to repair a lapse in God’s memory.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The call to Daughter Zion has a close connection with Matthew 21:1–11. Matthew’s account of Jesus entering Jerusalem uses the summons to tell Zion that her King is coming. The entry identifies Jesus as the King received by the crowd, bringing Isaiah’s language of arrival into the Gospel story.

Revelation 22:12–17 takes up the promise of coming with a reward, wording also found earlier in Isaiah 40:10. Jesus identifies Himself as the Root and Offspring of David, and the passage ends with an invitation to receive the water of life freely.

The announced coming and the offered life belong together. God’s people are encouraged to await His promised salvation while hearing and passing on His invitation.
