Passage

1 Kings 10:1-9

What does the queen of Sheba discover about Solomon’s wisdom?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Now when the queen of Sheba heard about the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD, she came to test him with difficult questions. 2 She arrived in Jerusalem with a very large caravan—with camels bearing spices, gold in great abundance, and precious stones. And she came to Solomon and spoke to him all that was on her mind. 3 And Solomon answered all her questions; nothing was too difficult for the king to explain. 4 When the queen of Sheba saw all the wisdom of Solomon, the palace he had built, 5 the food at his table, the seating of his servants, the service and attire of his attendants, his cupbearers, and the burnt offerings he presented at the house of the LORD, it took her breath away.

6 She said to the king, “The report I heard in my own country about your words and wisdom is true. 7 But I did not believe these things until I came and saw with my own eyes. Indeed, not even half was told to me. Your wisdom and prosperity have far exceeded the report I heard. 8 How blessed are your men! How blessed are these servants of yours who stand continually before you and hear your wisdom! 9 Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delighted in you to set you on the throne of Israel. Because of the LORD’s eternal love for Israel, He has made you king to carry out justice and righteousness.”

What is the context?

The queen of Sheba hears of Solomon’s fame in connection with the LORD and comes to test him with difficult questions. She brings a great caravan of gifts and speaks freely about what is on her mind. Solomon answers every question.

The Hebrew word behind difficult questions is chidah, pronounced khee-DAH. Brown–Driver–Briggs explains that it can refer to a riddle or a perplexing question, and specifically identifies the queen’s questions here. Her visit puts the reports of Solomon’s wisdom to a real test.

She is overwhelmed by both his answers and what she sees of his household and worship. The report was true, she says, and still did not tell half the story. Her praise ends with the LORD: His love for Israel has placed Solomon on the throne to carry out justice and righteousness.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Henry emphasize the queen’s desire to hear wisdom and learn. Henry also follows Jesus’ use of her journey as a challenge to people who will not listen to Him.

Gill stresses the purpose she names for Solomon’s kingship: justice for the people, rather than rule for personal pleasure. The USCCB notes similarly draw attention to her reminder of that responsibility amid the splendor of the court.

These emphases help keep the visit centered on more than wealth. The queen recognizes wisdom, but she also identifies what such a gifted ruler is meant to do.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus directly returns to this visit in Matthew 12:38–42. When some scribes and Pharisees ask for a sign, He recalls the Queen of the South coming from far away to hear Solomon’s wisdom. Her response will stand as a rebuke to people refusing to receive what is before them.

Jesus then identifies Himself as greater than Solomon. The connection is stated by the New Testament itself: this queen’s willingness to seek wisdom helps expose the responsibility to listen when the greater King is present. The story leads beyond admiration of Solomon to the question of how people respond to Jesus.
