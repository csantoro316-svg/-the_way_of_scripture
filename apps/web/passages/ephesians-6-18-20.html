Passage

Ephesians 6:18-20

What should we pray for as we stand together?

Berean Standard Bible

18 Pray in the Spirit at all times, with every kind of prayer and petition. To this end, stay alert with all perseverance in your prayers for all the saints. 19 Pray also for me, that whenever I open my mouth, words may be given me so that I will boldly make known the mystery of the gospel, 20 for which I am an ambassador in chains. Pray that I may proclaim it fearlessly, as I should.

What is the context?

The call to prayer continues the instructions about God’s armor. Paul tells believers to pray in the Spirit at all times, with every kind of prayer and petition, while staying alert and persevering in prayer for all the saints.

He then includes himself in their prayers. His request is specific: words to make the mystery of the gospel known and boldness to speak as he should. He describes himself as an ambassador in chains.

His imprisonment has not removed his calling to proclaim the gospel, and his role as an apostle has not removed his need for others’ prayers. The community’s prayer includes those who lead and speak as well as the wider body.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as a call to persistent prayer for fellow believers and for the clear, courageous proclamation of the gospel. Wesley’s notes on verses 18–20 explain prayer in the Spirit through the Spirit’s influence and emphasize intercession. The USCCB note on 6:10–20 also gives prayer a central place in the call to stand firm. The passage names both the broad concern for all God’s people and Paul’s particular request.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 4:23–31 supplies a close parallel. After threats against the apostles, the believers recognize God’s authority and purposes, ask for boldness to speak, and ask Him to act through healing and signs in Jesus’ name.

The account then describes them being filled with the Spirit and speaking God’s word boldly. Prayer supports witness as God’s people depend on Him together. Paul’s request here belongs within that larger pattern of the gospel being made known despite opposition.

What is our position?

Our study What Does Prayer Look Like? uses Paul’s request here as an example of prayer for faithfulness under pressure. He asks for words and courage to make the gospel known while in chains. The study emphasizes seeking God’s strength to carry out His purposes in the circumstances we face.
