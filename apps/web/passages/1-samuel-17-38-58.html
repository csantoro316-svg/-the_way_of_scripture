Passage

1 Samuel 17:38-58

How does David’s victory become a rescue for the whole army?

Berean Standard Bible

38 Then Saul clothed David in his own tunic, put a bronze helmet on his head, and dressed him in armor. 39 David strapped his sword over the tunic and tried to walk, but he was not accustomed to them. “I cannot walk in these,” David said to Saul. “I am not accustomed to them.” So David took them off. 40 And David took his staff in his hand, selected five smooth stones from the brook, and put them in the pouch of his shepherd’s bag. And with his sling in hand, he approached the Philistine.

41 Now the Philistine came closer and closer to David, with his shield-bearer before him. 42 When the Philistine looked and saw David, he despised him because he was just a boy, ruddy and handsome. 43 “Am I a dog,” he said to David, “that you come at me with sticks?” And the Philistine cursed David by his gods. 44 “Come here,” he called to David, “and I will give your flesh to the birds of the air and the beasts of the field!” 45 But David said to the Philistine, “You come against me with sword and spear and javelin, but I come against you in the name of the LORD of Hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied. 46 This day the LORD will deliver you into my hand. This day I will strike you down, cut off your head, and give the carcasses of the Philistine army to the birds of the air and the creatures of the earth. Then the whole world will know that there is a God in Israel. 47 And all those assembled here will know that it is not by sword or spear that the LORD saves; for the battle is the LORD’s, and He will give all of you into our hands.”

48 As the Philistine started forward to attack him, David ran quickly toward the battle line to meet him. 49 Then David reached into his bag, took out a stone, and slung it, striking the Philistine on the forehead. The stone sank into his forehead, and he fell facedown on the ground. 50 Thus David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and a stone; without a sword in his hand he struck down the Philistine and killed him. 51 David ran and stood over him. He grabbed the Philistine’s sword and pulled it from its sheath and killed him, and he cut off his head with the sword. When the Philistines saw that their hero was dead, they turned and ran. 52 Then the men of Israel and Judah charged forward with a shout and pursued the Philistines to the entrance of Gath and to the gates of Ekron. And the bodies of the Philistines were strewn along the Shaaraim road to Gath and Ekron. 53 When the Israelites returned from their pursuit of the Philistines, they plundered their camps. 54 David took the head of the Philistine and brought it to Jerusalem, and he put Goliath’s weapons in his own tent.

55 As Saul had watched David going out to confront the Philistine, he said to Abner the commander of the army, “Abner, whose son is this young man?” “As surely as you live, O king,” Abner replied, “I do not know.” 56 “Find out whose son this young man is!” said the king. 57 So when David returned from killing the Philistine, still holding his head in his hand, Abner took him and brought him before Saul. 58 “Whose son are you, young man?” asked Saul. “I am the son of your servant Jesse of Bethlehem,” David replied.

What is the context?

David tries Saul’s equipment but takes it off because he is not accustomed to it. He carries his staff, sling and five smooth stones. The account gives the number of stones without explaining a hidden meaning for it.

Goliath despises the young opponent and threatens him. David answers that he comes in the name of the LORD. He declares two purposes for the victory: the world will know there is a God in Israel, and those gathered will learn that the LORD’s saving power does not depend on sword or spear.

David runs forward and strikes Goliath with a sling stone. He then takes Goliath’s sword and cuts off his head. The Philistines flee, and the Israelites pursue and plunder the camp. The victory one man wins changes the position of the army that had been afraid to answer the challenge.

The account records Goliath’s head being brought to Jerusalem and his weapons being kept. It then returns to Saul’s question to Abner as David went out: whose son is he? After the battle, David identifies himself as Jesse’s son from Bethlehem. The question concerns his family, which also matters to the reward described earlier.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill understand David’s rejection of the armor as lack of experience using it. The BSB expresses this as not being accustomed to it. The statement does not require making David’s own untested readiness for kingship the direct meaning of the words.

Henry emphasizes the humility and faith in David’s answer and God receiving the credit for the outcome. He also applies the conflict to Christian resistance against the devil. That is a wider devotional comparison; Goliath remains a human opponent in the story.

The closing family question is explained in different ways. Gill highlights Saul’s need to know the family into which his daughter might marry, while also discussing forgetfulness. Wesley allows several reasons Saul might not recall the earlier servant clearly. The USCCB study notes instead understand parts of the chapter as a different account of David’s introduction brought together with the preceding story. These are explanations of how the accounts fit; Saul’s actual question is about whose son David is.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The victory invites a comparison with rescue won by one on behalf of others. Israel benefits from David defeating the opponent it feared. The New Testament describes a greater deliverance in Hebrews 2:14–18: Jesus shares our humanity, breaks the devil’s power through His death, and frees those enslaved by fear of death.

This is a parallel, rather than a New Testament statement that every weapon or stone predicts a separate event. David defeats an enemy in battle; Jesus overcomes through His own death and saving work. First Corinthians 15:54–58 places the final victory over death in Him and calls His people to steadfast service because of the victory they receive.
