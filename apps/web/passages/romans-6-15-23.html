Passage

Romans 6:15-23

What kind of freedom leads to holiness and life?

Berean Standard Bible

15 What then? Shall we sin because we are not under law, but under grace? Certainly not! 16 Do you not know that when you offer yourselves as obedient slaves, you are slaves to the one you obey, whether you are slaves to sin leading to death, or to obedience leading to righteousness? 17 But thanks be to God that, though you once were slaves to sin, you wholeheartedly obeyed the form of teaching to which you were committed. 18 You have been set free from sin and have become slaves to righteousness.

19 I am speaking in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh. Just as you used to offer the parts of your body in slavery to impurity and to escalating wickedness, so now offer them in slavery to righteousness leading to holiness. 20 For when you were slaves to sin, you were free of obligation to righteousness. 21 What fruit did you reap at that time from the things of which you are now ashamed? The outcome of those things is death. 22 But now that you have been set free from sin and have become slaves to God, the fruit you reap leads to holiness, and the outcome is eternal life. 23 For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

What is the context?

Paul again rejects the idea that being under grace permits sin. He uses the relationship between a slave and master to explain allegiance: what people obey shapes the life they serve. Sin leads toward death; obedience leads toward righteousness.

He thanks God that the believers have obeyed from the heart the teaching entrusted to them. Their change is described as release from sin and service to righteousness. Paul acknowledges that he is using human terms to make the point understandable.

The fruit of the former life was shame and death. The fruit of belonging to God is holiness, with eternal life as the outcome. Holiness means a life increasingly given to God and shaped by what is right.

The final contrast keeps the gift clear. Death is the wage sin pays; eternal life is God’s gift in Christ Jesus. Paul’s call to serve God does not turn that gift into a wage people earn. The gift changes the life they are called to live.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the passage as freedom from one master for a new allegiance to God, rather than freedom to serve every desire. The contrast between wages and gift keeps grace central, while the account of obedience and holiness keeps the changed life in view.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John 8:31–36 likewise speaks of slavery to sin and the Son who sets people free. Freedom is connected with continuing in Jesus’ word, rather than merely claiming a privileged ancestry.

Ephesians 2:8–10 puts the gift of salvation beside being created in Christ for good works. That connection helps hold Paul’s two emphases together: God gives life freely, and the people He makes alive are called into a life that serves His purposes.

What is our position?

The Gospel According to Jesus uses Romans 6:23 to distinguish the wages sin earns from the gift God gives. Our position is that eternal life comes through Jesus Christ as a gift received in faith, rather than a wage for religious effort or moral achievement. The life He gives also calls the believer into repentance and following Him.
