Passage

2 Samuel 20:14-22

How does a wise woman save her city from destruction?

Berean Standard Bible

14 Sheba passed through all the tribes of Israel to Abel-beth-maacah and through the entire region of the Berites, who gathered together and followed him. 15 And Joab’s troops came and besieged Sheba in Abel-beth-maacah and built a siege ramp against the outer rampart of the city. As all the troops with Joab were battering the wall to topple it, 16 a wise woman called out from the city, “Listen! Listen! Please tell Joab to come here so that I may speak with him.” 17 When he had come near to her, the woman asked, “Are you Joab?” “I am,” he replied. “Listen to the words of your servant,” she said. “I am listening,” he answered.

18 Then the woman said, “Long ago they used to say, ‘Seek counsel at Abel,’ and that is how disputes were settled. 19 I am among the peaceable and faithful in Israel, but you are trying to destroy a city that is a mother in Israel. Why would you swallow up the LORD’s inheritance?” 20 “Far be it!” Joab declared. “Far be it from me to swallow up or destroy! 21 That is not the case. But a man named Sheba son of Bichri, from the hill country of Ephraim, has lifted up his hand against the king, against David. Deliver him alone, and I will depart from the city.” “Look,” the woman replied, “his head will be thrown to you over the wall.” 22 Then the woman went to all the people with her wise counsel, and they cut off the head of Sheba son of Bichri and threw it to Joab. So he blew the ram’s horn and his men dispersed from the city, each to his own home. And Joab returned to the king in Jerusalem.

What is the context?

Sheba reaches Abel-beth-maacah, and Joab’s army besieges the city. The troops build a ramp and batter the wall. A wise woman calls for Joab and asks him to listen.

She appeals to Abel’s reputation as a place where disputes were settled and identifies herself with the peaceable and faithful in Israel. She challenges Joab’s attack on a city that belongs to the LORD’s inheritance.

Joab says his target is Sheba alone and offers to withdraw if the city hands him over. The woman takes the matter to the people. They cut off Sheba’s head and throw it over the wall. Joab sounds the horn, ends the siege and returns to Jerusalem.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley emphasizes that the decisive wisdom comes from a woman whom the account does not name. Her ability to speak and act wisely matters more than whether she holds a position usually associated with men.

Henry draws attention to the value of making the actual dispute clear. Joab seeks one rebel; the woman speaks for a city that does not want to be destroyed with him. Their conversation makes an agreement possible.

The outcome is severe: Sheba is killed. The woman’s intervention saves the city from the wider destruction the siege threatened. Both parts belong to the account.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

This scene connects with the Bible’s wider teaching about wisdom that makes peace. The woman listens, identifies the issue and speaks to protect her people. Wisdom becomes visible in what she does.

James 3:13–18 contrasts jealousy and selfish ambition with wisdom that is humble, peace-loving and full of mercy. It adds a question for readers of this chapter: do our words and decisions stir up conflict, or help bring it to an end?

James does not interpret this siege or give Christians instructions to copy its execution. The connection is the value of practical wisdom that seeks peace amid destructive conflict.
