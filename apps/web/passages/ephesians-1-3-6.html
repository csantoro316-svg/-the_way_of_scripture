Passage

Ephesians 1:3-6

Why does Paul begin by praising God for choosing and adopting us?

Berean Standard Bible

3 Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly realms. 4 For He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world to be holy and blameless in His presence. In love 5 He predestined us for adoption as His sons through Jesus Christ, according to the good pleasure of His will, 6 to the praise of His glorious grace, which He has freely given us in the Beloved One.

What is the context?

Paul begins an extended blessing of God that continues through verse 14. He praises the Father for every spiritual blessing given in Christ. The gifts named in the paragraph include adoption, redemption, forgiveness and the promised Spirit.

He says God chose us in Christ before the foundation of the world, with the purpose that we should be holy and blameless in His presence. The blessing therefore reaches back beyond the believers’ own lifetimes and points toward the kind of people God intends them to be.

Paul then speaks of being predestined for adoption through Jesus Christ. The Greek word is προορίζω, proorizō (proh-or-ID-zoh), meaning to decide or appoint beforehand. Thayer’s lexicon gives that sense and identifies its use here with the purpose God has appointed for His people.

The purpose named is adoption as God’s sons through Jesus Christ. Paul speaks of God’s good pleasure and His freely given grace in the Beloved One. The passage is an expression of praise for that grace.

Holiness, belonging and praise all appear together. God’s choosing is described in relation to Christ and the family He is bringing into His presence.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly see God’s initiative, grace and purpose of adoption at the center of these verses. They differ over how His choosing relates to a person’s coming to faith.

The Reformed Westminster Confession, chapter 3, teaches that God chose particular people in Christ through His free grace and love, without basing that choice on foreseen faith or good works. In this reading, His choice is the source of their coming to faith and salvation.

In the Wesleyan reading, Wesley’s notes on verses 4–5 understand the chosen as those God foreknew as believing in Christ. He explains predestination here as God’s prior purpose that those who believe should enjoy adoption and inheritance with Christ. The Methodist Articles of Religion, article VIII, also insist that people cannot turn to faith by their own natural strength: God’s grace enables their response.

The Catholic Church describes God’s eternal plan as including each person’s free response to His grace in the Catechism, paragraph 600. Paragraphs 2012–2013 connect predestination and conformity to the Son with the call to holiness, lived through the strength Christ gives. This keeps God’s prior purpose and a grace-enabled human response together.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Galatians 4:4–7 describes the Father sending His Son at the appointed time so that we might receive adoption, then sending the Spirit of His Son into our hearts. What Ephesians traces to God’s purpose before creation is also described as His work carried out through the Son and Spirit.

Romans 8:14–23 carries the family picture forward into shared inheritance with Christ, suffering, coming glory and the redemption of our bodies. Adoption belongs within that larger hope: God’s purpose brings His people into a life and future shared with His Son.
