Passage

1 Peter 4:12-16

What does suffering for Christ mean?

Berean Standard Bible

12 Beloved, do not be surprised at the fiery trial that has come upon you, as though something strange were happening to you. 13 But rejoice that you share in the sufferings of Christ, so that you may be overjoyed at the revelation of His glory. 14 If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you.

15 Indeed, none of you should suffer as a murderer or thief or wrongdoer, or even as a meddler. 16 But if you suffer as a Christian, do not be ashamed, but glorify God that you bear that name.

What is the context?

Peter tells his beloved readers not to treat their fiery trial as something entirely unexpected. The suffering tests them and joins their experience with Christ’s, while their hope looks toward the revelation of His glory.

Insults for Christ’s name do not mean God has abandoned them. Peter says the Spirit of glory and of God rests on them. The judgment of those who ridicule them is not the measure of their standing before Him.

He again distinguishes suffering for Christ from suffering for wrongdoing. Murder, theft, evil and meddling give no reason to claim persecution as a Christian. But those suffering because they belong to Christ need not be ashamed of His name.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley read these trials in relation to suffering for Christ, rather than as approval of whatever behavior brings criticism. Both connect present endurance with the hope of sharing His glory.

Calvin explicitly allows present joy and grief to exist together. The promise of glory is consolation within suffering, rather than a demand to pretend that pain is not real.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus tells His disciples in John 15:18–21 that the world’s hostility to them follows its hostility to Him. Peter’s description of sharing Christ’s sufferings carries that connection into the life of these churches.

The letter places this experience on the way to Christ’s revealed glory. Their hope is grounded in belonging to the risen Lord even while others reject His name.
