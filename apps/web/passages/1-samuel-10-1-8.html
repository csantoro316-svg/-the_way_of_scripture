Passage

1 Samuel 10:1-8

How will Saul know that God has called him?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then Samuel took a flask of oil, poured it on Saul’s head, kissed him, and said, “Has not the LORD anointed you ruler over His inheritance? 2 When you leave me today, you will find two men at Rachel’s tomb in Zelzah on the border of Benjamin. They will say to you, ‘The donkeys you seek have been found, and now your father has stopped worrying about the donkeys and started worrying about you, asking, “What should I do about my son?”’ 3 Then you will go on from there until you come to the Oak of Tabor. Three men going up to God at Bethel will meet you there, one carrying three young goats, another carrying three loaves of bread, and another carrying a skin of wine. 4 They will greet you and give you two loaves of bread, which you will accept from their hands.

5 After that you will come to Gibeah of God, where the Philistines have an outpost. As you approach the city, you will meet a group of prophets coming down from the high place, preceded by harps, tambourines, flutes, and lyres, and they will be prophesying. 6 Then the Spirit of the LORD will rush upon you, and you will prophesy with them; and you will be transformed into a different person. 7 When these signs have come, do as the occasion demands, for God is with you. 8 And you shall go before me to Gilgal, and surely I will come to you to offer burnt offerings and to sacrifice peace offerings. Wait seven days until I come to you and show you what you are to do.”

What is the context?

In private, Samuel pours oil on Saul’s head, kisses him, and announces that the LORD has anointed him ruler over His inheritance. Israel still belongs to God. Saul is being appointed to a responsibility over God’s people, rather than receiving them as possessions to use however he wishes.

Samuel then describes three encounters. Two men will report that the donkeys are found and Saul’s father is worried about him. Three men on their way to worship at Bethel will give him bread. A group of prophets will meet him at Gibeah, and the Spirit of the LORD will come upon him so that he prophesies with them.

These signs will confirm the message and God’s presence with him. They are accompanied by an instruction to wait at Gilgal for Samuel to come and show him what to do. Being equipped for kingship does not remove the need to listen and obey.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Henry understand the anointing as a sign of God equipping Saul for his office. Wesley also emphasizes that the people remain God’s inheritance: the appointment is a responsibility under God’s will. The signs give Saul confirmation rather than permission to treat kingship as unrestricted personal power.

The encounters have also prompted devotional comparisons. Wesley and Henry take Rachel’s tomb as a reminder of mortality even while Saul is receiving royal honor. That is a lesson they draw from the setting; Samuel’s explicit words in this scene concern the messengers, the donkeys and Saul’s father. Any further meaning should remain distinguishable from the stated sign.

Wesley understands the prophesying here as potentially including Spirit-prompted praise and speech about God, rather than only predictions of future events. The accompanying instruments and group setting belong to the description, while the exact words Saul will speak are not given.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The anointing and the Spirit’s enabling lead Wesley and Henry to a larger Christian comparison with Jesus, the anointed one. That connection has a direct New Testament anchor in Luke 4:16–21. Jesus reads Isaiah’s announcement of the Spirit’s anointing for good news and release, and says it is fulfilled in their hearing.

Saul is equipped for a particular task in Israel’s history. Jesus announces a mission of rescue reaching the poor, captives and oppressed. The connection is the pattern of anointing and divine appointment; it does not make Saul’s whole life an example of Jesus’ faithfulness or turn each item carried by the travelers into a hidden prediction.
