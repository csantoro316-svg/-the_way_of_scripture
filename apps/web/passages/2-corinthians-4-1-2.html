Passage

2 Corinthians 4:1-2

Why must the message be shared openly and honestly?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Therefore, since God in His mercy has given us this ministry, we do not lose heart. 2 Instead, we have renounced secret and shameful ways. We do not practice deceit, nor do we distort the word of God. On the contrary, by open proclamation of the truth, we commend ourselves to every man’s conscience in the sight of God.

What is the context?

Paul carries forward the discussion of the Spirit’s life-giving ministry. God has given him this work in mercy, so he continues rather than abandoning it. His confidence rests on what he has received, not a claim that the task is easy.

He describes the methods he has rejected: secret shameful practices, deceit and distortion of God’s word. In their place he sets an open proclamation of the truth, before people’s consciences and in God’s sight.

This is part of his explanation of trustworthy ministry. The message is not to be adjusted behind the scenes to serve the messenger’s advantage. Its truth is to be set out plainly for people to consider.

Not losing heart here describes continuing in the work God has given. It should be read alongside Paul’s earlier honesty about anguish and being burdened beyond his strength, rather than turned into a rule that faithful workers never feel distressed.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as perseverance through God’s mercy joined with honest handling of His word. Wesley’s notes on verses 1–2 emphasize continuing the ministry and rejecting deceit or alterations that suit the hearers. The USCCB note on verses 1–2 likewise connects the ministry with confidence and openness.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In John 17:17–20, Jesus prays for His followers to be sanctified in the truth, identifies the Father’s word as truth and sends them into the world. He also looks toward those who will believe through their message.

Honest proclamation serves that mission. God’s purpose of making Christ known calls for messengers who serve the truth they have received, rather than reshaping it to attract attention or secure their own standing.
