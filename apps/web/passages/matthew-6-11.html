Passage

Matthew 6:11

Our Daily Bread

Berean Standard Bible

11 Give us this day our daily bread.

What is the context?

This request follows the petitions for God's name, kingdom, and will in Matthew 6:9–13. The person praying depends on the Father to give what sustains life. The request is for us, drawing others into that dependence, and for this day, rather than claiming control of the whole future.

The word translated daily is ἐπιούσιος, epiousios (eh-pee-OO-see-oss). Thayer's Greek Lexicon discusses necessary sustenance and the coming day among its explanations. The term is unusual, and its formation has been debated. It should not be made to prove a whole interpretation by itself.

The ordinary word for bread can also mean food more broadly. Thayer's entry for bread lists that use in Matthew 6:11 and separately explains Jesus' description of Himself as the bread of life in John 6. Bread therefore has an ordinary use for food and a use as a picture of life in Christ. The context matters in deciding how it is being used.

Luke 11:1–4 places a similar daily request within Jesus' teaching on prayer. Later in Matthew 6, Jesus speaks of the Father's knowledge of food and clothing needs and calls His hearers to seek God's kingdom instead of being ruled by anxiety. Readers bring that surrounding teaching to the different understandings below.

How is this passage commonly understood?

In the Reformed tradition, the Westminster Larger Catechism, question 193, understands the request as asking God for sufficient provision for this life, with contentment and a right use of what He gives.

Catholic teaching includes bodily nourishment, God's Word, and Christ in the Eucharist (the Lord's Supper); see the Compendium, questions 592–593. Wesley's note on Matthew 6:11 also includes the needs of soul and body, the bread of the Lord's Supper, and God's grace. These readings share dependence on God while differing over how the petition includes spiritual nourishment.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Bread and dependence have a larger place in Scripture. In Exodus 16:4–5,14–21, God provides manna, and Israel gathers what is needed day by day. That account gives a useful parallel for receiving life and provision from God rather than treating them as independently secured.

In John 6:26–35,47–58, Jesus explicitly compares the wilderness manna with the life He gives. The manna's recipients still died; the life He gives lasts beyond death. This connection moves from God's sustaining people in the wilderness to His Son giving life to the world. This is a comparison with the prayer's theme of dependence; John does not directly explain Matthew 6:11.

What is our position?

Our study Prayer and Faith connects this petition with Jesus' teaching in John 6 about Himself as the bread of life. It understands daily bread as dependence on Christ for continued life in Him, rather than a request for physical food. In that reading, the request remains centered on seeking Him and living in His will. The study develops the connection with abiding in Christ in John 15.
