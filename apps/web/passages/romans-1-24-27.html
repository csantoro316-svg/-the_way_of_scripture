Passage

Romans 1:24-27

What does Paul mean when he says God gave people over?

Berean Standard Bible

24 Therefore God gave them over in the desires of their hearts to impurity for the dishonoring of their bodies with one another. 25 They exchanged the truth of God for a lie, and worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator, who is forever worthy of praise! Amen.

26 For this reason God gave them over to dishonorable passions. Even their women exchanged natural relations for unnatural ones. 27 Likewise, the men abandoned natural relations with women and burned with lust for one another. Men committed indecent acts with other men and received in themselves the due penalty for their error.

What is the context?

Paul connects the exchange of the Creator for created things with a further handing over: God gives people over to the desires that dishonor their bodies. The phrase appears again in verse 26 and will appear a third time in verse 28. The repeated pattern presents wrongdoing and judgment as connected.

Verse 25 keeps worship at the center of the passage. People have exchanged God’s truth for a lie and served the creature rather than the Creator. Paul interrupts the description with praise to the Creator, whose worth does not change when people refuse Him.

He then describes women exchanging natural relations for unnatural ones and men leaving relations with women to desire one another. Paul calls these passions dishonorable and the acts indecent. Christians differ over how the scope and setting of this description apply to same-sex relationships today, as the readings below explain.

The passage does not name a particular disease as the penalty in verse 27. Nor does it end the account of wrongdoing with sexual behavior. Paul immediately continues with a broad list that includes greed, deceit, arrogance, gossip and mercilessness.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly connect God’s giving people over with judgment in the midst of human wrongdoing. Wesley on verse 24 describes a withdrawal of restraining grace: God ceases to hold people back from the course they pursue. That is his explanation of the action, rather than wording Paul supplies about every step of the process.

Where traditions differ, the Catholic Church reads the passage as part of the scriptural basis for rejecting same-sex sexual acts. The Catechism, paragraphs 2357–2359, also requires respect, compassion and sensitivity toward people with same-sex attraction and rejects unjust discrimination. The teaching distinguishes its judgment about acts from its obligation to care for people.

A traditional Reformed reading likewise understands the acts as contrary to God’s created order. Calvin on verse 26 takes this approach; the Westminster Confession 24.1 defines marriage as between one man and one woman. Calvin’s explanation and the confession’s marriage teaching are related sources, rather than identical kinds of authority.

An affirming Protestant reading places greater weight on the setting of idolatry, excessive desire and ancient expectations of male and female roles. In an essay published by the United Methodist Church, John C. Holbert understands Paul’s description within that first-century framework and does not apply it as a universal rejection of faithful same-sex relationships today. This is a named teacher’s reading, not a claim that all Methodists interpret the passage alike. The disagreement concerns the reach of Paul’s description, not whether verses 26–27 contain a negative judgment.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The attention to bodies has a place in the letter’s wider story. Romans 8:18–25 looks toward creation’s freedom from decay and the redemption of believers’ bodies. God’s saving purpose concerns embodied life and the creation He made, not only thoughts or an escape from the physical world.

This is a connection across the letter rather than a separate ruling on the disputed applications above. The Creator who is refused in chapter 1 remains the source of the hope described in chapter 8.
