Passage

Ephesians 5:1-2

What does it mean to love as God’s children?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Be imitators of God, therefore, as beloved children, 2 and walk in love, just as Christ loved us and gave Himself up for us as a fragrant sacrificial offering to God.

What is the context?

The word therefore carries forward the call to forgive as God forgave in Christ (4:32). Paul now calls believers to imitate God as children who are loved by Him. God’s love is the starting point for their response.

Walking in love describes the way they are to live. Christ supplies the pattern: He loved us and gave Himself for us. Paul describes His self-giving as an offering and sacrifice pleasing to God.

This short passage connects the practical kindness and forgiveness of chapter 4 with the warnings that follow. Loving others belongs with turning from conduct that uses or harms them.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a call to reflect the Father’s love through the pattern of Christ’s self-giving. Wesley’s note on verse 1 and the USCCB note connect imitating God with forgiving and loving others. The passage grounds that love in what Christ has already done for His people.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The pleasing-aroma language has a clear scriptural background. Exodus 29:15–18 describes a burnt offering to the Lord with that language. Ephesians explicitly applies sacrificial language to Christ’s gift of Himself. This is a connection the passage makes, rather than a claim that every detail of that offering supplies a separate symbol here.

2 Corinthians 5:14–15 connects Christ’s death with His people living for the One who died and rose for them. His saving work gives direction to their life: received love becomes the reason for loving service.
