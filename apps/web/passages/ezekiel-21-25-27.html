Passage

Ezekiel 21:25-27

Who has the right to rule when the crown is taken away?

Berean Standard Bible

25 And you, O profane and wicked prince of Israel, the day has come for your final punishment.’ 26 This is what the Lord GOD says: ‘Remove the turban, and take off the crown. Things will not remain as they are: Exalt the lowly and bring low the exalted. 27 A ruin, a ruin, I will make it a ruin! And it will not be restored until the arrival of Him to whom it belongs, to whom I have assigned the right of judgment.’

What is the context?

The Lord addresses Israel’s wicked prince as the time for punishment arrives. The turban and crown are to be removed, and the existing order will not continue unchanged.

The lowly are to be raised and the exalted brought low. The repeated declaration of ruin gives the collapse its full weight.

The final line also looks beyond the removal. Restoration waits for the One to whom it belongs, the One assigned the right of judgment. The future depends on God’s appointment, rather than the present prince’s ability to hold his crown.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley identify the prince facing removal as Zedekiah and the coming rightful ruler as the Messiah. Henry sees hope for the Messiah’s kingdom within the warning of overturned governments.

The Cambridge note on verse 27 likewise reads the coming figure as the Messiah. These readings connect the loss of Judah’s royal rule with a future that God gives to His chosen ruler, rather than with an assurance that the present king will escape.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Luke 1:30–33 identifies Jesus as the Son who receives David’s throne and whose kingdom has no end. The authority is given by God, matching the larger hope of a ruler appointed by Him.

Revelation 19:11–16 presents the faithful and true King who judges in righteousness. His titles and authority stand above the nations and their rulers.

Neither passage explicitly quotes Ezekiel 21. They explain why Christian readers connect its promise of rightful rule and judgment with Christ: the answer to failed human kingship is the King God appoints.
