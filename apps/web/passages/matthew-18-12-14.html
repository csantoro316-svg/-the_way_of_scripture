Passage

Matthew 18:12-14

The Father Cares About the One Who Strays

Berean Standard Bible

12 What do you think? If a man has a hundred sheep and one of them goes astray, will he not leave the ninety-nine on the hills and go out to search for the one that is lost? 13 And if he finds it, truly I tell you, he rejoices more over that one sheep than over the ninety-nine that did not go astray. 14 In the same way, your Father in heaven is not willing that any of these little ones should perish.

What is the context?

Jesus has told His disciples not to look down on any of the little ones. He now asks them to picture a shepherd with a hundred sheep. One wanders, and the shepherd leaves the ninety-nine to search for it. If he finds it, that return brings him particular joy.

Verse 14 explains the comparison through the Father's concern that none of these little ones perish. The one who has wandered still matters. The disciples are being taught to share that concern, and the next passage shows them how to seek the restoration of a brother who sins.

Luke 15:1–7 also tells a lost-sheep parable, in the setting of Jesus welcoming sinners. Here in Matthew, the surrounding teaching especially directs the disciples toward care for one another.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the shepherd's search as an expression of God's care and a pattern for seeking those who stray. Wesley on 18:14 stresses that the Father does not despise the least of them; the US Catholic bishops' note on 18:10–14 applies the story to bringing wandering disciples back to the community.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The searching shepherd is part of a larger biblical picture. In Ezekiel 34:11–16, God promises to search for His scattered flock, rescue them, bring back the strays, and strengthen the weak. The parallel helps explain the Father's concern in Jesus' story: the lost are sought and cared for.

Jesus takes that care further in John 10:11–18, where He calls Himself the good shepherd and says He lays down His life for the sheep. He also speaks of bringing other sheep into one flock under one shepherd. God's saving purpose gathers people to the Shepherd who gives His life for them; the disciples learn to care for the people He gathers.
