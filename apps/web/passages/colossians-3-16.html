Passage

Colossians 3:16

How should Christ’s word shape our singing and teaching?

Berean Standard Bible

16 Let the word of Christ richly dwell within you as you teach and admonish one another with all wisdom, and as you sing psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs with gratitude in your hearts to God.

What is the context?

Paul has called the believers to compassion, forgiveness, love and the peace of Christ as one body. He now describes how Christ’s word should make its home among them.

Teaching and admonishing are mutual activities. The community is to help one another understand and live the message, with wisdom. Psalms, hymns and spiritual songs belong within this shared life.

The verse also directs gratitude toward God. The surrounding paragraph repeatedly calls for thanksgiving and ends by placing every word and deed under the name of the Lord Jesus. Singing serves both a grateful relationship with God and the building up of His people.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as a call for the community’s life and singing to be filled with Christ’s message. Wesley’s note on verse 16 stresses the word’s lasting, active place in believers. The USCCB note on verses 5–17 includes the congregation’s hymns and prayers within its response to God through Christ. The verse joins wisdom, mutual instruction and gratitude to God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 5:18–21 closely parallels the shared singing and thanksgiving here, placing them within a community filled by the Spirit. The Spirit’s work shapes how believers speak to one another and give thanks to the Father through Christ.

Ephesians 4:11–16 supplies the wider purpose: the whole body grows toward maturity in Christ as its members contribute in love. Songs that carry His message belong within that work of forming and strengthening His people.

What is our position?

In Worship as the Early Church Knew It, we emphasize the mutual purpose named here: songs should teach, admonish and build up the people beside us. Clear, meaningful content matters to the gathered church. The verse also names gratitude to God; thanksgiving and care for one another belong together in this life shaped by Christ’s word.
