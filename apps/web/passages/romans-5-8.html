Passage

Romans 5:8

When did God show His love for sinners?

Berean Standard Bible

8 But God proves His love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

What is the context?

Paul has described Christ dying for the ungodly while they were still powerless. This verse identifies that death as the demonstration of God’s love. People do not first make themselves worthy and then become the objects of His concern.

The following verses connect Christ’s death with reconciliation and His life with the hope of salvation. Read Romans 5:6–11 to follow the whole movement from enemies to reconciliation and rejoicing in God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this verse as a direct statement of God’s initiating love, displayed in Christ’s self-giving for people who are still sinners. It grounds hope in what He has done, rather than a claim that the recipients had already become worthy of His gift.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The verse shows the character of the God who provides the rescue. Romans 5:9–11 carries that love into reconciliation and confidence through the living Christ. The gift addresses hostility by bringing people into a restored relationship, rather than waiting for them to repair it unaided.

What is our position?

The Gospel According to Jesus uses Romans 5:8 to explain that God’s love toward sinners precedes their worthiness. Our position is that Christ died for sinners and that salvation is a gift of mercy, rather than a reward for making oneself acceptable first. His self-giving also reveals the love behind His command to love enemies.
