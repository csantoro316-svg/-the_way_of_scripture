Passage

Luke 8:26-39

What changes when Jesus frees the man among the tombs?

Berean Standard Bible

26 Then they sailed to the region of the Gerasenes, across the lake from Galilee. 27 When Jesus stepped ashore, He was met by a demon-possessed man from the town. For a long time this man had not worn clothing or lived in a house, but he stayed in the tombs. 28 When the man saw Jesus, he cried out and fell down before Him, shouting in a loud voice, “What do You want with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I beg You not to torture me!” 29 For Jesus had commanded the unclean spirit to come out of the man. Many times it had seized him, and though he was bound with chains and shackles, he had broken the chains and been driven by the demon into solitary places. 30 “What is your name?” Jesus asked. “Legion,” he replied, because many demons had gone into him. 31 And the demons kept begging Jesus not to order them to go into the Abyss.

32 There on the hillside a large herd of pigs was feeding. So the demons begged Jesus to let them enter the pigs, and He gave them permission. 33 Then the demons came out of the man and went into the pigs, and the herd rushed down the steep bank into the lake and was drowned. 34 When those tending the pigs saw what had happened, they ran off and reported this in the town and countryside. 35 So the people went out to see what had happened. They came to Jesus and found the man whom the demons had left, sitting at Jesus’ feet, clothed and in his right mind; and they were afraid. 36 Meanwhile, those who had seen it reported how the demon-possessed man had been healed.

37 Then all the people of the region of the Gerasenes asked Jesus to depart from them, because great fear had taken hold of them. So He got into the boat and started back. 38 The man whom the demons had left begged to go with Jesus. But He sent him away, saying, 39 “Return home and describe how much God has done for you.” So the man went away and proclaimed all over the town how much Jesus had done for him.

What is the context?

Across the lake, Jesus meets a man whose life has been dominated by demons. He has lived among the tombs without clothing, broken restraints and been driven into solitary places. The encounter shows a man cut off from ordinary life with other people.

The spirits recognize Jesus as the Son of the Most High God. The answer Legion is explained in the text by the many demons in the man. They beg not to be sent into the Abyss and ask permission to enter a herd of pigs.

The word Abyss comes from ἄβυσσος, abussos (ah-BOOS-soss), a term for an immeasurable depth or pit. Thayer identifies its use here with the dwelling of demons. Their plea shows their fear of Jesus’ authority to send them there. (Thayer, G12)

After the spirits enter the pigs and the herd drowns, the people find the man clothed, in his right mind and sitting at Jesus’ feet. They ask Jesus to leave because they are afraid. The restored man wants to accompany Him, but Jesus sends him home to tell what God has done. He responds by telling the town what Jesus has done for him.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly understand the scene as Jesus exercising authority over evil spirits and restoring the man to life among others. His commission to speak of God’s help turns the deliverance into a witness within his own community. (Catechism 550; Calvin on Luke 8:38–39)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Paul describes Christ’s cross as a victory in which the powers and authorities are disarmed. The release of this man belongs within the larger saving work that overcomes evil’s hold, a work carried through the death and resurrection of Jesus. (Colossians 2:13–15)

Revelation also describes evil being confined and finally judged. It distinguishes the Abyss, where Satan is bound for a period, from the lake of fire that marks his final judgment. These passages place the spirits’ fear here within the larger biblical certainty that evil remains subject to God and will not rule forever. (Revelation 20:1–3, 10)
