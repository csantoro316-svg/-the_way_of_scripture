Passage

Matthew 15:29-31

The Crowd Praises the God of Israel

Berean Standard Bible

29 Moving on from there, Jesus went along the Sea of Galilee. Then He went up on a mountain and sat down. 30 Large crowds came to Him, bringing the lame, the blind, the crippled, the mute, and many others, and laid them at His feet, and He healed them. 31 The crowd was amazed when they saw the mute speaking, the crippled restored, the lame walking, and the blind seeing. And they glorified the God of Israel.

What is the context?

Jesus returns to the Sea of Galilee and sits on a mountain. People bring those who are lame, blind, crippled, mute, and suffering in other ways, laying them at His feet. Matthew describes healing that the crowd can see: people speak, walk, and see again.

The crowd's amazement leads to praise of the God of Israel. The account follows the help given to the Canaanite woman and comes before another large feeding. Need, mercy, and recognition of God's work connect the scenes.

Mark 7:31–37 places the related return journey in the region of the Decapolis and describes a deaf man whose speech is restored. That setting helps readers consider the reach of Jesus' work, while Matthew does not identify the background of every person in this crowd.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand these healings as signs of Jesus' mercy and God's saving work, drawing the people toward praise. The US Catholic bishops' reference note on Matthew 15:30 connects the healings with Isaiah 35:5–6. The emphasis is on restored people and the God they glorify, rather than on a separate hidden meaning assigned to each illness.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Isaiah 35:3–10 joins God's coming to save with blind eyes opened, the lame leaping, and mute tongues rejoicing. It places those restorations within a wider promise of a renewed creation and God's redeemed people returning with joy. The healings in Matthew form a strong parallel with that promised restoration.

Earlier, Jesus directs John's messengers to the blind seeing and the lame walking as evidence of His work (Matthew 11:2–6). These scenes therefore help answer who Jesus is and what God is bringing through Him. The signs give a glimpse of restoration greater than the needs of one gathering.
