Passage

Romans 15:30-32

What is Paul asking the church to pray for?

Berean Standard Bible

30 Now I urge you, brothers, by our Lord Jesus Christ and by the love of the Spirit, to join me in my struggle by praying to God for me. 31 Pray that I may be delivered from the unbelievers in Judea, and that my service in Jerusalem may be acceptable to the saints there, 32 so that by God’s will I may come to you with joy and together with you be refreshed.

What is the context?

Paul is preparing to take a collection to Jerusalem before his hoped-for visit to Rome. He asks the church to share his struggle in prayer for safety, the acceptance of his service and a joyful visit that brings mutual refreshment.

The words by God’s will belong to the request itself. Prayer is presented as partnership with Paul’s work, while the outcome remains in God’s hands.

Acts records a difficult journey that includes rescue from a violent crowd, Roman custody and encouragement from believers who meet him near Rome (Acts 21:31–36; 28:15–16). Those details help readers assess the requests without reducing the entire story to comfort or to failure.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as an appeal for earnest prayer and partnership in the gospel. Specific requests and submission to God’s will appear together. The request is not a guarantee that the journey will be comfortable or that events will follow Paul’s preferred route.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Acts 23:11, the Lord assures Paul that he will testify in Rome as he has in Jerusalem. Acts 28:15–16, 30–31 shows that witness continuing through a journey and circumstances that include Roman custody.

The connection places the prayer request within God’s purpose of making Christ known. Danger and confinement do not bring that purpose to an end. The narrative also keeps room for genuine encouragement and help along a difficult route.

What is our position?

Prayer and Faith emphasizes entrusting the outcome to God. It applies this passage as a warning against treating prayer as a way to secure personal comfort or control the route ahead. The call is to trust God’s will and seek His purposes even when the path includes hardship.
