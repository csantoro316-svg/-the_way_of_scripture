Passage

John 11:1-16

Why does Jesus wait before going to Lazarus?

Berean Standard Bible

1 At this time a man named Lazarus was sick. He lived in Bethany, the village of Mary and her sister Martha. 2 (Mary, whose brother Lazarus was sick, was to anoint the Lord with perfume and wipe His feet with her hair.) 3 So the sisters sent word to Jesus, “Lord, the one You love is sick.” 4 When Jesus heard this, He said, “This sickness will not end in death. No, it is for the glory of God, so that the Son of God may be glorified through it.” 5 Now Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus. 6 So on hearing that Lazarus was sick, He stayed where He was for two days,

7 and then He said to the disciples, “Let us go back to Judea.” 8 “Rabbi,” they replied, “the Jews just tried to stone You, and You are going back there?” 9 Jesus answered, “Are there not twelve hours of daylight? If anyone walks in the daytime, he will not stumble, because he sees by the light of this world. 10 But if anyone walks at night, he will stumble, because he has no light.”

11 After He had said this, He told them, “Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep, but I am going there to wake him up.” 12 His disciples replied, “Lord, if he is sleeping, he will get better.” 13 They thought that Jesus was talking about actual sleep, but He was speaking about the death of Lazarus. 14 So Jesus told them plainly, “Lazarus is dead, 15 and for your sake I am glad I was not there, so that you may believe. But let us go to him.” 16 Then Thomas called Didymus said to his fellow disciples, “Let us also go, so that we may die with Him.”

What is the context?

Lazarus is ill in Bethany, the village of Mary and Martha. The sisters send word to Jesus, appealing to His love for their brother. John identifies Mary by looking ahead to her anointing of Jesus, which he records in John 12:1–8. This account does not name her as the unnamed woman who anoints Jesus in Luke 7.

Jesus says the illness will not end in death but will show the glory of God and His Son. Lazarus does die, as verse 14 makes explicit. The promise concerns the outcome Jesus intends to bring, rather than a statement that His friend will never enter the grave.

John places Jesus’ love for the family directly alongside His decision to remain where He is for two more days. The delay is part of the story, but it is not explained as indifference. Nor does John give enough information here to reconstruct the hour of Lazarus’s death or the messenger’s whole journey.

Returning to Judea brings danger back into view. The disciples remember the attempted stoning. Jesus answers with the picture of walking in daylight, continuing the Gospel’s theme of light and the work given Him by the Father. He then speaks of Lazarus sleeping. When they take that as ordinary sleep and hope of recovery, He plainly tells them Lazarus is dead.

Jesus says that what follows will serve their faith and announces that He will go to Lazarus. Thomas urges the others to go and die with Him. His words acknowledge the danger and express willingness to accompany Jesus; the text does not give a complete account of his motives. The disciples are being led toward seeing more of Jesus’ power than they yet understand.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the delay as part of Jesus’ purpose to reveal God’s glory and strengthen faith, rather than a failure of love. Calvin on verses 4–5 and 15 and Wesley on verses 4 and 11 connect the promise with the life Jesus will bring from death.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus knowingly returns toward danger to bring life to His friend. As the chapter unfolds, the raising of Lazarus leads to a decision to kill Jesus (John 11:45–53). The sign of life therefore becomes part of the road to His own death.

This anticipates a larger saving purpose. Hebrews 2:14–18 describes the Son sharing our humanity, entering death and freeing people from its fear. Lazarus’s story gives a preview of His authority over death; the cross and resurrection will reveal the greater work through which He brings His people into life.
