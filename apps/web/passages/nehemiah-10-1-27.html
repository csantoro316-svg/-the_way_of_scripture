Nehemiah 10:1-27

Who puts their name to the community’s commitment?

What is the context?

The chapter supplies the names attached to the written agreement announced at the end of the prayer. Nehemiah the governor appears first, followed by priests, Levites and leaders of the people.

The list gives the community’s commitment identifiable witnesses. Its setting is the confession of chapter 9: God has been faithful, the people have been unfaithful, and they are now recording their intention to follow His Law.

The promises themselves follow the names. The next verses also make clear that the wider community joins the commitment, including wives and children who are able to understand.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Gill understands the names as the leaders who seal the agreement on behalf of the community. Henry reads the chapter as a renewed commitment to the whole of God’s instruction. The record joins identifiable leadership with the people’s own participation, rather than leaving the response as an unnamed promise.
