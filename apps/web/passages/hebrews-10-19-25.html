Passage

Hebrews 10:19-25

How should we respond to the access Christ has opened?

Berean Standard Bible

19 Therefore, brothers, since we have confidence to enter the Most Holy Place by the blood of Jesus, 20 by the new and living way opened for us through the curtain of His body, 21 and since we have a great priest over the house of God, 22 let us draw near with a sincere heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled to cleanse us from a guilty conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.

23 Let us hold resolutely to the hope we profess, for He who promised is faithful. 24 And let us consider how to spur one another on to love and good deeds. 25 Let us not neglect meeting together, as some have made a habit, but let us encourage one another, and all the more as you see the Day approaching.

What is the context?

The writer now calls for a response to Christ’s priesthood and sacrifice. Because Jesus has opened the way into God’s presence, believers are invited to draw near sincerely and with assurance. The confidence rests on Him.

Three calls shape the paragraph: draw near, hold firmly to hope, and consider how to encourage one another toward love and good deeds. The second call explicitly rests on God’s faithfulness to His promise.

The third call gives meeting together a purpose. Believers need one another’s encouragement as the Day approaches. The passage addresses a habit of abandoning the gathering; it does not reduce Christian care to having one’s attendance recorded.

The sprinkling and washing language recalls cleansing for access to the sanctuary. Christ opens that access for people whose hearts and lives are being cleansed, rather than inviting a performance that hides an unchanged heart.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin, Wesley and the USCCB notes understand the paragraph as a call to sincere approach, enduring hope and mutual care. Confidence in Christ strengthens life together.

They differ over the bodies washed with pure water in verse 22. The USCCB notes understand a reference to baptism. Calvin acknowledges that common reading but favors cleansing by God’s Spirit, drawing on Ezekiel 36:25. Wesley emphasizes a holy way of life in place of merely outward washings. These readings share an emphasis on cleansing while differing over the water image.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 2:18–22 joins access to the Father through Christ with believers becoming one household and a dwelling for God. Access is personal, and it also brings people together.

Ephesians 4:11–16 describes the whole body growing as its members serve one another. Hebrews’ call to encourage love and good deeds fits that purpose: a people growing together toward Christ while awaiting Him.

What is our position?

Our study Who Shepherds the Church? understands these verses as direct access to God through Christ. Believers do not need a human priestly class standing between them and God. The study also preserves the work of elders who teach, correct and care for people; that service does not replace Christ’s unique role.
