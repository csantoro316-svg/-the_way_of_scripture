Passage

Mark 10:51-52

A Plain Request and Faith in Jesus

Berean Standard Bible

51 “What do you want Me to do for you?” Jesus asked. “Rabboni,” said the blind man, “let me see again.” 52 “Go,” said Jesus, “your faith has healed you.” And immediately he received his sight and followed Jesus along the road.

What is the context?

These verses close the encounter with Bartimaeus beside the road at Jericho. He has called on Jesus for mercy despite people trying to silence him. Jesus stops, calls him forward, and asks what he wants Him to do.

Bartimaeus answers with a specific request to see again. Jesus credits his faith, and he immediately receives sight and follows Him. Mark records neither a required prayer formula nor an elaborate test of how he phrases the request.

The question recalls Jesus' earlier question to James and John in Mark 10:35–40. The brothers ask for places of glory; Bartimaeus asks for sight. His response at the end is to follow Jesus along the road.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly connect the healing with trust in Jesus and the man's grateful following. Calvin on Mark 10:52 understands his faith as recognition of the promised Messiah, and reads the rescue as including more than physical recovery. The narrated result remains clear: his sight is restored, and he follows Jesus.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The restoration of sight belongs with Jesus' announced mission in Luke 4:16–21, where He includes recovery of sight in proclaiming God's favor. This makes the exchange part of the Savior's work, rather than an isolated lesson in how to obtain a desired result.

The healed man's following also has a wider parallel in Philippians 3:7–11. Paul describes knowing Christ, receiving righteousness through faith, and sharing in His sufferings and resurrection hope as greater than what he once counted gain. Bartimaeus' story does not supply Paul's whole teaching, but it gives a concrete picture of turning toward Jesus and going with Him.

What is our position?

In Prayer and Faith, section 5, we draw on this exchange because it contains a specific request and Jesus' explicit recognition of faith. Bartimaeus speaks his need when Jesus asks, and Jesus credits his trust. The study's point here is that faith rests in Christ rather than in a formula of words. We retain the spoken request as part of what the passage actually says.
