Passage

Genesis 28:6-9

Esau Marries Mahalath

Berean Standard Bible

6 Now Esau learned that Isaac had blessed Jacob and sent him to Paddan-aram to take a wife there, commanding him, “Do not marry a Canaanite woman,” 7 and that Jacob had obeyed his father and mother and gone to Paddan-aram.

8 And seeing that his father Isaac disapproved of the Canaanite women, 9 Esau went to Ishmael and married Mahalath, the sister of Nebaioth and daughter of Abraham’s son Ishmael, in addition to the wives he already had.

What is the context?

Esau watches Isaac bless Jacob and direct him to seek a wife away from the Canaanites. He also sees Jacob obey his parents and leave for Paddan-aram. The instructions make Isaac's disapproval of Esau's own marriages unmistakable to him.

Esau responds by marrying Mahalath, Ishmael's daughter and Nebaioth's sister. She belongs to Abraham's wider family, but he takes her in addition to the wives he already has. Genesis records the decision without telling us how his parents respond or whether this change resolves any of the existing grief.

The brief account keeps Esau's response in view before following Jacob on his journey. It shows an effort to respond to a recognized family concern, but does not describe a change in the LORD's earlier announcement about the brothers.

How is this passage commonly understood?

A common Christian reading sees Esau responding to his father's displeasure by seeking a wife within Abraham's wider family. Wesley's note on Genesis 28:6 treats Jacob's example as an influence on that decision, without making the new marriage a transfer of Jacob's blessing.
