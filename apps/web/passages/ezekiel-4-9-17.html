Passage

Ezekiel 4:9-17

Why are Ezekiel’s food and water measured out?

Berean Standard Bible

9 But take wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet, and spelt; put them in a single container and make them into bread for yourself. This is what you are to eat during the 390 days you lie on your side. 10 You are to weigh out twenty shekels of food to eat each day, and you are to eat it at set times. 11 You are also to measure out a sixth of a hin of water to drink, and you are to drink it at set times. 12 And you shall eat the food as you would a barley cake, after you bake it over dried human excrement in the sight of the people.” 13 Then the LORD said, “This is how the Israelites will eat their defiled bread among the nations to which I will banish them.”

14 “Ah, Lord GOD,” I said, “I have never defiled myself. From my youth until now I have not eaten anything found dead or mauled by wild beasts. No unclean meat has ever entered my mouth.” 15 “Look,” He replied, “I will let you use cow dung instead of human excrement, and you may bake your bread over that.” 16 Then He told me, “Son of man, I am going to cut off the supply of food in Jerusalem. They will anxiously eat bread rationed by weight, and in despair they will drink water by measure. 17 So they will lack food and water; they will be appalled at the sight of one another wasting away in their iniquity.

What is the context?

The sign now reaches into Ezekiel’s daily food. He is to make bread from wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet and spelt, with a fixed weight of food and a fixed measure of water at set times.

The rationing depicts scarcity. The closing explanation is that Jerusalem’s food supply will be cut off. People will eat anxiously and drink in despair as they see one another wasting away.

The required baking fuel adds a picture of defilement among the nations where the people will be scattered. Ezekiel objects that he has guarded himself from unclean food throughout his life. The Lord allows cow dung in place of human excrement.

The concession changes that part of the sign while leaving its warning intact. The combination of restricted food, measured water and defilement describes the humiliation and deprivation of siege and exile.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley read the mixed ingredients and small rations as signs of a severe shortage. The concern is what people will be reduced to eating under judgment.

Henry emphasizes that Ezekiel’s objection concerns faithfulness to the requirements of clean food. The Lord hears the objection and allows the substitution. Wesley also understands the scene as a visionary representation of the conditions to come.

The chapter’s own explanation keeps the food within that warning. It presents the effects of scarcity and exile, rather than prescribing an ordinary pattern of eating for God’s people.
