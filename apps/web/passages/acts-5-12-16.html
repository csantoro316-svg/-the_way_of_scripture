Passage

Acts 5:12-16

How do the signs affect the growing community?

Berean Standard Bible

12 The apostles performed many signs and wonders among the people, and with one accord the believers gathered together in Solomon’s Colonnade. 13 Although the people regarded them highly, no one else dared to join them. 14 Yet more and more believers were brought to the Lord—large numbers of both men and women.

15 As a result, people brought the sick into the streets and laid them on cots and mats, so that at least Peter’s shadow might fall on some of them as he passed by. 16 Crowds also gathered from the towns around Jerusalem, bringing the sick and those tormented by unclean spirits, and all of them were healed.

What is the context?

Luke returns to the apostles’ signs and the believers’ life together in Solomon’s Colonnade. The community is held in high regard, yet people also hesitate to associate with it. The recent judgment helps explain why joining is not presented as a casual matter.

At the same time, more men and women come to believe in the Lord. Luke holds the hesitation and the growth together. Respect, fear and genuine faith are not all the same response.

People bring the sick into the streets, hoping even Peter’s shadow will fall on them. Crowds also come from surrounding towns with the sick and people tormented by unclean spirits. Luke reports that all those brought in this scene are healed.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this summary as a display of God’s power through the apostles, accompanying the spread of the message. Calvin on verse 15 sees God’s power at work even through Peter’s shadow in support of the gospel, rather than treating Peter as a source of power independent of God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The gathering of sick and troubled people recalls Luke 6:17–19, where crowds come to Jesus to hear Him and receive healing, including those troubled by unclean spirits. This is a parallel between the scenes: the work now associated with His witnesses points back to the Lord they proclaim.

The movement also reaches beyond Jerusalem as people come from nearby towns. The gift promised to the witnesses is making its effects known more widely, while the message remains centered on the Lord rather than the reputation of an individual apostle.
