Passage

2 Timothy 2:1-7

How can Timothy keep serving and prepare others to teach?

Berean Standard Bible

1 You therefore, my child, be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus. 2 And the things that you have heard me say among many witnesses, entrust these to faithful men who will be qualified to teach others as well.

3 Join me in suffering, like a good soldier of Christ Jesus. 4 A soldier refrains from entangling himself in civilian affairs, in order to please the one who enlisted him. 5 Likewise, a competitor does not receive the crown unless he competes according to the rules. 6 The hardworking farmer should be the first to partake of the crops. 7 Consider what I am saying, for the Lord will give you insight into all things.

What is the context?

Paul calls Timothy to draw strength from the grace found in Christ Jesus. What Timothy heard from Paul is to be entrusted to reliable people who can teach others. The work is meant to continue beyond either of them.

Three everyday pictures show the kind of commitment involved. A soldier stays focused on pleasing the one who enlisted him. An athlete competes according to the rules. A hardworking farmer is to receive a share of the crop.

The pictures join focus, faithful effort and the prospect of fruit from the work. Paul asks Timothy to consider them, while also trusting the Lord to give understanding. Reflection and dependence on God belong together.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley read these pictures as encouragement to devoted, patient service. The soldier is not to be pulled away by competing concerns; the athlete must compete faithfully; the farmer’s work comes before the harvest. The comparisons call for perseverance, rather than promising easy results.

Calvin makes a particular point that the Christian soldier’s hardship is endured patiently, rather than inflicted on others. The imagery serves Timothy’s teaching and witness; it does not turn the church’s mission into permission to use force.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Passing the message from Paul to Timothy, then to reliable teachers and those they teach, gives a concrete picture of the gospel continuing through generations. Matthew 28:18–20 likewise connects making disciples with teaching them to keep Jesus’ commands.

1 Corinthians 3:5–9 places the work of planting and watering under God, who gives the growth. Timothy’s effort and the service of later teachers have that same larger purpose: God’s people growing through the message entrusted to them.
