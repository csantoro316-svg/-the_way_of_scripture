Passage

Ezekiel 46:9-15

How do the people and prince take part in worship?

Berean Standard Bible

9 When the people of the land come before the LORD at the appointed feasts, whoever enters by the north gate to worship must go out by the south gate, and whoever enters by the south gate must go out by the north gate. No one is to return through the gate by which he entered, but each must go out by the opposite gate. 10 When the people enter, the prince shall go in with them, and when they leave, he shall leave. 11 At the festivals and appointed feasts, the grain offering shall be an ephah with a bull, an ephah with a ram, and as much as one is able to give with the lambs, along with a hin of oil per ephah. 12 When the prince makes a freewill offering to the LORD, whether a burnt offering or a peace offering, the gate facing east must be opened for him. He is to offer his burnt offering or peace offering just as he does on the Sabbath day. Then he shall go out, and the gate must be closed after he goes out.

13 And you shall provide an unblemished year-old lamb as a daily burnt offering to the LORD; you are to offer it every morning. 14 You are also to provide with it every morning a grain offering of a sixth of an ephah with a third of a hin of oil to moisten the fine flour—a grain offering to the LORD. This is a permanent statute. 15 Thus they shall provide the lamb, the grain offering, and the oil every morning as a regular burnt offering.’

What is the context?

At the appointed feasts, worshippers pass through the court rather than returning by the gate they entered. Those coming from the north leave by the south, and those coming from the south leave by the north. The prince goes in and out among the people.

The passage also provides for his voluntary offerings. For these, the east-facing gate opens and is closed after he leaves.

A year-old lamb, grain and oil are to be supplied every morning. Shared festival worship and regular daily offerings both have their place in the life described by the vision.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley suggests that passing through rather than turning back may prevent crowding and confusion. Henry emphasizes daily worship alongside the room left for willing expressions of devotion.

The practical explanation of the route is a suggestion rather than a reason stated by the passage. What is stated is that people and prince come before the Lord within an ordered pattern.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

First Corinthians 14:26–40 gives instructions for a different gathering, the church coming together. Paul’s concern is that its contributions build people up and that everything be done properly and in order.

This does not transfer Ezekiel’s gateways or sacrificial schedule into a Christian meeting. It carries forward a wider purpose of shared worship: people should be helped to hear, learn and be encouraged, rather than having the gathering overwhelmed by confusion.
