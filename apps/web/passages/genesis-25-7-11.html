Passage

Genesis 25:7-11

The Death of Abraham

Berean Standard Bible

7 Abraham lived a total of 175 years. 8 And at a ripe old age he breathed his last and died, old and contented, and was gathered to his people.

9 His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah near Mamre, in the field of Ephron son of Zohar the Hittite. 10 This was the field that Abraham had bought from the Hittites. Abraham was buried there with his wife Sarah.

11 After Abraham’s death, God blessed his son Isaac, who lived near Beer-lahai-roi.

What is the context?

Abraham dies at 175, described as old and contented. His life has included long waiting, serious failures, costly tests, and fulfilled promises. The closing description does not claim that everything he did was right; it brings the long account of his life to rest.

Isaac and Ishmael bury him together in the cave of Machpelah, the property he bought when Sarah died. The sons' shared presence at the burial is stated plainly, though the passage does not tell us whether they had fully resolved every earlier family tension. Abraham is buried beside Sarah in Canaan.

The final verse looks forward: God blesses Isaac, who lives near Beer-lahai-roi. Abraham's death therefore closes one life without ending God's work through the family. The promises are not dependent on Abraham remaining alive to carry them forward.

How is this passage commonly understood?

A common Christian reading sees an honorable close to Abraham's life and the continuation of God's blessing through Isaac. Wesley's notes on Genesis 25:8–11 also connect the burial beside Sarah with hope beyond death.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 11:13–16 describes the faithful dying while still looking toward the fulfillment of God's promises and a better country prepared by Him. Abraham's burial belongs within that larger hope. He has seen Isaac born and established, but his lifetime does not contain everything God has promised.

Jesus later names Abraham when explaining the resurrection in Matthew 22:31–32: God identifies Himself as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and is the God of the living. Jesus is explaining God's words to Moses rather than quoting this burial scene, but His teaching shows why Abraham's death is not the end of his relationship with God.
