Passage

Galatians 6:1-5

How do we help someone who has fallen into sin?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Brothers, if someone is caught in a trespass, you who are spiritual should restore him with a spirit of gentleness. But watch yourself, or you also may be tempted. 2 Carry one another’s burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ.

3 If anyone thinks he is something when he is nothing, he deceives himself. 4 Each one should test his own work. Then he will have reason to boast in himself alone, and not in someone else. 5 For each one should carry his own load.

What is the context?

Paul carries the discussion of life by the Spirit into a situation where someone has been caught in a trespass. Those who are spiritual are to restore that person with gentleness. The quality just named as fruit of the Spirit in 5:23 is needed in the work of helping someone return.

The person offering help must also watch himself. Temptation is a danger for him too. Paul’s instruction calls for humility alongside the work of restoration.

Carrying one another’s burdens fulfills the law of Christ. The Greek word behind burdens is βάρος, baros (BAH-ross), meaning weight or burden. Thayer’s lexicon connects its use here with bearing another person’s troublesome faults. The surrounding passage brings that shared help into the setting of sin, temptation and restoration.

Paul then warns against self-deception and tells each person to test his own work. Comparing ourselves with someone else can give a false sense of importance. Each person has responsibility for his own conduct.

The call to carry one another’s burdens and the statement that each carries his own load belong together. Helping someone does not remove that person’s responsibility, and personal responsibility does not cancel the call to help.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as a call to restore others gently, remain humble about one’s own vulnerability and give practical help. Wesley’s notes on verses 1–5 and the USCCB notes on verses 1–5 both connect burden-sharing with Christ’s command of love and self-examination with the correction of pride.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John 13:34–35 connects love among Jesus’ disciples with His own love for them. 1 John 3:16–18 makes the connection concrete: Jesus laid down His life for us, and love for others is to take shape in action, including help for a brother in need.

These passages show how mutual burden-sharing belongs to God’s larger work through His Son. The love shown in Christ’s self-giving becomes visible in a family that cares for its members. Helping one another is part of making that love known.

What is our position?

Our study What Is the True Church?, in its section on bearing one another’s burdens, understands this as costly, practical care within God’s family. Sharing a burden means walking with someone through the struggle, offering sustained presence and help. The study treats that shared life as love in action, made possible by grace.
