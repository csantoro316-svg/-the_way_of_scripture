Passage

1 Corinthians 14:19

Why would Paul prefer five clear words?

Berean Standard Bible

19 But in the church, I would rather speak five coherent words to instruct others than ten thousand words in a tongue.

What is the context?

This sentence summarizes Paul’s discussion of prayer, interpretation and the listener’s need to understand. It belongs within 1 Corinthians 14:13–19, where he wants prayer and singing to involve both spirit and mind.

Paul is not rejecting tongues out of personal dislike: the previous verse thanks God that he speaks in tongues more than the Corinthians. His comparison is about what serves a church gathering. Five words that instruct others accomplish something that ten thousand words they cannot understand do not.

The numbers make a strong contrast, rather than setting a five-word limit on teaching. The test is whether people can receive the meaning and be helped.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as giving understandable instruction priority over uninterpreted speech in church. Wesley’s note on verse 19 stresses both understanding oneself and being understood by others. The USCCB note on verses 13–19 likewise emphasizes interpretation as the way a tongue can benefit the congregation.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Colossians 3:16–17 describes Christ’s word dwelling richly among His people through teaching, songs and thanksgiving to the Father through Jesus. Their shared life includes receiving truth from one another.

Paul’s preference serves that purpose. Clear words give another person something to understand, respond to and live by, helping a community grow together under Christ.

What is our position?

In Worship as the Early Church Knew It, we use this verse as a standard for understandable speech in the gathered church. A contribution should help other people grow in knowledge, faith or love. Its value cannot be measured mainly by the speaker’s performance or the intensity of the feelings it produces.
