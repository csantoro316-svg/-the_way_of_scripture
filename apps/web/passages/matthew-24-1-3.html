Passage

Matthew 24:1-3

The Temple's Future and the Disciples' Questions

Berean Standard Bible

1 As Jesus left the temple and was walking away, His disciples came up to Him to point out its buildings. 2 “Do you see all these things?” He replied. “Truly I tell you, not one stone here will be left on another; every one will be thrown down.”

3 While Jesus was sitting on the Mount of Olives, the disciples came to Him privately. “Tell us,” they said, “when will these things happen, and what will be the sign of Your coming and of the end of the age?”

What is the context?

Jesus has ended His lament over Jerusalem by announcing that its house is left desolate. As He leaves the temple, the disciples draw attention to the buildings. His answer is startling: the stones they admire will be thrown down. The warning about the house now becomes a direct prediction about the temple before them.

Later, on the Mount of Olives, the disciples ask Him privately when these things will happen and what will signal His coming and the end of the age. Their questions set up the teaching that continues through chapters 24 and 25. Keeping the questions in view helps us follow the warnings, promises, and calls to faithfulness that follow.

Coming is παρουσία, parousia (pah-roo-SEE-ah), which can mean presence or arrival. Thayer's entry includes this verse among references to Jesus' return. The word names what the disciples ask about; it does not by itself provide a date or settle how every later part of His answer fits together.

Mark 13:1–4 records the admiration of the temple's stones and names Peter, James, John, and Andrew as the private questioners. Luke 21:5–7 also records the temple prediction and their question about its signs. Each account helps us see the setting without replacing Matthew's own wording.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand Jesus to predict the temple's destruction and to answer questions that also concern His return and the end of the age. Wesley on 24:3 distinguishes those subjects, and the US Catholic bishops' notes on 24:2–3 likewise connect the temple warning with the preceding lament while distinguishing it from the final coming. Readers differ over how the later details relate to those questions.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The temple warning does not leave God's people without hope of His presence. In another temple scene, John 2:19–22 explains that Jesus' words about raising the temple concerned His body and resurrection. Ephesians 2:13–22 then describes people brought near through Him and built together as God's dwelling through the Spirit. Those passages develop the wider temple theme around the risen Christ.

After His resurrection, Jesus again hears a question about the timing of the kingdom in Acts 1:6–11. He places the times under the Father's authority and directs the disciples to their Spirit-empowered witness; His return is also promised. Learning about God's future work belongs together with faithful participation in the work He gives His people now.
