Passage

Revelation 10:1-7

What does the mighty angel announce about God’s purpose?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then I saw another mighty angel coming down from heaven, wrapped in a cloud, with a rainbow above his head. His face was like the sun, and his legs were like pillars of fire. 2 He held in his hand a small scroll, which lay open. He placed his right foot on the sea and his left foot on the land. 3 Then he cried out in a loud voice like the roar of a lion. And when he cried out, the seven thunders sounded their voices. 4 When the seven thunders had spoken, I was about to put it in writing. But I heard a voice from heaven saying, “Seal up what the seven thunders have said, and do not write it down.”

5 Then the angel I had seen standing on the sea and on the land lifted up his right hand to heaven. 6 And he swore by Him who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and everything in it, the earth and everything in it, and the sea and everything in it: “There will be no more delay! 7 But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he begins to sound his trumpet, the mystery of God will be fulfilled, just as He proclaimed to His servants the prophets.”

What is the context?

Before the seventh trumpet sounds, the vision pauses for another scene. A mighty angel descends from heaven with a cloud, a rainbow, a sunlike face and legs like pillars of fire. He holds an open small scroll and stands with one foot on the sea and the other on the land. The scene presents a messenger of extraordinary authority.

The Greek word biblaridion means a little book or scroll; see Thayer’s entry. The description tells us that it is small and open. It does not give its full contents or explicitly say that it is the same scroll seen in chapter 5.

When the angel cries out, seven thunders speak. John is ready to write their words, but a voice from heaven tells him to seal them up and leave them unwritten. Revelation therefore includes an express limit on what is disclosed. Readers are told that the thunders spoke, but are not given their message.

The angel raises his right hand and swears by the eternal Creator of heaven, earth and sea. There will be no more delay: in the days of the seventh angel’s trumpet, God’s mystery will be fulfilled as He announced to His prophets. The focus turns from the withheld words to the promise that God will complete His purpose.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The angel’s identity is disputed. Gill understands the figure as Christ, pointing to His majesty and authority. Wesley and Walvoord favor a created angel serving God, noting especially his oath by the Creator. The description has features associated with divine glory, but the passage itself calls the figure an angel and does not name him.

Readers also differ over the scroll. Gill and Wesley connect it with the earlier sealed scroll, now open and with much of its message already unfolded. Walvoord sees no certain identification with that scroll. The difference should be kept open rather than settled simply by the use of the word small.

The older wording about time being no more has sometimes been read as the end of time itself. Gill and Walvoord instead explain an end to delay before God’s purpose is fulfilled, although they place that fulfillment within different accounts of the coming kingdom. Wesley offers a historical calculation involving a long prophetic period. That calculation belongs to his interpretation, rather than to the BSB wording quoted above.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The raised hand and oath closely parallel Daniel 12:4–10. There, a figure above the river raises his hands and swears by the One who lives forever, while Daniel is told that words are sealed until the time of the end. Revelation draws on that earlier picture of God’s authority over the completion of events. The resemblance does not require us to identify the angel by name or imagine a physical scroll passed from Daniel to John.

The promise that God’s mystery will be fulfilled also belongs within the larger purpose stated in Ephesians 1:9–10: God intends to bring all things in heaven and on earth together in Christ. Revelation’s announcement gives confidence that His purposes will reach their goal. It does not disclose what John was forbidden to write or supply a date for readers to calculate.
