Passage

1 Peter 4:17-19

Why entrust ourselves to God while we keep doing good?

Berean Standard Bible

17 For it is time for judgment to begin with the family of God; and if it begins with us, what will the outcome be for those who disobey the gospel of God? 18 And, “If it is hard for the righteous to be saved, what will become of the ungodly and the sinner?” 19 So then, those who suffer according to God’s will should entrust their souls to their faithful Creator and continue to do good.

What is the context?

Peter sets the readers’ trials within God’s judgment, beginning with His own household. He asks what the outcome will be for those who disobey the Gospel if God’s people themselves pass through such difficulty.

The saying about the righteous being saved with difficulty echoes Proverbs 11:31, although its wording differs from the BSB translation of Proverbs. Peter uses it to make the contrast serious, rather than to describe salvation as casual or opposition as harmless.

His conclusion is active trust: entrust one’s soul to the faithful Creator and continue doing good. God’s faithfulness is the reason to persevere; suffering is not an invitation to answer evil with evil.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin understands judgment beginning with God’s people as His fatherly correction and care, distinguished from the final outcome for those who reject Him. He relates the difficulty of salvation to the hazards of the believer’s present course.

Wesley also describes God visiting His church in justice and mercy. He reads the difficulty primarily in terms of present deliverance, while applying the warning to eternal matters as well. Both conclude with trusting God’s care and continuing to do good.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

2 Thessalonians 1:4–10 likewise puts believers’ present persecution beside God’s righteous judgment. It looks toward relief for the afflicted and accountability for those who afflict them when Christ is revealed.

The connection places present suffering within a story whose outcome belongs to God. His people can leave judgment with Him while remaining faithful to the good He calls them to do.
