Passage

Acts 15:23

Who sends the letter, and whom does it address?

Berean Standard Bible

23 and sent them with this letter: The apostles and the elders, your brothers, To the brothers among the Gentiles in Antioch, Syria, and Cilicia: Greetings.

What is the context?

The apostles and elders, with the whole church, choose Judas Barsabbas and Silas to accompany Barnabas and Paul. Their letter addresses Gentile believers in Antioch, Syria and Cilicia. It explains that the unsettling teachers had gone out without the senders’ authorization.

The letter honors Barnabas and Paul as people who have risked their lives for Jesus. Judas and Silas will confirm its message in person. The decision is attributed to the Holy Spirit and the gathered leaders, and the four requirements are stated without imposing circumcision.

When the letter is read in Antioch, the believers rejoice at its encouragement. Judas and Silas also speak at length to strengthen them. The visitors are sent back in peace, while Paul and Barnabas remain with others to teach and preach.

The BSB’s main text moves from verse 33 to verse 35. Its footnote records a reading about Silas remaining that some manuscripts include as verse 34. That wording is not part of the main quotation on this page.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the greeting as identifying both the responsible senders and the Gentile believers to whom the decision is addressed. The language of brothers places them within the same fellowship rather than treating them as outsiders awaiting circumcision.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 4:1–6 calls believers to preserve the Spirit’s unity in peace, grounded in one body and one Lord. The council’s decision takes practical form through a letter, trusted messengers, teaching and the removal of an unauthorized burden.

That unity concerns people who are now addressed as brothers across the Jewish and Gentile boundary. The response in Antioch is joy and encouragement: clarity about the gospel helps the community continue its life and witness together.

What is our position?

Who Shepherds the Church? points to the whole church sharing in the decision in verse 22 and to the apostles and elders sending the letter in verse 23. It reads these as shared responsibility among recognized leaders and the community. The study therefore describes James’s contribution as part of that common work, rather than the whole decision being his solitary act.
