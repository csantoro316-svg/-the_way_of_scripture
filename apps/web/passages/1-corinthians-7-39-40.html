Passage

1 Corinthians 7:39-40

What freedom does Paul give to a widow?

Berean Standard Bible

39 A wife is bound to her husband as long as he lives. But if her husband dies, she is free to marry anyone she wishes, as long as he belongs to the Lord. 40 In my judgment, however, she is happier if she remains as she is. And I think that I too have the Spirit of God.

What is the context?

Paul ends with a wife whose husband has died. While he lives she is bound to him; after his death she is free to marry someone she chooses, provided he belongs to the Lord. He allows a widow to marry again.

Paul again recommends remaining unmarried, but gives that as his judgment rather than withdrawing the freedom he has just stated. He also affirms the Spirit’s part in his counsel. The chapter closes with both a serious recommendation and a permitted choice, rather than requiring every widow to make the same decision.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly recognize a widow’s freedom to remarry and Paul’s recommendation of remaining unmarried. Wesley’s notes on verses 39–40 read only in the Lord as marriage within the Christian faith and regard Paul’s reference to the Spirit as confidence in his counsel.

Traditions differ in their rules for marriage across differences of faith. The Reformed Westminster Confession 24.3 requires Christians to marry in the Lord. Catholic canons 1086 and 1124–1125 allow permission for marriage with another baptized Christian outside the Catholic Church, and a special exception can permit marriage with an unbaptized person. The stated conditions include safeguarding the Catholic spouse’s faith and commitments about raising children in that faith.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 7:1–6 uses the ending of a marriage bond at death as an illustration within a larger explanation of life in Christ. Paul says believers have died to the law through Christ’s body so that they may belong to the risen Christ and bear fruit for God.

The two passages address different questions, but the explicit marriage illustration connects the household rule with God’s purpose of bringing people into fruitful life with His Son. That larger purpose helps keep the choices discussed here directed toward belonging to the Lord and serving Him.
