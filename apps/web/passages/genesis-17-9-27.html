Passage

Genesis 17:9-27

The Covenant of Circumcision

Berean Standard Bible

9 God also said to Abraham, “You must keep My covenant—you and your descendants in the generations after you. 10 This is My covenant with you and your descendants after you, which you are to keep: Every male among you must be circumcised. 11 You are to circumcise the flesh of your foreskin, and this will be a sign of the covenant between Me and you.

12 Generation after generation, every male must be circumcised when he is eight days old, including those born in your household and those purchased from a foreigner—even those who are not your offspring. 13 Whether they are born in your household or purchased, they must be circumcised. My covenant in your flesh will be an everlasting covenant.

14 But if any male is not circumcised, he will be cut off from his people; he has broken My covenant.”

15 Then God said to Abraham, “As for Sarai your wife, do not call her Sarai, for her name is to be Sarah. 16 And I will bless her and will surely give you a son by her. I will bless her, and she will be the mother of nations; kings of peoples will descend from her.”

17 Abraham fell facedown. Then he laughed and said to himself, “Can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old? Can Sarah give birth at the age of ninety?” 18 And Abraham said to God, “O that Ishmael might live under Your blessing!”

19 But God replied, “Your wife Sarah will indeed bear you a son, and you are to name him Isaac. I will establish My covenant with him as an everlasting covenant for his descendants after him. 20 As for Ishmael, I have heard you, and I will surely bless him; I will make him fruitful and multiply him greatly. He will become the father of twelve rulers, and I will make him into a great nation. 21 But I will establish My covenant with Isaac, whom Sarah will bear to you at this time next year.”

22 When He had finished speaking with Abraham, God went up from him.

23 On that very day Abraham took his son Ishmael and all those born in his household or purchased with his money—every male among the members of Abraham’s household—and he circumcised them, just as God had told him.

24 So Abraham was ninety-nine years old when he was circumcised, 25 and his son Ishmael was thirteen; 26 Abraham and his son Ishmael were circumcised on the same day. 27 And all the men of Abraham’s household—both servants born in his household and those purchased from foreigners—were circumcised with him.

What is the context?

God now gives circumcision as the sign of the covenant. The command includes Abraham, his male descendants, and the males of his household, including those born there and those purchased from foreigners. Future sons are to receive the sign at eight days old. The warning about being cut off from the people shows that this sign is a serious covenant obligation.

God also changes Sarai's name to Sarah and identifies her as the mother of the promised son. Abraham laughs at the prospect of a child at their ages and asks for Ishmael to live under God's blessing. God promises blessing for Ishmael, but says the covenant will be established with Isaac, whom Sarah will bear the following year. God's care for Ishmael and His particular promise through Isaac are both stated.

Abraham responds on that very day, circumcising himself, Ishmael, and the men of his household. Ishmael receives the sign even though the promised covenant line is assigned to Isaac. The chapter therefore distinguishes receiving the household sign from being the particular son through whom God says He will establish the covenant.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand circumcision here as the covenant's bodily sign, while Sarah's promised son is distinguished from Ishmael. Where do traditions differ? A major later question is how this household sign relates to baptism and the children of believers.

Reformed teaching includes believers' children within the covenant community. The Westminster Larger Catechism, question 166, says that infants with at least one parent professing faith in Christ are within the covenant and should be baptized. It reads the place of children within God's covenant as continuing into the church.

Reformed Baptist teaching requires a personal profession of repentance and faith for baptism. The 1689 Baptist Confession, 29.2, limits the proper recipients to those who actually profess those things. On this reading, the command to circumcise infants in Abraham's household does not itself authorize baptizing infants in the church.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 4:9–12 explicitly distinguishes Abraham's faith from the later sign. He was counted righteous before circumcision; the sign confirmed the righteousness he already had through faith. Romans 2:28–29 also speaks of a work in the heart by the Spirit. The physical mark has a place in the story, but it cannot stand in for a person's relationship with God.

The promise concerning Sarah and Isaac also matters beyond this household. Romans 9:6–9 uses the distinction between physical descent and the children of promise when explaining why God's word has not failed. God's purposes are not secured simply by human ancestry or by Abraham's understandable desire for Ishmael. They unfold through the promise God Himself gives.
