Passage

Exodus 6:14-30

Why pause the account to name Moses and Aaron’s family?

Berean Standard Bible

14 These were the heads of their fathers’ houses: The sons of Reuben, the firstborn of Israel, were Hanoch and Pallu, Hezron and Carmi. These were the clans of Reuben. 15 The sons of Simeon were Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar, and Shaul, the son of a Canaanite woman. These were the clans of Simeon. 16 These were the names of the sons of Levi according to their records: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. Levi lived 137 years. 17 The sons of Gershon were Libni and Shimei, by their clans. 18 The sons of Kohath were Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel. Kohath lived 133 years. 19 The sons of Merari were Mahli and Mushi. These were the clans of the Levites according to their records.

20 And Amram married his father’s sister Jochebed, and she bore him Aaron and Moses. Amram lived 137 years. 21 The sons of Izhar were Korah, Nepheg, and Zichri. 22 The sons of Uzziel were Mishael, Elzaphan, and Sithri. 23 And Aaron married Elisheba, the daughter of Amminadab and sister of Nahshon, and she bore him Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar. 24 The sons of Korah were Assir, Elkanah, and Abiasaph. These were the clans of the Korahites. 25 Aaron’s son Eleazar married one of the daughters of Putiel, and she bore him Phinehas. These were the heads of the Levite families by their clans.

26 It was this Aaron and Moses to whom the LORD said, “Bring the Israelites out of the land of Egypt by their divisions.” 27 Moses and Aaron were the ones who spoke to Pharaoh king of Egypt in order to bring the Israelites out of Egypt. 28 Now on the day that the LORD spoke to Moses in Egypt, 29 He said to him, “I am the LORD; tell Pharaoh king of Egypt everything I say to you.” 30 But in the LORD’s presence Moses replied, “Since I am unskilled in speech, why would Pharaoh listen to me?”

What is the context?

The account pauses between God’s renewed command and the next meeting with Pharaoh to identify the men He has sent. It begins with the families of Reuben and Simeon, then gives more detail about Levi’s family. Reuben, Simeon and Levi are the first three sons in the sequence.

Within Levi’s descendants, the record names Amram and Jochebed as the parents of Aaron and Moses. It also names Aaron’s wife Elisheba and their sons, then continues through Eleazar to Phinehas. The genealogy places the brothers within the family of Israel and gives particular attention to Aaron’s line.

Verses 26–27 make the purpose explicit: these are the Aaron and Moses commanded to bring Israel out and sent to speak to Pharaoh. The final verses return to the conversation about that commission. Moses again raises his difficulty speaking, carrying the account into God’s answer in chapter 7.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared reading sees the genealogy as establishing the family identity of God’s appointed servants. Wesley and the Catholic USCCB notes explain the opening names as leading to Levi and then to Moses and Aaron.

One family relationship is read differently. Wesley takes the description of Jochebed in verse 20 more broadly as a kinswoman. Calvin and the USCCB notes understand her as Amram’s paternal aunt. The BSB retains the wording his father’s sister. These are different readings of the relationship, rather than a difference about which men God commissioned.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The family record helps explain a distinction the New Testament later makes about priesthood. Aaron and his descendants belong to Levi. Hebrews 7:11–17 contrasts the priesthood associated with Aaron with that of Jesus, who comes from Judah and is priest in the order of Melchizedek.

Hebrews grounds Jesus’ priesthood in the power of His indestructible life. Knowing the family line named here helps readers understand that comparison: the later argument is about how Christ’s enduring priesthood differs from a priesthood passed through Aaron’s family.
