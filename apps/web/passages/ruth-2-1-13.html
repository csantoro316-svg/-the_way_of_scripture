Passage

Ruth 2:1-13

How does Ruth find protection in a stranger’s field?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Now Naomi had a relative on her husband’s side, a prominent man of noble character from the clan of Elimelech, whose name was Boaz. 2 And Ruth the Moabitess said to Naomi, “Please let me go into the fields and glean heads of grain after someone in whose sight I may find favor.” “Go ahead, my daughter,” Naomi replied. 3 So Ruth departed and went out into the field and gleaned after the harvesters. And she happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz, who was from the clan of Elimelech.

4 Just then Boaz arrived from Bethlehem and said to the harvesters, “The LORD be with you.” “The LORD bless you,” they replied. 5 And Boaz asked the foreman of his harvesters, “Whose young woman is this?” 6 The foreman answered, “She is the Moabitess who returned with Naomi from the land of Moab. 7 She has said, ‘Please let me glean and gather among the sheaves after the harvesters.’ So she came out and has continued from morning until now, except that she rested a short time in the shelter.”

8 Then Boaz said to Ruth, “Listen, my daughter. Do not go and glean in another field, and do not go away from this place, but stay here close to my servant girls. 9 Let your eyes be on the field they are harvesting, and follow along after these girls. Indeed, I have ordered the young men not to touch you. And when you are thirsty, go and drink from the jars the young men have filled.” 10 At this, she fell on her face, bowing low to the ground, and said to him, “Why have I found such favor in your eyes that you should take notice of me, even though I am a foreigner?”

11 Boaz replied, “I have been made fully aware of all you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband, how you left your father and mother and the land of your birth, and how you came to a people you did not know before. 12 May the LORD repay your work, and may you receive a rich reward from the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have taken refuge.” 13 “My lord,” she said, “may I continue to find favor in your eyes, for you have comforted and spoken kindly to your maidservant, though I am not like one of your servant girls.”

What is the context?

The narrator introduces Boaz before Ruth meets him: he is a prominent man of noble character from Elimelech’s clan. Ruth asks Naomi for permission to glean, gathering grain left behind by harvesters. Leviticus 19:9–10 and Deuteronomy 24:19–22 provide for the poor, foreigners, and widows in this way.

She happens to enter Boaz’s part of the field. When he arrives from Bethlehem, he and the workers greet one another in the LORD’s name. The foreman identifies Ruth as the Moabite woman who came with Naomi and reports her request and steady work that morning.

Boaz tells her to stay close to his young women, use his fields, and drink from the jars his workers fill. He has ordered the young men not to touch her. His welcome includes practical safeguards and provision, rather than kind words alone.

Ruth is astonished that he notices her, a foreigner. Boaz explains that he has heard of her care for Naomi and her decision to leave her family and homeland. He prays that the LORD will reward her and describes her as seeking refuge under God’s wings. חָסָה, hasah (khah-SAH), means to seek refuge; Brown–Driver–Briggs specifically connects this verse with the image of shelter under God’s wings.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Henry understand Ruth’s apparently accidental arrival as God’s care working through an ordinary event. Ruth does not first choose the field because she knows its owner’s family connection. The reader, however, has been told why the meeting can matter.

Both emphasize her diligence and Boaz’s kindness. Henry sees the exchange between the owner and workers as evidence that genuine devotion to God affects everyday relationships. He also directly compares Boaz’s care for Ruth with the kindness of Jesus toward people in need.

Wesley and Gill explain the wings as an image of protection, whether recalling a bird sheltering its young or the cherubim associated with God’s presence. The point of the prayer is Ruth’s trust in the God of Israel, expressed through the choices Boaz has heard about.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The refuge Boaz describes is part of a wider biblical picture. Psalm 91:1–4 speaks of trust in God through the image of shelter beneath His wings. In Matthew 23:37–39, Jesus uses the image of a hen gathering her young when expressing His longing to gather Jerusalem’s people.

Henry’s comparison with Christ’s kindness also finds a clear invitation in Matthew 11:25–30. Jesus calls the weary to come to Him for rest and learn from His gentleness. Boaz gives Ruth a place to work in safety; Jesus brings people into the deeper rest of knowing the Father through the Son.
