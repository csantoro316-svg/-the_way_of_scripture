Passage

Ezekiel 3:16-21

What happens if the watchman does not give the warning?

Berean Standard Bible

16 At the end of seven days the word of the LORD came to me, saying, 17 “Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel. Whenever you hear a word from My mouth, give them a warning from Me. 18 If I say to the wicked man, ‘You will surely die,’ but you do not warn him or speak out to warn him from his wicked way to save his life, that wicked man will die in his iniquity, and I will hold you responsible for his blood. 19 But if you warn a wicked man and he does not turn from his wickedness and his wicked way, he will die in his iniquity, but you will have saved yourself.

20 Now if a righteous man turns from his righteousness and commits iniquity, and I put a stumbling block before him, he will die. If you did not warn him, he will die in his sin, and the righteous acts he did will not be remembered. And I will hold you responsible for his blood. 21 But if you warn the righteous man not to sin, and he does not sin, he will indeed live because he heeded your warning, and you will have saved yourself.”

What is the context?

After seven days, the Lord appoints Ezekiel as watchman for the house of Israel. He is to hear the word from God’s mouth and deliver His warning. The responsibility is defined by what the Lord gives him to say.

If he fails to warn a wicked person, that person dies in iniquity and Ezekiel is held responsible for the blood. If he gives the warning and it is refused, the person still faces the consequence, but Ezekiel has fulfilled his responsibility.

The same seriousness applies when a righteous person turns to wrongdoing. Earlier righteous acts will not shield that person from the announced judgment. If the warning is heard and heeded, the person lives and the watchman has delivered himself.

The passage holds together two responsibilities: Ezekiel must give the warning, and the hearer must respond. Neither the messenger’s silence nor the hearer’s past conduct makes present wrongdoing safe.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry relates the warning to Israel’s national covenant and also applies it to the final accountability of those who hear the gospel. Wesley stresses the seriousness of a person abandoning known ways of goodness. The USCCB notes emphasize Ezekiel’s role as sentinel, also developed later in chapter 33.

Readers also ask how the righteous person’s fall relates to lasting salvation. Here the churches’ wider teachings differ. The Reformed Westminster Confession, chapter 17, and the 1689 Baptist Confession, chapter 17, teach that those God truly calls will be preserved to the end, while serious sin can bring discipline and grave harm.

The Catholic Church teaches that freely chosen serious sin, committed with full knowledge, can result in loss of the state of grace, and that repentance and God’s forgiveness are needed; see the Catechism, paragraphs 1857–1861. The Wesleyan Methodist Article XII teaches that people can depart from grace after receiving the Holy Spirit and can rise again through God’s grace and repentance.

These wider teachings shape how Christians approach the warning; they are not separate official commentaries on each verse. The urgent responsibility to warn and turn from sin remains central to the passage.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 20:24–32 uses closely related language when Paul declares himself innocent of everyone’s blood because he has not held back God’s whole will. He urges the elders to keep watch over themselves and the flock, remembering his warnings given with tears.

Paul does not explicitly name Ezekiel in that speech, so this is a close parallel rather than a stated quotation. The responsibility of watchful care continues in the gospel mission: people are to be taught and warned faithfully, with concern for their life before God.

The speech ends by committing the hearers to God and the word of His grace. The watchman’s duty serves His care for people, rather than giving the messenger control over their response or destiny.
