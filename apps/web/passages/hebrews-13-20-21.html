Passage

Hebrews 13:20-21

Who equips us to live the life this letter calls for?

Berean Standard Bible

20 Now may the God of peace, who through the blood of the eternal covenant brought back from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great Shepherd of the sheep, 21 equip you with every good thing to do His will. And may He accomplish in us what is pleasing in His sight through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen.

What is the context?

The closing blessing turns from what the readers are called to do to the God who equips them. He is the God of peace who brought Jesus from the dead, the great Shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the eternal covenant.

The writer asks God to equip them for His will and work in them what pleases Him through Jesus Christ. Their response is real, and the help that makes it possible comes from God.

The blessing gathers the letter’s themes into a prayer: Christ lives, His covenant endures and His people are to live in a way that pleases God. Glory belongs to Jesus, rather than to the leaders or readers for sustaining themselves.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley understand the blessing as dependence on God’s continuing work through Christ. Calvin emphasizes God fitting and strengthening people for good, while Wesley emphasizes the enduring covenant and the holiness God produces. Both connect the life being asked for with the help God supplies.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ezekiel 34:11–16, 23–24 joins God’s promise to seek and care for His flock with the promise of one shepherd from David’s line. In John 10:11–18, Jesus identifies Himself as the good Shepherd who lays down His life and takes it up again.

Ephesians 2:8–10 connects salvation by grace with a people created in Christ for good works. The final prayer of Hebrews places those works within the risen Shepherd’s saving and equipping care.
