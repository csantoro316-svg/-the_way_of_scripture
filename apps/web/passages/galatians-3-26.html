Passage

Galatians 3:26

How do we become God’s children in Christ?

Berean Standard Bible

26 You are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus.

What is the context?

This verse follows Paul’s explanation of the law as a guardian leading toward Christ. He now describes the believers’ standing as sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus.

The full paragraph, Galatians 3:26–29, connects that standing with baptism into Christ, shared belonging across human boundaries and inheritance according to Abraham’s promise.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as locating belonging to God’s family in faith in Christ. Wesley’s note on verse 26 connects sonship with the change from guardianship, and the USCCB note on verses 23–29 places the statement within the promise and inheritance described throughout the paragraph.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 8:14–17 connects being God’s children with the Spirit’s witness, calling God Father and being heirs with Christ. That shared inheritance reaches through present suffering toward glory with Him.

The family language here therefore carries a future as well as a present hope. Those who belong to Christ share the promise and a life directed toward what God has prepared for His children.

What is our position?

Our study Baptism reads verses 26–27 as distinguishing sonship through faith from being clothed with Christ in baptism. Its position is that faith establishes the saved relationship with God, and water baptism follows as the appointed act in which the believer puts on Christ for a new life of obedience. The study sums up that distinction as faith saving and baptism clothing the believer with Christ.
