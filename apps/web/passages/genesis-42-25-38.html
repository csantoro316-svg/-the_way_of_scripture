Passage

Genesis 42:25-38

Joseph’s Brothers Return to Canaan

Berean Standard Bible

25 Then Joseph gave orders to fill their bags with grain, to return each man’s silver to his sack, and to give them provisions for their journey. This order was carried out, 26 and they loaded the grain on their donkeys and departed.

27 At the place where they lodged for the night, one of them opened his sack to get feed for his donkey, and he saw his silver in the mouth of the sack. 28 “My silver has been returned!” he said to his brothers. “It is here in my sack.” Their hearts sank, and trembling, they turned to one another and said, “What is this that God has done to us?”

29 When they reached their father Jacob in the land of Canaan, they described to him all that had happened to them: 30 “The man who is lord of the land spoke harshly to us and accused us of spying on the country.

31 But we told him, ‘We are honest men, not spies. 32 We are twelve brothers, sons of one father. One is no more, and the youngest is now with our father in the land of Canaan.’

33 Then the man who is lord of the land said to us, ‘This is how I will know whether you are honest: Leave one brother with me, take food to relieve the hunger of your households, and go. 34 But bring your youngest brother back to me so I will know that you are not spies but honest men. Then I will give your brother back to you, and you can trade in the land.’”

35 As they began emptying their sacks, there in each man’s sack was his bag of silver! And when they and their father saw the bags of silver, they were dismayed.

36 Their father Jacob said to them, “You have deprived me of my sons. Joseph is gone and Simeon is no more. Now you want to take Benjamin. Everything is going against me!”

37 Then Reuben said to his father, “You may kill my two sons if I fail to bring him back to you. Put him in my care, and I will return him.”

38 But Jacob replied, “My son will not go down there with you, for his brother is dead, and he alone is left. If any harm comes to him on your journey, you will bring my gray hair down to Sheol in sorrow.”

What is the context?

Joseph orders the brothers' bags filled with grain, their silver returned, and provisions supplied for the journey. They leave without knowing about the returned money. At an overnight stop, one brother opens his sack and discovers his silver. The men are frightened and ask what God has done to them.

Back in Canaan, they tell Jacob about the accusation of spying and the demand to bring Benjamin. Simeon has been kept in Egypt while they return with food. As they empty their sacks, every man finds his silver, deepening both their fear and their father's distress.

Jacob sees another threat of loss: Joseph is gone, Simeon is absent, and now Benjamin is being asked for. He concludes that everything is going against him. The reader knows more than he does: Joseph is alive and has supplied the food and money. Jacob must respond without that knowledge.

Reuben offers his own two sons' lives as a guarantee that Benjamin will return. Jacob rejects the proposal and refuses to send Benjamin, fearing that another loss would bring him to his death in sorrow. The chapter ends with food for the present but no resolution of Simeon's confinement or the demand that must be met for a return.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly see fear shaped by the family's unresolved past: the returned money appears threatening, and Jacob cannot bear the possibility of losing another son. Wesley's notes on Genesis 42:28, 38 connect the brothers' alarm with their awakened consciences and Jacob's refusal with his distrust after Joseph's disappearance.
