Passage

James 5:16

Why confess and pray for one another?

Berean Standard Bible

16 Therefore confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous man has great power to prevail.

What is the context?

The mutual confession and prayer follow James’ instructions about care for the sick. They extend the concern beyond a person privately carrying a need: believers are to be truthful with one another and seek healing together.

The following example of Elijah emphasizes that prayer has real effect even when the person praying shares ordinary human weakness. James directs confidence toward prayer before God, rather than toward a supposedly untouchable religious class.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley both understand confession as helping fellow believers pray with knowledge of one another’s needs. Wesley says it is not restricted to confession to elders and describes the healing here in spiritual terms.

The Catholic Church also teaches a distinct priestly ministry of reconciliation, while locating the source of forgiveness in God (Catechism, 1441–1448). That broader teaching differs from reading mutual confession here as the whole pattern for the church’s practice.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Galatians 6:1–2 calls for gentle restoration and carrying one another’s burdens as the law of Christ. That supplies a fitting wider setting for confession and prayer: helping someone return and heal, rather than using knowledge of a fault to humiliate or control that person.

What is our position?

Our study Prayer and Faith reads this passage as a call to seek spiritual restoration and renewed fellowship with God, with physical recovery remaining in His hands. It understands faithful prayer as dependence on God’s will, rather than an unconditional promise of healing produced by the strength of someone’s belief. Our study Baptism understands the mutual confession here as accountability and healing among believers, rather than a priestly requirement for receiving God’s forgiveness.
