Passage

Genesis 38:27-30

The Birth of Perez and Zerah

Berean Standard Bible

27 When the time came for Tamar to give birth, there were twins in her womb. 28 And as she was giving birth, one of them put out his hand; so the midwife took a scarlet thread and tied it around his wrist. “This one came out first,” she announced. 29 But when he pulled his hand back and his brother came out, she said, “You have broken out first!” So he was named Perez. 30 Then his brother came out with the scarlet thread around his wrist, and he was named Zerah.

What is the context?

Tamar gives birth to twins. One child puts out a hand, and the midwife ties a scarlet thread around his wrist to mark him as the first to emerge. He pulls his hand back, however, and his brother is born first.

The firstborn is named Perez, with the midwife's words connecting his name to breaking out ahead. His brother, born with the thread around his wrist, is named Zerah. The brief account preserves the unusual birth order and identifies the two sons who will appear in later family records.

The scene recalls the earlier attention Genesis gives to the birth order of Jacob and Esau. Here the immediate result is the establishment of Judah and Tamar's two sons. Later Scripture will follow Perez's descendants toward David and, eventually, Jesus.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as the birth record of Judah and Tamar's sons, with particular significance because Perez belongs to the ancestry of Christ. Wesley's note on Genesis 38:28 connects the twins with Matthew's genealogy, which names both brothers.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ruth 4:12, 18–22 recalls Tamar and traces the family from Perez to David. Matthew 1:3–6 takes up that same line within its account of Jesus' ancestry. The connection is stated in the biblical family records themselves.

This small birth scene thus becomes part of the longer movement from Abraham's family to Israel's king and the Messiah. Its importance comes from the family line Scripture actually follows; the thread and the order of the twins need no additional hidden meaning to give the passage a place in that larger story.
