Passage

Ezekiel 36:24-32

What does God change when He gives a new heart and His Spirit?

Berean Standard Bible

24 For I will take you from among the nations and gather you out of all the countries, and I will bring you back into your own land. 25 I will also sprinkle clean water on you, and you will be clean. I will cleanse you from all your impurities and all your idols. 26 I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you; I will remove your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh. 27 And I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes and to carefully observe My ordinances.

28 Then you will live in the land that I gave your forefathers; you will be My people, and I will be your God. 29 I will save you from all your uncleanness. I will summon the grain and make it plentiful, and I will not bring famine upon you. 30 I will also make the fruit of the trees and the crops of the field plentiful, so that you will no longer bear reproach among the nations on account of famine.

31 Then you will remember your evil ways and wicked deeds, and you will loathe yourselves for your iniquities and abominations. 32 It is not for your sake that I will act, declares the Lord GOD—let it be known to you. Be ashamed and disgraced for your ways, O house of Israel!

What is the context?

The Lord will gather His people from the countries and bring them to their land, but His promise goes deeper than changing their location. He will cleanse their impurities and idols.

He will give a new heart and a new spirit, taking away the heart of stone and giving a heart of flesh. The Hebrew word לֵב, lev (lehv), includes the inner person, mind and will in Brown–Driver–Briggs. The promise concerns a change within people who have resisted Him.

He will put His Spirit within them and cause them to walk in His ways. The relationship is restored: they will be His people, and He will be their God.

Plentiful food will replace famine and disgrace. Receiving mercy will not make them proud of their past; they will remember their evil ways with shame. The Lord repeats that His action is not something they have earned.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry, Wesley and the USCCB notes all understand the cleansing, new heart and Spirit as God renewing His people from within, making them responsive to His will. Henry and Wesley also apply the promise to the new covenant life of believers, with the Spirit bringing the change that human self-confidence cannot supply.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In John 3:1–12, Jesus tells Nicodemus that entry into God’s kingdom requires birth of water and the Spirit. When Nicodemus struggles to understand, Jesus challenges him as Israel’s teacher. Ezekiel’s promise provides a fitting prophetic background for that need for cleansing and new life.

Titus 3:3–7 makes the saving source explicit: God acts according to mercy, through the washing of new birth and renewal by the Holy Spirit, poured out through Jesus Christ. The shared themes lead toward a life received from God, with grace and the hope of eternal life at its center.
