Passage

Acts 4:29-31

How does God answer the prayer for boldness?

Berean Standard Bible

29 And now, Lord, consider their threats, and enable Your servants to speak Your word with complete boldness, 30 as You stretch out Your hand to heal and perform signs and wonders through the name of Your holy servant Jesus.” 31 After they had prayed, their meeting place was shaken, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and spoke the word of God boldly.

What is the context?

After their release, Peter and John report the threats to the other believers. The community turns to God together, addressing Him as the sovereign Creator. Their prayer begins with who God is and what He has spoken.

They quote Psalm 2 and connect its opposition to the Lord’s Anointed with the actions of Herod, Pilate, Gentiles and people of Israel against Jesus. Those actions were real opposition, yet God’s purpose was not defeated.

The prayer then names the present threats and asks for boldness to speak God’s word, alongside healing and signs through Jesus’s name. The meeting place is shaken, the believers are filled with the Spirit, and they speak boldly. Luke connects their request with the answer he records.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a prayer for God’s work to continue through threatened witnesses, with the Spirit giving fresh boldness. Wesley on verse 31 describes them as filled afresh, and Calvin on verse 31 emphasizes both the sign of God’s presence and the practical answer of strengthened witness.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The believers explicitly apply Psalm 2:1–2 to the opposition against Jesus. The wider psalm places the nations’ hostility alongside God’s established King and His purpose for the nations. Their prayer rests on that larger view of whose purposes will stand.

Ephesians 6:18–20 gives a later echo: Paul asks believers to pray that he will proclaim the gospel fearlessly while in chains. Prayer supports the witness of God’s people through hardship as the mission continues.

What is our position?

Prayer and Faith reads this as a specific request aimed at God’s purpose: boldness to proclaim His word and His power at work through Jesus’s name. What Does Prayer Look Like? also emphasizes the believers’ desire to remain faithful under pressure. The account records God strengthening them for the witness they have asked to continue.
