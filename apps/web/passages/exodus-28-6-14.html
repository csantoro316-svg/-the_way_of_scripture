Passage

Exodus 28:6-14

Why did Aaron carry Israel’s names on his shoulders?

Berean Standard Bible

6 They are to make the ephod of finely spun linen embroidered with gold, and with blue, purple, and scarlet yarn. 7 It shall have two shoulder pieces attached at two of its corners, so it can be fastened. 8 And the skillfully woven waistband of the ephod must be of one piece, of the same workmanship—with gold, with blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, and with finely spun linen.

9 Take two onyx stones and engrave on them the names of the sons of Israel: 10 six of their names on one stone and the remaining six on the other, in the order of their birth. 11 Engrave the names of the sons of Israel on the two stones the way a gem cutter engraves a seal. Then mount the stones in gold filigree settings. 12 Fasten both stones on the shoulder pieces of the ephod as memorial stones for the sons of Israel. Aaron is to bear their names on his two shoulders as a memorial before the LORD. 13 Fashion gold filigree settings 14 and two chains of pure gold, made of braided cord work; and attach these chains to the settings.

What is the context?

The ephod is a priestly garment made from finely spun linen worked with gold and the three colored yarns. Shoulder pieces and a woven waistband hold it together. The next instructions provide settings and chains that will connect it with the breastpiece.

Two onyx stones are engraved with the names of Israel’s sons, six on each stone in birth order. Set in gold on the shoulders, they are memorial stones before the LORD. Aaron carries the names as he serves. His place before God concerns the people he represents, whose names are visibly joined to his priestly clothing.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Calvin both emphasize representation: Aaron bears Israel before God rather than serving for his own private importance. They also read the shoulder stones as a picture of Christ bearing His people and appearing on their behalf. This is their Christ-centered reading of the priestly pattern, grounded in the later description of His intercession.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The representative role reaches its greater fulfillment in Christ. Hebrews 9:24 says that He enters God’s presence on our behalf, and Hebrews 7:23–28 explains that He lives permanently to intercede for those who draw near through Him. The comparison with Aaron’s carried names points toward a living priest who continues to represent His people.

God’s remembrance is also a theme of hope elsewhere in Scripture. When Zion fears she has been forgotten, Isaiah 49:14–16 answers with the image of a mother’s care and God’s people inscribed on His palms. This is a parallel of remembrance, not a description of Jesus literally wearing Aaron’s stones. Together the passages direct hope toward God’s faithful care for the people He has made His own.
