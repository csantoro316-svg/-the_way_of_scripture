Passage

Ephesians 4:11-12

Who is being prepared to serve?

Berean Standard Bible

11 And it was He who gave some to be apostles, some to be prophets, some to be evangelists, and some to be pastors and teachers, 12 to equip the saints for works of ministry and to build up the body of Christ,

What is the context?

Paul has just described the generosity of the risen Christ. He now names people Christ gives to help His church: apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers.

Verse 12 explains their purpose. They equip the saints, meaning God’s people, for works of ministry and for the building up of Christ’s body. The service of those named in verse 11 prepares others to serve.

The sentence continues through verse 13, and verses 14–16 explain the desired growth: steadiness in teaching, truth spoken with love and each part contributing under Christ. Read the whole connected passage for that fuller picture.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as Christ providing people who teach, guide and prepare His church for service. The USCCB note on verse 12 explicitly understands the whole people of God as those being equipped for ministry.

Christians differ over how the apostles’ and prophets’ ministries continue. The Catholic Catechism, paragraphs 857–862, distinguishes the apostles’ unique role as witnesses and foundation from the pastoral care continued by bishops as their successors.

Among evangelical Christians, Dallas Theological Seminary’s doctrinal statement, article XVI, teaches that the foundational offices of apostle and prophet ended with the New Testament period, while evangelists, pastors and teachers continue. It also affirms that God can perform miracles today.

The Pentecostal Assemblies of God distinguishes the original apostles’ unique authority from ongoing apostolic work, such as missionary work and starting churches, without requiring an apostolic title or office. It also affirms prophecy as a continuing gift, with messages tested by the church against Scripture. These are distinct accounts of continuing ministry, rather than a single view shared by all Christians.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 Corinthians 12:4–7, 21–27 places different gifts within the work of the same God and directs them toward the common good. It also calls the members to care for one another, including those who appear weaker.

This is a supported parallel to the purpose stated here. Christ prepares a people whose members contribute to one another’s life. The ministry of leaders belongs within that shared calling.

What is our position?

Our study What Is the True Church? recognizes the leadership roles described here. Their purpose is to prepare believers to take part in ministry. Leaders serve and equip the community, while the whole community shares responsibility for its life and service. The study calls for active participation by believers and affirms the need for spiritually mature leadership.
