Passage

Acts 9:20-31

How does Saul become a witness among people who fear him?

Berean Standard Bible

20 Saul promptly began to proclaim Jesus in the synagogues, declaring, “He is the Son of God.” 21 All who heard him were astounded and asked, “Isn’t this the man who wreaked havoc in Jerusalem on those who call on this name? And hasn’t he come here to take them as prisoners to the chief priests?” 22 But Saul was empowered all the more, and he confounded the Jews living in Damascus by proving that Jesus is the Christ. 23 After many days had passed, the Jews conspired to kill him, 24 but Saul learned of their plot. Day and night they watched the city gates in order to kill him. 25 One night, however, his disciples took him and lowered him in a basket through a window in the wall.

26 When Saul arrived in Jerusalem, he tried to join the disciples, but they were all afraid of him, not believing that he was a disciple. 27 Then Barnabas brought him to the apostles and described how Saul had seen the Lord, who had spoken to him on the road to Damascus, and how Saul had spoken boldly in that city in the name of Jesus. 28 So Saul stayed with them, moving about freely in Jerusalem and speaking boldly in the name of the Lord. 29 He talked and debated with the Grecian Jews, but they tried to kill him. 30 When the brothers learned of this, they took him down to Caesarea and sent him off to Tarsus. 31 Then the church throughout Judea, Galilee, and Samaria experienced a time of peace. It grew in strength and numbers, living in the fear of the Lord and the encouragement of the Holy Spirit.

What is the context?

Saul promptly begins proclaiming Jesus as God’s Son in the Damascus synagogues. The hearers recognize the man who had come to arrest believers. His message astonishes them, and he grows stronger in showing that Jesus is the Christ.

After many days, a plot against his life leads his disciples to lower him through a window in the wall in a basket. Luke’s brief account does not supply every journey during this period. Galatians 1:17–19 records a journey to Arabia, a return to Damascus and a Jerusalem visit after three years. 2 Corinthians 11:32–33 also describes the escape and identifies a governor under King Aretas as seeking Saul’s arrest.

In Jerusalem, the disciples fear Saul and do not believe he is a disciple. Barnabas brings him to the apostles and describes his encounter with Jesus and his bold witness in Damascus. Saul is then able to move among them and speak openly about the Lord.

When another attempt is made to kill him, the brothers take him to Caesarea and send him to Tarsus. Luke closes with the church throughout Judea, Galilee and Samaria enjoying peace, growing stronger and increasing in numbers through reverence for the Lord and the Spirit’s encouragement.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as the visible reversal of Saul’s former work and an account of the church learning to receive him through Barnabas’s help. Wesley on verses 26–27 connects the Jerusalem visit with Galatians 1 and treats evidence of Saul’s changed life as relevant to the disciples’ concerns.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Galatians 1:23–24 explains how other believers heard that their former persecutor was now proclaiming the faith he had tried to destroy, and glorified God because of him. The changed life points beyond Saul’s abilities to God’s mercy.

The threatened violence also begins to show the cost named in Acts 9:16. Saul’s rescue depends on other disciples, a basket and a night escape. Being chosen for a significant work does not make him self-sufficient or exempt him from danger.
