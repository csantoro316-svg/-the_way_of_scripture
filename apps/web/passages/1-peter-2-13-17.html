Passage

1 Peter 2:13-17

How do we honor authority while serving God?

Berean Standard Bible

13 Submit yourselves for the Lord’s sake to every human institution, whether to the king as the supreme authority, 14 or to governors as those sent by him to punish those who do wrong and to praise those who do right. 15 For it is God’s will that by doing good you should silence the ignorance of foolish men.

16 Live in freedom, but do not use your freedom as a cover-up for evil; live as servants of God. 17 Treat everyone with high regard: Love the brotherhood of believers, fear God, honor the king.

What is the context?

Peter now applies honorable conduct to public life. The king and governors make clear that the human institutions in view include civil authority. Submission is offered for the Lord’s sake, with doing good as the answer to ignorant accusations.

Freedom in Christ is not a cover for evil. These believers are free people who live as God’s servants. Peter ends with four short instructions: honor everyone, love the brotherhood, fear God and honor the king. The ruler receives honor; the fear that governs their lives belongs to God.

The instruction describes the public purpose of government in punishing wrongdoing and commending good. It does not declare every ruler’s action good. The letter will immediately address the reality of unjust treatment.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian teaching commonly joins respect for civil authority with a higher allegiance to God. The Catholic Catechism, 2238–2242, teaches service to the common good, allows just criticism and requires refusal of commands contrary to the moral order, basic human rights or the Gospel. The Reformed Westminster Confession, 23.4, calls for prayer, honor and obedience to lawful commands even when a ruler’s religion differs from the believer’s.

Wesley likewise explains obedience as offered for God’s sake. Respect for rulers is not permission to treat them as a higher authority than God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Acts 5:27–32, Peter and the other apostles continue the witness to Jesus when authorities forbid it. Their answer puts God’s command first. That episode helps us read the call to honor authority alongside the mission God has given His people.

1 Timothy 2:1–6 connects prayer for rulers and peaceful, godly lives with God’s desire for people to be saved and know the truth. Public conduct serves a larger purpose: a life that honors God while His saving message is made known.
