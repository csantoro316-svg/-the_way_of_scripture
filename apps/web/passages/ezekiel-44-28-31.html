Passage

Ezekiel 44:28-31

How will the priests be provided for?

Berean Standard Bible

28 In regard to their inheritance, I am their inheritance. You are to give them no possession in Israel, for I am their possession. 29 They shall eat the grain offerings, the sin offerings, and the guilt offerings. Everything in Israel devoted to the LORD will belong to them. 30 The best of all the firstfruits and of every contribution from all your offerings will belong to the priests. You are to give your first batch of dough to the priest, so that a blessing may rest upon your homes. 31 The priests may not eat any bird or animal found dead or torn by wild beasts.

What is the context?

The Lord identifies Himself as the priests’ inheritance and possession. They are not to receive a separate landed possession like the other Israelites.

The offerings, things devoted to the Lord, firstfruits and contributions provide for them. The people are to bring the first of their dough, with a blessing upon their homes.

The priests’ food also remains subject to a limit: they may not eat birds or animals found dead or torn by wild beasts. Provision comes through the people’s worship while remaining under the Lord’s instructions.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Cambridge understands these verses as the priests’ maintenance, with the Lord as their inheritance and offerings supplying their needs. Wesley likewise explains the firstfruits and contributions as provision for them.

The people’s gifts sustain those serving among them; the ministry is not presented as a way to seize someone else’s land.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

First Corinthians 9:7–18 explicitly draws on those who eat from the temple offerings to explain support for those who preach the gospel. Paul treats material provision for spiritual work as a proper responsibility.

He also says that he did not use that right among the Corinthians, so that nothing would hinder the gospel. The wider lesson includes both generous support and a servant’s willingness to put the gospel before personal advantage.
