Passage

Ezra 10:18-44

Why does the book end with a list of the men involved?

Berean Standard Bible

18 Among the descendants of the priests who had married foreign women were found these descendants of Jeshua son of Jozadak and his brothers: Maaseiah, Eliezer, Jarib, and Gedaliah. 19 They pledged to send their wives away, and for their guilt they presented a ram from the flock as a guilt offering. 20 From the descendants of Immer: Hanani and Zebadiah. 21 From the descendants of Harim: Maaseiah, Elijah, Shemaiah, Jehiel, and Uzziah. 22 From the descendants of Pashhur: Elioenai, Maaseiah, Ishmael, Nethanel, Jozabad, and Elasah.

23 Among the Levites: Jozabad, Shimei, Kelaiah (that is, Kelita), Pethahiah, Judah, and Eliezer. 24 From the singers: Eliashib. From the gatekeepers: Shallum, Telem, and Uri.

25 And among the other Israelites, from the descendants of Parosh: Ramiah, Izziah, Malchijah, Mijamin, Eleazar, Malchijah, and Benaiah. 26 From the descendants of Elam: Mattaniah, Zechariah, Jehiel, Abdi, Jeremoth, and Elijah. 27 From the descendants of Zattu: Elioenai, Eliashib, Mattaniah, Jeremoth, Zabad, and Aziza. 28 From the descendants of Bebai: Jehohanan, Hananiah, Zabbai, and Athlai. 29 From the descendants of Bani: Meshullam, Malluch, Adaiah, Jashub, Sheal, and Jeremoth. 30 From the descendants of Pahath-moab: Adna, Chelal, Benaiah, Maaseiah, Mattaniah, Bezalel, Binnui, and Manasseh. 31 From the descendants of Harim: Eliezer, Isshijah, Malchijah, Shemaiah, Shimeon, 32 Benjamin, Malluch, and Shemariah. 33 From the descendants of Hashum: Mattenai, Mattattah, Zabad, Eliphelet, Jeremai, Manasseh, and Shimei.

34 From the descendants of Bani: Maadai, Amram, Uel, 35 Benaiah, Bedeiah, Cheluhi, 36 Vaniah, Meremoth, Eliashib, 37 Mattaniah, Mattenai, and Jaasu. 38 From the descendants of Binnui: Shimei, 39 Shelemiah, Nathan, Adaiah, 40 Machnadebai, Shashai, Sharai, 41 Azarel, Shelemiah, Shemariah, 42 Shallum, Amariah, and Joseph. 43 And from the descendants of Nebo: Jeiel, Mattithiah, Zabad, Zebina, Jaddai, Joel, and Benaiah. 44 All these men had married foreign women, and some of them had children by these wives.

What is the context?

The closing list begins with priests, including men from Jeshua’s family. They pledge to send their wives away and present a ram as a guilt offering. Levites, singers, gatekeepers and other Israelites follow in the list.

The order makes clear that the matter reaches people in religious service as well as the wider community. The report names those involved rather than leaving the breach as an accusation against an anonymous group.

The final verse adds that some had children by these wives. The book ends at that point. It records the men’s responsibility and the presence of children without giving a full account of the later circumstances of the affected families.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Gill read the list as the identified cases addressed in the reform, with the priests’ pledge and guilt offering leading the account.

Readers draw different conclusions about the children. Wesley understands them as excluded from Israel’s community but thinks provision was probably made for them. Gill notes that the closing account does not repeat the proposal to send the children away and suggests they were retained and cared for. These are the commentators’ proposed conclusions; the narrative does not follow the families’ later lives.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The priests’ guilt offering belongs to the sacrificial setting of Israel’s Law. Hebrews 10:1–18 explains the limits of repeated animal offerings and presents Christ’s offering of Himself as the sacrifice for sins given once for all.

That wider teaching distinguishes acknowledging guilt and responding to it from the ultimate ground of forgiveness. Ezra’s ending shows a community confronting unfaithfulness. Hebrews directs readers to Christ’s completed offering, through which God provides the forgiveness the sacrifices could not finally accomplish.
