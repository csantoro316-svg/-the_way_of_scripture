Passage

Luke 8:1-3

Who helps support Jesus’ traveling ministry?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Soon afterward, Jesus traveled from one town and village to another, preaching and proclaiming the good news of the kingdom of God. The Twelve were with Him, 2 as well as some women who had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities: Mary called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out, 3 Joanna the wife of Herod’s household manager Chuza, Susanna, and many others. These women were ministering to them out of their own means.

What is the context?

Jesus continues from town to town proclaiming the good news of God’s kingdom. The twelve accompany Him, and Luke also names women who travel with the group and support the ministry from their own resources.

Mary Magdalene had been freed from seven demons. Joanna is identified through her marriage to Chuza, Herod’s household manager. Susanna is named without further explanation, and many others also contribute. Luke gives these women a visible place in the account of how the ministry is sustained.

The support is a practical response within a traveling ministry. These women have received help from Jesus and now use what they have in serving the group. The short passage draws attention to people whose participation could be missed if readers noticed only the twelve.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly see these women as active participants in the ministry, offering practical support from their resources. The Catholic bishops’ notes also trace their presence forward to the witnesses of Jesus’ death and resurrection. (USCCB note on Luke 8:1–3; Luke 24:1–10)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Luke later names Mary Magdalene and Joanna among the women who report the empty tomb to the apostles. The people supporting the ministry here become witnesses who carry news of the resurrection. Their place in the story reaches from ordinary provision during the journey to the announcement at its decisive turning point. (Luke 24:1–12)

Paul describes those who belong to Christ as children of God and heirs of the promise, including both men and women. That is a wider connection with the people Jesus gathers around Himself: women share in the promised inheritance and participate in the life formed around Him. (Galatians 3:26–29)
