Passage

1 Corinthians 3:11

Who is the church’s foundation?

Berean Standard Bible

11 For no one can lay a foundation other than the one already laid, which is Jesus Christ.

What is the context?

This verse stands within Paul’s picture of building God’s community. He laid the foundation through his preaching; others must take care how they build on it. That foundation is Jesus Christ, rather than Paul himself.

The surrounding verses test the quality of what is built. Teachers and members are accountable for their contribution, but no one is free to replace Christ with a rival foundation. The statement answers the church’s tendency to make a human leader the basis of its identity.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this verse as making Jesus Christ the indispensable foundation of the church. The surrounding building picture calls those who teach and serve to build faithfully on Him.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 2:19–22 uses a related building picture, naming the apostles and prophets in the foundation and Christ as the cornerstone. Their witness belongs to the structure whose unity and growth depend on Him.

Paul’s pictures give different details, but both direct the community to Christ. God is forming a household and dwelling by the Spirit, rather than rival groups whose ultimate identity rests on the servants who taught them.

What is our position?

Our study What Is the True Church? identifies the living person of Christ as the church’s foundation. No teaching, title, institution or admired personality can take His place. Those who lead and teach must help people remain faithful to Him.
