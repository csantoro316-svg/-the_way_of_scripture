Passage

Ezra 8:21-23

Why do the travelers fast before setting out?

Berean Standard Bible

21 And there by the Ahava Canal I proclaimed a fast, so that we might humble ourselves before our God and ask Him for a safe journey for us and our children, with all our possessions. 22 For I was ashamed to ask the king for an escort of soldiers and horsemen to protect us from our enemies on the road, since we had told him, “The hand of our God is gracious to all who seek Him, but His great anger is against all who forsake Him.” 23 So we fasted and petitioned our God about this, and He granted our request.

What is the context?

Ezra proclaims a fast by the Ahava Canal so that the company can humble themselves before God and ask for a safe journey for themselves, their children and their possessions.

He explains that he had been ashamed to ask the king for a military escort after speaking of God’s gracious care for those who seek Him. The group fasts and prays, and Ezra reports that God grants their request.

The account describes a particular decision made in light of Ezra’s testimony to the king. Nehemiah 2:1–9 later records another servant praying, receiving royal help and traveling with officers and cavalry.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry understands Ezra’s choice as concern that a lawful advantage might undermine his witness in this situation. He emphasizes seeking wisdom about when to use or decline such help. Gill likewise connects the choice with what Ezra had already said about God’s care. The fast expresses dependence on God as the company faces a real need.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus’ teaching in Matthew 6:16–18 also directs fasting toward the Father rather than toward an audience. The wider theme is an honest turning to God, not a performance designed to impress others.

In Matthew 26:36–46, Jesus brings His distress and request to the Father while submitting to His will. Ezra records a granted request for safe travel; Jesus’ prayer shows why trust reaches deeper than receiving the particular outcome requested.
