Passage

Ezekiel 22:13-16

Can dishonest gain give the city strength to endure judgment?

Berean Standard Bible

13 Now look, I strike My hands together against your unjust gain and against the blood you have shed in your midst. 14 Will your courage endure or your hands be strong in the day I deal with you? I, the LORD, have spoken, and I will act. 15 I will disperse you among the nations and scatter you throughout the lands; I will purge your uncleanness. 16 And when you have defiled yourself in the eyes of the nations, then you will know that I am the LORD.’”

What is the context?

The Lord strikes His hands together against the city’s unjust gain and bloodshed. The wealth accumulated through harm has become part of the accusation against it.

He asks whether the people’s courage and strength will endure when He acts. Their present ability to profit and dominate cannot determine the outcome.

He announces scattering among the nations and the purging of uncleanness. Before the same nations among whom the city will be exposed, the people will come to know that He is the Lord.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry connects the warning with the ruinous outcome of choosing one’s own desires as master. Wesley understands the question about strength as exposing the inability to withstand the evils now coming.

The passage overturns the appearance that successful wrongdoing has made Jerusalem secure. The Lord’s announced action is the answer to that confidence.
