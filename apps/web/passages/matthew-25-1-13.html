Passage

Matthew 25:1-13

The Ten Virgins: Ready When the Bridegroom Comes

Berean Standard Bible

1 “At that time the kingdom of heaven will be like ten virgins who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom. 2 Five of them were foolish, and five were wise. 3 The foolish ones took their lamps but did not take along any extra oil. 4 But the wise ones took oil in flasks along with their lamps. 5 When the bridegroom was delayed, they all became drowsy and fell asleep.

6 At midnight the cry rang out: ‘Here is the bridegroom! Come out to meet him!’ 7 Then all the virgins woke up and trimmed their lamps. 8 The foolish ones said to the wise, ‘Give us some of your oil; our lamps are going out.’ 9 ‘No,’ said the wise ones, ‘or there may not be enough for both us and you. Instead, go to those who sell oil and buy some for yourselves.’ 10 But while they were on their way to buy it, the bridegroom arrived. Those who were ready went in with him to the wedding banquet, and the door was shut.

11 Later the other virgins arrived and said, ‘Lord, lord, open the door for us!’ 12 But he replied, ‘Truly I tell you, I do not know you.’ 13 Therefore keep watch, because you do not know the day or the hour.

What is the context?

Jesus continues His teaching about being ready for His coming. Ten virgins go out with lamps to meet a bridegroom. Five bring extra oil and five do not. The delay affects everyone: all ten become drowsy and sleep. The difference is whether they have prepared for the wait.

When the announcement comes at midnight, all ten wake and prepare their lamps. The foolish ones discover that their lamps are going out. The others cannot provide enough oil for both groups, and while the unprepared women go to buy it, the bridegroom arrives. Those who are ready enter the wedding banquet, and the door is shut.

The late arrivals call for the door to be opened, but the bridegroom says he does not know them. Jesus closes by calling His hearers to keep watch because they do not know the day or hour. Since even the wise women slept, the story directs attention to lasting readiness rather than going without physical sleep.

The warning follows the faithful and wicked servants in Matthew 24:45–51 and leads into another story about a master returning after a long absence. Together, these scenes ask what it means to be prepared when the wait is longer than expected.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readers commonly see the bridegroom as Christ and the closed door as a serious warning about being unprepared for His coming. They differ over how much meaning to assign to the oil and other details.

Calvin on 25:1–13 emphasizes continuing in faith through the whole wait and cautions against giving every object a separate hidden meaning. Wesley on 25:3–4 understands the lamp as faith and the oil as love that keeps faith alive. The US Catholic bishops' note on 25:2–4 suggests that the oil may represent good deeds, connecting the wise and foolish women with the people who hear and either practice or neglect Jesus' teaching in Matthew 7:24–27.

Walvoord on 25:1–13 proposes the Holy Spirit and His saving work as the meaning of the oil. He places the scene at Christ's return to earth after an earlier gathering of the church, while applying its warning to Christians now as well. That timing is his reading; the different explanations share the call to genuine readiness rather than reliance on outward appearance.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The wedding picture belongs to a wider biblical promise of joyful life with the Lord. Revelation 19:6–9 describes the marriage of the Lamb, the bride made ready, and the blessing of those invited to the marriage supper. This is a supported parallel to the wedding banquet here, without requiring each woman, flask, or moment in the story to match a separate future event.

In Luke 12:35–40, Jesus also describes servants with lamps burning as they wait for their master. He adds the striking promise that the master will serve the servants he finds ready. The larger hope is a welcome into His presence. Readiness is a response to the Lord whose coming His people await, and it cannot be replaced simply by being near others who know Him.
