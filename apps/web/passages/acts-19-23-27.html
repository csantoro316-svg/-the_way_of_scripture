Passage

Acts 19:23-27

Why does the message about God threaten the idol trade?

Berean Standard Bible

23 About that time there arose a great disturbance about the Way. 24 It began with a silversmith named Demetrius who made silver shrines of Artemis, bringing much business to the craftsmen. 25 Demetrius assembled the craftsmen, along with the workmen in related trades. “Men,” he said, “you know that this business is our source of prosperity. 26 And you can see and hear that not only in Ephesus, but in nearly the whole province of Asia, this Paul has persuaded a great number of people to turn away. He says that man-made gods are no gods at all. 27 There is danger not only that our business will fall into disrepute, but also that the temple of the great goddess Artemis will be discredited and her majesty deposed—she who is worshiped by all the province of Asia and the whole world.”

What is the context?

Demetrius makes silver shrines of Artemis, and the business supports other craftsmen and related workers. He gathers them and begins with the issue of their prosperity. Paul’s message that man-made gods are not gods has persuaded many people to turn away, threatening the trade.

He then appeals to the honor of Artemis and her temple. Luke lets us hear how economic anxiety and religious loyalty are joined in the appeal. The claim that the whole world worships her is part of Demetrius’s speech, not Luke’s announcement that every person in every nation does so.

The account identifies a real trade interest without telling us that Demetrius holds formal authority over every worker or that all the city’s religious concern is knowingly dishonest. The disturbance grows out of what he says to this gathering.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as an example of opposition arising when a change of worship affects livelihoods and social loyalties. Demetrius’s own speech joins concern for income with concern for the goddess. The narrative does not command believers to provoke riots as proof that their witness is effective.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus’ warning in Luke 16:13 that a servant cannot serve both God and money helps explain the larger question of loyalty. Turning to the living God can unsettle arrangements built around another object of devotion. God’s purposes reach into work, spending and the things people protect, as well as what they say they believe.
