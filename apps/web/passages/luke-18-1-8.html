Passage

Luke 18:1-8

Why keep praying when justice seems delayed?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then Jesus told them a parable about their need to pray at all times and not lose heart: 2 “In a certain town there was a judge who neither feared God nor respected men. 3 And there was a widow in that town who kept appealing to him, ‘Give me justice against my adversary.’ 4 For a while he refused, but later he said to himself, ‘Though I neither fear God nor respect men, 5 yet because this widow keeps pestering me, I will give her justice. Otherwise, she will wear me out with her perpetual requests.’”

6 And the Lord said, “Listen to the words of the unjust judge. 7 Will not God bring about justice for His elect who cry out to Him day and night? Will He delay in helping them? 8 I tell you, He will promptly carry out justice on their behalf. Nevertheless, when the Son of Man comes, will He find faith on earth?”

What is the context?

Luke tells us why Jesus gives this parable: His followers need to keep praying and not lose heart. It follows the teaching about the day of the Son of Man, and its closing question returns to His coming.

A widow repeatedly asks a judge for justice against an adversary. The judge neither fears God nor respects people. He eventually acts because he wants to be free of her repeated requests.

The Greek verb behind the widow’s appeal is ἐκδικέω, ekdikeō (ek-dee-KEH-oh), meaning here to do someone justice or defend them against another person. Her request concerns justice, rather than a list of things she would like to have. Luke does not give the details of her legal dispute. (Thayer, Greek 1556)

Jesus draws attention to what this unjust judge says, then asks about God’s justice for His people who cry to Him day and night. The story moves from the judge’s reluctant action to confidence in God’s response. The final question asks whether faith will be found when the Son of Man comes.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly understand this as encouragement to persist in prayer and trust God through waiting and suffering. The Catholic bishops’ notes connect persistent prayer with remaining faithful; Wesley emphasizes God’s justice even while wrongs and suffering continue for a time. (USCCB notes on Luke 18:1–8; Wesley on Luke 18:1, 7–8)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Psalm 146 presents God as the faithful judge who gives justice to the oppressed and sustains the widow. That Scriptural picture makes the difference between God and the indifferent judge especially clear: the one to whom His people appeal already cares about those being wronged. (Psalm 146:5–9)

Revelation portrays people killed for their witness crying to God for judgment and being told to rest a little longer. This is a supported parallel to faithful prayer during delay. Their appeal is heard even while they await the completion of God’s justice; waiting does not mean He has forgotten them. (Revelation 6:9–11)

What is our position?

Prayer and Faith directly addresses this parable in its section Luke 18:1–8 — The Persistent Widow. The study understands it as a call to keep trusting God and seeking His justice through prolonged suffering. The judge acts after being worn down, but God’s character is different: persistent prayer does not make an unwilling God become generous. The study emphasizes enduring faith and continued prayer for His purposes while the answer is still awaited.
