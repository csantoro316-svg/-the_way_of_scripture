Passage

Ezekiel 47:7-12

What comes to life where the water flows?

Berean Standard Bible

7 When I arrived, I saw a great number of trees along both banks of the river. 8 And he said to me, “This water flows out to the eastern region and goes down into the Arabah. When it empties into the Dead Sea, the water there becomes fresh. 9 Wherever the river flows, there will be swarms of living creatures and a great number of fish, because it flows there and makes the waters fresh; so wherever the river flows, everything will flourish. 10 Fishermen will stand by the shore; from En-gedi to En-eglaim they will spread their nets to catch fish of many kinds, like the fish of the Great Sea.

11 But the swamps and marshes will not become fresh; they will be left for salt. 12 Along both banks of the river, fruit trees of all kinds will grow. Their leaves will not wither, and their fruit will not fail. Each month they will bear fruit, because the water from the sanctuary flows to them. Their fruit will be used for food and their leaves for healing.”

What is the context?

Trees line the riverbanks. The guide explains that the water travels into the Arabah and the Dead Sea, making the sea’s waters fresh. Living creatures and many kinds of fish flourish, and fishermen spread their nets along the shore.

The marshes and swamps remain for salt. The picture is not a statement that every part of the region becomes identical.

Along both banks grow fruit trees whose leaves do not wither and whose fruit does not fail. They bear every month because their water comes from the sanctuary. Their fruit provides food and their leaves provide healing: life flows outward from the place of God’s presence.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley uses the healed waters to describe grace renewing what was barren and corrupt. Henry similarly understands the fruit and healing leaves through the nourishing and restoring work of God’s word.

Cambridge emphasizes a renewed creation supplying real human needs, rather than reducing the picture to inward blessings alone. These readings share the importance of God as the source of life while differing over how the earthly details are fulfilled.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

A close parallel appears in Revelation 22:1–5: a river flows from the throne of God and of the Lamb, the tree of life bears fruit each month, and its leaves are for the healing of the nations.

Revelation takes the hope into the city where the curse is gone, God’s servants see His face and His light replaces the night. The connection reaches beyond restored fishing and fruitful banks to life sustained in God’s presence, free from the curse. It does not provide a medical explanation of how immortal bodies depend on eating particular leaves.
