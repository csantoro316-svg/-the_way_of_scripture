Passage

2 Corinthians 8:16-24

Why send trusted people to handle the gift?

Berean Standard Bible

16 But thanks be to God, who put into the heart of Titus the same devotion I have for you. 17 For not only did he welcome our appeal, but he is eagerly coming to you of his own volition. 18 Along with Titus we are sending the brother who is praised by all the churches for his work in the gospel. 19 More than that, this brother was chosen by the churches to accompany us with the gracious offering we administer to honor the Lord Himself and to show our eagerness to help.

20 We want to avoid any criticism of the way we administer this generous gift. 21 For we are taking great care to do what is right, not only in the eyes of the Lord, but also in the eyes of men. 22 And we are sending along with them our brother who has proven his earnestness to us many times and in many ways, and now even more so by his great confidence in you.

23 As for Titus, he is my partner and fellow worker among you. As for our brothers, they are messengers of the churches, the glory of Christ. 24 In full view of the churches, then, show these men the proof of your love and the reason for our boasting about you.

What is the context?

Paul introduces the people being sent to help with the collection. He thanks God for Titus’s devotion to the Corinthians and also describes Titus as eager to go of his own accord. God’s work in Titus and Titus’s willing response appear together in the account.

One companion is respected among the churches for his work in the gospel and has been chosen by them to travel with the gift. Another has repeatedly proved diligent and now has great confidence in the Corinthians. Paul leaves both men unnamed.

These arrangements serve a clear purpose: protecting the administration of a generous gift from criticism. Paul wants their conduct to be right before God and visibly honorable to other people. Trusted companions and the churches’ involvement give practical form to that concern.

Titus is Paul’s partner and fellow worker; the other brothers represent the churches. Paul asks the Corinthians to welcome this team with a response that shows their love and confirms his confidence in them. 1 Corinthians 16:3–4 likewise gives the Corinthians a role in approving people to carry their gift.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as responsible handling of a church collection, with trusted representatives and conduct others can examine. The USCCB notes on verses 16–24 emphasize the workers’ character and authority as church messengers; Wesley’s notes on verses 19 and 23–24 likewise describe their appointment and service. The passage gives the two brothers’ qualifications without supplying their names.
