Passage

1 Kings 11:40-43

How does Solomon’s reign come to an end?

Berean Standard Bible

40 Solomon therefore sought to kill Jeroboam. But Jeroboam arose and fled to Egypt, to Shishak king of Egypt, where he remained until the death of Solomon. 41 As for the rest of the acts of Solomon—all that he did, as well as his wisdom—are they not written in the Book of the Acts of Solomon? 42 Thus the time that Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel was forty years. 43 And Solomon rested with his fathers and was buried in the city of his father David. And his son Rehoboam reigned in his place.

What is the context?

Solomon tries to kill Jeroboam, who escapes to Shishak king of Egypt and remains there until Solomon’s death. The ruler who once received wisdom to govern now seeks the life of the man named to receive much of his kingdom.

The narrator refers readers to the Book of the Acts of Solomon for the rest of his deeds and wisdom. His reign over all Israel lasts forty years. He is buried in the City of David, and his son Rehoboam succeeds him.

The notice closes Solomon’s reign with both his achievement and his failure in view. The announced division of the kingdom remains ahead.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill believe Solomon repented, treating Ecclesiastes as evidence of his return after his fall. Henry, in his comments on verses 9–13, allows hope for repentance but emphasizes that Scripture does not expressly record it here.

This is a real difference in how confidently the writers draw a conclusion beyond the closing notice. Kings records the reign, death, burial and succession; it leaves Solomon’s final inward response unstated.
