Passage

Daniel 2:12-18

How does Daniel respond when the decree threatens his friends?

Berean Standard Bible

12 This response made the king so angry and furious that he gave orders to destroy all the wise men of Babylon. 13 So the decree went out that the wise men were to be executed, and men went to look for Daniel and his friends to execute them. 14 When Arioch, the commander of the king’s guard, went out to execute the wise men of Babylon, Daniel responded with discretion and tact. 15 “Why is the decree from the king so harsh?” he asked. Then Arioch explained the situation to Daniel. 16 So Daniel went in and asked the king to give him some time, so that he could give him the interpretation.

17 Then Daniel returned to his house and explained the matter to his friends Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, 18 urging them to plead for mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and his friends would not be killed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

What is the context?

The king orders the execution of all Babylon’s wise men, placing Daniel and his friends in danger too. Daniel asks Arioch about the harsh decree with discretion and tact, rather than answering recklessly.

He asks the king for time, then explains the situation to Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah. They are to plead for mercy from the God of heaven concerning the mystery.

The response combines practical action and shared prayer. Daniel seeks an opportunity to answer while acknowledging that the knowledge needed to save them must come from God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Gaebelein emphasize the friends seeking God’s mercy together. Henry also draws attention to valuing other people’s prayers and recognizing the answer as a gift, rather than something earned by the petitioners.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

A similar response appears in Acts 4:23–31. After Peter and John report the authorities’ threats, the believers pray together to the Creator, recognizing opposition to Jesus and asking for boldness to speak God’s word.

The outcomes are not identical: Daniel seeks the knowledge needed to avert executions, while the church asks for courage to continue its witness. Both show threatened people turning together to the God whose purpose is greater than the rulers confronting them.
