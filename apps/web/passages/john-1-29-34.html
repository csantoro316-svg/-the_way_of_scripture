Passage

John 1:29-34

Why does John call Jesus the Lamb of God?

Berean Standard Bible

29 The next day John saw Jesus coming toward him and said, “Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world! 30 This is He of whom I said, ‘A man who comes after me has surpassed me because He was before me.’ 31 I myself did not know Him, but the reason I came baptizing with water was that He might be revealed to Israel.”

32 Then John testified, “I saw the Spirit descending from heaven like a dove and resting on Him. 33 I myself did not know Him, but the One who sent me to baptize with water told me, ‘The man on whom you see the Spirit descend and rest is He who will baptize with the Holy Spirit.’ 34 I have seen and testified that this is the Son of God.”

What is the context?

The next day John sees Jesus approaching and identifies Him as the Lamb of God who takes away the world’s sin. He repeats that the One coming after him ranks above him because He was before him.

John explains that his water baptism serves the purpose of revealing Jesus to Israel. He testifies that he saw the Spirit descend from heaven like a dove and remain on Him.

This was the sign given by the One who sent John: the man on whom the Spirit descended and remained would baptize with the Holy Spirit. John’s testimony ends with his declaration that Jesus is the Son of God. The Gospel reports his witness to the sign and its meaning.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly understand the Lamb as pointing to Jesus’ saving death and removal of sin. The Catholic Catechism connects the title with Isaiah’s suffering Servant and the Passover lamb; Wesley and Calvin likewise explain it through sacrifice, sin-bearing and reconciliation with God. (Catechism §608; Wesley on John 1:29; Calvin on John 1:29)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Isaiah describes the Servant led like a lamb to slaughter and bearing the sins of others. Exodus describes the Passover blood marking the houses spared in judgment, and Paul explicitly calls Christ the sacrificed Passover lamb. These are supported connections behind the sacrificial title John gives Jesus. Revelation carries the picture forward with the slain Lamb whose blood purchases people from every tribe and nation. (Isaiah 53:7–12; Exodus 12:5–14; 1 Corinthians 5:7–8; Revelation 5:6–10)

The promise of baptism with the Holy Spirit also reaches beyond this first testimony in Israel. Peter later recalls the Lord’s contrast between John’s water baptism and baptism with the Spirit when the same gift is given to Gentiles. The church recognizes that God has granted them repentance leading to life. The One announced here gathers people into God’s saving work and gives the Spirit. (Acts 11:15–18)
