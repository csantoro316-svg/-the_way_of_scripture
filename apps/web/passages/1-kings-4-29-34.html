Passage

1 Kings 4:29-34

Why do people from other nations come to hear Solomon?

Berean Standard Bible

29 And God gave Solomon wisdom, exceedingly deep insight, and understanding beyond measure, like the sand on the seashore. 30 Solomon’s wisdom was greater than that of all the men of the East, greater than all the wisdom of Egypt. 31 He was wiser than all men—wiser than Ethan the Ezrahite, and wiser than Heman, Calcol, and Darda, the sons of Mahol. And his fame spread throughout the surrounding nations. 32 Solomon composed three thousand proverbs, and his songs numbered a thousand and five. 33 He spoke of trees, from the cedar in Lebanon to the hyssop growing in the wall, and he taught about animals, birds, reptiles, and fish. 34 So men of all nations came to listen to Solomon’s wisdom, sent by all the kings of the earth, who had heard of his wisdom.

What is the context?

God gives Solomon wisdom, deep insight and understanding beyond measure. His reputation surpasses the wisdom of the East and Egypt, and his fame spreads through the surrounding nations.

The account describes three thousand proverbs and a thousand and five songs, along with teaching about plants and animals. His learning reaches across many parts of life.

People sent by other kings come to hear him. The gift requested so that he could govern God’s people has become known beyond Israel.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry considers Solomon’s wisdom a greater glory than his wealth. He emphasizes using great gifts for the good of others.

Wesley also sees value in Solomon’s wide learning, while noting that his discourses about the natural world have not all been preserved. The biblical record describes a body of teaching larger than the writings we now possess.

The source of the gift remains central: the passage begins by saying that God gave this wisdom. Solomon’s fame leads readers back to the One who answered his request.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Jesus explicitly compares Himself with Solomon in Matthew 12:42. He recalls the Queen of the South coming to hear Solomon’s wisdom and says that One greater than Solomon is present.

Colossians 2:2–3 places the treasures of wisdom and knowledge in Christ. Solomon’s gifted understanding provides a point of comparison, while Jesus is the greater One to whom readers are directed.

This connection does not depend on treating every later decision of Solomon as wise. The rest of his story will show why God’s gift and a person’s faithful use of it must be kept together.
