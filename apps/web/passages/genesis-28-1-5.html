Passage

Genesis 28:1-5

Jacob’s Departure

Berean Standard Bible

1 So Isaac called for Jacob and blessed him. “Do not take a wife from the Canaanite women,” he commanded. 2 “Go at once to Paddan-aram, to the house of your mother’s father Bethuel, and take a wife from among the daughters of Laban, your mother’s brother. 3 May God Almighty bless you and make you fruitful and multiply you, so that you may become a company of peoples. 4 And may He give the blessing of Abraham to you and your descendants, so that you may possess the land where you dwell as a foreigner, the land God gave to Abraham.”

5 So Isaac sent Jacob to Paddan-aram, to Laban son of Bethuel the Aramean, the brother of Rebekah, who was the mother of Jacob and Esau.

What is the context?

Isaac now knowingly calls Jacob, blesses him, and sends him to Rebekah's family to seek a wife. The circumstances are very different from the disguised visit in chapter 27. He tells Jacob not to marry a Canaanite woman, but to go to Laban's household in Paddan-aram.

The blessing explicitly names Abraham: fruitfulness, many descendants, and possession of the land in which the family still lives as foreigners. Jacob's departure is therefore not presented as disinheritance. He leaves home bearing the blessing, though the danger and separation caused by the family conflict remain real.

This journey also recalls the search for Isaac's wife in chapter 24. Then a servant went to the relatives and Isaac remained in the land; now Jacob himself must go. The recurring concern is the future of the promised family, while the different journeys show that the next generation's path will not simply repeat the last.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as Isaac openly confirming Jacob as the bearer of Abraham's blessing before sending him away to marry. Wesley's notes on Genesis 28:1, 3–4 emphasize that leaving the land for a time does not cancel the promised inheritance.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The blessing carries the promise from Abraham through Isaac to Jacob. Hebrews 11:9, 13–16 names these heirs as people who live as strangers while looking toward what God will provide. A journey away from home does not, by itself, mean God has abandoned that future.

The larger promise will also reach beyond physical descent. Galatians 3:26–29 describes those who belong to Christ as Abraham's offspring and heirs according to the promise. Jacob's family remains an essential part of the story through which that blessing reaches others.
