Passage

1 Timothy 1:1-2

What is the foundation of Paul’s charge to Timothy?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus by the command of God our Savior and of Christ Jesus our hope, 2 To Timothy, my true child in the faith: Grace, mercy, and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.

What is the context?

Paul introduces himself as an apostle of Christ Jesus by the command of God our Savior and Christ Jesus our hope. His authority to give the coming charge rests in the Lord who appointed him.

Timothy is addressed as Paul’s true child in the faith. This describes their close relationship in the gospel. Paul wishes him grace, mercy and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus the Lord.

The following verses explain Timothy’s immediate responsibility in Ephesus. This greeting places that work within a relationship of faith and dependence on God’s saving care.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as Paul affirming his commission and encouraging Timothy in their shared faith. Wesley’s notes on verses 1–2 connect Christ with the ground of their hope and mercy with the needs of the person serving. The greeting gives the coming instructions a setting of grace, mercy and peace.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 9:10–16 describes the Lord appointing Paul to carry His name before Gentiles, kings and Israel. Timothy’s work belongs within the mission Paul received, rather than a private project detached from the spread of Christ’s message.

Titus 2:11–14 connects God’s saving grace and the hope of Christ’s appearing with a people He redeems and forms for good works. The Savior and hope named in the greeting point toward that larger purpose for the community Timothy serves.
