Passage

2 Samuel 12:24-25

What does Solomon’s birth reveal after the loss?

Berean Standard Bible

24 Then David comforted his wife Bathsheba, and he went to her and lay with her. So she gave birth to a son, and they named him Solomon. Now the LORD loved the child 25 and sent word through Nathan the prophet to name him Jedidiah because the LORD loved him.

What is the context?

David comforts Bathsheba, and afterward she gives birth to another son, Solomon. The account expressly says that the LORD loves this child and sends word through Nathan to name him Jedidiah.

Brown–Driver–Briggs gives the name Jedidiah the meaning beloved of Yah, referring to the LORD. The name expresses the love the narrative has just announced. This is another child, not a new name for the son who died.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley read the announcement as a sign of God’s continuing favor toward David’s family. The loss and the wrongdoing have not become the whole future of the household.

The description focuses on God’s love for Solomon and the comfort David gives Bathsheba. It does not ask us to treat one child as interchangeable with another or to assume that a later birth removes their grief.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Matthew 1:6–7, 16–17 explicitly includes Solomon in the family line leading to Jesus. It also recalls his mother as Uriah’s wife, keeping the painful history in view as it traces God’s work toward the Christ.

This is a direct connection to God’s saving purpose. The family’s story continues toward Jesus, while the account of David’s wrongdoing remains on the page. God’s faithfulness reaches through a damaged history without requiring us to rename its wrongs as good.
