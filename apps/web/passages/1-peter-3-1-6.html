Passage

1 Peter 3:1-6

How can faith be seen within a marriage?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Wives, in the same way, submit yourselves to your husbands, so that even if they refuse to believe the word, they will be won over without words by the behavior of their wives 2 when they see your pure and reverent demeanor.

3 Your beauty should not come from outward adornment, such as braided hair or gold jewelry or fine clothes, 4 but from the inner disposition of your heart, the unfading beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is precious in God’s sight.

5 For this is how the holy women of the past adorned themselves. They put their hope in God and were submissive to their husbands, 6 just as Sarah obeyed Abraham and called him lord. And you are her children if you do what is right and refuse to give way to fear.

What is the context?

The call to honorable conduct now reaches marriages in which a husband may refuse to believe the word. Peter addresses wives about the witness of their lives: their husbands may be won over as they see pure and reverent conduct.

The word for submitting describes placing oneself under another’s authority. Peter’s instruction concerns their own husbands. It does not tell every woman to submit to every man, and the next verse addresses the husband’s responsibility to honor his wife.

Peter turns attention from outward display to the lasting beauty of the heart. The holy women’s hope was in God. Sarah’s calling Abraham lord recalls Genesis 18:12; Peter’s closing description joins doing right with refusing to give way to fear.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians agree that faith should shape conduct within marriage, but differ over how the responsibilities are shared. The Southern Baptist Baptist Faith and Message, XVIII, teaches equal worth before God together with a husband’s servant leadership and a wife’s gracious submission. CBE International’s statement, family point 11, reads these verses alongside Ephesians 5:21 as mutual submission and responsibility, with headship expressed through self-giving love. CBE speaks as an organization, not for every church.

The outward-adornment instruction also has different readings. Calvin understands it as rejecting vanity and excessive display, rather than every ornament. Wesley takes a stricter view, forbidding ornamental gold, elaborate hair and costly dress. Both emphasize the inward character precious to God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 Corinthians 7:12–16 also addresses marriages in which only one spouse believes. It leaves room for the unbelieving spouse’s salvation while declining to guarantee that one spouse will save the other. Faithful witness is meaningful without placing another person’s response under the believer’s control.

Ephesians 5:21–33 sets marriage beside Christ’s self-giving love for the church. Read together, these passages bring everyday married life into the wider calling to reflect Christ’s character.
