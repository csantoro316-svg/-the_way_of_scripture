Passage

Genesis 13:14-18

God Renews the Promise to Abram

Berean Standard Bible

14 After Lot had departed, the LORD said to Abram, “Now lift up your eyes from the place where you are, and look to the north and south and east and west, 15 for all the land that you see, I will give to you and your offspring forever.

16 I will make your offspring like the dust of the earth, so that if one could count the dust of the earth, then your offspring could be counted. 17 Get up and walk around the land, through its length and breadth, for I will give it to you.”

18 So Abram moved his tent and went to live near the Oaks of Mamre at Hebron, where he built an altar to the LORD.

What is the context?

After Lot leaves, God tells Abram to look in every direction and promises the land to him and his offspring. Lot had looked and chosen what seemed best to him; Abram is now invited to look at what God promises to give. Letting his nephew choose first has not deprived him of God's care.

The promise grows more specific. Abram himself is included with his offspring, and their number is compared with the dust of the earth. Sarai still has no child at this point, so the words ask Abram to trust a future he cannot produce or yet see. He is told to walk through the land, then moves to Hebron and builds another altar.

The word translated forever is עוֹלָם, ʿōlām (oh-LAHM). Brown–Driver–Briggs explains its uses for long duration and enduring existence, including God's covenants and promises. The word does not supply a fixed number of years. Here it stresses the lasting character of what God promises; the wider question of fulfillment involves the rest of Scripture as well.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians agree that God promises Canaan to Abram and his offspring, while explaining the promise's ultimate reach in different ways. Where do readings differ? Some expect a future restoration of Israel to its land when Christ returns. Dallas Theological Seminary's doctrinal statement, Article XX, states that expectation as part of Christ's coming earthly reign.

Reformed teaching often emphasizes how the earthly gift points beyond itself to the lasting inheritance in Christ. The Westminster Confession, 7.5–6, places the Old Testament promises within the same covenant of grace fulfilled under Christ; the Orthodox Presbyterian Church's teaching guide The Covenant of Grace explains Abraham's earthly blessings alongside his hope for the heavenly city in Hebrews 11. For this reading, the promise looks beyond possession of Canaan to lasting life with God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 11:13–16 explains how the patriarchs could live with promises not fully received in their lifetimes: they welcomed them from afar and sought a better, heavenly country. Abram's tent and altar belong to that life of waiting. Trust does not require him to possess everything immediately before he can worship.

The promise also becomes a source of hope for people beyond Abraham's physical descendants. Romans 4:13–16 connects the inheritance with faith and grace, and Galatians 3:26–29 says that those who belong to Christ are Abraham's offspring and heirs according to the promise. The family God is gathering is larger than Abram could count or see from the place where he stands.
