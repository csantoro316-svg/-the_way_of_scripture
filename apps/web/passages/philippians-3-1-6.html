Passage

Philippians 3:1-6

Where should our confidence before God rest?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Finally, my brothers, rejoice in the Lord. It is no trouble for me to write the same things to you again, and it is a safeguard for you. 2 Watch out for those dogs, those workers of evil, those mutilators of the flesh! 3 For it is we who are the circumcision, we who worship by the Spirit of God, who glory in Christ Jesus, and who put no confidence in the flesh—

4 though I myself could have such confidence. If anyone else thinks he has grounds for confidence in the flesh, I have more: 5 circumcised on the eighth day, of the people of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin; a Hebrew of Hebrews; as to the law, a Pharisee; 6 as to zeal, persecuting the church; as to righteousness in the law, faultless.

What is the context?

Paul again calls the believers to rejoice in the Lord and says that repeating his instruction is a safeguard. He then warns sharply against teachers whose emphasis on circumcision would lead people to put confidence in outward standing.

His contrast names three marks of the community to which he belongs: worship by God’s Spirit, glorying in Christ Jesus and no confidence in the flesh. The Greek verb for worship is latreuō (lah-TREW-oh). Thayer’s lexicon includes religious service and worship, and connects this verse with service prompted by God’s Spirit.

Paul can speak about outward credentials from experience. He lists his circumcision, Israelite ancestry, tribe, Pharisaic commitment, zeal and record under the law. This is the background against which the next paragraph will describe what he has come to value above those advantages: knowing Christ.

The warning targets the misplaced confidence being taught. Paul’s own identification as an Israelite is part of his explanation, and the passage should be read through that contrast rather than as contempt for an entire people.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a warning against resting one’s confidence before God in ancestry, ritual standing or personal credentials. Wesley’s note on verse 3 and the USCCB notes on verses 2–11 both connect the identity of God’s people with life in Christ, worship by the Spirit and a confidence that comes from God.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Deuteronomy 30:1–10 speaks of Israel returning to the Lord and of God circumcising the hearts of His people so they will love Him. The concern for the heart is already present in the earlier Scriptures.

Romans 2:28–29 also connects circumcision of the heart with the Spirit. Galatians 3:26–29 describes people from different backgrounds belonging to Christ and sharing the promised inheritance through faith. These are supported connections with Paul’s emphasis here on what God’s Spirit does and the confidence found in Christ.

What is our position?

On verse 3, our study Worship as the Early Church Knew It understands worship by the Spirit as sacred service to God expressed in daily obedience. The study emphasizes a life led by His Spirit rather than reading this verse as an instruction about music. Its focus is the service of people whose confidence is in Christ.
