Passage

Galatians 1:8-9

Why must even a messenger be tested by the gospel?

Berean Standard Bible

8 But even if we or an angel from heaven should preach a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be under a curse! 9 As we have said before, so now I say again: If anyone is preaching to you a gospel contrary to the one you received, let him be under a curse!

What is the context?

These repeated warnings belong to Galatians 1:6–10. Paul is alarmed that the churches are turning from the One who called them by grace while people trouble them with a distorted message.

He includes himself and his fellow workers in the warning, then imagines even an angel from heaven bringing a contrary gospel. No messenger’s standing is allowed to change the message they already received.

The repetition shows how seriously he regards the danger. In the surrounding verses he connects this warning with his service to Christ and his responsibility to seek God’s approval.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as making fidelity to the gospel the measure of a messenger. Wesley’s notes on verses 8–9 and the USCCB notes on verses 6–10 place the strong warning within Paul’s response to the distortion troubling the churches.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 Corinthians 15:1–4 states the gospel Paul received and passed on: Christ died for our sins, was buried and was raised on the third day, according to the Scriptures.

That stated content gives the warning here a positive center. God’s saving work through His Son is the message being guarded, and its roots lie in what Scripture had promised.

What is our position?

Our study The Gospel According to Jesus, in its discussion of Galatians 1:8–9, treats the gospel as a settled proclamation whose core must be preserved. That core includes Christ’s death for our sins and His resurrection. His saving work supplies the foundation for the love and obedience that follow from it.
