Passage

Judges 7:15-25

How is the victory won with three hundred men?

Berean Standard Bible

15 When Gideon heard the dream and its interpretation, he bowed in worship. He returned to the camp of Israel and said, “Get up, for the LORD has delivered the camp of Midian into your hand.” 16 And he divided the three hundred men into three companies and gave each man a ram’s horn in one hand and a large jar in the other, containing a torch. 17 “Watch me and do as I do,” Gideon said. “When I come to the outskirts of the camp, do exactly as I do. 18 When I and all who are with me blow our horns, then you are also to blow your horns from all around the camp and shout, ‘For the LORD and for Gideon!’”

19 Gideon and the hundred men with him reached the outskirts of the camp at the beginning of the middle watch, just after the changing of the guard. They blew their horns and broke the jars that were in their hands. 20 The three companies blew their horns and shattered their jars. Holding the torches in their left hands and the horns in their right hands, they shouted, “A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!” 21 Each Israelite took his position around the camp, and the entire Midianite army fled, crying out as they ran. 22 And when the three hundred rams’ horns sounded, the LORD set all the men in the camp against one another with their swords. The army fled to Beth-shittah toward Zererah as far as the border of Abel-meholah near Tabbath.

23 Then the men of Israel were called out from Naphtali, Asher, and all Manasseh, and they pursued the Midianites. 24 Gideon sent messengers throughout the hill country of Ephraim to say, “Come down against the Midianites and seize the waters of the Jordan ahead of them as far as Beth-barah.” So all the men of Ephraim were called out, and they captured the waters of the Jordan as far as Beth-barah. 25 They also captured Oreb and Zeeb, the two princes of Midian; and they killed Oreb at the rock of Oreb and Zeeb at the winepress of Zeeb. So they pursued the Midianites and brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon on the other side of the Jordan.

What is the context?

After hearing the dream and its interpretation, Gideon bows in worship and returns ready to lead. He divides the three hundred into three companies, each man carrying a ram’s horn and a jar with a torch inside. They are to follow his signal and call out for the LORD and Gideon.

At the beginning of the middle watch, just after the changing of the guard, they blow the horns and break the jars. The torches become visible, and the men hold their positions around the camp. The enemy flees in panic. The account explicitly says that the LORD turns the men’s swords against one another; the decisive victory is credited to Him.

The action then broadens. Men from Naphtali, Asher, and Manasseh pursue, and Ephraim is called to seize the Jordan waters ahead of the fleeing Midianites. Oreb and Zeeb, two Midianite princes, are captured and killed. The first three hundred have a particular part in the rescue, and other Israelites join in carrying it forward.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley reads the horns, sudden lights, and surrounding groups as a plan that makes Gideon’s force appear much larger. He also sees confusion and darkness contributing to the enemy fighting itself. Those suggestions explain how the attack might work while keeping the account’s central claim that God brings the rout.

Henry draws a lesson from the later cooperation of the tribes: people should be willing to join and complete good work others have begun. He joins recognition of God’s victory with the value of the servants who participate in it.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Henry also notices a parallel between the light revealed from the jars and Paul’s picture in Second Corinthians 4:6–12. Paul describes God’s light making Christ known and the treasure carried in jars of clay, so that the extraordinary power is seen to come from God. This is a useful comparison of images, rather than a statement that Paul explicitly identifies Gideon’s jars as a prediction of gospel ministry.

The shared theme is God making His power known through fragile servants. Paul’s account includes pressure, persecution, and the life of Jesus being made visible in mortal people. The wider purpose goes beyond defeating one army: God brings the knowledge of Christ to others through those who depend on Him.
