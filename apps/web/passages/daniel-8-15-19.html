Passage

Daniel 8:15-19

Why does Daniel need Gabriel to explain the vision?

Berean Standard Bible

15 While I, Daniel, was watching the vision and trying to understand it, there stood before me one having the appearance of a man. 16 And I heard the voice of a man calling from between the banks of the Ulai: “Gabriel, explain the vision to this man.” 17 As he came near to where I stood, I was terrified and fell facedown. “Son of man,” he said to me, “understand that the vision concerns the time of the end.” 18 While he was speaking with me, I fell into a deep sleep, with my face to the ground. Then he touched me, helped me to my feet, 19 and said, “Behold, I will make known to you what will happen in the latter time of wrath, because it concerns the appointed time of the end.

What is the context?

As Daniel seeks understanding, a figure appears before him and a voice commands Gabriel to explain the vision. Daniel falls facedown in fear as Gabriel approaches.

Gabriel tells him that the vision concerns the time of the end. Daniel falls into a deep sleep, but the messenger touches him and helps him stand. He is to learn what will occur in the latter time of wrath, at the appointed end.

The explanation comes to a man who is overwhelmed by what he has seen. Daniel’s role is to receive and preserve what God makes known, including events that lie beyond his immediate experience.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley understands the appointed end here in relation to the end of Antiochus’s oppression. Gaebelein sees the language also pointing beyond Antiochus toward a final period of trouble before Christ’s return.

The difference concerns how far the vision reaches. Both readings recognize that the end is appointed by God rather than decided by the persecuting ruler.
