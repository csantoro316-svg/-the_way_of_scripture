Passage

Exodus 26:31-35

What did the inner veil separate?

Berean Standard Bible

31 Make a veil of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, and finely spun linen, with cherubim skillfully worked into it. 32 Hang it with gold hooks on four posts of acacia wood, overlaid with gold and standing on four silver bases. 33 And hang the veil from the clasps and place the ark of the Testimony behind the veil. So the veil will separate the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place.

34 Put the mercy seat on the ark of the Testimony in the Most Holy Place. 35 And place the table outside the veil on the north side of the tabernacle, and put the lampstand opposite the table, on the south side.

What is the context?

A veil made from the colored yarn and fine linen, worked with cherubim, divides the sanctuary into two rooms. Four gold-covered acacia posts on silver bases support it. The ark and mercy seat are placed behind it in the Most Holy Place.

The table remains outside the veil on the north side of the Holy Place, with the lampstand opposite it to the south. The separation orders the approach to God’s presence. Hebrews 9:6–7 describes the distinction: priests serve regularly in the first room, while the high priest alone enters the second on the yearly occasion, bringing sacrificial blood.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian commentators commonly understand the veil as marking the restricted approach under the first covenant and pointing toward the access Christ opens. Wesley, Calvin and Gill connect it with the New Testament’s account of Christ. Gill particularly emphasizes the connection with His body in Hebrews 10:20. Their further explanations of individual colors or materials go beyond that explicit connection.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 10:19–25 directly speaks of the new and living way into the Most Holy Place through Jesus’ blood and the curtain of His body. Believers are called to draw near in faith, hold to their hope and encourage one another in love and good deeds. The access Christ gives is meant to shape a faithful, caring community.

Matthew 27:45–54 reports the later temple’s veil being torn from top to bottom at Jesus’ death. It is the temple veil in that account, rather than the original wilderness fabric. Read alongside Hebrews, the scene brings the significance of the separation into focus: Christ’s saving work opens the way for people to approach God.

What is our position?

As explained in The True Church According to Jesus, Christ’s torn veil means that every believer now has direct access to God and a calling to minister. That existing conclusion guides our application here: access to God through Christ is shared by His people.
