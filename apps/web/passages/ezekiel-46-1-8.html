Passage

Ezekiel 46:1-8

When does the inner east gate open for worship?

Berean Standard Bible

1 “This is what the Lord GOD says: ‘The gate of the inner court that faces east must be kept shut during the six days of work, but on the Sabbath day and on the day of the New Moon it shall be opened. 2 The prince is to enter from the outside through the portico of the gateway and stand by the gatepost, while the priests sacrifice his burnt offerings and peace offerings. He is to bow in worship at the threshold of the gate and then depart, but the gate must not be shut until evening. 3 On the Sabbaths and New Moons the people of the land are also to bow in worship before the LORD at the entrance to that gateway.

4 The burnt offering that the prince presents to the LORD on the Sabbath day shall be six unblemished male lambs and an unblemished ram. 5 The grain offering with the ram shall be one ephah, and the grain offering with the lambs shall be as much as he is able, along with a hin of oil per ephah. 6 On the day of the New Moon he shall offer a young, unblemished bull, six lambs, and a ram without blemish. 7 He is to provide a grain offering of an ephah with the bull, an ephah with the ram, and as much as he is able with the lambs, along with a hin of oil per ephah. 8 When the prince enters, he shall go in through the portico of the gateway, and he shall go out the same way.

What is the context?

The east gate of the inner court remains shut through the six working days but opens on the Sabbath and New Moon. This is a different gate from the permanently closed outer east gate in chapter 44.

The prince stands at the gateway while the priests present his offerings. The people also bow before the Lord at its entrance. The passage distinguishes the prince, the priests and the people without separating them from the shared purpose of worship.

The prince provides the prescribed animals, grain and oil. With some offerings, the amount of grain is described according to what he is able to give. The gate remains open until evening on the appointed days.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Cambridge and the USCCB notes distinguish the prince’s place at the gateway from the priests’ service inside the court. The people worship from outside the inner court, looking through the opened gate.

The account concerns ordered approach and shared worship. It does not say that the worshippers are receiving different kinds of salvation because they stand in different places.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 4:1–11 develops the theme of God’s rest beyond a particular day on the calendar. It connects the seventh-day rest, Israel’s failure through unbelief, and the continuing invitation to hear God’s voice and enter His rest.

Ezekiel describes Sabbath worship at the sanctuary; Hebrews calls its hearers to a faithful response to God’s promise. This wider connection shows why rest in God cannot be reduced to attending a ceremony while refusing to trust and obey Him.
