Passage

Ephesians 6:10-17

How do we stand firm in the Lord’s strength?

Berean Standard Bible

10 Finally, be strong in the Lord and in His mighty power. 11 Put on the full armor of God, so that you can make your stand against the devil’s schemes. 12 For our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the powers of this world’s darkness, and against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms. 13 Therefore take up the full armor of God, so that when the day of evil comes, you will be able to stand your ground, and having done everything, to stand.

14 Stand firm then, with the belt of truth buckled around your waist, with the breastplate of righteousness arrayed, 15 and with your feet fitted with the readiness of the gospel of peace. 16 In addition to all this, take up the shield of faith, with which you can extinguish all the flaming arrows of the evil one. 17 And take the helmet of salvation and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God.

What is the context?

Paul closes his main instructions by directing believers to the Lord’s strength. He describes a struggle against the devil’s schemes and spiritual powers of evil, and tells them to take up God’s full armor so they can stand firm.

The armor gives a concrete picture of what God supplies: truth, righteousness, readiness associated with the gospel of peace, faith and salvation. Paul identifies the Spirit’s sword as the word of God. These belong together in the call to resist evil.

The statement that the struggle is not against flesh and blood matters. Paul directs the community’s attention to spiritual opposition rather than making other people the targets of its warfare.

The paragraph continues into prayer in verses 18–20. Remaining alert and praying for one another belong with taking up the armor. Paul then asks for prayer for his own gospel witness while he is in chains.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the armor as God’s provision for resisting spiritual evil through faith, truth, righteousness, salvation and His word. Wesley’s notes on verses 10–17 emphasize the need for His power and the completeness of the armor. The USCCB note likewise identifies spiritual opposition and connects the picture with Isaiah.

The imagery describes faithful resistance under the Lord’s strength. It gives no schedule of enemy movements or test for assigning a particular inconvenience to a demon. The text’s emphasis falls on what believers are to take up and the Lord in whom they are to stand.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Isaiah 59:15–20, the Lord acts when justice is absent. He wears righteousness as a breastplate and salvation as a helmet. Ephesians’ armor has a strong scriptural parallel: believers are told to stand in the Lord’s strength with qualities associated with His saving action.

Isaiah 11:1–5 describes the ruler from Jesse’s line with righteousness and faithfulness as His belt. Isaiah 52:7–10 connects the feet of the good-news messenger with peace, salvation and God’s reign. These parallels place the armor within the larger story of His faithful rule and rescue.

The details are not all transferred unchanged: Ephesians calls for the gospel of peace and identifies a spiritual struggle. The connection directs believers to God’s provision as they resist evil and bear witness to His good news.
