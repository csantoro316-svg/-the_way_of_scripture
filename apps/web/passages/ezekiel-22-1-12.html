Passage

Ezekiel 22:1-12

Why does God call Jerusalem a city of bloodshed?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying, 2 “As for you, son of man, will you judge her? Will you pass judgment on the city of bloodshed? Then confront her with all her abominations 3 and tell her that this is what the Lord GOD says: ‘O city who brings her own doom by shedding blood within her walls and making idols to defile herself, 4 you are guilty of the blood you have shed, and you are defiled by the idols you have made. You have brought your days to a close and have come to the end of your years. Therefore I have made you a reproach to the nations and a mockery to all the lands. 5 Those near and far will mock you, O infamous city, full of turmoil.

6 See how every prince of Israel within you has used his power to shed blood. 7 Father and mother are treated with contempt. Within your walls the foreign resident is exploited, the fatherless and the widow are oppressed. 8 You have despised My holy things and profaned My Sabbaths. 9 Among you are slanderous men bent on bloodshed; within you are those who eat on the mountain shrines and commit acts of indecency.

10 In you they have uncovered the nakedness of their fathers; in you they violate women during their menstrual impurity. 11 One man commits an abomination with his neighbor’s wife; another wickedly defiles his daughter-in-law; and yet another violates his sister, his own father’s daughter. 12 In you they take bribes to shed blood. You engage in usury, take excess interest, and extort your neighbors. But Me you have forgotten, declares the Lord GOD.

What is the context?

The Lord directs Ezekiel to confront Jerusalem with its abominations. Bloodshed and idolatry stand at the head of the charge: the city is bringing its own doom near through what it does.

The accusation becomes concrete. Princes use power to shed blood, parents are treated with contempt, and foreign residents, orphans and widows are oppressed. Holy things and Sabbaths are despised, while slander contributes to violence.

Sexual wrongdoing includes adultery and violations within the family. The reference to a father’s nakedness belongs to the language used in Leviticus 18:6–18, which includes dishonoring a father through relations with his wife.

The list ends with bribes for bloodshed, abusive lending and extortion. The Lord names what lies beneath these practices: the people have forgotten Him. The accusation joins worship, family life, public power and treatment of the vulnerable.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley understand the city’s guilt through the actual wrongs listed, including murder, idolatry and oppression. Henry emphasizes forgetting God as the root of the conduct.

The USCCB notes likewise connect bloodshed, oppression of the poor and corrupt worship. These are not separate problems that can be balanced against one another; the charge concerns a life and community that dishonor the Lord in all these ways.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

James 1:26–27 connects faithful religion with control of the tongue, care for orphans and widows, and freedom from the world’s pollution. James 2:14–17 asks what good a claim to faith does when someone withholds the food and clothing a brother or sister needs.

James does not explicitly quote Ezekiel 22. He develops the larger concern that a claimed relationship with God must shape the treatment of others. The care God desires has visible consequences for people who can easily be ignored or exploited.
