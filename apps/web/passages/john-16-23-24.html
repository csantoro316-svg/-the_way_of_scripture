Passage

John 16:23-24

What kind of asking belongs to the joy Jesus promises?

Berean Standard Bible

23 In that day you will no longer ask Me anything. Truly, truly, I tell you, whatever you ask the Father in My name, He will give you. 24 Until now you have not asked for anything in My name. Ask and you will receive, so that your joy may be complete.

What is the context?

These words follow Jesus’ promise that the disciples’ sorrow will turn to joy when they see Him again. Their questions about His departure belong to an understanding that is still incomplete. He points them toward life after His death and resurrection.

He directs them to ask the Father in His name. The promise continues what He has already taught about prayer in John 14:12–14 and remaining in Him in John 15:7. Asking belongs within their relationship with Him and the work He is giving them.

The complete joy is part of this same conversation about renewed life with Jesus. It should be read beside His promise to see them again, rather than separated into a guarantee that any desired possession or outcome will produce lasting joy.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly connect prayer in Jesus’ name with trust in Him and access to the Father through Him. The Reformed Westminster Larger Catechism, question 180, distinguishes that confidence from bare mention of His name. The Catholic Church’s Catechism, paragraphs 2614–2615, places this prayer within faith, love and life in the Spirit.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John 20:19–22 connects the disciples’ renewed joy with the risen Jesus, His peace and His sending of them. Ephesians 2:18 describes access to the Father through Christ by one Spirit. The prayer promise belongs to this life of being brought near to God.

What is our position?

Our study Prayer and Faith understands asking in Jesus’ name as asking in keeping with His character, authority and purposes. It reads the promise of complete joy within Jesus’ teaching about His resurrection, the Spirit’s coming and life with Him. The promise directs us toward that life, rather than making His name a formula for obtaining whatever we already wanted.
