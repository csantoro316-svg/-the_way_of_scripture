Passage

Matthew 20:29-34

The Son of David Gives Sight and Mercy

Berean Standard Bible

29 As they were leaving Jericho, a large crowd followed Him. 30 And there were two blind men sitting beside the road. When they heard that Jesus was passing by, they cried out, “Lord, Son of David, have mercy on us!” 31 The crowd admonished them to be silent, but they cried out all the louder, “Lord, Son of David, have mercy on us!”

32 Jesus stopped and called them. “What do you want Me to do for you?” He asked. 33 “Lord,” they answered, “let our eyes be opened.” 34 Moved with compassion, Jesus touched their eyes, and at once they received their sight and followed Him.

What is the context?

As Jesus and the crowd leave Jericho, two blind men beside the road hear that He is passing. They call to Him as Lord and Son of David, asking for mercy. The crowd tells them to be quiet, but they cry out even more loudly.

Jesus stops and asks what they want Him to do. They ask for their eyes to be opened. Moved with compassion, He touches their eyes; they receive sight at once and follow Him. The scene shows the service He has just taught: He gives His attention to people the crowd tries to silence.

Mark 10:46–52 gives an account of a blind beggar named Bartimaeus calling for mercy near Jericho and following Jesus after receiving sight. Matthew tells the scene with two men. The title Son of David connects their appeal with the royal line introduced in Matthew 1:1, just before Jesus' entry into Jerusalem in the next chapter.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a display of Jesus' mercy and a call to turn to Him with trust. Wesley on 20:30–31 emphasizes the men's persistent appeal despite the crowd's opposition; receiving sight leads them to follow Jesus.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The gift of sight belongs within the promised work of God's salvation. Isaiah 35:4–6 describes God coming to save and blind eyes being opened. When John the Baptist's messengers ask whether Jesus is the One to come, Jesus points them to what they have seen: blind people receiving sight and the good news reaching the poor (Luke 7:18–23).

The mercy beside the road is therefore part of a larger picture. The Son of David brings help to those calling on Him as He travels toward Jerusalem and the self-giving death He has just announced. Those whose eyes He opens join Him on the road.
