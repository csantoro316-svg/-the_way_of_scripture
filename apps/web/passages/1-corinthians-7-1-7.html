Passage

1 Corinthians 7:1-7

How should married believers care for one another?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Now for the matters you wrote about: It is good to abstain from sexual relations. 2 But because there is so much sexual immorality, each man should have his own wife, and each woman her own husband. 3 The husband should fulfill his marital duty to his wife, and likewise the wife to her husband. 4 The wife does not have authority over her own body, but the husband. Likewise the husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife.

5 Do not deprive each other, except by mutual consent and for a time, so you may devote yourselves to prayer. Then come together again, so that Satan will not tempt you through your lack of self-control. 6 I say this as a concession, not as a command. 7 I wish that all men were as I am. But each man has his own gift from God; one has this gift, another has that.

What is the context?

Paul now answers matters the Corinthians wrote about. He begins with abstaining from sexual relations, then explains the place of marriage and the responsibilities of both spouses. He does not treat intimacy within marriage as something unclean that a more spiritual couple must abandon.

The duties are reciprocal: the husband owes care to his wife, and the wife to her husband. Paul speaks about each person’s body in relation to the other and then requires mutual consent for a temporary interruption of intimacy for prayer. His instructions direct both spouses away from acting only for themselves.

After a time set aside for prayer, they are to come together again. Paul recognizes temptation and the limits of self-control. He also distinguishes a concession from a command and acknowledges that people receive different gifts from God. His own unmarried way of life is not a pattern everyone can simply be required to copy.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as honoring marital intimacy and mutual responsibility, with temporary abstinence by agreement rather than as a requirement for every married couple. Wesley’s notes on verses 3–7 emphasize that married people should not treat living as though unmarried as a higher form of purity, and that the gift of remaining unmarried is not given to everyone.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 5:25–30 connects a husband’s care for his wife with Christ’s self-giving love and care for the church. The body of the other person is a reason for faithful care, not simply a means of personal satisfaction.

That larger picture gives the practical instructions here a purpose beyond avoiding temptation. Life together should reflect the love of the Christ to whom both spouses belong. Attention to a spouse’s needs, shared decisions and prayer can become expressions of serving Him.
