Passage

1 Corinthians 7:25-28

Why does Paul advise remaining as they are?

Berean Standard Bible

25 Now about virgins, I have no command from the Lord, but I give a judgment as one who by the Lord’s mercy is trustworthy. 26 Because of the present crisis, I think it is good for a man to remain as he is. 27 Are you committed to a wife? Do not seek to be released. Are you free of commitment? Do not look for a wife. 28 But if you do marry, you have not sinned. And if a virgin marries, she has not sinned. But those who marry will face troubles in this life, and I want to spare you this.

What is the context?

Paul turns to the unmarried and gives his judgment as someone made trustworthy by the Lord’s mercy. He does not present a direct command Jesus had given about this situation. He offers counsel in view of the present crisis and the troubles marriage can bring.

The advice fits his recurring call not to assume that faith requires a change of outward condition. Those committed to a wife should not seek release, and those free of commitment should consider remaining that way. Yet he explicitly says that marrying is not a sin.

Paul is trying to spare his hearers added difficulty. Paul does not name the crisis in these verses. His counsel takes real pressures into account while preserving the permission to marry.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly distinguish this pastoral recommendation from a universal prohibition of marriage. Wesley’s notes on verses 26–28 focus on persecution, while Calvin’s comment on verse 26 takes a broader view of pressures Christians face throughout life; both preserve the distinction between recommending singleness and declaring marriage sinful.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

2 Timothy 2:3–13 places disciplined service and hardship within the hope of life and reign with the risen Christ. Its pictures of a soldier, athlete and farmer direct attention to faithful service and what lies ahead.

This is a parallel in purpose, rather than a command that all servants must have the same household arrangements. Paul’s practical advice belongs within a life directed toward Christ’s purposes and the hope He gives. Decisions about marriage can be considered honestly in that setting without treating marriage itself as a failure of devotion.
