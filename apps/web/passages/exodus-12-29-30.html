Passage

Exodus 12:29-30

What happened when the final warning was fulfilled?

Berean Standard Bible

29 Now at midnight the LORD struck down every firstborn male in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh, who sat on his throne, to the firstborn of the prisoner in the dungeon, as well as all the firstborn among the livestock. 30 During the night Pharaoh got up—he and all his officials and all the Egyptians—and there was loud wailing in Egypt; for there was no house without someone dead.

What is the context?

At midnight, the final judgment occurs as announced. The firstborn males die, from Pharaoh’s household to the household of the prisoner, and among the livestock. The narrative carries the warning of chapter 11 into its fulfillment.

Pharaoh, his officials and the Egyptians rise during the night to a great outcry. The repeated attention to households makes the grief concrete. The next verses describe the release that follows this terrible night.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared reading understands the deaths as the fulfillment of God’s final warning and the judgment that brings Israel’s release. Calvin emphasizes the announced timing and the reach from the royal household to the prisoner; Wesley places the event at the turning point from bondage to departure.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Hebrews 11:28 recalls this same judgment when it describes the firstborn being spared through Passover and the application of blood by faith. The deaths and the protection of Israel’s homes form the setting of the rescue.

The larger question of rescue from judgment continues in Hebrews 9:27–28. There, human death and judgment are set alongside Christ’s once-for-all offering to bear sins and His promised return to bring salvation. The Exodus is a particular historical deliverance; the New Testament takes the need for deliverance into the larger setting of sin, judgment and salvation through Christ.
