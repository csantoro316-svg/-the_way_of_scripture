Passage

1 Corinthians 6:12-17

Why does freedom not make the body unimportant?

Berean Standard Bible

12 “Everything is permissible for me,” but not everything is beneficial. “Everything is permissible for me,” but I will not be mastered by anything. 13 “Food for the stomach and the stomach for food,” but God will destroy them both. The body is not intended for sexual immorality, but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body. 14 By His power God raised the Lord from the dead, and He will raise us also.

15 Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ? Shall I then take the members of Christ and unite them with a prostitute? Never! 16 Or don’t you know that he who unites himself with a prostitute is one with her in body? For it is said, “The two will become one flesh.” 17 But he who unites himself with the Lord is one with Him in spirit.

What is the context?

Paul qualifies the claim that everything is permissible: not everything is beneficial, and he will not be mastered by anything. Freedom cannot simply mean giving every appetite control. The comparison between food and the stomach does not turn sexual behavior into a matter of indifference.

The body belongs to the Lord, and God’s raising of Jesus points toward believers’ own resurrection. What happens in bodily life therefore matters to Him. The body is not a disposable part of a person whose choices have no bearing on belonging to Christ.

Paul applies that belonging to sex with a prostitute. He draws on Genesis 2:24 to describe bodily union, then contrasts it with union with the Lord. Taking members of Christ into such a relationship contradicts the relationship they already have with Him. The text says prostitute without identifying a particular temple or religious ceremony.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as rejecting the use of freedom to excuse sexual immorality and affirming the body’s importance to the Lord. Calvin’s comment on verse 12 recognizes an appeal to liberty being misused, while Wesley’s note on verse 13 connects the body’s purpose with the Lord who will raise it.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 5:25–32 also draws on Genesis 2:24, there connecting the love and care of husband and wife with Christ’s love for the church. It explicitly relates the one-flesh picture to Christ and His people.

That connection adds a positive purpose beyond the warning against prostitution. Bodily relationships can express faithful love and care; they are not merely outlets for appetite. The church belongs to the Christ who gave Himself for her and works for her holiness.
