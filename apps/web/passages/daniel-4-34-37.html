Passage

Daniel 4:34-37

What does the restored king finally acknowledge?

Berean Standard Bible

34 But at the end of those days I, Nebuchadnezzar, looked up to heaven, and my sanity was restored to me. Then I praised the Most High, and I honored and glorified Him who lives forever: “For His dominion is an everlasting dominion, and His kingdom endures from generation to generation. 35 All the peoples of the earth are counted as nothing, and He does as He pleases with the army of heaven and the peoples of the earth. There is no one who can restrain His hand or say to Him, ‘What have You done?’”

36 At the same time my sanity was restored, my honor and splendor returned to me for the glory of my kingdom. My advisers and nobles sought me out, and I was restored to my throne, and surpassing greatness was added to me. 37 Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise and exalt and glorify the King of heaven, for all His works are true and all His ways are just. And He is able to humble those who walk in pride.

What is the context?

At the end of the appointed period, Nebuchadnezzar looks toward heaven and his sanity returns. He praises the Most High whose dominion is everlasting and whose rule cannot be restrained by human power.

His advisers and nobles seek him out, and his royal honor is restored. The remaining stump in the dream has its explanation in his return to the throne.

The closing statement answers his earlier boast. He now praises the King of heaven, calls His works true and His ways just, and acknowledges that He can humble those who walk in pride. The king’s own testimony has moved from self-glory to honoring God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley see the closing praise as evidence of real repentance and a changed heart. Cambridge emphasizes the acknowledgment of God’s sovereignty and the king’s public gratitude after restoration.

The restoration matters, but it is the acknowledgment of the King of heaven that gives the ending its meaning.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Philippians 2:3–11 brings humility and exaltation into the revelation of Christ. Jesus willingly takes the servant’s place and obeys to the point of death; God exalts Him, and every knee will bow and every tongue confess Him as Lord, to the Father’s glory.

There is an important difference: Christ does not need to be cured of pride as Nebuchadnezzar does. His willing humility shows the character of the true Lord, while His exaltation brings all people to acknowledge Him. The larger purpose reaches beyond one ruler learning a lesson to creation honoring Christ.
