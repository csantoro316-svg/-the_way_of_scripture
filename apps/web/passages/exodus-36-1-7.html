Passage

Exodus 36:1-7

Why does Moses stop the people from giving?

Berean Standard Bible

1 “So Bezalel, Oholiab, and every skilled person are to carry out everything commanded by the LORD, who has given them skill and ability to know how to perform all the work of constructing the sanctuary.” 2 Then Moses summoned Bezalel, Oholiab, and every skilled person whom the LORD had gifted—everyone whose heart stirred him to come and do the work. 3 They received from Moses all the contributions that the Israelites had brought to carry out the service of constructing the sanctuary. Meanwhile, the people continued to bring freewill offerings morning after morning,

4 so that all the skilled craftsmen who were doing all the work on the sanctuary left their work 5 and said to Moses, “The people are bringing more than enough for doing the work the LORD has commanded us to do.” 6 After Moses had given an order, they sent a proclamation throughout the camp: “No man or woman should make anything else as an offering for the sanctuary.” So the people were restrained from bringing more, 7 since what they already had was more than enough to perform all the work.

What is the context?

The appointed craftsmen begin the work with the skill God has given them. Moses calls those who have both the ability and the willingness to serve, and hands over the materials the people have brought. Contributions continue to arrive each morning even after construction begins.

Then the craftsmen leave their work to report that they have more than enough. Moses responds with a public order: people are to stop making further contributions for the sanctuary. The collection has a purpose, and that purpose determines when enough has been received. The materials already supplied are sufficient for the work.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin sees both prudence and honesty here: Moses distributes the materials, the workers begin their task, and they report the surplus instead of quietly accepting more. Wesley also emphasizes the combination of ability and willingness in those called to the work.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The New Testament connects giving with meeting needs and handling gifts faithfully. In 2 Corinthians 8:10–24, Paul asks believers to complete a willing gift according to their means, directs surplus toward need, and takes care that the collection is administered honorably before God and people. Generosity and accountability belong together.

The greater goal is what God does among His people. Paul’s prayer in Ephesians 3:14–21 asks for strength through the Spirit, Christ dwelling in their hearts, and a deeper knowledge of His love. God’s ability to do more than we ask serves His glory in the church; the completed material collection is part of service to Him, rather than its final purpose.
