Passage

Ephesians 4:7-10

Why does the risen Christ give gifts to His people?

Berean Standard Bible

7 Now to each one of us grace has been given according to the measure of the gift of Christ. 8 This is why it says: “When He ascended on high, He led captives away, and gave gifts to men.”

9 What does “He ascended” mean, except that He also descended to the lower parts of the earth? 10 He who descended is the very One who ascended above all the heavens, in order to fill all things.

What is the context?

After describing what all believers share, Paul turns to the grace given to each person according to Christ’s gift. The unity of the body includes His generosity toward its members.

Paul brings in the victory picture of Psalm 68:18: ascending on high and leading captives away. The psalm speaks of receiving gifts, while Ephesians emphasizes Christ giving gifts to people. Paul applies the victory language to the One who supplies His church.

He then pauses over the word ascended. The One who ascended is also the One who descended to the lower parts of the earth. Paul identifies the same Christ in both movements and describes Him as ascending above all the heavens to fill all things.

The next paragraph will name people Christ gives to equip His people and build His body. These verses introduce that service by directing attention to the authority and generosity of the risen Lord.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as connecting Christ’s victory and exaltation with the gifts He gives His church. Wesley’s notes on verses 7–10 and the USCCB notes on verses 1–10 both place the giving of gifts within that movement.

The descent is explained in different ways. The Reformed teacher John Calvin’s note on verse 9 understands the lower place as the earth compared with heaven: the Son came down into human life. The Catholic USCCB notes also consider His coming to earth the more likely meaning of this verse.

The Reformed Westminster Larger Catechism, question 50, explains the creed’s language of descent into hell as Christ’s burial and His remaining in the state of death until the third day. The Catholic Catechism, paragraph 631, connects these verses with His descent into the realm of the dead. Paragraphs 632–637 teach that He truly experienced death and freed the righteous who awaited Him, distinguishing this realm from the hell of final condemnation. These teachings and explanations give different accounts of the descent; the shared emphasis is Christ’s real death and triumphant life.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Acts 2:32–36, Peter connects Jesus’ resurrection and exaltation to the Father’s right hand with the promised Holy Spirit being poured out. The risen Christ is actively giving to His people.

Acts 2:42–47 shows the life that follows: devotion to the apostles’ teaching, fellowship, prayer, shared meals and practical care for those in need. The gift serves the formation of a people who learn, worship and care for one another together. This gives a concrete picture of the larger purpose behind Christ’s generosity.
