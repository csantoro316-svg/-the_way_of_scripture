Passage

Daniel 4:19-27

What does Daniel tell the king to change?

Berean Standard Bible

19 For a time, Daniel, who was also known as Belteshazzar, was perplexed, and his thoughts alarmed him. So the king said, “Belteshazzar, do not let the dream or its interpretation alarm you.” “My lord,” replied Belteshazzar, “may the dream apply to those who hate you, and its interpretation to your enemies! 20 The tree you saw that grew large and strong, whose top reached the sky and was visible to all the earth, 21 whose foliage was beautiful and whose fruit was abundant, providing food for all, under which the beasts of the field lived, and in whose branches the birds of the air nested— 22 you, O king, are that tree! For you have become great and strong; your greatness has grown to reach the sky, and your dominion extends to the ends of the earth.

23 And you, O king, saw a watcher, a holy one, coming down from heaven and saying: ‘Cut down the tree and destroy it, but leave the stump with its roots in the ground, with a band of iron and bronze around it, in the tender grass of the field. Let him be drenched with the dew of heaven, and graze with the beasts of the field till seven times pass him by.’ 24 This is the interpretation, O king, and this is the decree that the Most High has issued against my lord the king: 25 You will be driven away from mankind, and your dwelling will be with the beasts of the field. You will feed on grass like an ox and be drenched with the dew of heaven, and seven times shall pass you by, until you acknowledge that the Most High rules over the kingdom of mankind and gives it to whom He wishes. 26 As for the command to leave the stump of the tree with its roots, your kingdom will be restored to you as soon as you acknowledge that Heaven rules. 27 Therefore, may my advice be pleasing to you, O king. Break away from your sins by doing what is right, and from your iniquities by showing mercy to the oppressed. Perhaps there will be an extension of your prosperity.”

What is the context?

Daniel is alarmed by the meaning of the dream and wishes that it applied to the king’s enemies. He nevertheless tells him plainly: the great tree represents the king and his far-reaching dominion.

Nebuchadnezzar will be driven from human society to live and eat like an animal until he acknowledges the Most High’s rule. Leaving the stump means that his kingdom will be restored when he recognizes that Heaven rules.

Daniel ends with an appeal to act now: break away from sin by doing what is right and show mercy to the oppressed. His advice makes the response concrete. Acknowledging God should change the way the king treats those within his power.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry understands Daniel’s advice as repentance that includes both ceasing evil and doing good. Wesley emphasizes the tenderness and plainness of the warning, leaving room for mercy without promising that the king can control the outcome.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Luke 19:1–10 gives another picture of a wealthy person’s changed response. When Zacchaeus receives Jesus, he speaks of giving to the poor and restoring what he has taken dishonestly. Jesus announces salvation and His purpose of seeking and saving the lost.

The connection is repentance becoming visible in justice and mercy. God’s saving purpose reaches into the way people handle wealth and the lives of others. A changed relationship with Him has consequences for those a person could otherwise exploit or neglect.
