Passage

Isaiah 48:1-11

What good is a sacred name when the people will not receive God’s word?

Berean Standard Bible

1 “Listen to this, O house of Jacob, you who are called by the name of Israel, who have descended from the line of Judah, who swear by the name of the LORD, who invoke the God of Israel— but not in truth or righteousness— 2 who indeed call yourselves after the holy city and lean on the God of Israel; the LORD of Hosts is His name. 3 I foretold the former things long ago; they came out of My mouth and I proclaimed them. Suddenly I acted, and they came to pass. 4 For I knew that you are stubborn; your neck is iron and your forehead is bronze. 5 Therefore I declared it to you long ago; I announced it before it came to pass, so that you could not claim, ‘My idol has done this; my carved image and molten god has ordained it.’

6 You have heard these things; look at them all. Will you not acknowledge them? From now on I will tell you of new things, hidden things unknown to you. 7 They are created now, and not long ago; you have not heard of them before today. So you cannot claim, ‘I already knew them!’ 8 You have never heard; you have never understood; for a long time your ears have not been open. For I knew how deceitful you are; you have been called a rebel from birth.

9 For the sake of My name I will delay My wrath; for the sake of My praise I will restrain it, so that you will not be cut off. 10 See, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have tested you in the furnace of affliction. 11 For My own sake, My very own sake, I will act; for how can I let Myself be defamed? I will not yield My glory to another.

What is the context?

The Lord addresses people who carry Israel’s name, invoke His name and identify with the holy city, yet do not do so in truth or righteousness. Religious identity and language have become separated from faithful response.

He explains why He announced events before they happened: the people were stubborn enough to credit their idols instead. Now He announces new things they cannot claim to have known already. Their resistance is described as long-standing, not a brief failure of information.

Yet the Lord delays His anger for the sake of His name and praise, so that they are not cut off. He describes testing them in the furnace of affliction while refusing to yield His glory to another. Judgment, restraint and His purpose to make Himself known are held together.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley explain the opening as a warning against taking pride in religious descent, a holy place or the right name while failing to live faithfully. The Lord’s announced and fulfilled words leave no basis for giving an idol the credit.

For the furnace, Henry, Wesley and Gill emphasize testing that is accompanied by restraint. They explain the comparison with silver through the severity of a process that would otherwise consume the people.

These readings understand the purpose as exposing false confidence and bringing a people back to the Lord. The honor of His name remains the reason mercy is not exhausted by their failure.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The testing of faith is developed for believers in 1 Peter 1:3–9. Peter begins with new birth and living hope through Jesus’ resurrection, then speaks honestly about grief in various trials.

He compares proven faith with gold refined by fire and directs its outcome toward praise, glory and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ. The hope rests in God’s mercy and the coming salvation, rather than in the trial being pleasant.

This is a shared biblical theme, not a direct quotation or an explanation of the cause of every hardship. Testing belongs within the larger purpose of a people whose hope and trust are held by the God who saves them.
