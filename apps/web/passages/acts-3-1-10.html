Passage

Acts 3:1-10

What happens to the man at the Beautiful Gate?

Berean Standard Bible

1 One afternoon Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour. 2 And a man who was lame from birth was being carried to the temple gate called Beautiful, where he was put every day to beg from those entering the temple courts. 3 When he saw Peter and John about to enter, he asked them for money. 4 Peter looked directly at him, as did John. “Look at us!” said Peter. 5 So the man gave them his attention, expecting to receive something from them.

6 But Peter said, “Silver or gold I do not have, but what I have I give you: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, get up and walk!” 7 Taking him by the right hand, Peter helped him up, and at once the man’s feet and ankles were made strong. 8 He sprang to his feet and began to walk. Then he went with them into the temple courts, walking and leaping and praising God. 9 When all the people saw him walking and praising God, 10 they recognized him as the man who used to sit begging at the Beautiful Gate of the temple, and they were filled with wonder and amazement at what had happened to him.

What is the context?

Peter and John are going to the temple at the afternoon hour of prayer. A man unable to walk from birth is brought each day to the gate called Beautiful to ask for money. He is a familiar presence to the people entering the temple courts.

He asks the apostles for money, but Peter tells him that he has something different to give. In Jesus Christ’s name, he commands him to get up and walk. Peter takes his hand, his feet and ankles become strong, and he begins walking, leaping and praising God.

The crowd recognizes the man. Their amazement rests on the change in someone they know, rather than an anonymous story about a distant event. The next passage gives Peter’s explanation of whose power has brought the healing.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a real healing performed in Jesus’s name and a sign of His continuing saving power. The USCCB’s note on verses 6–10 connects it with the message Peter will proclaim. The account’s attention to the man’s lifelong condition, immediate strength and public recognition gives the sign its force.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The man’s leaping and praise recall Isaiah 35:4–6, where God’s coming to save is accompanied by opened eyes, hearing and the lame leaping. This is a parallel between the passages; Acts does not introduce the healing with an explicit quotation of Isaiah.

The sign points beyond the apostles to the risen Jesus and draws people toward hearing about Him. It gives a visible glimpse of restoration without turning this event into a promise that every person will receive the same healing on demand.
