Passage

Daniel 9:11-14

How does Daniel understand the disaster that came on Jerusalem?

Berean Standard Bible

11 All Israel has transgressed Your law and turned away, refusing to obey Your voice; so the oath and the curse written in the Law of Moses the servant of God has been poured out on us, because we have sinned against You. 12 You have carried out the words spoken against us and against our rulers by bringing upon us a great disaster. For under all of heaven, nothing has ever been done like what has been done to Jerusalem. 13 Just as it is written in the Law of Moses, all this disaster has come upon us, yet we have not sought the favor of the LORD our God by turning from our iniquities and giving attention to Your truth. 14 Therefore the LORD has kept the calamity in store and brought it upon us. For the LORD our God is righteous in all He does; yet we have not obeyed His voice.

What is the context?

Daniel connects the disaster with the oath and curse written in the Law of Moses. The people had turned away from God’s voice, and He had carried out the warnings given to them.

Leviticus 26:31–45 describes ruined cities, a desolate sanctuary, scattering among the nations and the land receiving its neglected rest. It also speaks of humbled hearts, confession and God remembering His covenant.

Daniel acknowledges that even after the disaster, the people have not turned from their iniquity or given attention to God’s truth. His confession therefore concerns the response still needed, as well as the sins that brought them into exile. God has remained righteous in all He has done.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley emphasizes God’s justice in bringing the announced judgment. Cambridge connects the covenant curse with the warnings in Moses’ law and understands attention to God’s truth as a changed response to His revealed will.
