Passage

Acts 21:18

Why does Luke mention James and all the elders together?

Berean Standard Bible

18 The next day Paul went in with us to see James, and all the elders were present.

What is the context?

Paul has arrived in Jerusalem and received a warm welcome. The next day he meets James, and all the elders are present. He reports what God has done among the Gentiles, and the gathered leaders glorify God before discussing the concerns about his teaching.

The verse names James without removing the elders from the scene. The advice that follows is presented within this gathered leadership. Reading only James’s name and leaving out the rest of the verse would give an incomplete picture of the meeting.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly recognize James’s prominence in Jerusalem and the presence of a group of elders. Traditions differ over how this relates to later church offices. The Catholic Catechism 1562–1567 describes bishops with priests as their coworkers. The Reformed Baptist 1689 Confession 26:8–9 describes bishops or elders as the same local office. These wider accounts of leadership shape how readers understand James’s standing; this single verse does not describe every later arrangement.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Paul’s report brings the Gentile mission before the Jerusalem believers, who respond by glorifying God. Romans 15:7–9 connects welcome between believers with God’s promises to Israel and mercy shown among the Gentiles. Their shared praise belongs to that larger concern for one people welcoming those God has received.

What is our position?

Who Shepherds the Church? directly discusses Acts 21:18 alongside the earlier Jerusalem meeting. Its position is that James has recognized personal standing while the elders continue to function together. Paul visits James with all the elders present; the study does not read this as a scene in which one man has replaced their shared responsibility.
