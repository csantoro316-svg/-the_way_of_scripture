Passage

2 Corinthians 10:12-18

Whose approval gives ministry its worth?

Berean Standard Bible

12 We do not dare to classify or compare ourselves with some who commend themselves. When they measure themselves by themselves and compare themselves with themselves, they show their ignorance. 13 We, however, will not boast beyond our limits, but only within the field of influence that God has assigned to us—a field that reaches even to you. 14 We are not overstepping our bounds, as if we had not come to you. Indeed, we were the first to reach you with the gospel of Christ.

15 Neither do we boast beyond our limits in the labors of others. But we hope that as your faith increases, our area of influence among you will greatly increase as well, 16 so that we can preach the gospel in the regions beyond you. Then we will not be boasting in the work already done in another man’s territory.

17 Rather, “Let him who boasts boast in the Lord.” 18 For it is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends.

What is the context?

Paul challenges a circle of self-approval: people who measure themselves against one another and commend themselves by their own standards. He explains his ministry instead in terms of the field God assigned him.

That field includes Corinth. Paul and his fellow workers really did reach the Corinthians with the gospel. Speaking about that work recognizes what happened among them and the responsibility God gave him.

His hope reaches further. As the Corinthians’ faith grows, he hopes his work can expand through them into regions beyond. He wants to bring the gospel to more people while respecting work already done by others.

Paul closes with the call to boast in the Lord. It echoes Jeremiah 9:23–24, where wisdom, strength and wealth give way to knowing the Lord who acts with loving devotion, justice and righteousness. The final measure is the Lord’s approval.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as a rejection of self-serving comparison and a recognition of work God has actually entrusted to someone. Wesley’s notes on verses 12–16 emphasize the assigned field and further gospel work; the USCCB notes on verses 12–18 distinguish self-commendation from boasting in what the Lord has accomplished.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 15:20–21 describes Paul’s ambition to preach where Christ has not yet been named, so people who have not heard can understand. That stated aim gives a wider setting for his hope of reaching regions beyond Corinth.

The growth of an existing congregation can help the message reach others. Paul’s defense of his work points forward to that continuing mission, with the Lord receiving the credit for what He brings about.
