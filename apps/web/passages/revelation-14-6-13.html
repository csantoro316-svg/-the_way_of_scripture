Passage

Revelation 14:6-13

What do the three angels announce, and what hope do the faithful receive?

Berean Standard Bible

6 Then I saw another angel flying overhead, with the eternal gospel to proclaim to those who dwell on the earth—to every nation and tribe and tongue and people. 7 And he said in a loud voice, “Fear God and give Him glory, because the hour of His judgment has come. Worship the One who made the heavens and the earth and the sea and the springs of waters.”

8 Then a second angel followed, saying, “Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great, who has made all the nations drink the wine of the passion of her immorality.”

9 And a third angel followed them, calling out in a loud voice, “If anyone worships the beast and its image and receives its mark on his forehead or on his hand, 10 he too will drink the wine of God’s anger, poured undiluted into the cup of His wrath. And he will be tormented in fire and sulfur in the presence of the holy angels and of the Lamb. 11 And the smoke of their torment rises forever and ever. Day and night there is no rest for those who worship the beast and its image, or for anyone who receives the mark of its name.”

12 Here is a call for the perseverance of the saints, who keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus. 13 And I heard a voice from heaven telling me to write, “Blessed are the dead—those who die in the Lord from this moment on.” “Yes,” says the Spirit, “they will rest from their labors, for their deeds will follow them.”

What is the context?

Three angels announce messages that answer the beast’s demand for worship. The first carries the eternal gospel to every nation, tribe, language and people. He calls them to fear God, give Him glory and worship the Creator, because the hour of His judgment has come.

The second announces the fall of Babylon, which has drawn the nations into its unfaithfulness. The third warns those who worship the beast, its image and receive its mark. The wine of Babylon’s immorality is answered by the wine of God’s anger. The warning describes fire, sulfur, rising smoke and the absence of rest.

The call to the saints is perseverance: keeping God’s commandments and the faith of Jesus. The passage then gives a blessing to those who die in the Lord. The Spirit affirms that they will rest from their labors and that their deeds follow them.

The contrast is between the worship demanded by the beast and faithfulness to God, including faithfulness through death. The warning is severe, while the promise of rest assures suffering believers that their labor is not forgotten.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Readers differ over the angels and their setting. Wesley understands them as messengers of God whose work is carried out through human proclamation. The USCCB notes present the announcements as impending judgment on the pagan world and identify Babylon with Rome. In Walvoord’s future reading, the announcements lead toward the defeat of the beast and its kingdom.

The duration and nature of the punishment are a substantial difference among Christians. The Catholic Church affirms the eternity of hell in Catechism 1035. The Reformed Westminster Confession 33.2 teaches eternal torment. Wesley reads this passage’s torment as continuing forever.

The Seventh-day Adventist Church instead teaches final destruction of the wicked in its official belief on the end of sin. Adventist writer Samuele Bacchiocchi understands the rising smoke in Revelation 14:11 as evidence of destruction with a permanent result. That position affirms final judgment while differing from the reading of suffering that continues without end.

These explanations should be weighed alongside the passage’s own wording, including both the smoke rising forever and the absence of rest. The encouragement to the faithful remains clear: those who die in the Lord are blessed, and their labor has not been wasted.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The announcement about Babylon recalls Isaiah 21:9, where Babylon’s fall is announced and its idols are shattered. The cup of anger recalls Isaiah 51:17. Revelation draws on those earlier pictures of God bringing proud powers and unfaithfulness under judgment.

The call to worship the Creator reaches every people, answering the beast’s claim over every people in chapter 13. Alongside judgment, the promise of rest gives a reason to remain faithful. God’s larger purpose includes the defeat of powers that demand false worship and the recognition of those who belong to Him, even when their service has cost them their lives.
