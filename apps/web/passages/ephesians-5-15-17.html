Passage

Ephesians 5:15-17

How can we make wise use of the time we have?

Berean Standard Bible

15 Pay careful attention, then, to how you walk, not as unwise but as wise, 16 redeeming the time, because the days are evil. 17 Therefore do not be foolish, but understand what the Lord’s will is.

What is the context?

These verses draw a practical conclusion from the call to live as children of light. Paul tells the readers to pay careful attention to their walk, using the contrast between wisdom and foolishness.

Redeeming the time means making good use of the opportunity before them in days marked by evil. Paul directs their attention to understanding the Lord’s will. The preceding instructions already show its moral direction: goodness, righteousness, truth and turning from darkness.

The next sentence contrasts drunkenness with being filled with the Spirit, followed by worship, thanksgiving and submission to one another. Wise living belongs within that shared life before God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read these verses as a call to thoughtful, faithful living and the use of present opportunities for good. Wesley’s notes on verses 15–17 emphasize using time well and attending to God’s will in the circumstances of life. The USCCB notes also connect the wording with the call to wise conduct in Colossians 4:5. No separate denominational breakdown is needed for that shared point.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 13:11–14 also connects awakening, the passing night and putting aside the works of darkness. There the reason includes the approaching completion of salvation.

This parallel adds a future horizon to the call to use the present well. Christian hope gives direction to ordinary choices now: believers are to live in keeping with the Lord they await.
