Passage

Revelation 22:16-17

Who is invited to receive the water of life?

Berean Standard Bible

16 “I, Jesus, have sent My angel to give you this testimony for the churches. I am the Root and the Offspring of David, the bright Morning Star.” 17 The Spirit and the bride say, “Come!” Let the one who hears say, “Come!” And let the one who is thirsty come, and the one who desires the water of life drink freely.

What is the context?

Jesus personally confirms that He sent His angel to give this testimony for the churches. He calls Himself the Root and Offspring of David and the bright Morning Star. The message ends with attention on His identity and on the communities receiving it.

The Spirit and the bride say Come, and the hearer is called to join them. The invitation then reaches the thirsty and anyone who desires the water of life. They may drink freely. After the visions of judgment and renewal, the book still speaks directly to those who hear its message.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The bride is understood as the church, as the USCCB notes explain. Wesley reads the first Come as a longing for Christ to fulfill the prophecy, followed by an invitation for the thirsty to receive His blessings. Walvoord emphasizes the open invitation to receive life. These emphases recognize both the hope for Christ and the gift offered to those who hear.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The Root and Offspring of David recalls the promised ruler from Jesse’s family in Isaiah 11:1–2. The star image also recalls the star and scepter arising from Israel in Numbers 24:17. The titles connect Jesus with the earlier promises of God’s appointed ruler.

The free water returns to Isaiah 55:1–3, where the thirsty are called to receive without money, and to the promise of life-giving water in John 4:10–14. The final invitation carries forward God’s purpose to give life through Christ.
