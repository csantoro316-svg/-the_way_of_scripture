Passage

Revelation 19:6-10

What does the marriage supper of the Lamb celebrate?

Berean Standard Bible

6 And I heard a sound like the roar of a great multitude, like the rushing of many waters, and like a mighty rumbling of thunder, crying out: “Hallelujah! For the Lord our God the Almighty reigns. 7 Let us rejoice and be glad and give Him the glory. For the marriage of the Lamb has come, and His bride has made herself ready. 8 She was given clothing of fine linen, bright and pure.” For the fine linen she wears is the righteous acts of the saints.

9 Then the angel told me to write, “Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb.” And he said to me, “These are the true words of God.” 10 So I fell at his feet to worship him. But he told me, “Do not do that! I am a fellow servant with you and your brothers who rely on the testimony of Jesus. Worship God! For the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy.”

What is the context?

The great multitude’s praise grows to the sound of rushing waters and thunder. God reigns, and the marriage of the Lamb has come. The bride has made herself ready and is given bright, pure linen. John explains the clothing as the righteous acts of the saints.

The scene holds together a gift and the faithful life of God’s people. The clothing is given to the bride, and the vision identifies what it represents. The image presents a prepared people celebrating their relationship with the Lamb.

An angel tells John to write a blessing on those invited to the marriage supper, assuring him that these are God’s true words. John then falls to worship the angel, who immediately refuses. He is a fellow servant, and God is the One to worship.

The closing statement connects the testimony of Jesus with the spirit of prophecy. The messenger directs attention toward God and the witness concerning Jesus, rather than toward himself. The next scene will reveal the rider named the Word of God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The bride is broadly understood as the church in relationship with Christ. The Catholic Catechism 796 uses the bride of the Lamb image for the church. The Reformed Westminster Confession 25.1 describes Christ’s church as His spouse and includes His people across the ages.

There is a difference over how the bride and guests relate. Wesley understands the bride as the whole company of faithful people belonging to Christ. Walvoord distinguishes the church of the present age as bride from Old Testament and tribulation believers as guests. That distinction belongs to his account of God’s people and is not a list of guest categories supplied in the verse.

The linen also receives different emphases. Wesley connects it with God making His people right with Him and holy in life. Walvoord emphasizes their righteous deeds. The BSB explicitly calls it the righteous acts of the saints and also says that the clothing was given.

The angel’s refusal of worship is clear across these readings. He is a servant, and the command directs worship to God. Interpretations of the wedding’s timing should be distinguished from that direct instruction and from the passage’s promise of blessing.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 5:25–32 explicitly connects marriage with Christ and the church. Christ loves her, gives Himself for her and prepares her to be holy and without blemish. Revelation’s prepared bride and pure clothing belong within that larger purpose of His self-giving love and the holiness of His people.

The blessing on the invited also recalls Jesus’ teaching about the kingdom as a great banquet in Luke 14:15–24. That parable includes an invitation reaching people who lack wealth or public standing. This is a scriptural parallel, rather than an exact seating plan for Revelation’s feast. The larger picture is fellowship with God made possible through Christ, received with readiness and faithfulness.

What is our position?

On the angel’s correction, our study Who is The Angel of the Lord? distinguishes ordinary angels, who refuse worship and redirect it to God. We understand this angel’s response in the same way: he is a fellow servant, and worship belongs to God.
