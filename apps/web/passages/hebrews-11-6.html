Passage

Hebrews 11:6

What does it mean to seek God in faith?

Berean Standard Bible

6 And without faith it is impossible to please God. For anyone who approaches Him must believe that He exists and that He rewards those who earnestly seek Him.

What is the context?

This verse explains why Enoch’s life pleased God and states a principle that runs through the chapter. Anyone approaching God must believe that He exists and that He rewards those who earnestly seek Him.

The word translated earnestly seek can describe seeking God and His favor. The verse joins belief with turning toward Him. It is more than a statement that God exists while He remains irrelevant to one’s life.

The surrounding examples keep the promise from becoming a guarantee of immediate comfort. Abel pleased God and was killed; Enoch pleased God and escaped death. Later in the chapter, some receive deliverance and others endure suffering while still awaiting what God promised.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin, Wesley and the USCCB note connect the verse with approaching the real God in trust, rather than treating belief as a detached opinion. Calvin stresses confidence in God’s goodness; Wesley explicitly applies the approach to prayer and worship. The shared emphasis is that seeking Him matters and is not empty.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Luke 18:1–8, Jesus teaches continued prayer, speaks of God bringing justice and asks whether the Son of Man will find faith when He comes. Trust in God and waiting for His action belong together.

Hebrews 11:35–40 shows why that waiting matters: some faithful people suffered and died without receiving the final promise in their lifetime. God’s response reaches beyond present circumstances into the completion He has prepared for His people.

What is our position?

Our study Prayer and Faith connects this verse with trusting and seeking God rather than finding the right wording for a request. It draws on Jesus crediting faith when people came to Him in different ways. Prayer is an expression of that trust, rather than a technique whose wording controls God’s response.
