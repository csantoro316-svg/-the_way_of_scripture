Passage

Exodus 8:16-19

What happened when the magicians could no longer imitate the sign?

Berean Standard Bible

16 Then the LORD said to Moses, “Tell Aaron, ‘Stretch out your staff and strike the dust of the earth, that it may turn into swarms of gnats throughout the land of Egypt.’” 17 This they did, and when Aaron stretched out his hand with his staff and struck the dust of the earth, gnats came upon man and beast. All the dust of the earth turned into gnats throughout the land of Egypt. 18 The magicians tried to produce gnats using their magic arts, but they could not. And the gnats remained on man and beast. 19 “This is the finger of God,” the magicians said to Pharaoh. But Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he would not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

What is the context?

At God’s command Aaron strikes the dust with his staff, and gnats come upon people and animals throughout Egypt. The Hebrew word translated gnats has also been understood as lice. Brown–Driver–Briggs records that uncertainty; the point of the account is an infestation the magicians cannot reproduce.

Their earlier imitations have now reached a limit. They tell Pharaoh that this is the finger of God, acknowledging a power beyond their own. Pharaoh still does not listen. Even the failure of the people he called to oppose Moses does not change his refusal.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared reading sees the magicians’ failure and admission as exposing the limits of their power. Calvin, Wesley and the Catholic USCCB notes all understand the finger of God as recognizing divine power at work beyond what they can match.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The expression finger of God returns in Luke 11:14–23. When Jesus is accused of driving out demons through their ruler, He says that if He does it by the finger of God, God’s kingdom has come upon His hearers. Matthew 12:28 describes that work as being by the Spirit of God.

The language provides a connection between the power recognized in Egypt and the power displayed through Jesus. In Exodus, the magicians admit that they have reached their limit; in the Gospels, Jesus’ victory over demons announces the arrival of God’s kingdom.
