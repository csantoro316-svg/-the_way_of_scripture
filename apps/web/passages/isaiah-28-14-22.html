Passage

Isaiah 28:14-22

What foundation can stand when a refuge of lies is swept away?

Berean Standard Bible

14 Therefore hear the word of the LORD, O scoffers who rule this people in Jerusalem. 15 For you said, “We have made a covenant with death; we have fashioned an agreement with Sheol. When the overwhelming scourge passes through it will not touch us, because we have made lies our refuge and falsehood our hiding place.” 16 So this is what the Lord GOD says: “See, I lay a stone in Zion, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone, a sure foundation; the one who believes will never be shaken.

17 I will make justice the measuring line and righteousness the level. Hail will sweep away your refuge of lies, and water will flood your hiding place. 18 Your covenant with death will be dissolved, and your agreement with Sheol will not stand. When the overwhelming scourge passes through, you will be trampled by it. 19 As often as it passes through, it will carry you away; it will sweep through morning after morning, by day and by night.” The understanding of this message will bring sheer terror.

20 Indeed, the bed is too short to stretch out on, and the blanket too small to wrap around you. 21 For the LORD will rise up as at Mount Perazim. He will rouse Himself as in the Valley of Gibeon, to do His work, His strange work, and to perform His task, His disturbing task. 22 So now, do not mock, or your shackles will become heavier. Indeed, I have heard from the Lord GOD of Hosts a decree of destruction against the whole land.

What is the context?

The warning now directly addresses the scoffing rulers in Jerusalem. Their claim to have an agreement with death and Sheol expresses confidence that the approaching disaster cannot reach them. The passage exposes their refuge as lies and falsehood.

Against that false security, the Lord lays a tested, precious cornerstone in Zion, a sure foundation for the one who believes. Justice and righteousness become the measuring line and level.

Their claimed protection will be swept away by hail and water. The agreement with death will not stand, and repeated disaster will turn understanding into terror. A bed too short and a blanket too small picture a refuge that cannot provide what it promises.

The Lord’s action is compared with His victories at Perazim and Gibeon, but now the warning is directed at His own people. The closing call is to stop mocking before their shackles become heavier.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill explain the covenant with death as a boast of safety, as though death had agreed to leave the rulers alone. Gill also discusses attempts to identify it with political agreements. These explanations distinguish the claim of security from a literal treaty signed with a person called Death.

Henry, Wesley and Gill identify the stone with the Messiah and the sure foundation He provides. They contrast trusting that foundation with relying on deception or a protection too small to shelter anyone.

For the Lord’s strange work, Wesley and Gill emphasize the reversal: the God who fought against Israel’s enemies now acts against wrongdoing among His own people. Their place in the covenant does not make the warning empty.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

First Peter 2:4–10 directly quotes Isaiah’s cornerstone promise and applies it to Christ. People who come to Him, the living stone, are themselves built into a spiritual house and holy priesthood.

Peter joins the promise of security for the believer with the warning about stumbling over the stone. He then describes a people called out of darkness to proclaim God’s virtues, a people who have received mercy.

The New Testament therefore makes the foundation personal: the lasting refuge is Christ. Those built upon Him are gathered into a people whose worship and witness show what His mercy has done.
