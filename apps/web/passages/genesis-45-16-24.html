Passage

Genesis 45:16-24

Pharaoh Invites Jacob to Egypt

Berean Standard Bible

16 When the news reached Pharaoh’s house that Joseph’s brothers had come, Pharaoh and his servants were pleased.

17 Pharaoh said to Joseph, “Tell your brothers, ‘Do as follows: Load your animals and return to the land of Canaan. 18 Then bring your father and your families and return to me. I will give you the best of the land of Egypt, and you shall eat from the fat of the land.’ 19 You are also directed to tell them: ‘Take wagons from the land of Egypt for your young children and your wives, and bring your father and come back. 20 But pay no regard to your belongings, for the best of all the land of Egypt is yours.’”

21 So the sons of Israel did as they were told. Joseph gave them wagons as Pharaoh had instructed, and he also gave them provisions for their journey. 22 He gave new garments to each of them, but to Benjamin he gave three hundred shekels of silver and five sets of clothes. 23 And he sent to his father the following: ten donkeys loaded with the best of Egypt, and ten female donkeys loaded with grain and bread and provisions for his father’s journey.

24 Then Joseph sent his brothers on their way, and as they were leaving, he said to them, “Do not quarrel on the way!”

What is the context?

Pharaoh and his servants are pleased to hear that Joseph's brothers have come. Pharaoh authorizes them to bring their father and families to Egypt, promises good land, and supplies wagons for the children, women, and Jacob. The invitation has the king's support as well as Joseph's affection.

They are told not to be anxious over leaving belongings behind because Egypt's best is available to them. Joseph provides wagons, food, and new garments. Benjamin receives additional clothing and three hundred shekels of silver. Donkeys loaded with Egypt's good things and provisions are sent for Jacob's journey.

The supplies make the invitation something the family can act on. A long move involving an elderly father, children, and possessions will require more than an encouraging message. The brothers return with transport and food already provided.

Joseph's parting instruction is not to quarrel on the way. The old wrong has now been exposed, and the family must carry the news home. Their restored relationship with Joseph calls for a different way of treating one another as they travel.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the gifts and wagons as practical provision for the family's move and Joseph's warning as a call to peace after forgiveness. Wesley's note on Genesis 45:24 applies the instruction to the brothers' need to stop blaming one another and live as a reconciled family.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Forgiveness received is meant to change relationships within the whole family. Joseph has welcomed the brothers together, and his warning directs them away from renewing their conflict on the journey. Mercy is to shape what happens after the emotional reunion.

The same wider theme appears in Ephesians 4:29–32: words should build others up, bitterness is to be put away, and believers are to forgive one another as God forgave them in Christ. Joseph's parting instruction gives that theme a concrete family setting. Reconciliation calls for a changed way of speaking as well as a willingness to meet again.
