Passage

Ezekiel 28:20-24

How will Sidon come to know the Lord’s holiness?

Berean Standard Bible

20 Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying, 21 “Son of man, set your face against Sidon and prophesy against her. 22 And you are to declare that this is what the Lord GOD says: ‘Behold, I am against you, O Sidon, and I will be glorified within you. They will know that I am the LORD when I execute judgments against her and demonstrate My holiness through her. 23 I will send a plague against her and shed blood in her streets; the slain will fall within her, while the sword is against her on every side. Then they will know that I am the LORD. 24 For the people of Israel will no longer face a pricking brier or a painful thorn from all around them who treat them with contempt. Then they will know that I am the Lord GOD.’

What is the context?

The Lord directs Ezekiel to speak against Sidon. He announces plague, bloodshed and the sword, saying that His judgments will demonstrate His holiness and make Him known.

The announcement ends by turning toward Israel’s experience among its neighbors. Their contempt has been like a pricking brier or painful thorn. The Lord promises an end to that hostility.

The purpose is repeated through the section: they will know that He is the Lord. The confrontation is not merely a contest between neighboring cities; it concerns His holiness and His people’s life among those who scorn them.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry connects Sidon’s hostility with its corrupting religious influence on Israel. Wesley explains the brier and thorn as the troublesome neighbors named in these judgments.

Wesley also says that the full removal of this threat had not yet been accomplished. The wording looks beyond an isolated military loss toward the security developed in the chapter’s closing promise.
