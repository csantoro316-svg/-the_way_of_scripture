Passage

1 Kings 14:1-6

Can Jeroboam’s disguise hide the truth from God?

Berean Standard Bible

1 At that time Abijah son of Jeroboam became ill, 2 and Jeroboam said to his wife, “Now get up, disguise yourself so they will not recognize you as my wife, and go to Shiloh. For Ahijah the prophet is there; it was he who spoke about my kingship over this people. 3 Take with you ten loaves of bread, some cakes, and a jar of honey, and go to him. He will tell you what will become of the boy.”

4 Jeroboam’s wife did as instructed; she arose and went to Shiloh and arrived at Ahijah’s house. Now Ahijah could not see, for his eyes were dim because of his age. 5 But the LORD had said to Ahijah, “Behold, the wife of Jeroboam is coming to ask you about her son, for he is ill. You are to say such and such to her, because when she arrives, she will be disguised.” 6 So when Ahijah heard the sound of her feet entering the door, he said, “Come in, wife of Jeroboam! Why are you disguised? For I have been sent to you with bad news.

What is the context?

Jeroboam’s son Abijah becomes ill. The king sends his wife to Ahijah, the prophet who announced his kingship, to ask what will happen to the boy. He tells her to disguise herself and take bread, cakes and honey.

Ahijah can no longer see because of his age, but the LORD tells him who is coming and gives him a message for her. When he hears her footsteps, he addresses her as Jeroboam’s wife and asks why she is disguised.

The contrast is striking: the prophet cannot see her, yet she is already known. The visit will bring a word about much more than the question the king has sent her to ask.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill see the disguise exposed as evidence that the couple cannot conceal themselves from God. The prophet’s knowledge depends on what the LORD reveals, rather than his eyesight.

Henry emphasizes the attempt to seek reassuring information without facing the need for change. The message refuses that restriction: Jeroboam’s conduct and the future of his household will be addressed.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

A related theme appears in Hebrews 4:12–16. God’s word reaches the thoughts and intentions of the heart, and nothing is hidden from His sight.

That passage also directs people to Jesus, the sympathetic and sinless high priest, and invites them to seek mercy and help. This is a wider connection, not an identification of Ahijah with Christ. Being fully known by God gives people reason to approach Him honestly and seek grace, rather than rely on concealment.
