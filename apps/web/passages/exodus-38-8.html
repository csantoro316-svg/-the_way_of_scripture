Passage

Exodus 38:8

Why does the account mention the women’s mirrors?

Berean Standard Bible

8 Next he made the bronze basin and its stand from the mirrors of the women who served at the entrance to the Tent of Meeting.

What is the context?

This verse adds a detail not given in the earlier basin instructions: the bronze basin and its stand are made from the mirrors of women serving at the entrance to the Tent of Meeting. Their contribution becomes part of the provision for the priests’ washing before service (Exodus 30:17–21).

The Hebrew verb behind serving is צָבָא, tsava (tsah-VAH). It can describe service at the sacred tent, and Brown–Driver–Briggs specifically includes the women in this verse among its examples. The account names their service and their gift without telling us precisely what duties they performed.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley understands the women as devoted participants who attended the place of worship, and sees their gift as worthy of honor. Gill instead reads the gathering as women arriving to bring their offerings. Both explanations recognize their contribution; the passage itself gives no detailed account of their duties or the process used to turn the mirrors into the basin.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The basin’s cleansing service belongs within a larger biblical concern for a people made ready to serve God. Ephesians 5:25–27 describes Christ giving Himself for the church and cleansing her through the word so that she may be holy and without blemish.

There is also a useful later illustration involving a mirror. James 1:19–27 compares hearing God’s word without acting on it to seeing a face in a mirror and immediately forgetting it. James is not explaining the basin’s construction, but his illustration directs our attention to the response God seeks: receiving His word and living it out. Cleansing and service involve the person, not only an outward object.
