Passage

Luke 9:37-45

Why does Jesus speak of being handed over after freeing the boy?

Berean Standard Bible

37 The next day, when they came down from the mountain, Jesus was met by a large crowd. 38 Suddenly a man in the crowd cried out, “Teacher, I beg You to look at my son, for he is my only child. 39 A spirit keeps seizing him, and he screams abruptly. It throws him into convulsions so that he foams at the mouth. It keeps mauling him and rarely departs from him. 40 I begged Your disciples to drive it out, but they were unable.”

41 “O unbelieving and perverse generation!” Jesus replied. “How long must I remain with you and put up with you? Bring your son here.” 42 Even while the boy was approaching, the demon slammed him to the ground in a convulsion. But Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, healed the boy, and gave him back to his father. 43 And they were all astonished at the greatness of God. While everyone was marveling at all that Jesus was doing, He said to His disciples,

44 “Let these words sink into your ears: The Son of Man is about to be delivered into the hands of men.” 45 But they did not understand this statement. It was veiled from them so that they could not comprehend it, and they were afraid to ask Him about it.

What is the context?

The day after the mountain scene, a father brings his only child’s distress before Jesus. An evil spirit has repeatedly seized and injured the boy, and the disciples have been unable to drive it out.

Jesus rebukes the unbelief around Him and has the boy brought forward. Even as the spirit throws the child down again, Jesus rebukes it, heals the boy and returns him to his father. The crowd is astonished at God’s greatness.

While they marvel, Jesus tells the disciples to pay close attention: the Son of Man is about to be delivered into human hands. They do not understand, the meaning remains veiled from them, and they are afraid to ask.

The two parts belong together in Luke’s account. The One who has just shown such authority also announces His coming surrender. The disciples still need to understand how His power and His path toward suffering belong within the same mission.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly recognize Jesus’ authority to restore the child and the disciples’ continuing need to understand His mission. Wesley highlights how the announcement of the cross is placed amid the crowd’s admiration, keeping the coming suffering in view alongside the display of power. (Wesley on Luke 9:44; Luke 9:42–45)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Colossians describes the cross itself as the place of Christ’s triumph over the powers and authorities. The release of the boy and the coming handing over of Jesus can therefore be read within the larger victory God accomplishes through Him. His suffering will not mean that evil has become the final ruler of the story. (Colossians 2:13–15)

After the resurrection, Jesus opens the disciples’ minds to understand the Scriptures and explains how His suffering and resurrection lead into the proclamation of forgiveness to the nations. Luke brings their present lack of understanding toward that later clarity and commission. (Luke 24:44–49)
