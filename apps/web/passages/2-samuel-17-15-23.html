Passage

2 Samuel 17:15-23

How does the warning reach David in time?

Berean Standard Bible

15 So Hushai told Zadok and Abiathar, the priests, “This is what Ahithophel has advised Absalom and the elders of Israel, and this is what I have advised. 16 Now send quickly and tell David, ‘Do not spend the night at the fords of the wilderness, but be sure to cross over. Otherwise the king and all the people with him will be swallowed up.’” 17 Now Jonathan and Ahimaaz were staying at En-rogel, where a servant girl would come and pass along information to them. They in turn would go and inform King David, for they dared not be seen entering the city. 18 But a young man did see them and told Absalom. So the two left quickly and came to the house of a man in Bahurim. He had a well in his courtyard, and they climbed down into it. 19 Then the man’s wife took a covering, spread it over the mouth of the well, and scattered grain over it so nobody would know a thing. 20 When Absalom’s servants came to the woman at the house, they asked, “Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?” “They have crossed over the brook,” she replied. The men searched but did not find them, so they returned to Jerusalem.

21 After the men had gone, Ahimaaz and Jonathan climbed up out of the well and went to inform King David, saying, “Get up and cross over the river at once, for Ahithophel has given this advice against you.” 22 So David and all the people with him got up and crossed the Jordan. By daybreak, there was no one left who had not crossed the Jordan. 23 When Ahithophel saw that his advice had not been followed, he saddled his donkey and set out for his house in his hometown. He put his affairs in order and hanged himself. So he died and was buried in his father’s tomb.

What is the context?

Hushai reports both plans to the priests and urges that David cross the river without delay. Jonathan and Ahimaaz receive information through a servant girl because they cannot safely appear in the city.

When a young man notices them and reports them to Absalom, they hide in a well at Bahurim. A woman covers the opening with a cloth and grain, then tells the searchers the men have crossed the brook. After the searchers leave, the messengers reach David.

David and everyone with him cross the Jordan before daybreak. Meanwhile Ahithophel, seeing that his advice has not been followed, returns home, puts his affairs in order and takes his own life. He is buried in his father’s tomb.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley understands the urgent warning as necessary because Absalom might still change course and pursue quickly. The accepted delay does not lead Hushai to assume that David can safely remain where he is.

Wesley also calls the woman’s answer to the searchers a lie. Her help preserves the messengers, but the account of their escape is not a blanket endorsement of every deception used in wartime.

Henry and Gill think Ahithophel recognized that Absalom’s cause was likely to fail and feared what would follow. Those are explanations of his response; the text itself connects his death with the rejection of his counsel without revealing every motive.
