Passage

Mark 4:21-25

The Lamp and the Way We Listen

Berean Standard Bible

21 Jesus also said to them, “Does anyone bring in a lamp to put it under a basket or under a bed? Doesn’t he set it on a stand? 22 For there is nothing hidden that will not be disclosed, and nothing concealed that will not be brought to light. 23 If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear.”

24 He went on to say, “Pay attention to what you hear. With the measure you use, it will be measured to you, and even more will be added to you. 25 For whoever has will be given more. But whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him.”

What is the context?

Jesus moves from seed to light. A lamp belongs on a stand, where it can give light, rather than beneath a basket or bed. He adds that what is hidden will be disclosed. Following His words about the kingdom's mystery, this points toward truth being brought into the open.

He again calls people to hear, then tells them to pay attention to what they hear. The measure saying places responsibility on the listener: what is received matters, and more can be given. The warning about losing even what one has makes careless hearing a serious concern.

Luke 8:16–18 places the same lamp and listening sayings after the soils and speaks of losing even what someone thinks he has. The concern is how people respond to what God makes known.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly connect the lamp with truth meant to be made known and the measure with responsible hearing. Wesley's notes on Mark 4:21–25 emphasize both sharing received light with others and allowing Jesus' teaching to shape one's life.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Matthew 10:26–27, Jesus joins a similar hidden-and-revealed saying with His followers' public witness: what they hear from Him is to be proclaimed openly. The private instruction of disciples serves a purpose larger than their private understanding.

2 Corinthians 4:1–7 likewise joins God's light in the heart with openly proclaiming Jesus Christ as Lord. The knowledge of God's glory comes through Christ and is carried by ordinary, fragile people. Light received from God becomes a message His people are entrusted to share.
