Passage

1 Kings 14:7-16

What does Ahijah announce about Jeroboam’s house and Israel?

Berean Standard Bible

7 Go, tell Jeroboam that this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: ‘I raised you up from among the people and appointed you ruler over My people Israel. 8 I tore the kingdom away from the house of David and gave it to you. But you have not been like My servant David, who kept My commandments and followed Me with all his heart, doing only what was right in My eyes. 9 You have done more evil than all who came before you. You have proceeded to make for yourself other gods and molten images to provoke Me, and you have flung Me behind your back. 10 Because of all this, behold, I am bringing disaster on the house of Jeroboam: I will cut off from Jeroboam every male, both slave and free, in Israel; I will burn up the house of Jeroboam as one burns up dung until it is gone! 11 Anyone belonging to Jeroboam who dies in the city will be eaten by dogs, and anyone who dies in the field will be eaten by the birds of the air.’ For the LORD has spoken.

12 As for you, get up and go home. When your feet enter the city, the child will die. 13 All Israel will mourn for him and bury him. For this is the only one belonging to Jeroboam who will receive a proper burial, because only in him has the LORD, the God of Israel, found any good in the house of Jeroboam. 14 Moreover, the LORD will raise up for Himself a king over Israel who will cut off the house of Jeroboam. This is the day—yes, even today! 15 For the LORD will strike Israel as a reed is shaken in the water. He will uproot Israel from this good land that He gave their fathers, and He will scatter them beyond the Euphrates, because they have made their Asherah poles, provoking the LORD to anger. 16 So He will give Israel over on account of the sins Jeroboam has committed and has caused Israel to commit.”

What is the context?

The message begins with what the LORD has done for Jeroboam: He raised him up and gave him rule over Israel. In return, Jeroboam has made images, led the people into other worship and cast God aside.

Judgment will fall on his royal house. His ill son will die when his mother returns, yet this boy is distinguished from the coming destruction: Israel will mourn and bury him because God has found good in him.

The warning then reaches beyond one family. Another king will end Jeroboam’s dynasty, and Israel will eventually be uprooted from the land and scattered beyond the Euphrates. The people share in the idolatry their king has encouraged.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry understands the boy’s death as removal from the evil coming on the household, with his burial showing the regard God has for him. Gill emphasizes the good disposition toward God that sets him apart within the family.

The text gives that distinction but does not answer every question about why he dies. Its own description prevents his death from being read as proof that he was the worst member of the household.

Wesley also emphasizes the wider responsibility of a ruler who draws others into sin. The judgment concerns both Jeroboam’s own choices and the destructive course he establishes for the people.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Two later passages show how the warning unfolds. 1 Kings 15:25–30 recounts Baasha killing Nadab and destroying Jeroboam’s household, expressly connecting the event with Ahijah’s word.

2 Kings 17:7–23 later explains Israel’s exile in terms of persistent idolatry, including the course introduced by Jeroboam. The larger story therefore joins a leader’s choices, the people’s response and the long consequences of refusing repeated calls to return to God.
