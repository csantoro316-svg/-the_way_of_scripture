Passage

Romans 12:3-8

How do different gifts serve one body?

Berean Standard Bible

3 For by the grace given me I say to every one of you: Do not think of yourself more highly than you ought, but think of yourself with sober judgment, according to the measure of faith God has given you. 4 Just as each of us has one body with many members, and not all members have the same function, 5 so in Christ we who are many are one body, and each member belongs to one another.

6 We have different gifts according to the grace given us. If one’s gift is prophecy, let him use it in proportion to his faith; 7 if it is serving, let him serve; if it is teaching, let him teach; 8 if it is encouraging, let him encourage; if it is giving, let him give generously; if it is leading, let him lead with diligence; if it is showing mercy, let him do it cheerfully.

What is the context?

Paul makes the call to a renewed mind practical by addressing how believers see themselves. They are to avoid an inflated sense of importance and think with sound judgment, recognizing what God has given.

The body picture explains why differences need not create rivalry. Many members have different functions, yet they belong to one body in Christ and to one another. A gift is part of shared life, rather than a reason to imagine one can do without the rest.

Paul names prophecy, serving, teaching, encouraging, giving, leading and showing mercy. He joins the gifts to the manner of their use: generosity, diligence and cheerfulness matter. The emphasis is on putting God’s grace to work for others, rather than collecting titles or comparing status.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a call to humble, active service in a community whose members need one another. Traditions differ over how the gift of prophecy operates.

Calvin’s comment on verse 6 and Wesley’s note understand prophecy here chiefly as explaining Scripture and God’s will faithfully. These are their readings of this passage, rather than a claim that all Reformed or Wesleyan Christians explain the gift identically.

The Assemblies of God affirms prophecy as a continuing gift through which the Spirit strengthens the Church. Its position paper on modern-day prophecy requires messages to be judged by Scripture and the believing community; it does not give present prophecies Scripture’s authority.

The Catholic Church also recognizes gifts of the Spirit, including extraordinary gifts, for the Church’s common good. Its Catechism 2003–2004 places them at the service of love and cites this passage’s gifts of ministry. Across these differences, the gifts are directed toward building up others.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 Corinthians 12:12–27 develops the same body picture. No part can dismiss another as unnecessary, and members share in one another’s suffering and honor. Difference serves mutual care rather than superiority.

Ephesians 4:7–16 connects Christ’s gifts with equipping His people and bringing the body toward maturity in Him. Together these passages place each person’s service within Christ’s purpose for a people growing in unity, truth and love.
