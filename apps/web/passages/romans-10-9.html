Passage

Romans 10:9

What does confessing Jesus as Lord mean?

Berean Standard Bible

9 that if you confess with your mouth, “Jesus is Lord,” and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.

What is the context?

Paul is explaining the message of faith through Moses’ picture of the word being near the mouth and heart. In this verse, confession and belief focus on Jesus as Lord and on God raising Him from the dead.

The surrounding verses keep the statement within a wide invitation: the same Lord is Lord of Jew and Greek, and everyone who calls on Him is invited to salvation. Verse 10 joins the heart’s belief with the mouth’s confession.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as a call to genuine faith in the risen Jesus and acknowledgment of Him as Lord. The words express who is trusted. They are not a verbal technique that works independently of the heart’s response.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In verses 12–13 Paul connects this confession with Joel 2:32, the promise that everyone calling on the Lord’s name will be saved. The confession of Jesus belongs within the scriptural promise of deliverance and Paul’s insistence that the same Lord is Lord of Jew and Greek.

What is our position?

The Gospel According to Jesus and I Am a Disciple connect confession of Jesus as Lord with faith in His resurrection as the foundation of a life following Him. The Gospel study emphasizes trust and a turning of the heart, rather than a saving formula. Baptism also cites this verse in teaching that forgiveness is received through faith in Christ and is not withheld until water baptism.
