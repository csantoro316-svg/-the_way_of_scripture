Passage

Galatians 3:6-14

How does Abraham’s blessing reach people through Christ?

Berean Standard Bible

6 So also, “Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness.” 7 Understand, then, that those who have faith are sons of Abraham. 8 The Scripture foresaw that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, and foretold the gospel to Abraham: “All nations will be blessed through you.” 9 So those who have faith are blessed along with Abraham, the man of faith.

10 All who rely on works of the law are under a curse. For it is written: “Cursed is everyone who does not continue to do everything written in the Book of the Law.” 11 Now it is clear that no one is justified before God by the law, because, “The righteous will live by faith.” 12 The law, however, is not based on faith; on the contrary, “The man who does these things will live by them.”

13 Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us. For it is written: “Cursed is everyone who is hung on a tree.” 14 He redeemed us in order that the blessing promised to Abraham would come to the Gentiles in Christ Jesus, so that by faith we might receive the promise of the Spirit.

What is the context?

Paul turns from the Galatians’ experience to Scripture. He recalls Genesis 15:6: Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness. Those who have faith are identified as Abraham’s sons.

He then brings in the promise of blessing for all nations. Paul says Scripture foretold the gospel to Abraham, looking ahead to God justifying Gentiles by faith. Those who believe share the blessing with Abraham.

The next verses explain the problem with relying on works of the law. Paul cites Deuteronomy 27:26, with its curse on failing to practice the law. He places Habakkuk 2:4, about the righteous living by faith, alongside Leviticus 18:5, about living by doing what the law commands.

The turning point is Christ’s redemption. He became a curse for us, and Paul connects His death with Deuteronomy 21:22–23, where an executed person’s body hung on a tree is described as under a curse.

Paul names the purpose of this redemption: Abraham’s promised blessing comes to Gentiles in Christ Jesus, and the promised Spirit is received through faith. Christ’s self-giving brings the account back to the gift of the Spirit with which the chapter began.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as connecting Abraham’s faith, Christ’s redemption and blessing for the nations. Wesley’s notes on verses 6–14 and the USCCB notes on verses 6–14 both follow that movement and emphasize that the promised blessing comes through faith in Christ.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Genesis 12:1–3 begins Abraham’s calling with a promise that all the families of the earth will be blessed through him. Paul explicitly identifies the gospel’s reach to the Gentiles with that long-standing purpose.

Acts 10:44–48 gives a concrete account of Gentiles hearing the message of Christ and receiving the Holy Spirit. The promised blessing takes shape in people being welcomed into life with God through His Son and Spirit.
