Passage

2 Corinthians 11:14

Can something seem like light and still deceive?

Berean Standard Bible

14 And no wonder, for Satan himself masquerades as an angel of light.

What is the context?

This verse belongs to Paul’s warning in 2 Corinthians 11:12–15. He describes deceitful workers presenting themselves as apostles of Christ and explains their disguise by pointing to Satan appearing as an angel of light.

The surrounding paragraph supplies the point of the comparison: apparent righteousness does not establish whose service someone is actually performing. Paul is exposing misleading claims to represent Christ.

Verse 15 then turns to their actions and their eventual end. Reading the verses together keeps the vivid image connected to discernment and accountability.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the image as a warning about deceptive appearances. Wesley’s note on verse 14 describes an attractive disguise, and the USCCB note on verses 13–15 connects it with the false workers in the surrounding paragraph.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 John 4:1–6 also warns about false prophets and calls believers to test what they hear by the truth about Jesus and the apostolic message. It joins discernment with confidence that the One in God’s people is greater than the one in the world.

That wider setting gives both seriousness and hope to Paul’s warning. God’s people are called to remain faithful to Christ while relying on the God who is with them. The reality of deception belongs alongside His greater power.

What is our position?

Our study Should We Seek Signs From God?, in its section on testing what we are told, draws on verse 14 to explain why a reassuring appearance or feeling cannot establish that something comes from God. Scripture supplies the standard by which an experience is tested. This applies the verse’s warning about disguise without making a feeling the proof of its source.
