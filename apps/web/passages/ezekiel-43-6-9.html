Passage

Ezekiel 43:6-9

What must no longer stand beside the Lord’s dwelling?

Berean Standard Bible

6 While the man was standing beside me, I heard someone speaking to me from inside the temple, 7 and He said to me, “Son of man, this is the place of My throne and the place for the soles of My feet, where I will dwell among the Israelites forever. The house of Israel will never again defile My holy name—neither they nor their kings—by their prostitution and by the funeral offerings for their kings at their deaths. 8 When they placed their threshold next to My threshold and their doorposts beside My doorposts, with only a wall between Me and them, they defiled My holy name by the abominations they committed. Therefore I have consumed them in My anger. 9 Now let them remove far from Me their prostitution and the funeral offerings for their kings, and I will dwell among them forever.

What is the context?

Ezekiel hears a voice from inside the temple while the guide stands beside him. The Lord calls this the place of His throne and the soles of His feet, where He will dwell among the Israelites forever.

The promise also names what must change. The people and their kings had defiled His holy name through prostitution and funeral offerings for their kings. They had placed their thresholds and doorposts beside His, with only a wall between.

The Lord calls for those defiling practices to be removed far from Him. His return does not mean that the earlier wrongdoing was harmless. Lasting fellowship with Him goes together with honoring His holy name.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley understands the prostitution and neighboring thresholds through the people’s idolatry. Cambridge also connects prostitution with idolatry, but takes the language about dead kings as referring to royal burials near the temple, rather than as another name for idols.

The BSB speaks of funeral offerings for the kings. These explanations differ over the exact practice, while agreeing that royal privilege could not make the defilement of God’s dwelling acceptable.
