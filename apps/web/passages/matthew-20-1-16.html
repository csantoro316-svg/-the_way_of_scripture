Passage

Matthew 20:1-16

The Generous Owner and the Vineyard Workers

Berean Standard Bible

1 “For the kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out early in the morning to hire workers for his vineyard. 2 He agreed to pay them a denarius for the day and sent them into his vineyard. 3 About the third hour he went out and saw others standing in the marketplace doing nothing. 4 ‘You also go into my vineyard,’ he said, ‘and I will pay you whatever is right.’ 5 So they went. He went out again about the sixth hour and the ninth hour and did the same thing. 6 About the eleventh hour he went out and found still others standing around. ‘Why have you been standing here all day long doing nothing?’ he asked. 7 ‘Because no one has hired us,’ they answered. So he told them, ‘You also go into my vineyard.’

8 When evening came, the owner of the vineyard said to his foreman, ‘Call the workers and pay them their wages, starting with the last ones hired and moving on to the first.’ 9 The workers who were hired about the eleventh hour came and each received a denarius. 10 So when the original workers came, they assumed they would receive more. But each of them also received a denarius. 11 On receiving their pay, they began to grumble against the landowner. 12 ‘These men who were hired last worked only one hour,’ they said, ‘and you have made them equal to us who have borne the burden and the scorching heat of the day.’

13 But he answered one of them, ‘Friend, I am not being unfair to you. Did you not agree with me on one denarius? 14 Take your pay and go. I want to give this last man the same as I gave you. 15 Do I not have the right to do as I please with what is mine? Or are you envious because I am generous?’ 16 So the last will be first, and the first will be last.”

What is the context?

Jesus has just promised that those who sacrifice for His name will inherit eternal life, then warned that many who are first will be last. This story follows that promise and ends with the same reversal. It helps the disciples think about God's generosity as they consider what they will receive.

A landowner hires workers early in the morning, then returns at the third, sixth, ninth, and eleventh hours. The first workers agree to a denarius for the day; those hired later trust him to pay what is right. The BSB footnotes identify a denarius as a customary day's wage and the later hiring times as roughly nine, noon, three, and five.

At evening, the workers hired last are paid first and receive a denarius. Those who worked all day expect more, but receive the amount they agreed to. They object because the last group worked only one hour. The owner answers that he has kept his agreement and has chosen to be generous to the others.

The closing question brings their envy into the open. Their complaint arises when they compare what they received with the kindness shown to someone else. Jesus again tells the disciples that the last will be first and the first last.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly see the owner as a picture of God's generosity and the first workers' complaint as a warning against envy. Readers differ over what the different hiring times and equal payment especially represent.

The US Catholic bishops' notes on 20:1–16 emphasize all disciples sharing the gift of eternal life, including those who come late. Wesley on 20:1–16 applies the different calling times both to Jewish and Gentile believers and to people who come to faith at different stages of life. He also sees a reversal in which some called later receive greater honor. Both readings direct attention to God's generosity rather than a claim that longer service gives someone control over His gifts.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

God's promised inheritance comes through Christ. Ephesians 1:11–14 describes believers hearing and trusting the gospel, receiving the Holy Spirit, and looking toward their inheritance with His Spirit as its pledge. 1 Peter 1:3–5 connects that inheritance with God's mercy and Jesus' resurrection. The future He gives rests on His saving work.

The welcome of people called later also has a clear example in the church's life. In Acts 15:7–11, Peter says that God gave Gentile believers the Holy Spirit just as He did Jewish believers and cleansed their hearts by faith. Both are saved through the grace of Jesus. That is a related example of sharing God's gift without treating an earlier calling as a reason to resent another person's welcome.
