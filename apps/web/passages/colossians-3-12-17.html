Passage

Colossians 3:12-17

What does a community shaped by Christ look like?

Berean Standard Bible

12 Therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, clothe yourselves with hearts of compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. 13 Bear with one another and forgive any complaint you may have against someone else. Forgive as the Lord forgave you. 14 And over all these virtues put on love, which is the bond of perfect unity.

15 Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, for to this you were called as members of one body. And be thankful. 16 Let the word of Christ richly dwell within you as you teach and admonish one another with all wisdom, and as you sing psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs with gratitude in your hearts to God. 17 And whatever you do, in word or deed, do it all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through Him.

What is the context?

Paul addresses these believers as God’s chosen, holy and loved people. He describes what to put on in keeping with that belonging: compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience.

They are to bear with one another and forgive as the Lord forgave them. Love binds these qualities together. The peace of Christ is to rule among people called into one body, with thanksgiving.

Christ’s word is to dwell richly among them through wise teaching and admonition, including psalms, hymns and spiritual songs. The singing includes gratitude in their hearts to God. Learning, care for others and thanksgiving are woven together.

The paragraph reaches beyond a meeting: every word and deed is to be done in the Lord Jesus’ name, with thanks to the Father through Him. The household instructions that follow bring that whole-life call into particular relationships.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as a call for the community’s life and singing to be filled with Christ’s message. Wesley’s note on verse 16 stresses the word’s lasting, active place in believers. The USCCB note on verses 5–17 includes the congregation’s hymns and prayers within its response to God through Christ. The verse joins wisdom, mutual instruction and gratitude to God.

The instructions to forgive and bear with others belong to the same shared life. Christ’s forgiveness supplies the stated pattern, and the peace of the one body is a community concern.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

John 13:34–35 connects love patterned on Jesus with the world recognizing His disciples. The relationships Paul describes belong within that public witness to the Lord they follow.

Ephesians 2:19–22 places their life together within God’s purpose of a household and dwelling for His Spirit. Compassion, forgiveness, peace and shared teaching serve the building up of that people. The church’s life together displays more than individual moral improvement.

What is our position?

In Worship as the Early Church Knew It, we emphasize the mutual purpose named here: songs should teach, admonish and build up the people beside us. Clear, meaningful content matters to the gathered church. The verse also names gratitude to God; thanksgiving and care for one another belong together in this life shaped by Christ’s word.
