Passage

Philemon 1:23-25

Who joins Paul in his final greeting?

Berean Standard Bible

23 Epaphras, my fellow prisoner in Christ Jesus, sends you greetings, 24 as do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my fellow workers. 25 The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.

What is the context?

Paul closes by naming the people who send greetings with him. Epaphras is called his fellow prisoner in Christ Jesus. Mark, Aristarchus, Demas and Luke are named as fellow workers.

The names show that the appeal comes from within a wider circle of Christian relationships. Paul ends where he began: with the grace of Jesus Christ. After such a personal request, the final blessing includes the community addressed at the start of the letter.

How is this passage commonly understood?

These verses are generally read as greetings and a closing blessing. The USCCB notes connect the named workers with their appearances in Colossians, Acts and 2 Timothy. Those links help identify the people, but the greeting itself does not describe their later lives or the result of the appeal.

Calvin draws a warning about perseverance from Demas’s later desertion in 2 Timothy 4:10. That is a connection with another letter, rather than something Philemon’s closing greeting says has already happened. Here Demas is still included among Paul’s fellow workers.
