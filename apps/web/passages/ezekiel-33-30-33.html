Passage

Ezekiel 33:30-33

What is wrong with enjoying God’s word but not doing it?

Berean Standard Bible

30 As for you, son of man, your people are talking about you near the city walls and in the doorways of their houses. One speaks to another, each saying to his brother, ‘Come and hear the message that has come from the LORD!’ 31 So My people come to you as usual, sit before you, and hear your words; but they do not put them into practice. Although they express love with their mouths, their hearts pursue dishonest gain. 32 Indeed, you are to them like a singer of love songs with a beautiful voice, who skillfully plays an instrument. They hear your words but do not put them into practice. 33 So when it comes to pass—and surely it will come—then they will know that a prophet has been among them.”

What is the context?

The people invite one another to hear Ezekiel’s message. They come, sit before him as the Lord’s people, and listen. The outward attention looks promising.

The Lord identifies the divide beneath it: love is expressed with their mouths, while their hearts pursue dishonest gain. They hear the words but do not put them into practice.

To them Ezekiel is like a skilled singer with a beautiful voice. Enjoying the message has become a substitute for responding to it. When what he announces comes to pass, they will know that a prophet has been among them.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry notes that habit, curiosity and corrupt motives can bring people to hear God’s word. The USCCB notes explain the singer picture as an entertainer enjoyed and then forgotten.

Cambridge connects the desire for gain with self-advancement. The problem is not listening closely or appreciating good teaching; it is the refusal to let what is heard govern what is done.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

James 1:19–27 warns that hearing without doing becomes self-deception. The response reaches everyday conduct: speech, care for vulnerable people and freedom from the world’s pollution.

Jesus makes the same practical distinction in Luke 6:46–49. Calling Him Lord while refusing His words is like building without a foundation. These passages develop the larger concern exposed in Ezekiel: receiving the word is meant to produce a life that acts on it, rather than leave a listener satisfied with the experience of hearing.
