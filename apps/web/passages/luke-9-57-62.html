Passage

Luke 9:57-62

What can stand in the way of following Jesus?

Berean Standard Bible

57 As they were walking along the road, someone said to Jesus, “I will follow You wherever You go.” 58 Jesus replied, “Foxes have dens and birds of the air have nests, but the Son of Man has no place to lay His head.”

59 Then He said to another man, “Follow Me.” The man replied, “Lord, first let me go and bury my father.” 60 But Jesus told him, “Let the dead bury their own dead. You, however, go and proclaim the kingdom of God.”

61 Still another said, “I will follow You, Lord; but first let me bid farewell to my family.” 62 Then Jesus declared, “No one who puts his hand to the plow and then looks back is fit for the kingdom of God.”

What is the context?

As Jesus travels, three exchanges bring the cost of following Him into focus. One person offers to follow wherever He goes. Jesus answers by describing His lack of a place to lay His head, putting the promise beside the insecurity of His own journey.

Another receives the call to follow but asks first to bury his father. Jesus answers with the command to go and proclaim God’s kingdom. The exchange places the urgency of that call beside a deeply important family obligation.

A third offers to follow after saying goodbye at home. Jesus responds with the picture of someone taking hold of a plow and looking back. The three encounters ask what claim on a person’s life is allowed to govern the response to Him.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly understand the exchanges as a demanding call to wholehearted discipleship, with the kingdom’s claim reaching beyond comfort and competing obligations. The Catholic bishops’ notes emphasize its urgency, and Wesley points to the need to understand the terms of following and the earlier scene of Elisha’s call from the plow. (USCCB notes on Luke 9:57–62; Wesley on Luke 9:58–62)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Elisha’s call supplies a close Scriptural parallel. Elijah finds him plowing; Elisha asks to say goodbye to his parents, then leaves his former work and follows. Jesus’ image recalls that kind of decisive response while pressing His own hearers to attend to the kingdom before them. (1 Kings 19:19–21)

Hebrews describes people of faith as seeking the better home God prepares, then calls believers to run with endurance while fixing their eyes on Jesus. He Himself endures the cross for the joy ahead. The forward-looking call here belongs within that larger journey of faith toward what God promises. (Hebrews 11:13–16; Hebrews 12:1–3)
