Passage

Mark 9:14-29

Help My Unbelief

Berean Standard Bible

14 When they returned to the other disciples, they saw a large crowd around them, and scribes arguing with them. 15 As soon as all the people saw Jesus, they were filled with awe and ran to greet Him. 16 “What are you disputing with them?” He asked. 17 Someone in the crowd replied, “Teacher, I brought You my son, who has a spirit that makes him mute. 18 Whenever it seizes him, it throws him to the ground. He foams at the mouth, gnashes his teeth, and becomes rigid. I asked Your disciples to drive it out, but they were unable.” 19 “O unbelieving generation!” Jesus replied. “How long must I remain with you? How long must I put up with you? Bring the boy to Me.”

20 So they brought him, and seeing Jesus, the spirit immediately threw the boy into a convulsion. He fell to the ground and rolled around, foaming at the mouth. 21 Jesus asked the boy’s father, “How long has this been with him?” “From childhood,” he said. 22 “It often throws him into the fire or into the water, trying to kill him. But if You can do anything, have compassion on us and help us.” 23 “If You can?” echoed Jesus. “All things are possible to him who believes!” 24 Immediately the boy’s father cried out, “I do believe; help my unbelief!”

25 When Jesus saw that a crowd had come running, He rebuked the unclean spirit. “You deaf and mute spirit,” He said, “I command you to come out and never enter him again.” 26 After shrieking and convulsing him violently, the spirit came out. The boy became like a corpse, so that many said, “He is dead.” 27 But Jesus took him by the hand and helped him to his feet, and he stood up. 28 After Jesus had gone into the house, His disciples asked Him privately, “Why couldn’t we drive it out?” 29 Jesus answered, “This kind cannot come out, except by prayer.”

What is the context?

Jesus and the three return to find a crowd and scribes disputing with the other disciples. A father explains that he brought his son, who is tormented by a spirit that makes him unable to speak. The disciples have been unable to drive it out, although they had previously been given authority over unclean spirits in Mark 6:7–13.

When the boy is brought to Jesus, the spirit throws him into a convulsion. His father says this has happened since childhood and describes repeated attempts to destroy him in fire or water. The father's request includes doubt about what Jesus can do. Jesus answers that doubt, and the father openly asks for help with his unbelief.

Jesus commands the spirit to leave and never return. After a violent departure, the boy lies so still that many think he is dead. Jesus takes his hand and helps him stand. Mark reports what the onlookers say, without declaring that the boy actually died.

In private, the disciples ask why they could not drive the spirit out. Jesus directs them to prayer. Their earlier experience does not remove their need to depend on God now. The father also comes with dependence rather than a claim to flawless confidence: he asks for the help he needs, including help with his faith.

The BSB footnote to verse 29 records manuscripts that add fasting. The Scripture above follows its main text, which names prayer. The passage does not give a detailed ranking of spirits or a set of ritual steps for readers to reproduce.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly emphasize reliance on Jesus and God rather than confidence in the disciples' own ability. Calvin on Mark 9:24 recognizes real faith alongside remaining unbelief, while the USCCB notes on verses 14–29 explain prayer as conscious reliance on God's power.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

This deliverance belongs to the wider work of the Son against what destroys people. 1 John 3:8 states that He was revealed to destroy the devil's works. Hebrews 2:14–18 carries that purpose through His own humanity, suffering, and death: He breaks the devil's hold and frees people enslaved by fear of death. The rescue of this child offers a concrete picture of His power to help, while the larger victory comes through the mission He has just announced.
