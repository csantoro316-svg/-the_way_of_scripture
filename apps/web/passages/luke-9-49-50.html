Passage

Luke 9:49-50

Why does Jesus tell John not to stop the other man?

Berean Standard Bible

49 “Master,” said John, “we saw someone driving out demons in Your name, and we tried to stop him, because he does not accompany us.” 50 “Do not stop him,” Jesus replied, “for whoever is not against you is for you.”

What is the context?

John reports that the disciples saw someone driving out demons in Jesus’ name and tried to stop him. Their stated reason is that he does not accompany their group.

Jesus tells them not to stop him and says that whoever is not against them is for them. The correction follows the dispute about greatness and continues to challenge the way the disciples draw boundaries around themselves.

The encounter concerns a particular man doing this work in Jesus’ name. Jesus directs the disciples to receive that work without making membership in their immediate circle the deciding question.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly understand Jesus as correcting an attempt to exclude someone simply because he is outside the disciples’ immediate group. The Catholic bishops’ notes place the incident alongside the preceding rivalry as another lesson in the attitudes of discipleship. (USCCB note on Luke 9:46–50; Luke 9:49–50)

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Numbers gives a close parallel. When Eldad and Medad prophesy in the camp, Joshua asks Moses to stop them. Moses answers by rejecting jealousy for his own position and expressing the wish that all the Lord’s people might receive His Spirit. In both scenes, a leader corrects resistance to God’s work beyond the expected gathering. (Numbers 11:24–30)

Paul later describes different gifts, ministries and ways of working under the same Spirit, Lord and God, given for the common good. That is a wider connection with receiving genuine service as part of God’s work rather than treating one’s own circle as its whole extent. (1 Corinthians 12:4–7)
