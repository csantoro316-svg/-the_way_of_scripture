Passage

1 Samuel 3:1-14

How does Samuel learn who is calling him?

Berean Standard Bible

1 And the boy Samuel ministered to the LORD before Eli. Now in those days the word of the LORD was rare, and visions were scarce. 2 And at that time Eli, whose eyesight had grown so dim that he could not see, was lying in his room. 3 Before the lamp of God had gone out, Samuel was lying down in the temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was located. 4 Then the LORD called to Samuel, and he answered, “Here I am.” 5 He ran to Eli and said, “Here I am, for you have called me.” “I did not call,” Eli replied. “Go back and lie down.” So he went and lay down. 6 Once again the LORD called, “Samuel!” So Samuel got up, went to Eli, and said, “Here I am, for you have called me.” “My son, I did not call,” Eli replied. “Go back and lie down.” 7 Now Samuel did not yet know the LORD, because the word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him.

8 Once again, for the third time, the LORD called to Samuel. He got up, went to Eli, and said, “Here I am, for you have called me.” Then Eli realized that it was the LORD who was calling the boy. 9 “Go and lie down,” he said to Samuel, “and if He calls you, say, ‘Speak, LORD, for Your servant is listening.’” So Samuel went and lay down in his place. 10 Then the LORD came and stood there, calling as before, “Samuel! Samuel!” And Samuel answered, “Speak, for Your servant is listening.”

11 Then the LORD said to Samuel, “I am about to do something in Israel at which the ears of all who hear it will tingle. 12 On that day I will carry out against Eli everything I have spoken about his house, from beginning to end. 13 I told him that I would judge his house forever for the iniquity of which he knows, because his sons blasphemed God and he did not restrain them. 14 Therefore I have sworn to the house of Eli, ‘The iniquity of Eli’s house shall never be atoned for by sacrifice or offering.’”

What is the context?

Samuel continues to serve under Eli during a time when messages and visions from the LORD are rare. At night he hears his name and repeatedly runs to Eli, assuming the priest has called. Eli eventually recognizes what is happening and tells him how to answer. Samuel’s willingness to respond comes before his understanding of who is speaking.

The word translated temple is הֵיכָל, hekal (hay-KAHL), a word used for a palace or temple. Brown–Driver–Briggs specifically lists the pre-Solomon place of worship at Shiloh. This is not Solomon’s later temple in Jerusalem. The word itself does not settle exactly where Samuel’s bed stood.

Verse 7 explains that the LORD’s word had not yet been revealed to Samuel. When he finally listens, the first message is difficult: God will carry out the earlier warning against Eli’s household. Eli knew about his sons’ conduct and did not restrain them. The judgment is confirmed, and sacrifices will not turn it aside.

The account introduces Samuel as a recipient of God’s word. It does not give his exact age or describe a procedure that every person must repeat to hear a private message. The repeated calls belong to this particular encounter.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill understand Samuel’s not yet knowing the LORD as unfamiliarity with this kind of prophetic revelation. Gill explicitly distinguishes this from having no prior knowledge of God or His worship. In that reading, Samuel is learning to recognize God speaking to him in a new way.

Henry emphasizes God’s persistence in calling and Eli’s responsibility to help the younger servant respond. The chapter’s central development is Samuel receiving God’s word; identifying the exact moment when he first possessed saving faith goes beyond what these commentators understand verse 7 to establish.

Wesley reads the statement about sacrifice and offering as confirmation that the announced punishment will occur. Eli’s household cannot avoid it by continuing to offer sacrifices. The warning addresses a known course of wrongdoing and failed restraint, rather than suggesting that performing a ritual can make ongoing abuse acceptable.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

God making Himself known through His word is a theme that reaches beyond Samuel’s call. Hebrews 1:1–4 looks back on God speaking through the prophets and then announces His speech through the Son. Jesus reveals God and provides purification for sins.

Samuel’s calling belongs within that earlier prophetic history. Hebrews directs the larger story toward Jesus, while distinguishing Him from the prophets who came before. The connection rests on God speaking and making Himself known; it does not depend on identifying the speaker in this scene as an angel when the passage itself does not use that description.
