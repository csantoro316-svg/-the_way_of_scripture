Passage

Galatians 6:6-10

Why should we keep doing good when the harvest takes time?

Berean Standard Bible

6 Nevertheless, the one who receives instruction in the word must share in all good things with his instructor. 7 Do not be deceived: God is not to be mocked. Whatever a man sows, he will reap in return. 8 The one who sows to please his flesh, from the flesh will reap destruction; but the one who sows to please the Spirit, from the Spirit will reap eternal life.

9 Let us not grow weary in well-doing, for in due time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up. 10 Therefore, as we have opportunity, let us do good to everyone, and especially to the family of faith.

What is the context?

Paul continues with practical responsibilities in the community. Someone receiving instruction in the word is to share good things with the instructor. Giving and receiving within the family of faith includes supporting those who teach.

He then warns that God cannot be mocked. The picture of sowing and reaping makes the connection between a life’s direction and its outcome. Sowing to please the flesh leads to destruction; sowing to please the Spirit leads to eternal life.

Paul’s encouragement recognizes that doing good can become tiring. The harvest comes in its proper time, so he urges his readers to keep going. Perseverance matters in the life he is describing.

He ends with the opportunities in front of them: do good to everyone, especially the family of faith. Care within the church has a particular place, while the call to do good reaches beyond it.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as joining support for teachers, responsibility before God and patient persistence in doing good. Wesley’s notes on verses 6–10 connect the harvest of life with God’s free grace and power. The USCCB notes on verses 6 and 10 identify practical support for instruction and the wide reach of doing good, with particular responsibility toward fellow believers.

The sowing picture follows Paul’s contrast between flesh and Spirit. It does not supply a guaranteed financial return for a donation. The outcome he names is much larger than personal prosperity.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 Corinthians 15:50–58 places perseverance within the hope of resurrection and God’s victory over death through Jesus Christ. Paul therefore urges believers to remain steadfast, knowing their labor in the Lord is not in vain.

That connection gives a wider reason to continue when doing good is costly or its results are hidden. The coming harvest belongs to the God who gives life through His risen Son. Present faithfulness is lived in the light of that future.
