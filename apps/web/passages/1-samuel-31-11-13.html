Passage

1 Samuel 31:11-13

Why do the men of Jabesh-gilead risk recovering the bodies?

Berean Standard Bible

11 When the people of Jabesh-gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul, 12 all their men of valor set out, journeyed all night, and retrieved the bodies of Saul and his sons from the wall of Beth-shan. When they arrived at Jabesh, they burned the bodies there. 13 Then they took their bones and buried them under the tamarisk tree in Jabesh, and they fasted seven days.

What is the context?

The people of Jabesh-gilead hear what the Philistines have done. Their men of valor travel through the night, recover Saul’s body and those of his sons from Beth-shan’s wall, and bring them back to Jabesh.

They burn the bodies, bury the bones under a tamarisk tree and fast seven days. The book closes with mourning and an act of care for the dead, following the defeat and public disgrace.

Their response recalls 1 Samuel 11, when Saul rescued Jabesh-gilead from Nahash and the Ammonites. Remembering his later failures does not require forgetting the help he once gave them.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley reads the recovery as gratitude for Saul’s rescue of their town. Gill likewise emphasizes their determination to recover the bodies after the Philistines’ treatment. Their courage answers humiliation with care.

Gill discusses possible reasons for burning the bodies, including preventing further abuse and their condition after death. The passage reports the act without specifying the reason. The burning, burial of bones and fasting should each be allowed their place in the account.

In 2 Samuel 2:4–7, David explicitly praises their kindness in burying Saul. The next king recognizes mercy shown to his former pursuer, extending the refusal to let hostility erase respect for a human life.
