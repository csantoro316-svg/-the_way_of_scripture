Passage

Romans 13:11-14

How should the coming day change life now?

Berean Standard Bible

11 And do this, understanding the occasion. The hour has come for you to wake up from your slumber, for our salvation is nearer now than when we first believed. 12 The night is nearly over; the day has drawn near. So let us lay aside the deeds of darkness and put on the armor of light. 13 Let us behave decently, as in the daytime, not in carousing and drunkenness, not in sexual immorality and debauchery, not in dissension and jealousy. 14 Instead, clothe yourselves with the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the desires of the flesh.

What is the context?

Paul gives urgency to the life of love he has described. Salvation is nearer than when his readers first believed, and he pictures the night nearing its end and the day drawing close. Hope for what God will complete becomes a reason to wake up and live differently now.

The change includes putting aside the deeds of darkness and putting on the armor of light. His examples cover drunken excess, sexual wrongdoing, conflict and jealousy. The warning reaches both outward indulgence and the resentments that damage relationships.

He gathers the response into being clothed with the Lord Jesus Christ and making no provision for sinful desires. The clothing picture concerns a life shaped by belonging to Him. Believers are to stop arranging their lives around satisfying the desires they are called to leave behind.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a call to alert, faithful living in light of the salvation God will complete. Readiness is expressed through present conduct, not through calculating a date. Being clothed with Christ connects the way people live with the Lord to whom they belong.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

1 Thessalonians 5:1–11 develops the same pattern of night, day, wakefulness and armor. Paul names faith and love as a breastplate and the hope of salvation as a helmet, then directs believers to encourage and build one another up.

That connection shows what expectation of the coming day is meant to produce: a people living with faith, love and hope, helping one another remain ready. The future promise gives direction to daily life while its timing remains outside the schedule supplied in these verses.
