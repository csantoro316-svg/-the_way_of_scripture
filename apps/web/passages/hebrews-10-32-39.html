Passage

Hebrews 10:32-39

What helps faith endure when following Christ is costly?

Berean Standard Bible

32 Remember the early days that you were in the light. In those days, you endured a great conflict in the face of suffering. 33 Sometimes you were publicly exposed to ridicule and persecution; at other times you were partners with those who were so treated. 34 You sympathized with those in prison and joyfully accepted the confiscation of your property, knowing that you yourselves had a better and permanent possession.

35 So do not throw away your confidence; it holds a great reward. 36 You need to persevere, so that after you have done the will of God, you will receive what He has promised.

37 For, “In just a little while, He who is coming will come and will not delay. 38 But My righteous one will live by faith; and if he shrinks back, I will take no pleasure in him.” 39 But we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls.

What is the context?

The writer turns from warning to encouragement by reminding the readers of their earlier faithfulness. They endured public abuse, stood beside others who suffered, cared for prisoners and accepted the loss of property because they knew they had something lasting.

The call is to keep that confidence and persevere in doing God’s will. Earlier courage is a reason to continue, rather than a reason to assume the remaining journey no longer matters.

The closing words draw on Habakkuk 2:3–4: God’s promise calls for waiting and a life of faith. Hebrews directs that expectation toward the One who is coming. Its final contrast is between shrinking back to destruction and faith that preserves life.

The writer places himself alongside his readers in the hopeful final sentence. He expects faith to endure, while his call to endurance remains real. That prepares for chapter 11’s examples of people who trusted God before receiving everything He promised.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley both read the passage as a serious call to continuing faith with eternal life in view. Calvin explains that receiving reward is consistent with relying on God’s mercy, and recognizes that joy in God’s promise can coexist with real grief over loss.

Their broader accounts of perseverance differ, as in the preceding warning. The Free Grace reading represented by Tanner instead emphasizes the lasting reward and inheritance a believer can forfeit without losing eternal salvation. These differences should not hide the shared call to endure, help those suffering and trust God’s promise.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Matthew 6:19–21 directs disciples toward lasting treasure rather than possessions that can be taken away. Romans 8:18–25 describes present suffering alongside hope for the redemption still to come.

The next chapter develops the same connection through earlier believers: some received remarkable help, and others suffered without receiving the final promise in their lifetime. God’s purpose gives hope beyond immediate relief, without calling present cruelty good.
