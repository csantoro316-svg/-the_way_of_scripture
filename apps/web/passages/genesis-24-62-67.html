Passage

Genesis 24:62-67

Isaac Marries Rebekah

Berean Standard Bible

62 Now Isaac had just returned from Beer-lahai-roi, for he was living in the Negev. 63 Early in the evening, Isaac went out to the field to meditate, and looking up, he saw the camels approaching.

64 And when Rebekah looked up and saw Isaac, she got down from her camel 65 and asked the servant, “Who is that man in the field coming to meet us?”

“It is my master,” the servant answered. So she took her veil and covered herself. 66 Then the servant told Isaac all that he had done.

67 And Isaac brought her into the tent of his mother Sarah and took Rebekah as his wife. And Isaac loved her and was comforted after his mother’s death.

What is the context?

The travelers reach Isaac, who is living in the Negev and has returned from Beer-lahai-roi. He is in the field toward evening when he sees the camels approaching. Rebekah sees him, gets down from her camel, and asks the servant who he is. Once told, she covers herself with her veil.

The servant reports what has happened, bringing the mission to its conclusion. Isaac takes Rebekah as his wife and loves her. The account ends by saying that he is comforted after his mother's death, joining this marriage back to Sarah's burial in chapter 23. The next generation's future and a son's grief meet in the same household.

The story tells us what Isaac and Rebekah do at their meeting, but does not explain every meaning of the veil or describe a complete wedding ceremony. Its final emphasis is the marriage, Isaac's love, and the comfort he receives.

How is this passage commonly understood?

A common Christian reading sees a successful conclusion to the servant's mission, with God's care expressed in marriage, love, and comfort after loss. Wesley's overview of Genesis 24 connects Isaac's marriage with Sarah's death and the passing of one generation to the next.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Scripture uses marriage to help explain Christ's relationship with His people. Ephesians 5:25–32 describes Christ loving the church, giving Himself for her, and preparing her to be with Him. Isaac's love for Rebekah can be read alongside that larger theme: the long search and journey lead to a relationship of love, rather than ending with the delivery of a stranger to a household.

There is also a helpful parallel between Rebekah setting out before she has met Isaac and believers trusting Christ before seeing Him. 1 Peter 1:8–9 describes love and faith directed toward the unseen Christ. Neither passage identifies Rebekah as a prediction of the church. These are comparisons that illuminate the story; they do not require us to identify the unnamed servant as the Holy Spirit or turn the gifts and camels into a hidden timetable.
