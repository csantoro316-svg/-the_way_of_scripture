Passage

Acts 13:26-41

How does Jesus’ resurrection fulfill the promised good news?

Berean Standard Bible

26 Brothers, children of Abraham, and you Gentiles who fear God, it is to us that this message of salvation has been sent. 27 The people of Jerusalem and their rulers did not recognize Jesus, yet in condemning Him they fulfilled the words of the prophets that are read every Sabbath. 28 And though they found no ground for a death sentence, they asked Pilate to have Him executed. 29 When they had carried out all that was written about Him, they took Him down from the tree and laid Him in a tomb. 30 But God raised Him from the dead, 31 and for many days He was seen by those who had accompanied Him from Galilee to Jerusalem. They are now His witnesses to our people.

32 And now we proclaim to you the good news: What God promised our fathers 33 He has fulfilled for us, their children, by raising up Jesus. As it is written in the second Psalm: ‘You are My Son; today I have become Your Father.’ 34 In fact, God raised Him from the dead, never to see decay. As He has said: ‘I will give you the holy and sure blessings promised to David.’ 35 So also, He says in another Psalm: ‘You will not let Your Holy One see decay.’ 36 For when David had served God’s purpose in his own generation, he fell asleep. His body was buried with his fathers and saw decay. 37 But the One whom God raised from the dead did not see decay.

38 Therefore let it be known to you, brothers, that through Jesus the forgiveness of sins is proclaimed to you. 39 Through Him everyone who believes is justified from everything from which you could not be justified by the law of Moses. 40 Watch out, then, that what was spoken by the prophets does not happen to you: 41 ‘Look, you scoffers, wonder and perish! For I am doing a work in your days that you would never believe, even if someone told you.’”

What is the context?

Paul tells the hearers that this message of salvation has been sent to them. He describes Jesus’ condemnation, death and burial, then announces God’s reversal: Jesus has been raised, and witnesses have seen Him alive.

He explains the resurrection through the promised Son in Psalm 2, the sure blessings promised to David in Isaiah 55 and the Holy One who does not see decay in Psalm 16. David served God in his generation and died; Jesus, whom God raised, did not remain in decay.

Paul then announces forgiveness through Jesus and says that everyone who believes is justified through Him from what the law of Moses could not provide. To be justified concerns being put right with God. The message is offered to the people listening, not merely explained as something that happened elsewhere.

The closing warning draws on Habakkuk: the hearers must not dismiss God’s work as unbelievable. Paul’s speech joins promise, resurrection, forgiveness and a call to take what they have heard seriously.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the resurrection as revealing the Son’s identity and fulfilling God’s promise, rather than making Jesus begin to exist. Wesley on verse 33 and the Catholic Catechism, paragraph 445, both describe His sonship being made known through the resurrection.

Traditions differ over how to explain being put right with God through faith. The Reformed Westminster Confession, chapter 11, paragraphs 1–2, describes God pardoning sinners and counting them righteous by counting Christ’s obedience as theirs, received through faith alone. It distinguishes this acceptance from God’s inward renewal of their lives, while insisting that living faith works through love.

The Catholic Church’s Catechism, paragraphs 1989–1993, includes forgiveness and inward renewal in being put right with God. It locates the gift in Christ’s saving work, received through faith and given in baptism, with the person’s response itself enabled by grace.

The Methodist Articles of Religion, Article IX, teach that people are counted righteous only because of what Christ has done, received through faith, rather than their own works or deserving. These explanations share dependence on Christ while differing over how forgiveness, renewal, faith and baptism relate.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Paul himself makes the connections to Psalm 2:7, Isaiah 55:3 and Psalm 16:10. The resurrection is not an isolated marvel added to the story; he presents it as central to God keeping His promise and giving the blessings announced through David.

The warning in Habakkuk 1:5–11 originally concerns God’s unexpected work in raising up the Chaldeans. Paul applies its warning against unbelieving dismissal to his hearers’ response to the good news. This is his stated use of the prophetic warning, not a claim that every detail of the earlier crisis is repeated here.
