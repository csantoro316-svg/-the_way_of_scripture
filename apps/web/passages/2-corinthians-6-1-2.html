Passage

2 Corinthians 6:1-2

Why does Paul say the time to respond is now?

Berean Standard Bible

1 As God’s fellow workers, then, we urge you not to receive God’s grace in vain. 2 For He says: “In the time of favor I heard you, and in the day of salvation I helped you.” Behold, now is the time of favor; now is the day of salvation!

What is the context?

Paul continues the appeal to be reconciled to God. As God’s fellow workers, he and his companions urge the Corinthians not to receive His grace in vain: the gift is to meet a real response, rather than leave life untouched.

He supports the appeal with Isaiah 49:8, where God speaks of hearing and helping in a time of favor and a day of salvation. Paul brings that promise into the present urgency of the message: now is the time of favor and salvation.

The warning calls for a response to God, not simply admiration of the message or confidence in having heard it before. The following account of the workers’ lives shows the seriousness with which they carry that appeal.

Paul does not give a date for the end of the world or explain every reader’s standing before God. His point is that God’s present offer should not be set aside for an imagined better moment.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as an urgent call for God’s grace to bear fruit in the life of those receiving it. Wesley’s note on verse 1 connects receiving grace by faith with persevering holiness. The USCCB notes on verses 1–2 likewise connect the appeal with living in keeping with the gift of new creation and reconciliation.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The promise Paul quotes belongs to Isaiah 49:5–13, where the servant’s work includes restoring Israel and bringing God’s salvation to the ends of the earth. God’s care reaches prisoners, people in darkness and His afflicted people.

In Acts 13:46–48, Paul and Barnabas explicitly apply the same chapter’s light-to-the-nations promise to their gospel mission. The appeal here belongs within that outward reach of God’s salvation. Now is a summons to receive what God is making known, rather than merely a demand to become busier.
