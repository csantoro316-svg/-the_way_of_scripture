Passage

2 Corinthians 11:30-33

Why end this account with an escape in a basket?

Berean Standard Bible

30 If I must boast, I will boast of the things that show my weakness. 31 The God and Father of the Lord Jesus, who is forever worthy of praise, knows that I am not lying.

32 In Damascus, the governor under King Aretas secured the city of the Damascenes in order to arrest me. 33 But I was lowered in a basket through a window in the wall and escaped his grasp.

What is the context?

Paul states the direction of his boasting: he will speak of things that show his weakness. He calls on God, the Father of the Lord Jesus, as the One who knows he is telling the truth.

He recalls danger in Damascus. The governor under King Aretas secured the city to arrest him, and Paul escaped by being lowered in a basket through a window in the wall.

Acts 9:23–25 also describes a plot in Damascus and disciples helping him escape through the wall in a basket. Fellow believers provided the help he needed to get away.

The image fits the point Paul has just made. His account of service includes needing help and escaping danger in a vulnerable position. This is the story he chooses to tell as he turns toward boasting in weakness.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read the escape as an example of the weakness Paul has chosen to describe. Wesley’s notes on verses 30–33 explain the danger and escape; the USCCB note on verses 31–32 likewise connects the episode with weakness and deliverance.
