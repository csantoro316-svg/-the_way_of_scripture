Passage

Ezekiel 37:24-28

What makes the promised peace and God’s dwelling last?

Berean Standard Bible

24 My servant David will be king over them, and there will be one shepherd for all of them. They will follow My ordinances and keep and observe My statutes. 25 They will live in the land that I gave to My servant Jacob, where your fathers lived. They will live there forever with their children and grandchildren, and My servant David will be their prince forever. 26 And I will make a covenant of peace with them; it will be an everlasting covenant. I will establish them and multiply them, and I will set My sanctuary among them forever. 27 My dwelling place will be with them; I will be their God, and they will be My people. 28 Then the nations will know that I the LORD sanctify Israel, when My sanctuary is among them forever.’”

What is the context?

The reunited people will have one shepherd, the Lord’s servant David. They will follow the Lord’s ways and dwell with their descendants in the land given to Jacob.

The Lord will make an everlasting covenant of peace, establish and multiply them, and set His sanctuary among them forever. The promise joins faithful leadership, restored people, secure life and His presence.

The nations will know that He sanctifies Israel when His sanctuary is among them. The closing assurance answers the earlier departure of His glory: His dwelling will be with His people, and He will be their God.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Henry and Wesley understand David as Christ, the promised shepherd from David’s line. Walvoord favors the resurrected David serving under Christ. This is the same difference in reading the name found in Ezekiel 34:23–24.

Wesley understands the sanctuary through a spiritual temple and worship brought to completion in heaven. Cambridge emphasizes the temple as the Lord’s dwelling, made holy by His presence. The central promise is His lasting relationship with His people; the fuller account of the sanctuary comes later in Ezekiel.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

In Second Corinthians 6:14–18, Paul calls believers the temple of the living God and takes up God’s covenant promise to dwell with His people and be their God. That belonging becomes a call to leave uncleanness and live faithfully before Him.

Revelation 21:1–4 carries the dwelling theme to the new creation, where God is with His people and death, sorrow and pain are removed. These New Testament passages show the larger reach of the hope at Ezekiel’s conclusion: peace is bound to God’s presence and to the life He gives His people.
