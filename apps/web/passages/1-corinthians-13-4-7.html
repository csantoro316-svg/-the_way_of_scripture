Passage

1 Corinthians 13:4-7

What does love look like in the way we treat people?

Berean Standard Bible

4 Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. 5 It is not rude, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no account of wrongs. 6 Love takes no pleasure in evil, but rejoices in the truth. 7 It bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.

What is the context?

Paul makes love visible through conduct. It is patient and kind; it refuses envy, boasting and pride. In a church divided over status and gifts, those qualities answer the behavior he has been correcting throughout the letter.

Love does not behave rudely or make its own interests the center. It is not easily angered and does not keep a running account of wrongs to use against someone. These are choices in a relationship, rather than simply a feeling we either happen to have or lack.

Love also rejoices with the truth and takes no pleasure in evil. That matters for reading the sweeping statements that follow. Believing, hoping and enduring do not require calling a lie true, celebrating harm or refusing to confront wrongdoing. Paul has already required the church to address serious misconduct in chapter 5.

The final four expressions describe love’s persistence. It does not become cynical, abandon hope or discard a person as soon as care becomes costly. Truth and perseverance belong together in that commitment.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand these verses as describing love through action and character. Calvin’s comment on verse 4 connects the description with preserving the church’s unity and correcting the Corinthians’ faults. Wesley’s notes on verses 5–7 likewise distinguish refusing baseless suspicion from being unable to recognize evil.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Ephesians 5:1–2 calls God’s beloved children to imitate Him and walk in love, connecting that life directly with Christ loving them and giving Himself for them.

Paul’s description is therefore part of more than a general call to be agreeable. God’s children are being formed to reflect the love they have received through His Son. Patience, kindness, truth and endurance give that love a recognizable shape in everyday life.
