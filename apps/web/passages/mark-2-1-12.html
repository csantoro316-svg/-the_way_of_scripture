Passage

Mark 2:1-12

The Man Lowered Through the Roof: Forgiveness and Healing

Berean Standard Bible

1 A few days later Jesus went back to Capernaum. And when the people heard that He was home, 2 they gathered in such large numbers that there was no more room, not even outside the door, as Jesus spoke the word to them. 3 Then a paralytic was brought to Him, carried by four men. 4 Since they were unable to get to Jesus through the crowd, they uncovered the roof above Him, made an opening, and lowered the paralytic on his mat.

5 When Jesus saw their faith, He said to the paralytic, “Son, your sins are forgiven.” 6 But some of the scribes were sitting there and thinking in their hearts, 7 “Why does this man speak like this? He is blaspheming! Who can forgive sins but God alone?” 8 At once Jesus knew in His spirit that they were thinking this way within themselves. “Why are you thinking these things in your hearts?” He asked. 9 “Which is easier: to say to a paralytic, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Get up, pick up your mat, and walk’?

10 But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins...” He said to the paralytic, 11 “I tell you, get up, pick up your mat, and go home.” 12 And immediately the man got up, picked up his mat, and walked out in front of them all. As a result, they were all astounded and glorified God, saying, “We have never seen anything like this!”

What is the context?

Jesus returns to Capernaum after some days, and people crowd the house where He is teaching. Four men bring a paralyzed man but cannot reach Him through the crowd. They uncover the roof, make an opening, and lower him on his mat. Mark does not name the house's owner or the men carrying him.

Jesus sees their faith and first tells the man that his sins are forgiven. Forgive comes from ἀφίημι, aphiēmi (ah-FEE-ay-mee). Thayer's lexicon includes releasing or remitting a debt and forgiving sins, explicitly listing this passage. Jesus is making a claim about the man's standing before God, before his physical condition changes.

Some scribes silently object that only God can forgive sins. Jesus knows their thoughts and asks which is easier to say: that the sins are forgiven, or that the man should rise and walk. He then heals him to show that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive. What the crowd can see supports the claim they cannot inspect in the same way.

The man gets up, carries his mat, and walks out before them all. Their amazement turns to praise of God. The story joins the man's bodily restoration with the greater question of Jesus' authority, rather than using the healing only to display power.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christian readings commonly understand the visible healing as confirming Jesus' authority to forgive sins. Calvin on the parallel Matthew 9:2–6 and the US Catholic bishops' note on Mark 2:5 also include the man's own faith alongside that of those bringing him, rather than making another person's faith an automatic substitute for his.

There is a smaller difference over how to read verse 10. Calvin reads the claim about the Son of Man as part of Jesus' spoken response. The bishops' note on 2:10 suggests that Mark pauses the scene to explain its meaning to Christian readers. That is a view in their explanatory notes, rather than a teaching every Catholic must hold; both readings see the miracle as revealing Jesus' authority.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The scribes' question concerns something God claims for Himself. In Isaiah 43:24–25, He declares that He blots out His people's transgressions. Jesus' answer brings His own identity into view. The title Son of Man also recalls the figure receiving enduring authority in Daniel 7:13–14. This is a scriptural parallel; later in Mark Jesus explicitly joins that title with coming on heaven's clouds (Mark 14:61–62).

Forgiveness also belongs to God's promised new covenant in Jeremiah 31:31–34. Hebrews 10:10–18 connects that promise with Christ's once-for-all offering and the forgiveness it brings. In Mark 14:22–25, Jesus will speak of His own blood and the covenant. The authority shown in this house points forward to His giving Himself for the people He forgives.
