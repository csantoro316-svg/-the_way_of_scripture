Passage

Acts 23:1-5

How does Paul respond when the high priest orders him to be struck?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Paul looked directly at the Sanhedrin and said, “Brothers, I have conducted myself before God in all good conscience to this day.” 2 At this, the high priest Ananias ordered those standing near Paul to strike him on the mouth. 3 Then Paul said to him, “God will strike you, you whitewashed wall! You sit here to judge me according to the law, yet you yourself violate the law by commanding that I be struck.” 4 But those standing nearby said, “How dare you insult the high priest of God!” 5 “Brothers,” Paul replied, “I was not aware that he was the high priest, for it is written: ‘Do not speak evil about the ruler of your people.’”

What is the context?

Paul stands before the Sanhedrin, the Jewish council, and says he has conducted himself before God in good conscience. This does not erase his own account of persecuting believers in the preceding chapter. A claim about his conscience is not a claim that every action in his past was right.

The high priest Ananias orders him struck on the mouth. Paul calls him a whitewashed wall and challenges the contradiction of judging according to the law while ordering an unlawful blow. When others object that he has insulted the high priest, Paul says he was not aware the man was high priest and recalls the command not to speak evil of the ruler of the people.

The command comes from Exodus 22:28. Luke reports Paul’s answer without explaining exactly why he says he did not know. The account does not supply a diagnosis of his eyesight, details about the priest’s clothing or a full history of who held the office.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly recognize both the injustice of the blow and the importance of Paul’s appeal to Scripture about speaking of a ruler. Interpreters differ over the intention of his reply.

The Catholic USCCB note on verse 5 understands Paul as withdrawing the accusation in reverence for the law when told Ananias is high priest. Calvin’s comment on verse 5 instead reads an ironic rebuke: Paul recognizes nothing in the man’s conduct worthy of the priestly calling. These are explanations offered in commentaries, not official church rulings that settle Paul’s precise intention for every reader.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The scene recalls John 18:19–23, where Jesus is struck during questioning and asks why He was struck if He spoke rightly. This is a supported parallel in the unjust treatment of a witness and a challenge to it; the words and responses are not identical.

1 Peter 2:19–23 identifies Jesus as the example for His people under unjust suffering, entrusting Himself to the One who judges justly. That wider teaching keeps the focus on following Christ, rather than using a disputed detail of Paul’s response as permission for every angry reaction.
