Passage

Genesis 41:53-57

The Famine Begins

Berean Standard Bible

53 When the seven years of abundance in the land of Egypt came to an end, 54 the seven years of famine began, just as Joseph had said. And although there was famine in every country, there was food throughout the land of Egypt. 55 When extreme hunger came to all the land of Egypt and the people cried out to Pharaoh for food, he told all the Egyptians, “Go to Joseph and do whatever he tells you.”

56 When the famine had spread over all the land, Joseph opened up all the storehouses and sold grain to the Egyptians; for the famine was severe in the land of Egypt. 57 And every nation came to Joseph in Egypt to buy grain, because the famine was severe over all the earth.

What is the context?

The seven good years end, and the famine begins just as Joseph said. The shortage reaches beyond Egypt, but Egypt has food because preparations were made during the abundance. The warnings that sounded remote have become the conditions under which people must now live.

When the Egyptians cry to Pharaoh for food, he directs them to Joseph and tells them to follow his instructions. Joseph opens the storehouses and sells grain. The authority entrusted to him during the years of preparation now becomes responsibility for distributing what has been preserved.

People from other nations also come to Egypt to buy food because the famine is widespread. The account moves from an Egyptian crisis to a need that draws outsiders to Joseph. That prepares the next chapter, where Jacob learns there is grain in Egypt and sends Joseph's brothers to obtain it.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand the famine as the fulfillment of the warning and the moment when Joseph's preparation preserves life. Wesley's introduction to Genesis 41 and note on verse 54 connect his appointment with provision for Jacob's family and the surrounding lands.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 7:11–14 explicitly connects the famine with the family's journeys for grain and eventual reunion with Joseph. Psalm 105:16–22 also places the famine and Joseph's advance arrival within God's care. The food available in Egypt becomes a means of preserving the family through which the promise continues.

Joseph's role as the appointed provider of food also offers a helpful Christian parallel to Jesus as the giver of life. Wesley's note on Genesis 41:44 develops Joseph's role as a distributor in its comparison with Christ. In John 6:27–35, Jesus directs people beyond food that perishes to the life the Father gives through Him. Joseph's grain sustains people through famine; the greater gift Jesus offers is eternal life.
