Passage

Exodus 14:1-14

Why did God lead Israel into a place that looked like a trap?

Berean Standard Bible

1 Then the LORD said to Moses, 2 “Tell the Israelites to turn back and encamp before Pi-hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea. You are to encamp by the sea, directly opposite Baal-zephon. 3 For Pharaoh will say of the Israelites, ‘They are wandering the land in confusion; the wilderness has boxed them in.’ 4 And I will harden Pharaoh’s heart so that he will pursue them. But I will gain honor by means of Pharaoh and all his army, and the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD.” So this is what the Israelites did.

5 When the king of Egypt was told that the people had fled, Pharaoh and his officials changed their minds about them and said, “What have we done? We have released Israel from serving us.” 6 So Pharaoh prepared his chariot and took his army with him. 7 He took 600 of the best chariots, and all the other chariots of Egypt, with officers over all of them. 8 And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt so that he pursued the Israelites, who were marching out defiantly. 9 The Egyptians—all Pharaoh’s horses and chariots, horsemen and troops—pursued the Israelites and overtook them as they camped by the sea near Pi-hahiroth, opposite Baal-zephon.

10 As Pharaoh approached, the Israelites looked up and saw the Egyptians marching after them, and they were terrified and cried out to the LORD. 11 They said to Moses, “Was it because there were no graves in Egypt that you brought us into the wilderness to die? What have you done to us by bringing us out of Egypt? 12 Did we not say to you in Egypt, ‘Leave us alone so that we may serve the Egyptians’? For it would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness.” 13 But Moses told the people, “Do not be afraid. Stand firm and you will see the LORD’s salvation, which He will accomplish for you today; for the Egyptians you see today, you will never see again. 14 The LORD will fight for you; you need only to be still.”

What is the context?

Israel’s apparent trap is part of the route God commands. He tells Moses to turn back and camp beside the sea, and explains that Pharaoh will take this as a sign that the people are confused and cornered. God will gain honor through what happens to Pharaoh and his army, and the Egyptians will know who the LORD is.

Pharaoh and his officials regret losing Israel’s service. He gathers his chariots and pursues the people, who have been marching out boldly. Their confidence collapses when they see the approaching army. They cry to God, accuse Moses of bringing them out to die and say slavery would have been better.

Moses answers their fear with a promise about this particular rescue: the LORD will fight for them. They are to stand firm and watch what He does. The next part of the account shows how this trust goes together with obedience: when God tells them to move forward, they must go.

The places named in verse 2 have not been definitively identified, as the USCCB study notes acknowledge. The account’s central situation is clear: Israel is beside the sea with an army approaching, and God has brought the people there.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Calvin read Moses’ reassurance as a call to trust God’s power when Israel cannot rescue itself. The people’s fear and complaints are met by a deliverance that depends on the LORD.

The explanation of God hardening Pharaoh differs. Calvin understands God to be actively directing the Egyptians toward the judgment that will display His power. Wesley’s earlier note on Exodus 4:21 describes hardening as God finally permitting Satan to harden a ruler who has repeatedly refused the light already given. These are the two teachers’ explanations of God’s action and Pharaoh’s refusal.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Psalm 106:6–13 looks back on this crisis and says Israel rebelled by the sea. God nevertheless saved the people for the sake of His name, to make His power known. Their rescue displays His faithfulness even when they are frightened and ungrateful; it leads them to believe His promises and sing His praise.

This belongs to a wider biblical theme: God’s rescue reveals His mercy and calls forth a changed life. Ephesians 2:4–10 describes salvation in Christ as God’s gift, through faith, so that no one can boast. Those He makes alive are created in Christ for good works. This is a connection between the two accounts of God’s grace, rather than a claim that every detail of the sea crossing describes a stage of Christian conversion.
