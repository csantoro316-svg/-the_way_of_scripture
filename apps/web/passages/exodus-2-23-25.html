Passage

Exodus 2:23-25

What does it mean that God remembered His covenant?

Berean Standard Bible

23 After a long time, the king of Egypt died. The Israelites groaned and cried out under their burden of slavery, and their cry for deliverance from bondage ascended to God. 24 So God heard their groaning, and He remembered His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. 25 God saw the Israelites and took notice.

What is the context?

Time passes and the king of Egypt dies, but Israel remains enslaved. Their groaning and cry for deliverance rise to God. The change of king has not itself brought freedom.

The account gives a series of responses: God hears, remembers His covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, sees the people and takes notice. Their suffering is brought into direct relationship with the promises He made to their ancestors. The next chapter shows His response taking shape in Moses’ call.

How is this passage commonly understood?

The shared reading understands God remembering as His faithfulness moving toward action. Calvin and Wesley both explain that God has remained mindful of His promise. The language tells the reader that He is attending to the people’s distress and acting on the covenant; it does not describe Him recovering information He had forgotten.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Genesis 15:13–16 joins the promised family’s period of oppression with God’s judgment of the oppressing nation and their eventual departure. Exodus now brings the people’s cry and God’s remembrance of that promise together. The deliverance that follows is rooted in His faithfulness.

A later connection appears in Luke 1:68–75, where Zechariah praises God for redemption, mercy and remembrance of His covenant with Abraham. That song looks toward salvation and service to God in holiness. The repeated language links the hope surrounding Jesus with the earlier story of God hearing and delivering His people.
