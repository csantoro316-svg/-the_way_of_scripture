Passage

2 Timothy 1:6-7

How is Timothy to use the gift God has given him?

Berean Standard Bible

6 For this reason I remind you to fan into flame the gift of God, which is in you through the laying on of my hands. 7 For God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control.

What is the context?

Because Paul recognizes Timothy’s sincere faith, he urges him to put God’s gift fully to use. He recalls the laying on of his hands and uses the picture of a fire stirred into flame. Thayer’s entry confirms the sense of kindling or rekindling. The picture calls for active care; it does not tell us exactly how far Timothy’s zeal had declined.

Power belongs with love and self-control. Courage in ministry is not permission to become harsh or reckless. The word translated self-control includes moderation and disciplined judgment (Thayer). Verse 8 will show what this strength is for: bearing witness without shame and enduring suffering for the gospel.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Calvin and Wesley read the fire image as a call to exercise God’s gifts rather than let them lie unused. Both connect courage with love and a disciplined life. Calvin particularly emphasizes God’s Spirit equipping ministers for their calling; Wesley applies the encouragement to Christians more broadly.

Traditions explain the laying on of hands differently. The Catholic Church describes apostolic laying on of hands as transmitting the Spirit’s gift in ordination (Catechism 1556). The Baptist 1689 Confession 26.9 describes setting apart a minister already fitted and gifted by the Spirit. Both recognize a calling from God and a public act of setting someone apart.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Acts 4:23–31 gives a related picture of believers facing threats. They turn to God, ask for boldness and speak His word through the Holy Spirit’s power. Their courage serves the spread of the message.

In 1 Corinthians 12:4–7, the Spirit’s varied gifts are given for the common good. Timothy’s gift belongs within that purpose: God equips people so that others can be helped and the church built up.
