Passage

Judges 6:33-40

What is Gideon asking God to confirm?

Berean Standard Bible

33 Then all the Midianites, Amalekites, and other people of the east gathered together, crossed over the Jordan, and camped in the Valley of Jezreel. 34 So the Spirit of the LORD came upon Gideon, who blew the ram’s horn and rallied the Abiezrites behind him. 35 Calling them to arms, Gideon sent messengers throughout Manasseh, as well as Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali, so that they came up to meet him.

36 Then Gideon said to God, “If You are going to save Israel by my hand, as You have said, 37 then behold, I will place a fleece of wool on the threshing floor. If there is dew only on the fleece and all the ground is dry, then I will know that You are going to save Israel by my hand, as You have said.” 38 And that is what happened. When Gideon arose the next morning, he squeezed the fleece and wrung out the dew—a bowlful of water. 39 Then Gideon said to God, “Do not be angry with me; let me speak one more time. Please allow me one more test with the fleece. This time let it be dry, and the ground covered with dew.” 40 And that night God did so. Only the fleece was dry, and dew covered the ground.

What is the context?

The invading coalition gathers in the Valley of Jezreel. The Spirit of the LORD comes upon Gideon, and he sounds the ram’s horn. His own Abiezrite community rallies, and messengers summon men from Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali.

The Hebrew verb לָבֵשׁ, labash (lah-VAHSH), ordinarily concerns putting on clothing. In this verse, Brown–Driver–Briggs describes the Spirit as clothing Himself with Gideon—an image of taking hold of him for the work. The story gives the credit for his ability to lead to God’s Spirit.

Gideon then asks for confirmation of the rescue God has already promised. First he asks for dew on a fleece while the ground remains dry. After receiving that sign, he asks that the fleece be dry while the ground is wet. God grants both requests. Gideon is asking about the promise already spoken to him, not inventing a meaning for an unrelated event.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley understands the requests as prayer for stronger faith and encouragement for those preparing to fight. He emphasizes God’s tenderness toward weakness. Gill similarly considers encouragement for the soldiers as a possible reason and distinguishes a request for confirmation from complete disbelief in God’s power. The text records Gideon’s repeated requests and God’s patient response without reporting every motive behind them.

Henry also draws an illustration from the wet fleece and ground: God can distinguish the small people He is rescuing from the larger enemy around them. He extends the dew image to God’s blessing reaching beyond Israel. That is his devotional comparison, rather than an explanation the passage itself supplies for each object.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The clothing image has a helpful later parallel. After His resurrection, Jesus tells His disciples to remain in Jerusalem until they are clothed with power from on high (Luke 24:44–49). Acts 1:4–8 identifies the promised gift as the Holy Spirit and connects that power with bearing witness to Jesus to the ends of the earth.

The assignment expands from leading a rescue in Israel to announcing the risen Savior among the nations. The common theme is God equipping people for the work He gives them. The shared image helps explain that dependence without making Gideon’s fleece the model for how the disciples must receive guidance.
