Passage

2 Corinthians 1:8-11

Where did Paul turn when the burden was beyond his strength?

Berean Standard Bible

8 We do not want you to be unaware, brothers, of the hardships we encountered in the province of Asia. We were under a burden far beyond our ability to endure, so that we despaired even of life. 9 Indeed, we felt we were under the sentence of death, in order that we would not trust in ourselves, but in God, who raises the dead.

10 He has delivered us from such a deadly peril, and He will deliver us. In Him we have placed our hope that He will yet again deliver us, 11 as you help us by your prayers. Then many will give thanks on our behalf for the favor shown us in answer to their prayers.

What is the context?

Paul tells the Corinthians how severe the danger in the province of Asia had been. He and his companions were burdened beyond their ability to endure and despaired even of life. He does not give enough detail here to identify the event with certainty.

The experience left them expecting death. Paul describes its effect on their trust: they could not rest their hope on their own resources, but on the God who raises the dead. His honesty about being overwhelmed belongs within the testimony of faith, rather than being edited out of it.

God delivered them from deadly peril, and Paul continues to place his hope in Him. This confidence does not mean Paul expects never to die. His description of God as the one who raises the dead puts hope beyond the limits of escaping one danger.

The Corinthians also have a part through prayer. Paul connects their help with the favor received and the thanksgiving that many will offer. He presents people bringing their needs to God and thanking Him together, not prayer as a way to control Him.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as a testimony of deliverance that teaches dependence on God and invites shared prayer. They do not all identify the danger in the same way. Wesley’s note on verse 8 suggests the events in Acts 19; the USCCB note on verses 9–10 leaves open whether the threat was an illness or an external danger. The passage’s lesson does not depend on settling that question.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Romans 4:17–25 likewise describes faith directed toward the God who gives life to the dead. It connects Abraham’s hope with believers’ trust in the God who raised Jesus our Lord.

That connection places Paul’s rescue within a larger hope. God’s saving purpose is not exhausted by preserving a person’s present circumstances. The resurrection of Jesus gives reason to trust Him even when human strength has reached its limit.
