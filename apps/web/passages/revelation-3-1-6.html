Passage

Revelation 3:1-6

What if a church looks alive but Jesus says it is dead?

Berean Standard Bible

1 “To the angel of the church in Sardis write: These are the words of the One who holds the seven spirits of God and the seven stars. I know your deeds; you have a reputation for being alive, yet you are dead. 2 Wake up and strengthen what remains, which is about to die; for I have found your deeds incomplete in the sight of My God. 3 Remember, then, what you have received and heard. Keep it and repent. If you do not wake up, I will come like a thief, and you will not know the hour when I will come upon you.

4 But you do have a few people in Sardis who have not soiled their garments, and because they are worthy, they will walk with Me in white. 5 Like them, he who overcomes will be dressed in white. And I will never blot out his name from the Book of Life, but I will confess his name before My Father and His angels. 6 He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches.

What is the context?

Sardis has a reputation for life, but Jesus sees a different reality. He holds the seven spirits of God and the seven stars, images already introduced in chapter 1. The church’s reputation cannot substitute for His assessment of its deeds.

His instructions give a way forward: wake up, strengthen what remains, remember what they received and heard, keep it, and repent. Something remains that can be strengthened, but the danger is urgent. If they ignore Him, His coming upon them will be as unexpected as a thief.

Jesus distinguishes a few who have not soiled their garments. Their faithfulness is known to Him even within this failing church. They will walk with Him in white. The one who overcomes receives the promises of white clothing, a name retained in the Book of Life, and Christ’s acknowledgment before His Father and the angels.

The letter holds warning and encouragement together. It calls a complacent community to repentance while giving faithful people reason to continue. It does not report the church’s later response.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill both see a contrast between outward reputation and spiritual reality. Wesley understands the remaining things as knowledge, desires and convictions that need strengthening. Gill includes gospel truths and struggling members, while denying that God’s saving work itself can die. Both call for an active response to Christ’s warning.

They differ over the promise about the Book of Life. Gill connects the book with God’s certain choice of His people and says their names can never be erased. Wesley treats the promise as belonging to those who overcome and contrasts it with the failing leader at Sardis. These readings reflect a wider difference over whether a believer can finally fall away. The promise to the one who overcomes should be heard alongside the call to repent in either reading.

Gill also applies Sardis to the churches of the Reformation and their later decline. Wesley reads the seven churches as conditions found across ages, without assigning each one a successive historical period. Those applications should be distinguished from what the letter directly says to Sardis.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Christ’s promise to acknowledge the faithful before His Father echoes His teaching in Matthew 10:32–33. The Book of Life appears again in Revelation 21:22–27, where it identifies those admitted to the holy city. Sardis therefore brings a church’s present response into view against its ultimate hope: being known by Christ and sharing life in God’s presence. A public reputation is temporary; His acknowledgment reaches into that lasting future.

What is our position?

Our study I Am a Disciple describes following Jesus as a life shaped by His teaching, conduct and self-giving love. That gives us a direct point of application here: the name or appearance of discipleship must be accompanied by actually following Him.
