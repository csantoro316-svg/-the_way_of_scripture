Passage

Matthew 23:16-22

Promises Cannot Be Separated from God

Berean Standard Bible

16 Woe to you, blind guides! You say, ‘If anyone swears by the temple, it means nothing; but if anyone swears by the gold of the temple, he is bound by his oath.’ 17 You blind fools! Which is greater: the gold, or the temple that makes it sacred? 18 And you say, ‘If anyone swears by the altar, it means nothing; but if anyone swears by the gift on it, he is bound by his oath.’ 19 You blind men! Which is greater: the gift, or the altar that makes it sacred?

20 So then, he who swears by the altar swears by it and by everything on it. 21 And he who swears by the temple swears by it and by the One who dwells in it. 22 And he who swears by heaven swears by God’s throne and by the One who sits on it.

What is the context?

Jesus next calls the leaders blind guides and examines their rules about oaths. They treat swearing by the temple as nonbinding but swearing by its gold as binding. They make a similar distinction between the altar and the gift placed on it.

Jesus asks what makes the gold and the gift sacred. The temple and altar give them their setting before God; placing greater weight on the gold or gift reverses that relationship. Exodus 29:36–37 describes the altar's consecration and the holiness associated with it.

Jesus then traces the oaths back to God. An oath by the altar includes what is on it; an oath by the temple involves the One who dwells there; an oath by heaven involves God's throne. These distinctions do not create a way to speak as though God is involved while escaping responsibility to Him. The law already called people to keep the promises they made (Numbers 30:1–2; Deuteronomy 23:21–23).

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly read this as exposing false distinctions that make some promises appear binding while excusing others. Wesley on 23:20 emphasizes that an oath involving sacred things appeals to God; the US Catholic bishops' note on 23:16–22 stresses the reversal of what has greater value.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

God forms a people whose words can be trusted. Jesus' teaching in Matthew 5:33–37 also challenges oath-making and calls for honest speech. Ephesians 4:20–25 connects putting away falsehood with the new life learned in Christ. Truthfulness matters because believers belong to one another; honest speech serves the shared life God is building.
