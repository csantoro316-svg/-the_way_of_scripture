Passage

Acts 10:1-16

Why does God prepare both Cornelius and Peter?

Berean Standard Bible

1 At Caesarea there was a man named Cornelius, a centurion in what was called the Italian Regiment. 2 He and all his household were devout and God-fearing. He gave generously to the people and prayed to God regularly. 3 One day at about the ninth hour, he had a clear vision of an angel of God who came to him and said, “Cornelius!” 4 Cornelius stared at him in fear and asked, “What is it, Lord?” The angel answered, “Your prayers and gifts to the poor have ascended as a memorial offering before God. 5 Now send men to Joppa to call for a man named Simon who is called Peter. 6 He is staying with Simon the tanner, whose house is by the sea.” 7 When the angel who spoke to him had gone, Cornelius called two of his servants and a devout soldier from among his attendants. 8 He explained what had happened and sent them to Joppa.

9 The next day at about the sixth hour, as the men were approaching the city on their journey, Peter went up on the roof to pray. 10 He became hungry and wanted something to eat, but while the meal was being prepared, he fell into a trance. 11 He saw heaven open and something like a large sheet being let down to earth by its four corners. 12 It contained all kinds of four-footed animals and reptiles of the earth, as well as birds of the air. 13 Then a voice said to him: “Get up, Peter, kill and eat!” 14 “No, Lord!” Peter answered. “I have never eaten anything impure or unclean.” 15 The voice spoke to him a second time: “Do not call anything impure that God has made clean.” 16 This happened three times, and all at once the sheet was taken back up into heaven.

What is the context?

Cornelius is a centurion in Caesarea. Luke describes him and his household as devout and God-fearing, and specifically mentions his generosity and regular prayer. An angel tells him that his prayers and gifts have been remembered before God and directs him to send for Peter in Joppa.

Cornelius obeys by sending two servants and a devout soldier. Meanwhile, Peter goes to the roof to pray. He becomes hungry while food is being prepared and sees a vision of a sheet containing animals, reptiles and birds.

The command to kill and eat meets Peter’s objection that he has never eaten anything impure or unclean. The voice tells him not to call impure what God has made clean. The event happens three times. Peter does not yet understand its full meaning; the next encounter will bring the vision and the people sent by Cornelius together.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand these visions as God preparing a Gentile household and a Jewish apostle for the same meeting. Peter’s explanation in verse 28 and his retelling in Acts 11:1–18 make the welcome of Gentiles central to their meaning.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

The events are coordinated, but not through a plan either man has made. One receives instructions to send, while the other is being prepared to go. God’s purpose includes a real encounter in which people hear the message about Jesus.

In Acts 11:13–18, Peter explains that Cornelius was promised a message through which his household would be saved. The church’s eventual conclusion is that God has granted Gentiles repentance leading to life. That later explanation supplies the larger purpose of these unusual events.

What is our position?

Should We Seek Signs From God? includes Cornelius in Acts 10 among the specific biblical accounts of God communicating through visions at important points in His larger purpose. The study distinguishes these recorded events from treating every present-day dream, impression or secondhand story as a dependable message from God. It leaves the broader debate about continuing visions open while insisting that claims must be tested.
