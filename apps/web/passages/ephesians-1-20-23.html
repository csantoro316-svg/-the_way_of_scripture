Passage

Ephesians 1:20-23

What does Christ’s resurrection show us about His authority?

Berean Standard Bible

20 which He exerted in Christ when He raised Him from the dead and seated Him at His right hand in the heavenly realms, 21 far above all rule and authority, power and dominion, and every name that is named, not only in the present age but also in the one to come.

22 And God put everything under His feet and made Him head over everything for the church, 23 which is His body, the fullness of Him who fills all in all.

What is the context?

Paul now identifies the mighty work behind his prayer about God’s power: the Father raised Christ from the dead and seated Him at His right hand in the heavenly realms.

Christ is far above every rule, authority, power and dominion, and above every name in the present age and the age to come. Paul’s description reaches beyond any single ruler or present circumstance.

God has placed everything under Christ’s feet and made Him head over everything for the church. The people Paul is praying for belong to the One whose authority he has just described.

He calls the church Christ’s body and speaks of His fullness. The closing description holds together the exalted Lord and the people who share life in relation to Him. It completes the prayer’s movement from their need for understanding to the greatness of the risen Christ.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Christians commonly understand this as proclaiming Christ’s resurrection, supreme authority and relationship with the church as His body. Wesley’s notes on verses 20–23 emphasize His rule and the body’s dependence on Him. The USCCB notes on verses 15–23 likewise connect the church’s hope with the Father’s power in exalting Christ.

The closing expression about fullness has been explained in more than one way. Wesley’s note on verse 23 relates the fullness to Christ as the fullness of the Father. The USCCB note also discusses Christ filling the church, alongside other possible explanations of the sentence. These readings differ in where they place the emphasis: on Christ’s fullness from the Father or on the church receiving its fullness through Christ.

Where does this fit in God's plan?

Psalm 8:3–9 praises God for the place He gives humanity within His creation and speaks of all things being placed under human feet. Hebrews 2:5–9 takes up that wording and directs attention to Jesus, crowned with glory and honor after suffering death.

The New Testament itself therefore connects the creation theme of Psalm 8 with the crucified and exalted Jesus. Read alongside Ephesians, this places His authority within God’s larger purpose for creation and the world to come. The church’s life belongs under the Lord through whom that purpose is carried forward.
