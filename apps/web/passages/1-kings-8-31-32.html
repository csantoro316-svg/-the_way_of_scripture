Passage

1 Kings 8:31-32

Who can judge rightly when people dispute what happened?

Berean Standard Bible

31 When a man sins against his neighbor and is required to take an oath, and he comes to take an oath before Your altar in this temple, 32 then may You hear from heaven and act. May You judge Your servants, condemning the wicked man by bringing down on his own head what he has done, and justifying the righteous man by rewarding him according to his righteousness.

What is the context?

Solomon’s prayer turns to particular situations the people may face. The first concerns a person who has sinned against a neighbor and is required to take an oath before the temple altar.

The king asks God to hear from heaven and judge rightly: let wrongdoing fall back on the wrongdoer, and let the person in the right be vindicated. The appeal is to God’s ability to establish the truth and act justly.

How is this passage commonly understood?

Wesley and Gill understand this as a disputed case brought before God, especially when the available testimony does not settle it. The oath is a solemn appeal to Him as witness and judge.

On that reading, the request concerns justice between neighbors. The prayer asks God to distinguish guilt from innocence; it does not say that taking an oath makes a dishonest person truthful or guarantees that every human verdict is correct.
